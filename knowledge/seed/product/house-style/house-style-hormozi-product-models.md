**When to use this:** By default whenever you frame a feature, offer, market, price or retention problem: apply these models in our own brand's voice, never his name, likeness, catchphrases or figures with customers. Compliance (section 4) overrides them.

**Source section:** 2. House style: Alex Hormozi's product-relevant models

### What "house style" means and how far it reaches

**[HOUSE STYLE]** Sections 2 and 3 describe Alex Hormozi's published models as the owner's chosen default wherever he has published one. It is a preference. It is not independent evidence, no independent study of these models was found in the research, and every performance figure attached to them is his own unaudited claim. His examples come from gyms, coaching, supplements and community businesses, and one study guide cautions that his illustrative comparisons are simplified and are not evidence of universal behaviour ([SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/)). Agents apply the structures in the company's own voice and under the precedence rules in section 4.

### The value equation

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** Hormozi's value equation is: value equals dream outcome multiplied by perceived likelihood of achievement, divided by time delay multiplied by effort and sacrifice. The instruction is to raise the top two and cut the bottom two, and driving delay and effort toward zero is presented as the strongest lever. He advises building early wins so that customers feel on track ([Dean Yeong, book notes on $100M Offers](https://www.deanyeong.com/book-notes/100m-offers); [SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/)). Perceived likelihood is raised by proof, credibility and guarantees ([SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/)). The equation is a framing device and not a measured formula; no source assigns units to its terms.

### The value equation as a product-design test

**[INFERENCE; adapts HOUSE STYLE]** The department uses the value equation as a design test on every proposed feature, flow or offer by asking which lever it moves. Dream outcome maps to the end result of the customer's job. Perceived likelihood maps to reliability, proof, evaluation pass rates for AI features, and guarantees. Time delay maps to time-to-first-value and time to activation. Effort and sacrifice map to setup steps, integrations, learning curve and form length. A feature that moves none of the four levers is a candidate to cut. The mapping is the researchers' adaptation; it lines up with mainstream activation practice (the earliest milestone that predicts retention, section 9) and with Torres's opportunities (section 6), and the levers are defined in the second-hand summaries cited above ([Dean Yeong, book notes on $100M Offers](https://www.deanyeong.com/book-notes/100m-offers); [SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/)).

**[INFERENCE]** The table gives the test in a form an agent can apply to a specification. The questions and measures are the researchers' synthesis.

| Lever | Question to ask of the feature | Software measure to attach |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome (raise) | Does this make the end result of the customer's job bigger or more complete? | Outcome metric of the opportunity; north-star input |
| Perceived likelihood (raise) | Does this make success more certain or more believable before and after purchase? | Task success rate, eval pass rate, proof shown, uptime |
| Time delay (cut) | Does this shorten the time to the first real result? | Time-to-first-value, activation window |
| Effort and sacrifice (cut) | Does this remove steps, decisions, learning or waiting from the customer? | Step count, form elements, Single Ease Question, support contacts |

### Market selection: four indicators, all required

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** In a video dated 21 January 2021 Hormozi gives four indicators for choosing a market and treats all four as required: the customer feels real pain (a need and not a nice-to-have), the market is stable or growing, prospects are easy to reach through places where they gather, and they have money to spend. Missing one is a dealbreaker. He names health, wealth and relationships as lasting needs. His examples: an unemployed audience fitted on paper but could not pay, and moving the same coaching offer from students to seniors improved the market. He ranks personal experience of a market above the four checks, because knowing its pains leads to a better product ([Hormozi, The Key To Finding a Profitable Market, third-party transcript, video dated 21 Jan 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/the-key-to-finding-a-profitable-market)). **[INFERENCE]** The four indicators work as a segment-screening checklist; "easy to reach" and "money to spend" correspond to the standard segment criteria of accessibility and ability to pay.

### Market first, then offer, then persuasion

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** Book notes on $100M Offers give his order of importance as market first, offer strength second and persuasion skill third. He advises picking a market with existing demand and staying in it long enough to learn, because switching audiences restarts the learning ([Dean Yeong, book notes on $100M Offers](https://www.deanyeong.com/book-notes/100m-offers)). **[GAP]** His claims about niche price multiples (a niche product priced at a multiple of a generic one) and the instruction to commit to the niche were not in any fetched source; they are believed to be in the book and are unverified.

### Offer creation in five steps

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** A study guide to $100M Offers describes five steps: define the dream outcome; list every obstacle the customer meets before, during and after; turn each obstacle into a "how to" solution; generate many delivery vehicles for each solution; then trim the high-cost, low-value items and stack the high-value remainder into one offer. One-to-many assets such as templates and systems are favoured because they carry high value at low ongoing cost ([SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/)). **[GAP]** "Trim and stack" has no primary source in this research: the delivery-cube video does not mention it, and his free course and audiobook episodes, the likely primary sources, were not reachable.

### The delivery cube: six ways to vary how a solution is delivered

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** In a video dated 17 May 2021 Hormozi breaks delivery into six parts. Ratio: one-to-one, small group or one-to-many. Client involvement: do it yourself, done with you or done for you. Support level: which channels the customer can use, from email or chat up to phone or video. Consumption: live or recorded, and written, audio or video. Speed and convenience: faster results or less friction. The ten-times and one-tenth test: what you would add at ten times the price, and how you would deliver more at a tenth of it ([Hormozi, I Broke Down Every High Margin Service Into 6 Buckets (Delivery Cube), third-party transcript, video dated 17 May 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/i-broke-down-every-high-margin-service-into-6-buckets-delivery-cube)). He observes that done-for-you is the easiest to sell and the hardest to run, and that the harder something is to fulfil the harder he finds it to sell at scale. Support channels can separate tiers, for example email for basic and phone or video for premium ([Hormozi, I Broke Down Every High Margin Service Into 6 Buckets (Delivery Cube), third-party transcript, video dated 17 May 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/i-broke-down-every-high-margin-service-into-6-buckets-delivery-cube)).

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** The stated aim of the delivery cube is to find value already inside the business so that revenue rises without much new cost; his analogy is a sawmill selling its sawdust, and he cites delivery and streaming brands to show that customers pay for speed and convenience ([Hormozi, I Broke Down Every High Margin Service Into 6 Buckets (Delivery Cube), third-party transcript, video dated 17 May 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/i-broke-down-every-high-margin-service-into-6-buckets-delivery-cube)).

### Over-deliver first, then make fulfilment efficient

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** The summaries of $100M Offers report his advice to over-deliver at first to create demand and cash, then make fulfilment more efficient without reducing customer value, balancing ease of selling against ease of fulfilling ([SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/); [Dean Yeong, book notes on $100M Offers](https://www.deanyeong.com/book-notes/100m-offers)). Components can be presented as stacked bonuses, which widens the gap between price and perceived value. Guarantees improve an offer but cannot rescue a weak product, and on a weak product they drive refunds ([Dean Yeong, book notes on $100M Offers](https://www.deanyeong.com/book-notes/100m-offers)).

### Pricing power and premium positioning

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** Hormozi advises charging a premium supported by higher perceived value, argues that a price that stings slightly makes customers more invested in getting results, and aims for a wide gap between cost and price. A well-built offer is meant to make buyers judge value instead of comparing prices ([Dean Yeong, book notes on $100M Offers](https://www.deanyeong.com/book-notes/100m-offers); [SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/)). A summary of a 12 May 2026 episode ties premium pricing to delivery: his claim is that a business with unmatched speed, reliability or quality can charge 20 to 40% more and that the higher price funds better talent and delivery ([Shortform summary of The Game Ep 969, 12 May 2026](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2026-05-12-episode-summary-solving-6-scaling-problems-ep-969)). The 20 to 40% figure is his own unaudited claim.

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** A summary of a 23 April 2026 episode reports his claim that cutting a coaching price from $3,000 to $2,500 a month did not change retention and cost roughly $500,000 a month in revenue, from which he concludes that retention in such businesses depends on activation and engagement more than on price ([Shortform summary of The Game Ep 964, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2026-04-23-episode-summary-before-you-run-ads-fix-this-one-thing-first-ep-964)). This is one unaudited case from a coaching business.

### The offer ladder: what a money model is

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** In the 2025 book $100M Money Models, as summarised, a money model is a sequence of offers in which the business finds each further customer problem and offers to solve it, with the aim of recovering customer acquisition cost within about 30 days ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/)). A write-up of the August 2025 launch event records his stated rule that 30-day gross profit should exceed twice the sum of acquisition cost and cost of goods, and gives Skool's own offer as an example: 14 days free, then $99 a month ([jxnl.co report on the Money Models launch, 20 Aug 2025](https://jxnl.co/writing/2025/08/20/the-100m-money-models-the-complete-report)). The business figures he cited at that event (his gym, Gym Launch, Prestige Labs, Skool user growth) are in the source and are unaudited.

### The offer ladder: attraction offers

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** The attraction offers listed in the summary of $100M Money Models are win-your-money-back, giveaway, decoy, buy-X-get-Y, and pay-less-now-or-pay-more-later ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/)). These are front-of-ladder offers meant to turn strangers into customers. Several of them need legal review before use with EU consumers (section 4).

### The offer ladder: upsell offers

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** The upsell types in the summary of $100M Money Models are the classic upsell (offer the next solution when the need appears), the menu upsell (un-sell what the customer does not need, prescribe what they do, give an A or B choice, keep a card on file), the anchor upsell, and the rollover upsell (credit past spend toward a bigger offer) ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/)).

### The offer ladder: downsell offers

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** The downsell types in the summary of $100M Money Models are a payment plan, a trial with a penalty, and a feature downsell. His rule is never to sell the same product for less: change the terms or the features instead ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/)).

### The offer ladder: continuity offers and sequencing

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** The continuity offers in the summary of $100M Money Models are a continuity bonus, a discount for commitment, and a waived fee. His sequencing advice is to build one stage at a time with continuity last, to launch new offers cheaply for feedback and then raise the price until revenue stops rising, and to prefer many ways of selling the same product over many products ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/)).

### Solving the next problem

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary] [GAP]** "Solving the next problem" as a named problem-solution cycle was not found in any primary source. It appears only as the definition of a money model (each offer solves the problem the previous one creates or reveals) and as the classic upsell, both via a second-hand summary ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/)). Agents may use the idea as a sequencing heuristic and must not attribute a named cycle or exact wording to him.

### Focus and saying no

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** In a video dated 16 August 2021 Hormozi borrows the woman in the red dress from The Matrix as an image for attractive side opportunities that pull an owner off the main goal, and says such temptations become more appealing as the business succeeds. Side deals look like free money but cost the focus and time the core business needs; he frames strategy as choosing what to refuse and describes declining consulting requests. He argues that simple businesses scale and elaborate ones fail, and that concentrating on the main revenue source and letting it compound produces the large results. By his own account, starting a second business too early stalled growth for about two years, and he advises that a business should run without the owner's daily work before the owner adds another ([Hormozi, The Biggest Mistake Everyone is Making, third-party transcript, video dated 16 Aug 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/the-biggest-mistake-everyone-is-making)). A second-hand community post from October 2024 likewise attributes to him the view that opportunities multiply with success and become harder to refuse ([Skool community post by Alexi Drouin, Oct 2024](https://www.skool.com/acceleratoruniversity/the-woman-in-the-red-dress-alex-hormozis-lesson-from-the-matrix)).

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** In a video dated 11 March 2026 he treats saying no as a discipline problem: the more capable a person becomes, the more opportunities arrive and the more must be declined; one thing done well for a long time can become large, while two things split attention so that neither does. He tells a founder that adding info products or software alongside the core business is a distraction, likening it to a juice seller adding newspapers, and tests new ideas by whether they serve the core ([Hormozi, You Have To Learn To Say "No", third-party transcript, 11 Mar 2026](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/you-have-to-learn-to-say-no)). A second-hand summary of a 12 May 2026 episode adds related advice: weigh opportunity cost across businesses, divest lower-potential ones even at a loss, pick one main sales channel and extend what works, and favour recurring revenue with switching costs ([Shortform summary of The Game Ep 969, 12 May 2026](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2026-05-12-episode-summary-solving-6-scaling-problems-ep-969)). **[GAP]** His keynote "New Distractions Kill" exists ([Global Player podcast listing, New Distractions Kill](https://globalplayer.com/podcasts/episodes/7DroGbD); [Podscripts listing, New Distractions Kill, not read](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/new-distractions-kill-keynote-speech-at-future-flipper-pt-2-ep-461)) but its transcript blocks automated access and was not read.

### Retention figures he cites for community businesses

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript] [PLATFORM CLAIM]** In a transcript of his retention video (transcribed August 2026; original upload date unknown; the transcript writes "School" where Skool is evidently meant) he gives what he presents as platform figures for communities on Skool: average monthly retention of 80%, the best communities under 10% monthly churn, and retention under 70% described as poor and as making retention the highest-return fix. He describes churn as front-loaded: over 20% in months one to three, about 10% or less by day 90, and about 2% by month six. He states that halving churn from 20% to 10% doubles lifetime value ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)). These are his own unaudited platform figures for paid community memberships and are far worse than acceptable B2B SaaS retention (section 3).

### Activation: a fast first win

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** His activation advice in the retention video is to put the most valued element of the product into onboarding so that a new customer gets a fast win, ideally within 24 hours; to set expectations accurately at the point of sale; and to aim for a real outcome by day 90 and customer-to-customer connection by month six ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)). A second-hand summary of a 23 April 2026 episode adds the method for finding activation steps: compare high performers with churned clients, identify the steps the retained ones took, and standardise them ([Shortform summary of The Game Ep 964, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2026-04-23-episode-summary-before-you-run-ads-fix-this-one-thing-first-ep-964)). A community post attributes to him a 14-item churn checklist that includes a cancellation video and finding the activation point, the first "aha" moment; only two of the items were visible ([Skool community post on the churn checklist, Nov 2024](https://www.skool.com/acceleratoruniversity/churn-checklist-alex-hormozis-14-strategies)).

### Subtract before adding

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** By his account in the retention video, overwhelm is the top churn driver after price. His method is to ask customers which single thing they would keep if everything else were deleted and which thing they would cut. He cites one case that he says cut monthly churn from 30% to 5% by deleting content ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)). The case is a single unaudited community example.

### Cancellation feedback as a product input

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** His feedback routine in the retention video is to ask every cancelling customer why, tag the reasons (price, overwhelm, not using, missing feature, wrong expectation), act on the top one or two reasons after roughly every 20 cancellations and never on a single reply, and ask the best customers why they stay and do more of that. He also recommends seating support leadership next to the chief executive so that problems reach product quickly ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)).

### Price as a filter, and annual plans

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** In the retention video he advises ignoring price complaints from non-ideal customers; if ideal customers cite price, add a lower tier with features held back for the higher tier (his example is about $9 against $99 a month). He recommends annual plans at roughly 16 to 17% off, framed as "buy 10, get two", on the reasoning that retention stabilises because the customer decides once a year ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)). The figures are his own.

### Delivery capacity before paid growth

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** The summary of the 23 April 2026 episode reports his view that paid advertising multiplies revenue only when delivery capacity exists, and that a one-off transformation and an ongoing service should be charged for separately ([Shortform summary of The Game Ep 964, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2026-04-23-episode-summary-before-you-run-ads-fix-this-one-thing-first-ep-964)). **[INFERENCE] [GAP]** "Product quality before scaling" is best presented as an inference from this point, his retention-first material and his own story of adding a second business too early ([Hormozi, The Biggest Mistake Everyone is Making, third-party transcript, video dated 16 Aug 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/the-biggest-mistake-everyone-is-making)); no primary statement of such a rule was found, and the 12 May 2026 episode summary does not state one ([Shortform summary of The Game Ep 969, 12 May 2026](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2026-05-12-episode-summary-solving-6-scaling-problems-ep-969)).
