### B2B-only versus consumer-facing: the scope question that decides most of this

**[INFERENCE]** For a Swedish SaaS company the single most consequential compliance question is whether consumers can buy. The accessibility act's e-commerce category ([Riksdagen, SFS 2023:254, consolidated text amended up to SFS 2026:1026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2023254-om-vissa-produkters-och-tjansters_sfs-2023-254/)) and the withdrawal function ([CMS, Ny "ångerknapp" krävs på e-handelsplattformar, 15 Jun 2026](https://cms.law/sv/swe/legal-updates/ny-aangerknapp-kraevs-paa-e-handelsplattformar-fraan-den-19-juni-2026)) both follow consumer contracts, so a purely B2B product appears to sit outside both, while any self-serve plan open to consumers or to sole traders acting privately brings both into scope. Cookie consent, GDPR deceptive-design rules, AI disclosure and the Data Act apply regardless of customer type. The scope reading is the researchers' and must be confirmed by Legal.
