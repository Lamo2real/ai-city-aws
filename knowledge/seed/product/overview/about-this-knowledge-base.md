**When to use this:** Before relying on any Product document: to read a label, a "primary transcript", "second-hand summary" or "checked on primary source" qualifier, or a caveat correctly.

**Source section:** Opening summary and "How to read and split this document"

As of 11 November 2026 the foundational knowledge for an AI Product department is a three-layer stack: compliance rules that always win, independent evidence that sets methods and thresholds, and Alex Hormozi's published models as the default framing wherever he has published one. The house style gives the manager agent and its workers a compact set of tests: score every feature on the four levers of the value equation (bigger outcome, higher perceived likelihood, less delay, less effort), screen markets on four indicators (pain, growth, reachability, money), generate delivery options with the six-part delivery cube, sequence attraction, upsell, downsell and continuity offers, refuse side projects, and fix activation and retention before buying growth; every number attached to these models is his own unaudited claim from gyms, coaching and community businesses, and several pieces (trim and stack, the next-problem cycle, quality before scaling) were verified only through second-hand summaries or not at all. The evidence layer supplies what he has not published: the SVPG product operating model and its four risks, Teresa Torres's opportunity solution tree and assumption tests, scoring models used as aids and not as deciders, activation defined as the earliest milestone that predicts retention, A/B testing reserved for cases with enough traffic (a 5% revenue lift can need about 147,000 users per arm), evaluation suites as the specification for AI features, and hybrid pricing with predictable bills. The same layer covers design: Nielsen's heuristics, one question per page, five users per round for finding problems and twenty or more for numbers, SUS with an average of 68, WCAG 2.2 AA as the build target, and human-AI guidance from Microsoft, Apple, Google and NN/g. The compliance layer is concrete for a Sweden-based SaaS company selling in the EU: the Swedish accessibility act has applied to consumer e-commerce services since 28 June 2025 with sanction fees up to SEK 10 million, the online withdrawal function has applied since 19 June 2026, AI Act Article 50 disclosure has applied since 2 August 2026, analytics cookies need opt-in consent, and the Digital Fairness Act was still a reported draft on the research date. The benchmarks agents most want are the weakest part of the record: the best retention figures date from 2020, activation from 2022, feature adoption from 2019, and no 2025 to 2026 replacement was retrieved. The practical conclusion is that agents should treat discovery, judgment and evaluation design as the scarce work now that delivery is cheap, measure against the company's own trailing data, and keep pricing, legal wording, customer-facing claims and irreversible actions behind human approval. Where the research found no fetched source, this document says so instead of filling the hole from memory.

## How to read and split this document

This document is written to be cut into atomic knowledge-base entries for a vector index and a graph database. Every body paragraph is meant to stand alone: it opens with one or more labels, names its subject in full, and keeps each number next to its date and source link. Section numbers group domains; sub-headings name the single topic of the paragraphs beneath them. Tables repeat their source link in every row so that a row can be split out as its own entry. When splitting, keep the label, the date and the link with the claim.

The labels mean the following. **[HOUSE STYLE]** marks Alex or Leila Hormozi's published frameworks, which the owner has chosen as the department's default style where they exist; it is a preference and not independent evidence, and every number attached to it is his own self-reported, unaudited claim. No Leila Hormozi material was retrieved in this research, so every house-style entry here is Alex Hormozi's. **[DURABLE]** marks a principle, definition, formula or long-standing standard that has held for years. **[FAST-MOVING, date]** marks a figure, survey result, tool capability or legal status that will age, with the date of the source. **[VENDOR]** marks a figure or recommendation from a party with a commercial interest in the conclusion (tool makers, training firms, consultancies that sell the method, research firms that sell their research). **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** marks a platform's or tool maker's statement about itself that nobody independently verified. **[CONTESTED]** marks a point where sources disagree or where house style and evidence pull in different directions. **[INFERENCE]** marks a synthesis, template or convention written by the researchers that no single fetched source states; adopt it as a default and tune it against your own data. **[GAP]** marks something the research did not verify or could not find.

Qualifiers are added inside labels where they matter. For house-style material, "primary transcript" means the point was read on a third-party-hosted transcript of Hormozi's own video or on acquisition.com, and "second-hand summary" means it comes from someone else's summary of his book, podcast or event (SuperSummary, Shortform, Dean Yeong's notes, jxnl.co, Skool community posts) and was not checked against the original. For other material, "practitioner opinion" means an expert's published view without a study behind it, "survey" means self-reported data, and "academic" means a peer-reviewed or pre-print paper. For legal, tax and regulatory material, "checked on primary source" means the statute, standard or authority page was read during the research, "secondary source only" means a law firm, trade press, trade body or unofficial reproduction was read and the official text was not, and "unverified background" means the point comes from the researchers' prior knowledge and was not fetched. Agents must not gate a live decision on a secondary or unverified legal point without confirmation from Legal.

Seven caveats apply to the whole document. First, legal, tax and regulatory content is orientation as of 11 November 2026 and is not professional advice. Second, none of Hormozi's books ($100M Offers, $100M Leads, $100M Money Models) was read directly; book content is second-hand, and his video material was read through auto-generated caption transcripts hosted by third parties, so everything here is paraphrase and may carry caption errors. Third, his three primary videos on market selection, the delivery cube and the red-dress focus principle date from 2021 and describe service businesses; they pre-date his 2025 book and may not match his current wording. Fourth, the research ran out of search and page-fetch budget several times, so a number of standard topics were never reached; they are listed as gaps and are not filled from memory. Fifth, most benchmarks are US-weighted, self-reported and venture-skewed, and the central ones are old (retention 2020, activation 2022, free-to-paid 2023, feature adoption 2019). Sixth, templates, checklists and decision rules labelled as inference are the researchers' synthesis and not published templates. Seventh, where a later gap-fill pass corrected or superseded an earlier research note, only the corrected claim appears here.
