### Continuous discovery: the opportunity solution tree

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion, page revised 29 Sep 2026]** Teresa Torres's opportunity solution tree has four levels: an outcome at the root; opportunities, meaning unmet customer needs, pains and desires drawn from interview stories; solutions beneath opportunities; and assumption tests beneath solutions. If a statement can be addressed in only one way, it is a solution in disguise and not an opportunity ([Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees, Dec 2023, modified 29 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)). Torres sells courses on this method, so it is a practitioner position; it remains the reference practice in 2026.

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** The build steps for an opportunity solution tree are: start with a customer theory, a defined outcome and three to four story-based interviews; map the customer's experience; group the opportunities; choose one target opportunity; brainstorm solutions for that opportunity only; pick three; break each into assumptions; test the riskiest assumptions; then discard, refine or pick a new opportunity ([Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees, Dec 2023, modified 29 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)).

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** The operating rules for an opportunity solution tree are: one target opportunity at a time; compare at least three solutions when the opportunity is risky or differentiating; interview week over week and revisit the opportunity space every three to four interviews; work as a trio of product manager, designer and engineer; and keep one tree per outcome ([Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees, Dec 2023, modified 29 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)).

### Choose a product outcome as the root

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** Torres distinguishes three outcome types: business outcomes, which are financial; product outcomes, which are a customer behaviour or sentiment and are the recommended root of the tree; and traction metrics, which measure adoption of a single feature and are usually too narrow ([Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees, Dec 2023, modified 29 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)). **[INFERENCE]** The manager agent assigns each worker a product outcome and keeps the business outcome at department level.

### Jobs-to-be-done

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion, page modified 30 May 2025]** In the Christensen Institute's statement of jobs-to-be-done theory, a job is the progress a person seeks in a given circumstance, and people "hire" products when the job arises. Every job has functional, social and emotional dimensions. To find a job, look past demographics and features to circumstances; ask for the story behind a decision instead of only asking why; and look for circumstances shared across people. Jobs shift when circumstances shift ([Christensen Institute, Jobs to Be Done, page modified 30 May 2025](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/)).

**[DURABLE; illustrative cases]** Two jobs-to-be-done cases from the Christensen Institute: morning milkshake buyers wanted something to fill them and occupy a commute, while afternoon buyers wanted a treat for children; and a tax-software firm found the main job was spending less time on taxes, after which simplifying the product raised sales ([Christensen Institute, Jobs to Be Done, page modified 30 May 2025](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/)). **[GAP]** Moesta's switch interview and "forces of progress" method were not fetched.

### Interview technique: ask for specific past events

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** NN/g's interviewing guidance (25 July 2010) says to avoid leading questions, hypothetical "would you use" questions, questions about specific design choices, "how do you usually" questions (they yield idealised accounts) and detailed recall of past use. Prefer critical-incident questions about a specific hard or smooth case, open exploration of attitudes, and questions asked right after an observed task. People will voice an opinion on almost anything when asked, so weight unprompted comments more ([NN/g, Interviewing Users, 25 Jul 2010](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/interviewing-users/)). Torres's method likewise builds opportunities from stories about specific past events ([Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees, Dec 2023, modified 29 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)).

### An interview snapshot format

**[INFERENCE]** After each discovery interview a worker agent writes a snapshot with five parts: participant and context; the specific story told; opportunities heard, in the customer's words; short quotations; and follow-ups. The interviewer asks about the last time something happened and not for opinions about features. The outline is the researchers' synthesis from Torres's method ([Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees, Dec 2023, modified 29 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)).

### Assumption testing: five categories and a ranking method

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion, page revised 30 Sep 2026]** Torres names five categories of assumption: desirability, viability, feasibility, usability and ethical. Generate assumptions by story-mapping the solution step by step; rank them with David Bland's assumption map, which plots importance against evidence; and test the ones that are important and weakly evidenced ([Product Talk, Assumption Testing, Oct 2023, modified 30 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/assumption-testing/)).

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** Torres's rules for designing an assumption test: phrase assumptions as things customers will do; choose among four test types (prototype tests, one-question surveys, data mining and research spikes); begin with something that can be completed in about an hour; set success criteria before running the test; and compare solutions against each other instead of asking whether one is good enough ([Product Talk, Assumption Testing, Oct 2023, modified 30 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/assumption-testing/)). **[GAP]** Her assumption-testing page gives no sample sizes, and her interview guide sits inside a course that was not accessible.

### Prototypes: build to learn before build to earn

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul and Nov 2026; practitioner opinion]** SVPG's 2026 position is that AI has made prototypes cheap, so teams should prototype to learn before building to earn. Prototypes are tested with users for value and usability, with stakeholders for viability, and with engineers for feasibility. AI helps with research but does not supply product judgment ([SVPG, AI and the Future of the Product Role, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.svpg.com/ai-and-the-future-of-the-product-role/); [SVPG, The AI Productivity Paradox, 23 Jul 2026](https://www.svpg.com/the-ai-productivity-paradox/)). **[INFERENCE]** Dual-track work in 2026 terms is "build to learn" (throwaway prototypes) running alongside "build to earn" (production quality); the two tracks should not share quality standards.

### Why test before building: most ideas do not work

**[DURABLE; academic, 2022]** A 2022 review of online controlled experiments reports that more than half of tested ideas fail to produce a meaningful improvement, which is the empirical case for testing before building ([Larsen, Stallrich, Sengupta, Deng, Kohavi and Stevens, Statistical Challenges in Online Controlled Experiments, 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11366v1)). **[GAP]** No rigorous study quantifying the outcome uplift from continuous discovery itself was found; the support is practitioner opinion plus this experiment-failure data.

### How much discovery teams actually do

**[FAST-MOVING, Q4 2025 fieldwork; survey]** In the PLA/ProductPlan survey, 34% of respondents regularly collect insights and use them in prioritisation, 18.9% collect but struggle to act, 12.3% have no consistent process and 10.2% rarely research. On AI in discovery, 47.1% use it to summarise feedback, 39.8% to find themes, and 27.9% do not use it; 39.3% name research and synthesis as their top 2026 investment ([Product-Led Alliance statistics page citing the PLA/ProductPlan State of Product Management 2026, published May 2026](https://www.productledalliance.com/product-management-statistics/)). In Atlassian's State of Product 2026, 80% of teams do not involve engineers in ideation, problem definition or roadmap creation ([Mind the Product summary of Atlassian's State of Product 2026, Sep 2025](https://www.mindtheproduct.com/inside-atlassians-state-of-product-2026-key-takeaways/)).

### Sweden and EU: recording customer interviews

**[GAP]** The GDPR practicalities of recording customer interviews in Sweden and the EU (consent, retention periods, participant data handling) were not researched. **[INFERENCE]** Until Legal provides a rule, agents record or transcribe interviews only with documented consent and store participant data under controlled permissions, in line with NN/g's repository advice on participant data ([NN/g, Research Repositories, 5 Jul 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/research-repositories/)).
