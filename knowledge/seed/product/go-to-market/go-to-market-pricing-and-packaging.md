**When to use this:** When planning a launch, choosing a pricing model, pricing an AI feature, changing packaging, tiers or trials, or defining product-qualified leads. Price and packaging changes need human approval (section 21).

**Source section:** 10. Go-to-market, pricing and packaging

### Launch tiers

**[VENDOR; practitioner opinion, page modified 2 Nov 2026, original date not shown]** Pragmatic Institute defines three launch tiers. Tier 1 is a rare launch with major strategic impact, such as a new category or an acquisition, and gets the most resources. Tier 2 is a meaningful incremental improvement and gets some support. Tier 3 has minimal market impact, such as a maintenance release, and gets general communications only. The tier is assigned by expected market impact and business impact, not by engineering effort: the same work could be tier 3 as internal tooling and tier 1 if it fixes a severe churn problem ([Pragmatic Institute, Use product launch tiers, page modified 2 Nov 2026](https://www.pragmaticinstitute.com/blog/post/prioritize-product-launch-resources-with-launch-tiers)).

**[INFERENCE]** The department's tiering rule: score each release on market impact and business impact; only releases high on both get tier 1; and decide the tier at kickoff so that Marketing's lead time is known. **[GAP]** Product-marketing handoff checklists and per-tier deliverable lists were not found; Intercom's announcement-tier worksheet appeared only as a third-party copy and was not read.

### Pricing models are moving from seats and flat fees to hybrid

**[FAST-MOVING, Apr-May 2025; survey]** A survey of more than 240 software and AI companies (24% in Europe or Israel) found that over 12 months flat-fee pricing fell from 29% to 22% of companies, seat-based fell from 21% to 15%, and hybrid rose from 27% to 41%. AI was included in the core product at 53% of companies, sold as an add-on or standalone at 16%, and absent at 20%. Outcome pricing was out of reach for about 95%. Internal cost and margin was the top factor in pricing AI ([Growth Unhinged, 2025 State of B2B Monetization, survey fielded Apr-May 2025](https://kylepoyar.substack.com/p/2025-state-of-b2b-monetization)).

**[FAST-MOVING, Oct 2026; survey as cited by a practitioner]** The 2026 State of Monetization, as cited in October 2026, reports that 76% of AI-native companies use variable or hybrid pricing against 49% of SaaS companies, and gives investor preference as 35% hybrid, 26% outcome, 24% usage and 15% flat or seat ([Growth Unhinged, The AI monetization debate shifts again, Oct 2026](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/usage-based-pricing-for-ai)). The report itself was not fetched.

### Outcome pricing remains rare and hard to measure

**[CONTESTED] [VENDOR; Aug 2026]** Sources differ on how common outcome pricing is. The 2025 survey put it out of reach for about 95% of companies ([Growth Unhinged, 2025 State of B2B Monetization, survey fielded Apr-May 2025](https://kylepoyar.substack.com/p/2025-state-of-b2b-monetization)). Sequence, a vendor, put outcome pricing at roughly 10% of companies in its own data in August 2026, up from 5% a year earlier, and reports that 29% of companies let customers choose between pricing models, up from 21% ([Sequence, What changed in SaaS pricing in the past 12 months, Aug 2026](https://www.sequencehq.com/blog/what-changed-in-saas-pricing-in-the-past-12-months)). The chief executive of Metronome, as quoted by Growth Unhinged, argues that outcome pricing is mostly impractical outside very large contracts and recommends pricing on countable outputs, such as a resolved conversation or a generated asset, with tokens hidden behind unified credits ([Growth Unhinged, The AI monetization debate shifts again, Oct 2026](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/usage-based-pricing-for-ai)).

### Make variable pricing predictable

**[FAST-MOVING, Oct 2026; practitioner opinion]** Growth Unhinged lists four ways to make variable pricing predictable for buyers: hard budget caps; usage tiers with annual drawdown; a platform fee plus included usage; and an adaptive flat rate with a true-up the next year. Its checks before a pricing change are whether customers can predict the bill, whether the company knows ramp time and share of wallet, and whether sales compensation rewards the wanted behaviour ([Growth Unhinged, The AI monetization debate shifts again, Oct 2026](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/usage-based-pricing-for-ai)). **[VENDOR; Aug 2026]** Sequence adds that credits persist but buyers face inconsistent credit definitions, and that a platform fee plus pass-through model yields about 80% margin on the core and 20% on the pass-through ([Sequence, What changed in SaaS pricing in the past 12 months, Aug 2026](https://www.sequencehq.com/blog/what-changed-in-saas-pricing-in-the-past-12-months)).

### Review pricing about every six months

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul 2026; practitioner opinion]** Growth Unhinged argues that pricing used to hold for about 18 months and can now be stale within 6. Its health signals: about 20% of lost deals lost on price is healthy, 40% or more is a red flag, and almost no pushback suggests under-pricing. Its self-serve pricing metrics are pricing-page session-to-signup, free-to-paid conversion, cohort gross and net revenue retention, and the share of churn citing cost ([Growth Unhinged, Pricing used to last 18 months, Jul 2026](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/saas-pricing-out-of-date)).

### Publish machine-readable pricing

**[VENDOR; Aug 2026]** Sequence reports a case in which an AI engine invented a price range for a vendor, and concludes that published pricing must be machine-readable so that AI assistants quote it correctly ([Sequence, What changed in SaaS pricing in the past 12 months, Aug 2026](https://www.sequencehq.com/blog/what-changed-in-saas-pricing-in-the-past-12-months)).

### A decision outline for pricing an AI feature

**[INFERENCE]** The department's default sequence for pricing an AI feature: bundle AI into the core product when cost-to-serve is low and it defends retention, which was the majority pattern in 2025 ([Growth Unhinged, 2025 State of B2B Monetization, survey fielded Apr-May 2025](https://kylepoyar.substack.com/p/2025-state-of-b2b-monetization)); add credits or usage pricing when marginal cost is material or value scales with volume; price per countable output when a unit of work is obvious ([Growth Unhinged, The AI monetization debate shifts again, Oct 2026](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/usage-based-pricing-for-ai)); always give caps or drawdown so that bills are predictable; and schedule a pricing review every six months ([Growth Unhinged, Pricing used to last 18 months, Jul 2026](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/saas-pricing-out-of-date)). The sequence is the researchers' synthesis. **[GAP]** No AI gross-margin benchmark or cost-to-serve benchmark per AI interaction was found.

### Packaging: the house-style triggers

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript and second-hand summary]** Two Hormozi rules serve as packaging triggers: add a cheaper tier only when ideal customers cite price, holding features back for the higher tier ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)), and never sell the same product for less, changing terms or features instead ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/)). **[INFERENCE]** The delivery cube's ten-times and one-tenth test ([Hormozi, I Broke Down Every High Margin Service Into 6 Buckets (Delivery Cube), third-party transcript, video dated 17 May 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/i-broke-down-every-high-margin-service-into-6-buckets-delivery-cube)) is the department's exercise for drafting the top and bottom tiers. **[GAP]** A value-metric selection method and sourced good-better-best design guidance were not researched.

### Free trial versus freemium, dated 2023

**[FAST-MOVING, 1 Aug 2023, pre-2024; self-reported survey]** A survey of more than 1,000 B2B SaaS products, with conversion measured over six months, gives these free-to-paid levels: freemium self-serve, good 3 to 5% and great 6 to 8%; freemium with sales assist, good 5 to 7% and great 10 to 15%; free trial, good 8 to 12% and great 15 to 25%. Trials attract fewer signups than freemium, about 5% of visitors against 9%. In 44% of trial companies sales contacts more than half of signups, against 24% of freemium companies ([Lenny's Newsletter, What is a good free-to-paid conversion rate, 1 Aug 2023](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-free-to-paid-conversion)).

**[VENDOR; 6 Feb 2025; self-reported survey]** A ProductLed survey with Gainsight of more than 600 SaaS companies reports visitor conversion medians of 12% for freemium and 5% for free trials, and a 9% average free-to-paid rate across models. Per-metric sample sizes are not given and the page has internal date inconsistencies ([ProductLed with Gainsight, Product-Led Growth Benchmarks, 6 Feb 2025](https://productled.com/blog/product-led-growth-benchmarks)). **[INFERENCE]** The 2023 and 2025 surveys measure different things and should not be mixed. Read together they suggest freemium wins on top-of-funnel volume, trials win on conversion rate, and sales assist roughly doubles freemium conversion; neither survey is audited. **[GAP]** Neither survey covers opt-in versus card-required trials.

### Product-qualified leads

**[VENDOR; 6 Feb 2025; self-reported survey]** In the ProductLed survey, conversion from product-qualified lead to paid at free-trial companies has a median of 25%, with 10% at the lower quartile and 50% at the upper; by contract value it is 30% at $1,000 to $5,000 and 39% at $5,000 to $10,000. Companies using product-qualified leads report about three times the free-account conversion, yet only about a quarter of companies use them ([ProductLed with Gainsight, Product-Led Growth Benchmarks, 6 Feb 2025](https://productled.com/blog/product-led-growth-benchmarks)). **[INFERENCE]** A first definition the department can use: an account that reached the activation milestone plus one buying signal, such as a seat invite, a usage limit hit or a pricing-page visit. This definition is the researchers' suggestion. **[GAP]** A sourced definition and scoring model for product-qualified leads was not retrieved.

### Feedback loops from sales and support

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026; survey]** ProductPlan's 2026 report says a confidence gap keeps product strategy from landing with Sales and Customer Success; the figure is not on the landing page ([ProductPlan, State of Product Management Report 2026, landing page](https://www.productplan.com/ebooks/the-state-of-product-management-report-2026)). **[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** Hormozi's loop is to tag cancellation reasons, review the top one or two after roughly every 20 cancellations, and seat support leadership next to the chief executive so that problems reach product quickly ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)).

### Sweden and EU: launch checklist items

**[INFERENCE]** For a Swedish seller, three items belong on every launch checklist: AI disclosure under Article 50 for any chat or generative feature (section 18); the withdrawal function and accessibility duties wherever consumers can buy (sections 17 and 18); and prices in EUR or SEK with VAT-clear invoices. **[GAP]** Swedish consumer-price and VAT specifics for SaaS pricing were not researched; the VAT point is unverified background and belongs to Finance and Legal. This is orientation and not professional advice.
