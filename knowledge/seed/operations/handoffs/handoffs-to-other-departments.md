**When to use this:** When passing work to or needing work from another department: what Operations hands over and receives from each department.

**Source section:** 25. Handoffs between Operations and the other departments

**[INFERENCE]** The table describes how the Operations department hands work to and receives work from the other departments. No source in the research describes these handoffs; every row is the researchers' inference and should be adjusted to the company's real structure. The general rule is that every handoff names one provider owner, one consumer owner, the artefact, the needed-by date and where it is recorded, as in the dependency log in section 6.

| Department | Operations hands over | Operations receives |
|---|---|---|
| IT infrastructure | Access requests from onboarding and offboarding checklists; vendor register entries and renewal dates for IT tools; continuity requirements (recovery objectives) | Provisioning and de-provisioning confirmations; security attestations and incident reports; system inventory for the systems-of-record map |
| Marketing | Planning calendar and quarterly priorities; vendor and agency contract terms; employer-brand facts for job advertisements | Campaign plans and spend forecasts for the budget; draft job-advertisement copy for review against discrimination and pay rules |
| Application | Process requirements and SOPs that need product or tooling support; project briefs and status reports | Delivery estimates and actuals for the reference class; release and incident information that affects service levels |
| Data and AI | Metric definitions and the KPI glossary; agent task classifications against AI Act Annex III; requests for dashboards | Dashboards and scorecard data; agent evaluation results, logs and cost per workflow; data-quality reports |
| Finance | Headcount plans, pay and benefit changes approved by a human, vendor spend register, renewal forecasts, redundancy or hiring cost estimates | Budgets and approval limits; payroll, pension and tax execution; invoice and card data for monthly spend reconciliation |
| Sales | Capacity and service-level commitments; onboarding of new sales hires; decision records that affect customer terms | Pipeline and demand forecasts for capacity planning; CRM hygiene issues; hiring requests with role scorecards |
| Customer Support | Service-level templates, queue and capacity plans, SOPs and checklists | Ticket volumes and handling times; recurring issues that reveal process constraints; staffing requests |
| Legal | Draft contracts, data processing agreements, policy drafts, and every people matter that needs legal review before a human decides (dismissal, redundancy, monitoring, contractor classification) | Legal sign-off or required changes; confirmations of unverified legal points in this document; updates on law changes |
| Media | Planning calendar; vendor terms for production tools; internal announcements to publish | Production schedules and resource needs; assets for onboarding and training walkthroughs |
| Operations (internal) | The manager agent hands worker agents task briefs with a definition of done, limits and escalation targets | Worker agents hand back outputs with evidence, logs and open questions; the manager agent hands the named human approver decision records |
| Product | Operating-rhythm calendar, cross-team dependency log, risk register items that touch the roadmap | Roadmap and launch dates for capacity and vendor planning; requirements for internal tooling |
| Research | Questions and unverified points from the gaps section; requests for benchmarks | Verified findings with sources and dates, labelled for the knowledge base |
