**When to use this:** Before any AI use that touches a decision about a person: GDPR Article 22, the AI Act high-risk regime for employment and its dates after the Digital Omnibus, and what it means for the department's agents. Orientation for a human to confirm.

**Source section:** 21. AI in people decisions

### GDPR Article 22 applies today

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source (IMY's wording); orientation only. Candidates and employees have a right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effect. IMY's own example of what individuals have a right not to face is automatic rejection by a recruitment system with no human involvement ([IMY, recruitment systems](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/dataskydd-pa-olika-omraden/arbetsliv/rekryteringssystem-och-kompetensdatabaser/)).

### Employment uses of AI are high-risk under the AI Act

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul 2026]** Legal status: read on a non-official reproduction of the AI Act; orientation only. Annex III point 4 lists as high-risk AI intended for recruitment or selection (targeted job advertisements, filtering applications, evaluating candidates) and AI for decisions on terms, promotion or termination, for task allocation based on behaviour or traits, and for monitoring and evaluating performance ([AI Act Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)).

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul 2026]** Legal status: read on a non-official reproduction of the AI Act; orientation only. Deployer duties under Article 26 are to follow the provider's instructions; assign human oversight to natural persons with competence, training, authority and support; keep input data relevant; monitor and suspend on risk; keep logs at least six months; tell workers' representatives and affected workers before a high-risk system is used at work (26(7)); tell individuals when such a system makes or assists decisions about them (26(11)); and use provider information for the GDPR impact assessment ([AI Act Art. 26](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/)).

### The dates after the Digital Omnibus

**[FAST-MOVING, 27 Jul 2026]** Legal status: application dates read on the consolidated text (Article 113); the Omnibus chronology rests on secondary sources only; orientation only. The consolidated AI Act now gives 2 December 2027 for Annex III high-risk systems and 2 August 2028 for Annex I product systems ([AI Act Art. 26 page showing Art. 113](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/)). A law firm gives the chronology of the amending "Digital Omnibus": Commission proposal 19 November 2025, Parliament endorsement 16 June 2026, Council approval 29 June 2026, Official Journal 24 July 2026 and in force 27 July 2026 ([DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act)).

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul 2026]** Legal status: secondary source only. Unchanged and already applying: prohibited practices and the AI-literacy duty for staff operating AI since 2 February 2025, and general-purpose model duties since 2 August 2025. The marking of synthetic content under Article 50(2) moved to 2 December 2026 ([VerifyWise](https://verifywise.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-omnibus-what-changed)).

**[CONTESTED]** The date of the provisional political agreement on the Omnibus is 7 May 2026 in one secondary source and 27 May 2026 in another; the difference does not affect the application dates ([VerifyWise](https://verifywise.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-omnibus-what-changed); [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act)).

### What this means for the department's agents

**[INFERENCE]** The deferral to December 2027 does not relax GDPR: Article 22, impact-assessment duties and IMY's monitoring limits apply today to any AI screening or performance tool.

**[INFERENCE]** An AI agent that ranks or filters candidates, allocates tasks by personal traits, or scores staff performance is itself an Annex III point 4 system. From 2 December 2027 the company is a deployer and, if it built or substantially modified the agent, possibly a provider. An agent that only schedules or formats is unlikely to be high-risk; one that scores or ranks people is. Classify each agent task against Annex III point 4 and record the reasoning.

**[INFERENCE]** The Article 26(7) duty to inform workers lines up with MBL information duties, so one combined notice and consultation routine is sensible.

**[GAP]** Not read: Article 5(1)(f) on emotion recognition at the workplace and Article 6(3) on the exemption for narrow procedural tasks, including whether the Omnibus changed them or the Article 4 AI-literacy duty. Whether the Article 6(3) exemption would cover a summarising or scheduling agent needs legal review. The Omnibus regulation number, the Swedish market-surveillance authority for employment AI, guidance from DO or IMY on AI in recruitment, and evidence on measured bias in AI screening tools were not found.
