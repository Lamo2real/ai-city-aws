**When to use this:** As the default approach to diagnosing the business: place it on the ten-level roadmap, name the one constraint, choose more, better or new, keep it simple, and diagnose non-performance (what, how, why). Law and evidence override it; use the brand's own voice.

**Source section:** 2. House style: Alex and Leila Hormozi's operating models (part 1 of 2)

### What "house style" means and how reliable the sourcing is

**[HOUSE STYLE]** The owner has chosen the Hormozis' published operating models as the default style for this department wherever they have published one. It is a preference, not independent evidence. Every figure in this section is the Hormozis' own claim and is unaudited; none of their revenue, portfolio or outcome figures can be independently verified. Most of the material was read through caption transcripts hosted by third parties or through third-party summaries, so it is paraphrase and never their wording.

**[HOUSE STYLE] [PLATFORM CLAIM]** The "$100M Scaling Roadmap" is a marketing asset. The page acquisition.com/roadmap is a lead-capture form that offers a personalised roadmap, claims more than 250,000 requests and carries a disclaimer that the Hormozis' results are not typical; the roadmap content is not public there and the gated document was not read ([acquisition.com/roadmap, primary site, read Nov 2026](https://www.acquisition.com/roadmap)). The headcount bands in the model are the authors' own heuristic with no published data behind them.

Headcount bands are stated from level 4 upward. **[HOUSE STYLE]** How to use the roadmap in operations planning: place the company on a level by headcount, read off the one constraint named for that level, and treat the listed moves as the default agenda before anything from a later level. A third-party gloss describes the later moves as triage and organise at level 7, replace generalists with specialists at level 8, and one concentrated bet through acquisition or research and development at level 9; that wording is the summarising site's own ([third-party roadmap summary, second-hand](https://astro-pages-7lw.pages.dev/hormozi-scaling-roadmap-stages-7-9-seo-navigator)).

Both are unaudited and second-hand ([Dickie Bush, 12 takeaways from Hormozi's scaling workshop](https://dickiebush.substack.com/p/12-takeaways-from-alex-hormozis-scaling)).

### Identify the constraint and fix one thing at a time

**[HOUSE STYLE]** Alex Hormozi's premise is that each business has one constraint limiting growth and that long hours without growth usually mean effort is going to the wrong thing. His diagnostic question is why the business cannot be ten times larger. His example is a media company with more than 40 million subscribers that believed it needed better production when the real limit was having no product to sell; improving the weak area was said to be worth roughly ten times more than polishing the strong one. The source is a timestamped transcript index whose source video and date were not identified, so this is second-hand and the figures are unaudited ([Tubegraph index of Alex Hormozi videos](https://tubegraph.vercel.app/alexhormozi/identifying_and_addressing_constraints_in_business_growth)).

**[HOUSE STYLE]** The same second-hand source adds diagnostic warnings: a lead-generation problem can really be a product or customer-experience problem, product quality is an often-missed constraint, a mediocre employee can absorb about 25% of an owner's bandwidth, and confronting an underperformer leads to improvement about one time in five and a clean exit otherwise. All figures are unaudited claims ([Tubegraph index](https://tubegraph.vercel.app/alexhormozi/identifying_and_addressing_constraints_in_business_growth)). The last point collides with Swedish law as described in section 3.

**[HOUSE STYLE]** A channel clip description says that to find the constraint one asks why the business cannot do more and works through four areas: market, metrics, model and money. The detail of that walk-through was not available, so only the four headings are sourced, second-hand ([Recapio digest of "You Need to Find Your Constraint"](https://recapio.com/digest/you-need-to-find-your-constraint-by-hormozi-highlights)).

**[HOUSE STYLE]** The "one thing" question, from Alex Hormozi's video "Less is More" dated 22 May 2025: name the single outcome that, if true in 12 months, would change everything for the business. His examples were an owned lead channel, a steady supply of technicians, and cutting cash collection from 90 days to 30 ([Rosetta transcript, primary transcript, 22 May 2025](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/less-is-more-the-magic-of-a-simple-business)).

**[HOUSE STYLE]** When two paths are possible, Alex credits Leila with the framing of comparing the problems that come with each path and choosing the set of problems the team is better equipped to solve. On the coaching call where this appears, the bottleneck named was the lack of a proven year-round acquisition channel ([Audioscrape excerpt of "Fix These Bottlenecks to Scale Past $1M", recorded 22 May 2025](https://audioscrape.com/podcast/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/episode/fix-these-bottlenecks-to-scale-past-1m--ep-891)).

### "More, better, new"

**[HOUSE STYLE]** The order of preference for growth work is more, then better, then new. More means doing more of what already works and is described as the fastest and lowest-effort option. Better means improving the efficiency or effectiveness of existing systems when no obvious "more" exists. New means new products or systems and comes only after the first two are exhausted, because new is slow and costly to start. The source is twice removed (a blogger summarising another writer who credits Hormozi), dated 15 July 2024 ([Goodreads blog post "More, Better, New"](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/24931865-more-better-new)).

**[HOUSE STYLE]** The reason given at the scaling workshop for preferring more or better is that any change costs performance, about 20% (the note-taker says arguably 40%), so a new initiative should clear at least that hurdle before it is worth starting. This is an unaudited rule of thumb from attendee notes ([Dickie Bush, 12 takeaways](https://dickiebush.substack.com/p/12-takeaways-from-alex-hormozis-scaling)).

### Simplicity and focus

**[HOUSE STYLE]** Alex Hormozi argues that simplicity takes more work than complexity, and he attributes complexity to a failure to prioritise. His test is how much can be removed while the result stays good. He prefers one large bet to many small ones, and gives the example of cutting a community's weekly calls from three to one because members were cancelling out of overwhelm ([Rosetta transcript of "Less is More", 22 May 2025](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/less-is-more-the-magic-of-a-simple-business)). His stated preference in an earlier video is "simple scales, fancy fails" ([Rosetta transcript of the What-Why-How video, 5 Jul 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/how-to-scale-yourself-the-what-why-how-framework)).

**[HOUSE STYLE]** Projects in the house style use few people, regular check-ins and a limited time box of about 12 weeks ([Rosetta transcript of "Less is More", 22 May 2025](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/less-is-more-the-magic-of-a-simple-business)). In the same video he remarks that staff typically sit at 70 to 80% utilisation, which leaves roughly 20 to 30% of capacity for one major bet; this is his unaudited observation, not a measured benchmark.

**[HOUSE STYLE]** Workshop notes report that he defines intelligence as speed of learning and links company size to iteration speed supported by fast, constant feedback, and that he advises consolidating the most important metrics into one dashboard judged by daily use and by whether people can connect their work to the numbers ([Dickie Bush, 12 takeaways, second-hand](https://dickiebush.substack.com/p/12-takeaways-from-alex-hormozis-scaling)). The same notes report advice to track customer lifetime value and acquisition cost constantly, tie every project to one of them, and treat a ratio of about 8 to 1 or higher as the level for aggressively scaling a service business; unaudited.

### Management diagnosis: what, how, why

**[HOUSE STYLE]** When someone does not do what is wanted, Alex Hormozi gives three possible causes: the person did not know it was expected (communication), did not know how (training), or did not want to (motivation). He credits the training and motivation pair to Andy Grove's book High Output Management and says he added communication. The sources are his videos dated 5 July 2021 and 5 July 2022 ([Rosetta transcript, "The SIMPLE Managerial Framework", 5 Jul 2022](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/the-simple-managerial-framework-that-changed-my-business); [Rosetta transcript, "What-Why-How Framework", 5 Jul 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/how-to-scale-yourself-the-what-why-how-framework)).

**[HOUSE STYLE]** The manager's sequence from the 2022 video is: state the expectation, repeat it and put it in writing in more than one place such as checklists or reports; train by doing a few examples together and recording them; explain how results are to be reported; check competing priorities and whether pay and bonus are aligned, and explain the purpose; frame the conversation around the process and not the person; and only if the problem persists and is motivational, have a more direct conversation ([Rosetta transcript, 5 Jul 2022](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/the-simple-managerial-framework-that-changed-my-business)).

**[HOUSE STYLE]** From the 2021 video: the manager starts by assuming the fault is the manager's own; an expectation that is not written down in chat, email or a project tool is treated as never having been given; most cases involve one missing element of the three; the worst case is someone who knows what and how but is unmotivated; and the two things he says scale in a service business are culture and training ([Rosetta transcript, 5 Jul 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/how-to-scale-yourself-the-what-why-how-framework)).

**[HOUSE STYLE] [GAP]** A "management diamond" attributed to Alex Hormozi (check clarity of what, clarity of how, clarity of when with a deadline, and blockers, and examine motivation only last) appears on an unsourced blog that cites no video or date. Treat it as unverified ([Stormy.ai blog](https://stormy.ai/blog/alex-hormozi-management-diamond-playbook)).

**[HOUSE STYLE]** Reporting cadence signals priority. In the 5 July 2022 video he argues that meeting frequency tells staff what matters: a metric reviewed twice daily reads as critical, a quarterly slide reads as minor, what is never discussed reads as unimportant, and people treat whatever they must report on as the priority ([Rosetta transcript, 5 Jul 2022](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/the-simple-managerial-framework-that-changed-my-business)). In the same video he gives an example of why CRM notes matter across functions: call notes help finance collect, help customer success personalise onboarding and help screen out unqualified leads.

### Leila Hormozi's management system

**[HOUSE STYLE]** A summary of Leila Hormozi's episode "The 5 Systems That Will Make Your Business Run Itself" (26 March 2026, about 21 minutes) lists five systems: culture as a system, removing the founder bottleneck, the people flywheel, the talent engine and the operating system. The operating system has four parts: expectations, accountability, communication and cadence ([Podcast Rex summary, second-hand](https://podcastrex.com/shows/build-with-leila-hormozi/the-5-systems-that-will-make-your-business-run-itself-ep-346)).

**[HOUSE STYLE]** A summary of her episode "The Hiring System That Lets Your Business Run Solo" (21 May 2026, about 12 minutes) gives three ideas. Adding people does not repair a broken business; it multiplies the disorder, so systems come before scale. The people flywheel rests on functions, people and operations. An "accountability dial" with five levels is her frame for training a person up to full ownership; the page names the dial but does not list the five levels ([Podcast Rex summary, second-hand](https://podcastrex.com/shows/build-with-leila-hormozi/the-hiring-system-that-lets-your-business-run-solo-ep-362)). In the same episode she describes a founder who does a bit of everything as the company's junk drawer.

**[HOUSE STYLE]** Show notes for an older Leila Hormozi episode on team meetings (recorded 25 July 2023) list five questions to answer before a meeting is scheduled: who truly needs to attend, whether the CEO needs to be there, whether each person must stay for all of it, who owns the pre-work, and what outcome is intended. The notes add that meetings shape culture. This is publisher's show notes only, with no transcript ([Audioscrape, "5 Tips to Have More Effective Team Meetings"](https://www.audioscrape.com/podcast/build-with-leila-hormozi/episode/5-tips-to-have-more-effective-team-meetings-ep-57-79200)).

**[INFERENCE]** Leila Hormozi's four operating-system parts map onto Alex Hormozi's what, how, why diagnosis: expectations correspond to what, training and accountability to how, and communication and cadence to why and follow-up. The mapping is the researchers' synthesis.

Full transcripts of the two 2026 episodes above and several other episodes listed on the show page were not read ([Podcast Rex show page](https://podcastrex.com/shows/build-with-leila-hormozi)).
