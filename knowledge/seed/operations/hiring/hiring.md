**When to use this:** When planning or running a hire: selection evidence, structured interviews, recruiting benchmarks, Swedish legal constraints on hiring and the default hiring pipeline. A human decides every hire and offer.

**Source section:** 13. Hiring

### What the selection evidence says

**[DURABLE]** The 2022 re-analysis by Sackett, Zhang, Berry and Lievens (Journal of Applied Psychology 107, 2040-2068) gives these mean operational validities for predicting job performance: structured interview .42, job knowledge test .40, empirically keyed biodata .38, work sample .33, cognitive ability .31 and fit-based interests .24. The figures were read in a SIOP article summarising the paper, not in the paper itself ([SIOP TIP article](https://www.siop.org/tip-article/is-cognitive-ability-the-best-predictor-of-job-performance); [Sackett et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000994)).

**[DURABLE]** The same SIOP article notes that structured interviews have the highest mean validity but a wide spread across studies, that the 2022 paper's core argument is that earlier meta-analyses over-corrected for range restriction, and that work-framed personality measures predict clearly better than general personality inventories ([SIOP TIP article](https://www.siop.org/tip-article/is-cognitive-ability-the-best-predictor-of-job-performance)).

**[GAP]** The older Schmidt and Hunter (1998) coefficients and several Sackett 2022 values (unstructured interviews, integrity tests, assessment centres, conscientiousness, subgroup differences) could not be confirmed because the publisher's page refuses automated access. They are not reproduced here; check the papers directly ([Schmidt and Hunter 1998](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.124.2.262); [Sackett et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000994)).

### Structured interviewing in practice

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, Mar 2026]** Google's re:Work guide to structured interviewing (updated March 2026) defines the practice as assessing every candidate for the same job in the same way, with the same questions, the same grading scale and qualifications fixed in advance. Its five components are vetted, role-relevant questions with planned follow-ups; a mix of behavioural and hypothetical questions; detailed note-taking so others can review answers; standard rubrics that describe poor, borderline, solid and outstanding answers with examples; and interviewer training and calibration. The attributes Google defines before interviewing are role-related knowledge, problem solving and leadership ([Google re:Work, structured interviewing guide](https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/a-guide-to-structured-interviewing-for-better-hiring-practices)).

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, Mar 2026]** Google's own figures, self-reported with no method given: ready-made questions, guides and rubrics save an average of 40 minutes per interview, and rejected candidates who had a structured interview were 35% happier than those who had not. On evidence the guide says only that two decades of academic research find higher predictive validity and smaller group differences; it names no study and prints no coefficient ([Google re:Work, structured interviewing guide, updated Mar 2026](https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/a-guide-to-structured-interviewing-for-better-hiring-practices)). The re:Work hiring section lists four guides: candidate experience, structured interviewing and interviewer training (all updated March 2026) and a data-driven onboarding guide (August 2025) ([Google re:Work, hiring](https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/subjects/hiring)).

### Recruiting benchmarks

**[FAST-MOVING, Jun 2026]** SHRM's recruiting benchmarking summary (US-centred member survey; page metadata dated 24 June 2026; data from more than 4,600 organisations) reports a median time-to-fill of 39 calendar days for non-executive positions, down from the previous year, with executive time-to-fill unchanged and no figure shown. The median share of non-executive positions filled externally was 93% in 2025 and 97% in 2026. Executive cost-per-hire rose substantially while non-executive cost-per-hire stayed roughly stable, with no amounts printed on the public page. Median requisitions per recruiter at extra-large organisations rose 67% in 2026 ([SHRM Recruiting Benchmarking, accessed Nov 2026](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/2025-recruiting-benchmarking)).

**[INFERENCE]** The 39-day figure is time-to-fill (requisition opened to offer accepted), which is not the same measure as time-to-hire (candidate applied to offer accepted); the two should not be mixed in a scorecard. The definition difference is standard usage and was not read on the SHRM page. The page address says 2025 while the page reports 2026 data, so cite it by access date.

**[GAP]** Cost-per-hire amounts and time-to-hire sit in SHRM's member-only report, which was not read. No Swedish or European recruiting benchmark was located. Sourcing channels, candidate-experience evidence, reference-check validity and offer-process practice were not researched.

### Swedish legal constraints on the hiring process

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation, not legal advice. The Swedish Discrimination Act protects seven grounds: sex, transgender identity or expression, ethnicity, religion or other belief, disability, sexual orientation and age (1 kap. 1 and 5 §§). Recruitment and promotion is one of the five areas where an employer must run active anti-discrimination measures (3 kap. 4 to 5 §§) ([Diskrimineringslagen, riksdagen.se](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/diskrimineringslag-2008567_sfs-2008-567/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. An applicant who was not hired or not interviewed, and an employee passed over for promotion, can demand written information on the education, experience and other merits of the person chosen (Diskrimineringslagen 2 kap. 4 §). The process consequence is to keep a scorecard and merit record for each hire ([Diskrimineringslagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/diskrimineringslag-2008567_sfs-2008-567/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. The Parental Leave Act separately bans disadvantaging applicants and employees for reasons linked to parental leave, in hiring, promotion, pay and dismissal (16 §) ([Föräldraledighetslagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/foraldraledighetslag-1995584_sfs-1995-584/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source (the Swedish data protection authority, IMY); orientation only. For candidate data the usual legal basis is a balancing of interests and not consent. The employer collects only what is needed to judge fit for the specific role and deletes or anonymises applications, interview notes and references when the process ends, except that they may be kept for as long as the applicant could bring a legal claim. Keeping a candidate for future openings needs consent ([IMY, recruitment systems and competence databases](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/dataskydd-pa-olika-omraden/arbetsliv/rekryteringssystem-och-kompetensdatabaser/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. IMY treats personality-test results as privacy-sensitive and asks employers to weigh whether they are needed. Sensitive data and offence data are normally off limits in recruitment. IMY's own example of a prohibited solely automated decision is an online recruitment system that rejects a candidate with no human contact ([IMY, recruitment systems](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/dataskydd-pa-olika-omraden/arbetsliv/rekryteringssystem-och-kompetensdatabaser/)).

**[GAP]** The exact claim-limitation window for rejected applicants was read only in outline (the Discrimination Act refers to MBL time limits). IMY permits retention while a claim is possible but states no number of months on the page read. Many Swedish employers are said to use 24 months; the basis for that figure was not verified, and a lawyer should set the retention rule ([Diskrimineringslagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/diskrimineringslag-2008567_sfs-2008-567/)).

### A default hiring pipeline

**[INFERENCE]** A defensible default pipeline is: a role scorecard with four to six must-have outcomes and competencies; a structured screen; a work sample or job-knowledge test; a structured interview with anchored rating scales, each interviewer scoring independently before discussion; a reference check on the same criteria; and a written offer. It follows from the 2022 validity ranking and from the merit-information duty in Diskrimineringslagen 2 kap. 4 §.

**[HOUSE STYLE] [INFERENCE]** The house style fits on top of this pipeline where the law allows: treat the job description as a sales page and the interview as a sales call (Leila Hormozi), interview more candidates than feels necessary, prefer candidates who describe past work in metrics, and use the first good hire in a role as the pattern for later ones (Alex Hormozi) ([Podcast Rex summary, 21 May 2026](https://podcastrex.com/shows/build-with-leila-hormozi/the-hiring-system-that-lets-your-business-run-solo-ep-362); [Rosetta transcript, 4 Nov 2025](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/if-i-wanted-to-create-a-business-that-runs-itself-here-s-what-i-d-do)). Group interviews and interview recordings are used only inside the structured, equal-treatment and data-minimisation rules above.

**[INFERENCE]** Stop asking candidates about salary history now. It is not banned in Swedish law as of November 2026, but the EU Pay Transparency Directive contains such a ban (Article 5) and the question adds bias risk ([Directive (EU) 2023/970](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/970/oj)).
