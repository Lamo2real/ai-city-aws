### The binding duty today: the annual pay survey

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Every employer must each year survey and analyse pay rules, pay practice and pay differences between women and men in equal and in equivalent work (Diskrimineringslagen 3 kap. 8 to 10 §§). Written documentation of the pay survey is required from 10 employees (3 kap. 14 §) and of all active measures from 25 employees, counted at the start of the calendar year (3 kap. 13 §) ([Diskrimineringslagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/diskrimineringslag-2008567_sfs-2008-567/)).

### EU pay transparency: no Swedish law in force, timetable unsettled

**[FAST-MOVING, 26 Mar 2026]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. On 26 March 2026 the Swedish government said it does not at present intend to submit a bill on the Pay Transparency Directive to the Riksdag, will seek a renegotiation of the directive aimed at simpler rules, and will work to have the implementation date moved. The release gives no new date. It recalls that Sweden voted against the directive and that employers already have pay-survey duties ([Regeringen, 26 Mar 2026](https://regeringen.se/pressmeddelanden/2026/03/regeringen-vill-omforhandla-lonetransparensdirektivet/)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 21 Jul 2026]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. The Equality Ombudsman (DO), on a page last updated 21 July 2026, says the government announced in March that it will not for now put forward a bill, that DO was asked in early June to continue preparing, and that the shape of the Swedish law is not settled. The page still shows the earlier draft proposal (entry into force 1 July 2026) and the directive's reporting dates, noting that the timetable will be set once legislation exists ([DO, updated 21 Jul 2026](https://www.do.se/for-arbetsgivare-och-utbildningsanordnare/lonetransparens-och-jamstallda-loner/vad-ar-lonetransparensdirektivet)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 26 Mar 2026]** Corrected claim: an earlier government statement of 11 March 2026, which aimed at entry into force on 1 January 2027 with first reports by 20 May 2028, was overtaken fifteen days later by the 26 March statement. As far as could be verified, no bill exists and 1 January 2027 is not a planned entry-into-force date for pay transparency in Sweden ([Regeringen, 26 Mar 2026](https://regeringen.se/pressmeddelanden/2026/03/regeringen-vill-omforhandla-lonetransparensdirektivet/)). Sweden missed the directive's transposition deadline of 7 June 2026, and the Discrimination Act as published (amended through SFS 2025:736) contains no pay-transparency provisions ([Diskrimineringslagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/diskrimineringslag-2008567_sfs-2008-567/)).

**[VENDOR] [FAST-MOVING, 26 Mar 2026]** Legal status: secondary source only. KPMG's reading on 26 March 2026 is that the new transparency and reporting duties will not start on the earlier timetable, that employers should keep complying with the existing pay-survey and active-measures rules, and that the outcome of the EU renegotiation decides what comes next ([KPMG LegalNews, 26 Mar 2026](https://kpmg.com/se/en/insights/newsletters/legalnews/2026/regeringen-vill-omforhandla-lonetransparensdirektivet.html)). A trade body reports that DO is to report on its preparatory assignment by 1 November 2027 ([Installatörsföretagen](https://www.in.se/arbetsgivare/arbetsgivarfragor/lonetransperensdirektivet/)).

**[GAP]** No source newer than 21 July 2026 was found on pay transparency. Whether the government formed after the September 2026 election changed course, and whether the European Commission opened infringement proceedings against Sweden, are unknown. Re-check the government and DO pages before relying on any date.

### What the directive contains, for preparation

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source for the article list (EUR-Lex), though the full article text was not read; orientation only. Directive (EU) 2023/970 of 10 May 2023 entered into force on 6 June 2023. Its articles cover pre-employment pay transparency and a salary-history ban (Article 5), pay-setting criteria (Article 6), a right to pay information answered within two months, an annual reminder and no pay-secrecy clauses (Article 7), reporting (Article 9), a joint pay assessment at a 5% unexplained gap not fixed within six months (Article 10) and the burden of proof (Article 18) ([Directive (EU) 2023/970, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/970/oj)).

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul 2026]** Legal status: directive dates as summarised by DO; orientation only. The directive's reporting thresholds are: 250 or more employees, first report by 7 June 2027 and then yearly; 150 to 249, first report by 7 June 2027 and then every third year; 100 to 149, first report by 7 June 2031 and then every third year ([DO, updated 21 Jul 2026](https://www.do.se/for-arbetsgivare-och-utbildningsanordnare/lonetransparens-och-jamstallda-loner/vad-ar-lonetransparensdirektivet)).

**[CONTESTED]** Legal status: secondary sources only, describing a draft that has not become law. The proposed Swedish version would have required reporting from employers with 100 or more employees (yearly at 250 or more, every third year at 100 to 249), given employees a right to request their own pay level and sex-split average pay for their category with an answer within two months, required a yearly reminder of that right, required employers to tell applicants the starting pay or range before negotiation, banned questions about pay history, barred employers from stopping employees disclosing their own pay, and extended the limitation period from two to three years. The secondary sources disagree with each other on the 100 versus 150 threshold ([Lindahl](https://www.lindahl.se/aktuellt/insikter/nya-krav-pa-lonetransparens-det-har-behover-du-veta/); [Installatörsföretagen](https://www.in.se/arbetsgivare/arbetsgivarfragor/lonetransperensdirektivet/); [Srf konsulterna](https://www.srfkonsult.se/kunskap/lon/lonetransparensdirektivet); [Chefstidningen, 5 Feb 2026](https://chefstidningen.se/arbetsmiljo/jamstalldhet-arbetsmiljo/lonetransparensdirektivet-5-saker-att-ha-koll-pa-just-nu/)).

**[INFERENCE]** Practical reading for a company under 100 employees: no new pay-transparency duty is in force in November 2026, and the binding duty remains the annual pay survey, written from 10 employees. Low-regret preparation is still sensible because the directive itself has not been withdrawn: written pay criteria, a job architecture with levels and pay bands that can be shown in job advertisements, a documented annual pay survey, a way to answer an employee's pay-information request, and removal of any pay-confidentiality clause from contract templates.

### Pension and insurance: the collectively agreed benchmark

**[FAST-MOVING, Aug 2025]** Status: read on the collective-agreement parties' own fact sheet dated 13 August 2025; the 2026 and 2027 figures were not read. The ITP 1 old-age pension premium for 2025 is 4.5% of monthly pay up to 7.5 income base amounts (SEK 50,375 a month in 2025) and 30% on pay between that level and 30 income base amounts (SEK 201,500 a month). Add-ons in the lower band and upper band are: premium waiver 0.112% and 1.068%; work-injury insurance (TFA) 0.03% in both; transition support through TRR 0.55% in both; group life (TGL) about 0.08% in the lower band only; ITP sickness pension 0.030% and 0.115%. The total employer premium is about 5.30% of pay in the lower band and 31.76% in the upper band. Some components switch band at price-base-amount levels instead (7.5 price base amounts was SEK 36,750 a month and 10 was SEK 49,000 a month in 2025) ([Avtalat, ITP 1 premiums 2025, 13 Aug 2025](https://www.avtalat.se/media/tdndrimn/2025-premier_itp-1_13-08-2025.pdf)).

**[INFERENCE] [GAP]** For a company outside a collective agreement, the ITP 1 table is the market benchmark and not a legal duty. That reading is the researchers' inference: it was not verified that no statute obliges an employer without a collective agreement to pay an occupational pension, nor what brokers sell as "ITP-like" plans, nor the 2026 and 2027 income base amounts.

### Benefits and reference amounts

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** Legal status: checked on primary source (the Swedish Tax Agency); orientation, not tax advice. The wellness allowance (friskvårdsbidrag) is tax-free up to SEK 5,000 a year, and one krona more makes the whole amount taxable. It must be offered on equal terms to all staff, non-exercise treatments such as massage are capped at SEK 1,000 per occasion, it is reimbursed against a receipt in the tax year, and it excludes gift cards, equipment purchases and medical or cosmetic care. The agency no longer publishes an approved list ([Skatteverket, wellness benefit](https://www.skatteverket.se/privat/skatter/arbeteochinkomst/formaner/personalvardsformanmotionochfriskvard.4.7459477810df5bccdd4800014540.html)).

**[FAST-MOVING, Sep 2026]** Legal status: checked on primary source. The price base amount (prisbasbelopp) for 2027 is SEK 59,600, up 0.7%, and the raised amount is SEK 60,900 ([Regeringen, Sep 2026](https://regeringen.se/pressmeddelanden/2026/09/prisbasbeloppfor-2027-faststallt/)). The 2026 amount of about SEK 59,200 is backed out from the 0.7% rise and was not read directly.

**[FAST-MOVING, Jun 2026]** Legal status: checked on primary source. The median monthly salary the Migration Agency uses is SEK 38,300 (a Statistics Sweden figure listed on 16 June 2026), a usable anchor for benchmarking ([Migrationsverket, pay requirement](https://www.migrationsverket.se/ordforklaringar/lonekrav-for-arbetstillstand.html)).

**[HOUSE STYLE]** The house style on pay is thin and sales-specific: base pay plus incentives, and a profit-sharing pool of 10 to 20% with about two-thirds reserved for future hires, as reported second-hand and unaudited ([Shortform summary of "Diagnose Before You Scale Q&A"](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2026-02-05-episode-summary-diagnose-before-you-scale-q-a-ep-1001)). Any variable-pay or profit-share design in Sweden runs through the pay-survey criteria above.

**[GAP]** Not researched: Swedish salary benchmarking sources, variable-pay design evidence, qualified employee stock options, the customary pay-review month, and monthly employer-declaration deadlines.
