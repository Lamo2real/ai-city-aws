**When to use this:** Before showing or voicing any real person, an employee, a child, an avatar or a cloned voice. Orientation for a human to confirm.

**Source section:** 20. Likeness and voice

### Sweden: no name or picture in marketing without consent

**[DURABLE]** Checked on a primary source: under 1 § of Lag (1978:800) on names and pictures in advertising, a trader may not, when marketing goods, services or other utilities, use a presentation in which another person's name or picture is used without that person's consent; a designation clearly pointing to a specific person equals a name. Under 2 § an intentional or grossly negligent breach is punishable by fines. Under 3 § the person is owed reasonable compensation, plus damages if the breach was intentional or negligent, with regard to suffering, and the employer pays for employees' acts. Under 4 § materials can be ordered altered or destroyed. Under 5 § prosecution takes place only on the person's complaint or in the public interest. The statute text read does not mention protection after death ([Riksdagen, Lag (1978:800) om namn och bild i reklam](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1978800-om-namn-och-bild-i-reklam_sfs-1978-800/)). This is orientation and not legal advice.

**[INFERENCE]** The statute's wording is technology-neutral, so an AI-generated lookalike picture, or a designation that clearly points to a real person in an ad, plausibly falls within it. Voice is not mentioned in the statute, so a cloned voice is more likely handled through marketing law on misleading practices, GDPR and, where applicable, performers' rights. No case law was read.

### Three separate things are needed to show a person in Swedish marketing

**[INFERENCE]** For people in marketing material in Sweden, three separate things are needed: consent under Lag 1978:800 for use of name or picture in advertising; a GDPR legal basis and information to the person; and, for employees, a genuinely voluntary arrangement with an objection route.

### GDPR: images, film and voice are personal data

**[DURABLE]** Checked on a primary source: IMY's guidance, last updated 14 April 2021, says photos, film and an audio file with a person's voice are personal data when they identify a living person, and that publishing needs a defined purpose and a legal basis. Consent is often impractical for event photos. Companies can rely on legitimate interest if it outweighs the individuals' privacy interest, and the assessment must be documented. Event attendees should be told that images may be published, that they can object, and whom to contact ([IMY, publishing images, film and audio, last updated 14 Apr 2021](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/vi-guidar-dig/publicera-bilder-filmer-och-ljud-pa-internet/)). IMY's FAQ adds that a business may publish images of people on social media without consent if another basis such as legitimate interest applies, that asking first is still good practice, and that children's images deserve extra protection ([IMY FAQ, images on social media without consent](https://www.imy.se/vanliga-fragor-och-svar/ar-det-tillatet-for-en-verksamhet-att-publicera-bilder-pa-personer-i-sociala-medier-utan-samtycke/)). The GDPR basis does not replace the consent that Lag 1978:800 requires for marketing use.

### Employees on camera

**[DURABLE]** Checked on a primary source: for employees IMY says to assess case by case and always ask before publishing. Consent is often unsuitable because of the dependent relationship. Employers may have a strong enough interest for staff in outward-facing roles, and individuals can object to processing based on legitimate interest ([IMY, publishing images, film and audio, last updated 14 Apr 2021](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/vi-guidar-dig/publicera-bilder-filmer-och-ljud-pa-internet/)).

**[INFERENCE]** The default for employee photos and video is: ask first; rely on a documented legitimate interest for outward-facing roles; give an easy objection route; remove on objection or when employment ends unless otherwise agreed; and never make appearing on camera a condition of employment.

### What a consent or release should contain

**[INFERENCE]** The proposed minimum content of a consent or release is: the person's identity; the exact assets; purposes and channels (organic, paid, print); territory; duration and end date; whether AI editing, a synthetic avatar or a voice clone is permitted and for which scripts; an approval right over synthetic output; the fee; the withdrawal route; and, for employees, a statement that refusal has no consequences. Store the signed document's ID in the rights log.

### Vendor and platform rules on likeness and voice

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, Oct to Nov 2026]** OpenAI prohibits reproducing a person's likeness or voice without express consent and all necessary rights, and requires disclosure that generated speech is AI ([OpenAI Service Terms, updated 16 Oct 2026](https://openai.com/policies/service-terms/)). ElevenLabs allows voice models only from your own voice or one you are authorised to share ([ElevenLabs Terms, updated 9 Oct 2026](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)). HeyGen's terms say owning a recording does not establish permission to clone a voice ([HeyGen Terms, updated 16 Oct 2026](https://www.heygen.com/terms)). Runway forbids submitting a photo of another person without permission ([Runway Terms of Use, updated 23 Oct 2026](https://runway.com/terms-of-use)). TikTok bans AI likeness of anyone under 18 and of adult private figures without permission, and bans fake endorsements by public figures; YouTube requires disclosure where a real person appears to say or do something they did not ([TikTok Support, AI-generated content, read Nov 2026](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content); [YouTube Help, disclosing altered or synthetic content, read Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)).

### Voice cloning rule

**[INFERENCE]** Clone only the company's contracted voice talent with a written AI clause, or licensed stock voices from the vendor. Never clone a celebrity, competitor spokesperson, politician or deceased person.

### Children

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read Nov 2026]** Google blocks uploading or editing images containing minors in Gemini Omni Flash in the EEA, Switzerland and the UK ([Google, Gemini Omni docs, read Nov 2026](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)), and TikTok bans AI likeness of anyone under 18 ([TikTok Support, AI-generated content, read Nov 2026](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content)). **[INFERENCE]** Avoid synthetic depictions of real children entirely; for real footage require guardian consent and apply a stricter necessity test.

### Denmark's deepfake bill and other moves in Europe

**[FAST-MOVING, Jun to Oct 2026] [CONTESTED]** Checked on a primary source: Denmark's proposal amends its Copyright Act to protect individuals against publication of realistic digital imitations of their appearance or voice without consent, and performers against imitations of their performances, with exceptions for caricature, satire, parody, pastiche and criticism of power unless the imitation is seriously harmful misinformation. It was notified to the EU as TRIS 2025/0654/DK, and the Commission issued comments on 3 February 2026 ([European IP Helpdesk, 1 Jun 2026](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/denmarks-proposed-copyright-rules-deepfakes-2026-06-01_en)). On timing the sources differ: the draft expected entry into force on 1 July 2026 ([European IP Helpdesk, 1 Jun 2026](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/denmarks-proposed-copyright-rules-deepfakes-2026-06-01_en)), but a news report says the bill was to be introduced in parliament on 8 October 2026, with the minister hoping for effect at the start of 2027 and fast platform takedown as a main aim ([Euronews, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/08/denmark-introduces-a-bill-protecting-peoples-faces-and-voices-from-deepfakes)). It does not appear to be in force as of 11 November 2026.

**[FAST-MOVING, Oct 2026]** Secondary source only: the same news report says France already prohibits sharing AI-generated image or audio of a person without consent when its artificial nature is not obvious or disclosed; that in England and Wales creating or requesting non-consensual intimate deepfakes became an offence in February 2026; and that the EU directive on violence against women requires member states by 14 June 2027 to criminalise non-consensual sexually explicit manipulated material ([Euronews, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/08/denmark-introduces-a-bill-protecting-peoples-faces-and-voices-from-deepfakes)).
