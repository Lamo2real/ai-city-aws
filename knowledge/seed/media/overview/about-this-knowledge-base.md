# Rights gate media; plain structure scales it

As of 11 November 2026 the foundational knowledge for an AI media department is a three-layer stack: rights and disclosure rules that always win, evidence and official platform guidance that tune the craft, and Alex Hormozi's published content-production system as the default structure underneath both. The house style supplies the working skeleton: record long-form, cut it into many platform-native short pieces that each carry their own hook, build every piece as hook, retain, reward, open long-form with proof, promise and plan, state the outcome plainly in titles and thumbnails, move effort from post-production to pre-work, treat organic posts as cheap hook tests for ads, and judge content by revenue-linked signals; every number attached to that system (about 35,000 pieces, about $4 million, 7.8 million followers) is his own unaudited claim. Official platform guidance mostly agrees with it: YouTube's own title and thumbnail test picks winners on watch-time share and not click-through, YouTube warns that clickbait earns clicks with short viewing, and half of all channels sit between 2% and 10% impressions click-through rate. The rights layer is where an AI department can do real damage. Article 50 of the EU AI Act has applied since 2 August 2026 and requires a visible disclosure at first exposure for realistic synthetic image, audio or video; Swedish law forbids using a person's name or picture in marketing without consent; purely prompted AI output has no copyright according to both the US Copyright Office and Sweden's PRV; and of the vendor terms read, only OpenAI (API and Enterprise) and Google Cloud (generally available models) give an output indemnity, with Getty's being a vendor statement, while Midjourney, Suno, ElevenLabs, Runway, Luma and HeyGen put the infringement risk on the customer. The tool list is unstable: Google shut down the Veo 3.1 preview API on 22 October 2026 and Sora was reported discontinued during 2026, so prompts, seeds and references must be stored so that a shot can be regenerated elsewhere. Audience tests point one way: AI that viewers cannot see does little harm, while visibly artificial or fully AI-made ads scored lower than human-made ones (14% on short-term and 17% on long-term effectiveness measures in the Ipsos and Syracuse test of May 2026). The research behind this document ran out of search and fetch budget, so production workflow, graphic and brand design, photography, asset-management standards, official safe zones, stock, font and music licences and several vendor terms have little or no sourced coverage; this document says so in each place and in a consolidated gaps section instead of filling the holes from memory.

## How to read and split this document

**[INFERENCE]** This document is written to be cut into atomic knowledge-base entries for a vector index and a graph database. Every body paragraph is meant to stand alone: it opens with one or more labels, names its subject in full, and keeps each number next to its date and its source link. Numbered sections group domains, and sub-headings name the single topic of the paragraphs beneath them. Tables repeat their source link in every row so that a row can be split out as its own entry. When splitting, keep the label, the date and the link together with the claim.

**[INFERENCE]** The labels mean the following. **[HOUSE STYLE]** marks Alex or Leila Hormozi's published frameworks, which the owner has chosen as the department's default style where they exist; it is a preference and not independent evidence, and every number attached to it is his own self-reported, unaudited claim. **[DURABLE]** marks a principle, definition or long-standing rule that has held for years. **[FAST-MOVING, date]** marks a detail, number, spec, tool status or legal status that will age, with the date of the source or of the reading. **[VENDOR]** marks a figure or recommendation from a party with a commercial interest in the conclusion, including agency blogs and vendor compilations of platform limits. **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** marks something a platform or tool maker reports about itself, including its own help pages, terms and documentation. **[CONTESTED]** marks a point where sources disagree. **[INFERENCE]** marks a synthesis or proposed operating rule written by the researchers that no single source states. **[GAP]** marks something the research did not verify or could not find.

**[INFERENCE]** Two qualifiers are added inside labels or sentences where they matter. For house-style material, "primary transcript" means the point was read on an auto-generated transcript of Hormozi's own video hosted on the Rosetta archive, and "second-hand" means it comes from a third party's summary or analysis and was not checked against the original. For legal, tax and regulatory material, "checked on a primary source" means the statute, regulator page, standards body or the vendor's own terms was read during the research on 11 November 2026; "secondary source only" means a law-firm briefing, trade publication or reference site was read and the primary text was not; and "unverified background" means the point is the researchers' recall with a link to where it should be confirmed. Agents must not gate a live decision on an unverified legal point without confirmation.

**[INFERENCE]** Eight caveats apply to the whole document. First, legal, tax and regulatory content is orientation as of 11 November 2026 and is not professional advice. Second, the Hormozi transcripts are auto-captions read through a third-party archive, so wording and numbers can be garbled; this document paraphrases them and never reproduces his wording as a quotation. No material by Leila Hormozi was found in the notes, so every house-style item here is Alex Hormozi's. Third, the research sessions exhausted their shared search and fetch budgets, so several domains are thin or empty; those are stated as gaps. Fourth, where the later gap-fill research corrected or superseded the earlier notes, only the corrected claim is used. Fifth, platform specs, tool versions, prices and terms were read between 2 and 11 November 2026 unless another date is given, and each should be re-checked monthly. Sixth, most evidence is US-weighted; no Swedish or Nordic audience study was found. Seventh, no direct quotation in this document exceeds 14 words. Eighth, agents apply the house-style structures in the company's own voice and never imitate Hormozi or use his name, image or voice in company media (section 3).
