**When to use this:** When taking a brief from Marketing, planning a production, recording, or preparing a delivery master.

**Source section:** 4. Production workflow: from brief to delivery

### The honest status of this section

**[GAP]** Production workflow is the largest hole in this knowledge base. Neither research pass could open a production-workflow source before the fetch budget ran out, so nothing in this section about briefs, storyboards, shot lists, recording practice, review rounds or turnaround is sourced industry standard. The sourced points are limited to NN/g's web-video guidance, YouTube's encoding rules and Hormozi's pre-work claim. Everything else is proposed house practice and is labelled as inference. The first sources to read in a follow-up are the Frame.io Workflow Guide ([Frame.io Workflow Guide, unread](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/)), the Wistia Learning Center and StudioBinder templates for creative brief, shot list and storyboard.

### A proposed stage-gate sequence

**[INFERENCE]** A sensible stage-gate for an AI media department is: brief; script or outline; storyboard or shot list; pre-production checklist; record; rough cut; review round one for content; fine cut; review round two for polish, captions and legal; master; platform versions; delivery and archive. The researchers describe this as standard industry practice but did not source it. Use it as the default and revise it when the Frame.io guide has been read.

### Put approval effort on the brief and the script

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript] [FAST-MOVING, Apr 2024]** Hormozi claims that moving effort from post-production to pre-production cut his post-edit work by roughly 90 to 95% and allowed about four times the output ([Hormozi, "How I Gained 7.8 Million Followers In 40 Months", auto-transcript, 27 Apr 2024](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/how-i-gained-7-8-million-followers-in-40-months-6-key-lessons)). This is his own unaudited claim. **[DURABLE]** NN/g's research-based guidance from 2014 points the same way: keep web videos as short and tight as possible, and cut content and tighten scripts ([NN/g, Video Usability, 16 Nov 2014](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/video-usability/)). **[INFERENCE]** Approval effort therefore belongs on the brief and the script, where changes are cheap, and post-production review rounds should be capped. Two consolidated rounds is a common default in agency contracts according to the researchers' unverified recall.

### What a brief from Marketing to Media should carry

**[INFERENCE]** A marketing-to-media brief should carry: the audience and its problem, the single promise of the piece, the proof available and where it is documented, the required call to action, the platform list and aspect ratios, the deadline, the named approver, the metric the piece will be judged on, and a risk classification (real people, claims, paid use, minors, regulated sector, AI-generated elements). This is the researchers' proposal; no briefing template was sourced.

### Web video basics that have held since 2014

**[DURABLE]** NN/g advises identifying the source and topic within about 5 seconds, because repeated intros add up for people watching several videos; not autoplaying; giving users start, stop, restart, mute and volume control; giving a clear next step at the end, such as a call to action or related links; showing video length next to the title; and choosing a representative thumbnail that reads at small sizes ([NN/g, Video Usability, 16 Nov 2014](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/video-usability/)). The guidance dates from 16 November 2014 and is older than short-form feeds, so apply it to video on the company's own website and product pages.

**[DURABLE]** NN/g also advises not relying on video alone: present essential information as text as well, with captions and a full transcript ([NN/g, Video Usability, 16 Nov 2014](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/video-usability/)). This matches the accessibility requirements in section 10.

### Recording practice

**[GAP]** No source was read on lighting, framing or audio capture. The following are unverified background recall and must be checked before they are used as standards: dialogue recording peaks around -12 to -6 dBFS, capture at 48 kHz and 24-bit, three-point lighting, and the eye line on the upper-third line. A second-hand agency description of a Hormozi-style setup mentions only a clean background, good light and a decent microphone ([thinkdmg agency blog, 10 Mar 2025, updated Oct 2026](https://thinkdmg.com/the-alex-hormozi-content-strategy-how-to-repurpose-one-video-into-30-pieces/)).

### The delivery master for YouTube

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** YouTube's recommended upload settings are an MP4 container with the moov atom at the front (fast start) and no edit lists; H.264 video, progressive scan, High Profile, 2 consecutive B-frames, a closed GOP at half the frame rate, CABAC and 4:2:0 chroma subsampling; AAC-LC or Opus audio at 48 kHz; upload at the recorded frame rate; and deinterlace before upload ([YouTube Help, recommended upload encoding settings, read 11 Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171?hl=en)). Bitrates are in section 7.

### Formats for demos, explainers, testimonials and webinars

**[GAP]** No sourced, format-specific guidance was found for screen recordings, product demos, explainers, testimonials or webinars, and no sourced turnaround standards exist in the notes. **[INFERENCE]** Until a follow-up pass fills this, apply the general rules: one promise per piece, proof-promise-plan opening, the content unit for each segment, captions and transcript, and the rights checks in sections 19 to 22 (a testimonial needs the person's consent under Lag 1978:800 and a GDPR basis).
