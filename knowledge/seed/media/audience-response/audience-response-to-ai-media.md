**When to use this:** When deciding how visible AI should be in an asset, or predicting how audiences react to AI-generated media.

**Source section:** 16. Audience response to AI-generated media

### The pattern across the tests

**[INFERENCE]** The best available tests point the same way: AI use that viewers cannot see does little harm, but visibly artificial or fully AI-made ads underperform human-made ones on effectiveness and emotion, and a large share of consumers say they want disclosure. No study found measured the effect of a disclosure label itself.

### Kantar: seamless AI does well, obvious AI does worse

**[FAST-MOVING, 4 Nov 2025]** Kantar's analysis of ads using generative AI in its LINK database, using a survey plus facial coding and published 4 November 2025, found that over 40% of ads with seamless generative AI landed in the top tier for branded cut-through, while ads with obvious AI, often with distracting or unnatural visuals, did worse. AI ads drew stronger reactions but lower net positivity, and branding was lower on average mainly where AI was obvious. From Kantar's Media Reactions 2024 survey, 41% of consumers against 29% of marketers say AI-generated ads bother them ([Kantar, 4 Nov 2025](https://www.kantar.com/Inspiration/Advertising-Media/Rethinking-AI-generated-advertising)). Kantar sells ad testing, so its commercial interest should be kept in mind.

### Ipsos and Syracuse: human-made ads were stronger

**[FAST-MOVING, 18 May 2026]** Ipsos and Syracuse University's Newhouse School tested 20 ads in 10 brand pairs with 3,000 US consumers, with the AI versions made end to end by AI models. Human-made ads were 14% stronger on short-term and 17% stronger on long-term effectiveness measures. Viewers rated human ads more entertaining, unique and imaginative without knowing which were AI; 40% of viewers were unsure either way; and 79% said companies using AI should have to disclose it ([Ipsos, 18 May 2026](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/ai-ads-are-good-enough-and-thats-problem)). Syracuse's account adds that human ads over-indexed by 11 points against benchmark while AI ads under-indexed by 5, that the brands tested were large US consumer brands, and that the human originals predate 2021 ([Syracuse University, 18 May 2026](https://news.syr.edu/2026/05/18/newhouse-research-finds-ai-ads-fall-short-on-sales-impact/)).

**[CONTESTED] [FAST-MOVING, 18 May 2026]** The two accounts of the same study differ on detection. Ipsos' page was read as saying 25% of AI-ad viewers were at least somewhat confident the ad was AI-made; Syracuse's page says 13%, and that 13% also suspected the human ads. Both agree on 40% uncertain. Check the original report before quoting either figure ([Ipsos, 18 May 2026](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/ai-ads-are-good-enough-and-thats-problem); [Syracuse University, 18 May 2026](https://news.syr.edu/2026/05/18/newhouse-research-finds-ai-ads-fall-short-on-sales-impact/)).

### How platforms treat disclosed and undisclosed AI content

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read Nov 2026]** YouTube says disclosure alone does not limit a video's audience or its monetisation eligibility, while repeated non-disclosure can lead to forced labels, removal or suspension from the Partner Programme ([YouTube Help, disclosing altered or synthetic content, read Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). TikTok says unlabelled realistic AI content may be removed and that labelling real content as AI is also a violation ([TikTok Support, AI-generated content, read Nov 2026](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content)). Meta says AI content is generally left up and labelled and not removed, while fact-checked false or altered content is demoted and ads with debunked content are rejected ([Meta Newsroom, labelling AI content, post last modified 23 Oct 2025](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/); post last modified 23 October 2025).

### Practical reading for the department

**[INFERENCE]** Use AI where it is invisible or clearly stylised, keep human creative direction and storytelling, and pre-test fully synthetic hero ads against a human-made control before spending media budget. Since most viewers cannot tell and many want disclosure, honest labelling is unlikely to be the main driver of weaker results; visible artefacts and weak ideas are. This is an inference, because no cited study isolated the label effect.

### What was not found on audience response

**[GAP]** No study was found that measures the causal effect of an AI-generated label on ad performance or brand trust. A Nielsen study was indicated only by a search-result headline and was not read ([getcoai summary of a Nielsen study, unread](https://getcoai.com/news/nielsen-consumers-find-ai-generated-advertising-annoying-boring-and-confusing)). Search-result headlines indicate UK Advertising Standards Authority rulings in 2026 on ads for AI apps, also not read ([ASA ruling page, unread](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/dribble-media-ltd.html)). No verified data was found on algorithmic down-ranking of AI content on any platform, and no Swedish or Nordic audience study was found.
