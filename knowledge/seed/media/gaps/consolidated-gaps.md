**When to use this:** When the knowledge base has nothing on a question, or before stating a fact from an unsourced domain: check whether it is a known gap and say so instead of guessing.

**Source section:** 25. Consolidated gaps the knowledge base must not paper over (with the closing Conclusion)

### Why the gaps are large

**[GAP]** Both research passes on 11 November 2026 exhausted their shared search and fetch budgets. The first pass ran out after one batch of searches and about 45 page reads; the gap-fill pass ran 11 searches and about 23 successful page reads. Whole domains therefore rest on inference. Agents must treat every item below as unverified and must not fill it from memory.

### Gaps in production, craft and design

**[GAP]** Production workflow: no source on creative briefs, storyboards, shot lists, pre-production checklists, lighting, framing, audio capture, review and approval tooling, versioning, turnaround standards, content calendars or format-specific guidance for demos, explainers, testimonials and webinars. First lead: [Frame.io Workflow Guide, unread](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/). Unverified recall only: dialogue peaks of -12 to -6 dBFS, 48 kHz and 24-bit capture, three-point lighting, eyes on the upper third, two consolidated review rounds.

**[GAP]** Short-form and long-form craft: no platform-sourced guidance on Shorts hooks, first-seconds statistics, sound-off viewing shares, series formats, show notes or podcast structure; no independent benchmarks for retention, completion, saves or shares; no independent evidence on posting frequency.

**[GAP]** Design and photography: nothing sourced on brand guidelines, typography, colour, layout, template systems, motion graphics, data visualisation, presentation design, product or portrait photography, retouching limits or image compression. WCAG contrast ratios are unverified ([W3C Understanding SC 1.4.3, unread](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)). Konsumentverket's position on retouched or misleading product images was not searched.

### Gaps in specs, loudness and asset management

**[GAP]** Specs: no official safe-zone values from Meta, YouTube, LinkedIn, Pinterest or Snapchat; the official Meta, LinkedIn, Pinterest, Snap and X spec pages were not read and the organic and ad tables rest on one vendor compilation dated 2 November 2026; feed image sizes were not researched; the Instagram profile-grid crop was not sourced.

**[GAP]** Loudness: YouTube's target, EBU R 128 s2, AES TD1008 and Spotify's podcast specification were not read; a general speech target beyond Apple's is unsourced.

**[GAP]** Asset management: no source on naming, folders, DAM selection, archive formats, retention or IPTC metadata ([IPTC Photo Metadata user guide, unread](http://www.iptc.org/std/photometadata/documentation/userguide/)); the naming pattern and rights-log fields are proposals.

### Gaps in the house style

**[GAP]** The text of *$100M Leads* and the course modules were not read, so the hook-retain-reward unit rests on summaries. The 35,000-pieces figure is inconsistent between transcripts. No primary source covers his current team, output, clip selection, tools, turnaround, thumbnail rules or a formal organic-to-ad handoff. No material by Leila Hormozi on media production was found. All of his numbers are unaudited.

### Gaps in generative tools and audience evidence

**[GAP]** Tools: no vendor page read for Kling, Black Forest Labs, Ideogram, Synthesia, Udio, Shutterstock or Microsoft; the video-model table rests on trade guides that conflict with each other; Sora's discontinuation dates are unconfirmed on OpenAI's page; pricing for Gemini Omni Flash and OpenAI image models is unverified; only Google's prompt guide was read.

**[GAP]** Audience response: no study isolates the effect of an AI label; no Swedish or Nordic study; no data on down-ranking of AI content; the detection figure in the Ipsos and Syracuse study is inconsistent between the two accounts.

### Gaps in law, disclosure and licensing

**[GAP]** AI Act: Article 50 and the Omnibus regulation were not read on EUR-Lex; the Code's placement rules per modality and the full guidelines, including any statement on advertising, were not read; the Swedish supervisory authority for Article 50 was not found. LinkedIn's AI-labelling policy and the IAB framework were not read; Meta's and TikTok's advertiser-side AI rules were not verified.

**[GAP]** Copyright and vendor terms: Adobe's indemnity terms, Microsoft's commitment, and the terms of Kling, Black Forest Labs, Synthesia, Shutterstock and Udio were not read; US and UK appellate outcomes and label licensing deals were not verified; PRV's page is from November 2024.

**[GAP]** Non-AI licensing: no stock, font, production-music, platform music library or release-template source was read; Stim and SAMI licence scope for business social and advertising use is unverified; whether stock licences permit AI reference use is unverified.

**[GAP]** Swedish law: Marknadsföringslagen was read only through a law-firm summary; comparative advertising rules were not verified; the "not digitally" limits in 23 and 24 §§ of the Copyright Act and the existence of a general parody exception need re-reading; the accessibility act's supervisory authorities, the 20 November 2026 amendment to its definitions and the conformance level of EN 301 549 were not verified; whether a company's own uploads to third-party platforms fall under the accessibility act is unresolved; whether a B2B-only SaaS company is in scope is a legal question for counsel; Swedish deepfake or likeness legislation was not researched; retention periods for consent and provenance records were not researched; sector advertising rules were not researched.

## Conclusion

**[INFERENCE]** The research changes the usual picture of a media department in one respect: for an AI-staffed team the scarce resource is not production capacity but clearance. Agents can produce variants without limit, while each published asset needs a human approver, a consent trail, a licence trail, a disclosure decision and intact provenance metadata. The house style's volume claims therefore translate, for this company, into volume of drafts and hook tests upstream of a deliberately narrow publishing gate. The three layers also reinforce each other more than they collide: clear packaging, proof that can be documented and light post-production are what the house style asks for, what YouTube's own test rewards, and what marketing law requires.

**[INFERENCE]** Two things should happen before this knowledge base is treated as complete. A follow-up research pass should close the unsourced domains (workflow, design, asset-management standards, official safe zones, stock, font and music licences, the remaining vendor terms), because agents will otherwise act on proposals that look like standards. And the owner's counsel should confirm the handful of points on which daily decisions will hang: whether the accessibility act reaches the company's video, how Article 50 labels apply to realistic AI imagery in its ads, and the wording of the consent and release documents. Until then the dated facts here expire monthly, and the safe defaults are the ones the notes support from several directions: caption everything, label realistic synthetic media, never show a real person without consent on record, and keep the record of how each asset was made.
