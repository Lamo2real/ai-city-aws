**Source section:** 12. Copyright and text-and-data-mining law: lawful access, no reservation, no reproduction

## 12. Copyright and text-and-data-mining law: lawful access, no reservation, no reproduction

### 12.1 EU and Swedish statute

[DURABLE] EU DSM Directive 2019/790, checked on primary source: text and data mining is any automated analytical technique analysing digital text and data to generate information (Article 2(2)). Under Article 3, research organisations and cultural heritage institutions may copy lawfully accessed works for scientific-research mining, with secure storage. Under Article 4, anyone may copy lawfully accessible works for mining, keeping copies only as long as needed, unless the rightsholder has expressly reserved rights in an appropriate manner, with machine-readable means as the example for online content. Article 7(1) stops contract terms overriding Article 3; Article 4 is not listed ([EUR-Lex 32019L0790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790)).

[DURABLE] Recital 18 of the DSM Directive, checked on primary source, says that for online content a reservation by machine-readable means, including metadata and a site's terms, is appropriate ([EUR-Lex 32019L0790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790)).

[DURABLE] Swedish upphovsrättslagen (1960:729), checked on the consolidated text at lagen.nu, an unofficial mirror: under 15 a § a person with lawful access may make copies for text and data mining, kept only as long as needed and not used for other purposes, unless the rightsholder has appropriately reserved the right; 15 b § covers research organisations, libraries, museums and archives, requires secure storage and voids contrary contract terms; 15 c § holds the definitions. The provisions came in through SFS 2022:1712 ([lagen.nu 1960:729](https://lagen.nu/1960:729); [SFS 2022:1712](https://svenskforfattningssamling.se/sites/default/files/sfs/2022-12/SFS2022-1712.pdf)).

[DURABLE] Swedish quotation and attribution rules, checked via lagen.nu: 22 § allows quoting from published works in line with good practice and only to the extent the purpose requires, and 11 § requires the source to be credited as good practice requires ([lagen.nu 1960:729](https://lagen.nu/1960:729)).

[DURABLE] Swedish database (catalogue) right, checked via lagen.nu on a truncated page: 49 § protects compilations of a large number of data or resulting from substantial investment, gives an exclusive right to copy and make available for 15 years, and applies 13 to 16 §§, and so the mining rules ([lagen.nu 1960:729](https://lagen.nu/1960:729)). Later amendments after SFS 2022:1712 cannot be excluded.

[FAST-MOVING, May 2026] Swedish rightsholders are reserving rights in natural language. Sveriges Författarförbund published a reservation against mining for language-model training under 15 a § and calls it a disputed question whether the exception covers such training ([Författarförbundet, 2026](https://forfattarforbundet.se/app/uploads/2026/05/FORBEHALL-MOT-TDU.pdf)).

[INFERENCE] A company's research use falls under Article 4 and 15 a §, where the opt-out applies and contracts can restrict use, and not under the research-organisation exception unless the company meets the 15 c § definition. Practical rule: reading and analysing is permitted with lawful access and no reservation; check robots.txt, site terms and metadata for reservations before bulk collection; delete working copies when the task ends; and deliver facts, figures and own-words summaries with attribution, with short quotes only.

[INFERENCE] Facts and ideas are not protected, but a summary that tracks a source's structure and wording closely can still reproduce expression, and systematic extraction from a database can infringe 49 § even if each item is a bare fact. Purchased market reports are governed by their licence, so do not paste them into external tools or redistribute them beyond licensed users without checking the terms.

### 12.2 European case law, 2024 to November 2026

[FAST-MOVING, 3 Nov 2026] The CJEU has not yet ruled in C-250/25, Like Company v Google Ireland. Advocate General Szpunar delivered his opinion on 3 November 2026 and judgment will follow at a later, unstated date. Checked on the CJEU press release, not the full opinion ([CJEU press release No 155/26](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-11/cp260155en.pdf)). An Advocate General's opinion does not bind the Court.

[FAST-MOVING, 3 Nov 2026] The Advocate General proposes that when a chatbot retrieves a press publication, reuses it to generate an answer and shows that answer, the press publisher's right can be infringed in certain circumstances. For the reproduction right this depends on whether the chatbot accessed the publication unlawfully, with the release mentioning getting round paywalls or web-scraping restrictions, and whether the answer recognisably reuses the form of expression so the user no longer needs the original; the making-available right is as a rule infringed where the form of expression is systematically recognisable in answers. Checked on the press release ([CJEU press release No 155/26](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-11/cp260155en.pdf)).

[CONTESTED] [FAST-MOVING, 3 Nov 2026] On training, the Advocate General considers the two training questions inadmissible as hypothetical and adds, for completeness, that using protected works to train an LLM is as a rule freely permitted under the mining exception (checked on the press release, [CJEU press release No 155/26](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-11/cp260155en.pdf)). This sits uneasily with the Munich rulings on memorisation below, and the researchers advise not relying on it.

[FAST-MOVING, 3 Sep 2026] Kneschke v LAION: the Hamburg Higher Regional Court on 10 December 2025 upheld the mining exceptions for dataset creation and held that a natural-language opt-out was insufficient and must be machine-readable and actionable (secondary source only, [Taylor Wessing tracker](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/campaigns/de/2025/ai-and-copyright-tracker)). The Bundesgerichtshof heard the appeal on 3 September 2026, signalled a need for EU-law clarification and a possible referral to the CJEU, and set no decision date (secondary source only, [beck-aktuell, 3 Sep 2026](https://www.beck-aktuell.de/heute-im-recht/rechtsgeschehen/bgh-ki-training-urheberrecht-fotografie-2026-09-03)).

[FAST-MOVING, 30 Dec 2024] DPG Media v HowardsHome, Amsterdam District Court, 30 December 2024: summaries were justified under exceptions including mining, and a robots.txt rule blocking only some AI bots was not a valid opt-out. Secondary source only ([Taylor Wessing tracker](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/campaigns/de/2025/ai-and-copyright-tracker)).

[FAST-MOVING, 22 Oct 2025] BoligPortal v ReData, Danish court, 22 October 2025: database right confirmed, an HTML-linked statement was an effective opt-out, and scraping breached database rights and marketing-practice law. Secondary source only ([Taylor Wessing tracker](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/campaigns/de/2025/ai-and-copyright-tracker)).

[FAST-MOVING, 11 Nov 2025] GEMA v OpenAI, Munich Regional Court, 11 November 2025: lyrics memorised in the model are reproductions and the provider is liable for outputs; appeal pending. Secondary sources only ([Taylor Wessing tracker](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/campaigns/de/2025/ai-and-copyright-tracker); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nfis/gema-notches-a-second-transatlantic-ai-copyright-win-in-germany/)).

[FAST-MOVING, 31 Jul 2026] GEMA v Suno, Munich Regional Court, 31 July 2026: the mining exception covers extraction and conversion but not memorisation in the model; there is no lawful access where technical protection was circumvented; the provider and not the users is liable; appeal expected. Secondary sources only ([Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260811-munich-regional-court-finds-unlicensed-ai-music-training-infringing); [Herzog](https://herzoglaw.co.il/en/news-and-insights/recent-developments-in-ip-case-law-on-generative-ai-8th-update/)).

[CONTESTED] Courts disagree on what form an opt-out must take: Hamburg required machine-readable form, the Danish court accepted an HTML-linked statement, and Amsterdam required the reservation to be specific (all secondary, [Taylor Wessing tracker](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/campaigns/de/2025/ai-and-copyright-tracker)). The researchers' conservative policy is to honour any reservation the agent can detect, in any form.

[DURABLE] Ryanair v PR Aviation, CJEU C-30/14, 15 January 2015, checked on primary source: the Database Directive 96/9 does not apply to a database protected neither by copyright nor by the sui generis right, so its user rights and its rule voiding contrary contract terms do not apply, and the owner may set contractual limits on use, subject to national law ([EUR-Lex 62014CJ0030](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62014CJ0030)).

[INFERENCE] Ryanair means that a site of plain factual data such as prices or timetables, which qualifies for no database protection, can still bind users by contract, so terms of use matter most exactly where IP protection is weakest. Agents must read a site's terms before bulk collection even of mere facts.

[INFERENCE] If the Court follows the Advocate General in C-250/25, research output tightens on two points the department already treats as rules: no access past paywalls or scraping restrictions, and no answers that reproduce a publisher's wording closely enough to replace the article. Own-words summaries with a link stay the safe form. Treat this as direction and not law until the judgment.

[FAST-MOVING, 4 Nov 2025] UK, Getty Images v Stability AI, High Court, 4 November 2025: the secondary copyright infringement claim failed because the model stores no copies, with limited trade mark success; under appeal. Secondary source only ([Taylor Wessing tracker](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/campaigns/de/2025/ai-and-copyright-tracker)). The appeal outcome was not verified.

[FAST-MOVING, 2026] EU AI Act timing, secondary sources only: an AI Omnibus was provisionally agreed on 7 May 2026 and reported formally adopted on 1 July 2026; the Article 50(2) duty of machine-readable labelling of AI-generated content is delayed to 2 December 2026 for systems already on the market before 2 August 2026; Annex III high-risk rules move to 2 December 2027; and the AI-literacy duty is softened to best efforts ([Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/digital-omnibus-ai-is-nearing-the-finish-line-is-eu-data-law-heading-down-the-wrong-track); [Addleshaw Goddard](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/en/insights/insights-briefings/2026/data-protection/eu-digital-omnibus-european-council-adopts-its-position/)).

[GAP] One secondary source says the AI Act's Article 53 duties for general-purpose model providers (copyright policy and training summary) became applicable on 2 August 2026 ([Herzog](https://herzoglaw.co.il/en/news-and-insights/recent-developments-in-ip-case-law-on-generative-ai-8th-update/)). The researchers flagged this date as not confirmed on the Regulation and to be treated with caution; do not rely on it.

### 12.3 United States and other jurisdictions

[FAST-MOVING, July 2026] Bartz v Anthropic (N.D. Cal., 23 June 2025): training an LLM on lawfully acquired books was held highly transformative fair use, while building a central library from pirated books was not; a settlement of about USD 1.5 billion, roughly USD 3,000 per book, received preliminary approval in September 2025 and is described as final as of July 2026. Secondary sources only ([Traverse Legal, 26 Mar 2026](https://www.traverselegal.com/blog/ai-copyright-litigation-where-the-key-cases-stand/); [Contreras slides, 30 Jul 2026](https://share.ansi.org/Shared%20Documents/Meetings%20and%20Events/2026%20ANSI%20Innovation%20Summit/Legal_Issues_Forum_2026_Presentations/Contreras%20-%20AI%20Copyright%202026-07-30.pdf)).

[FAST-MOVING, July 2026] Kadrey v Meta (N.D. Cal., 25 June 2025): summary judgment for Meta on fair use on the record before the court, with the judge stressing that the plaintiffs had not built a market-harm record and leaving room for better-evidenced claims; the case continues on other theories. Secondary sources only ([Traverse Legal](https://www.traverselegal.com/blog/ai-copyright-litigation-where-the-key-cases-stand/); [Contreras slides](https://share.ansi.org/Shared%20Documents/Meetings%20and%20Events/2026%20ANSI%20Innovation%20Summit/Legal_Issues_Forum_2026_Presentations/Contreras%20-%20AI%20Copyright%202026-07-30.pdf)). The slide summary is compressed and should not be quoted as the holding.

[FAST-MOVING, 30 Jul 2026] Thomson Reuters v Ross Intelligence (D. Del., February 2025): fair use was rejected for a non-generative legal research tool built from Westlaw headnotes, the use being commercial and not transformative with market harm favouring Thomson Reuters; an interlocutory appeal to the Third Circuit was pending as of 30 July 2026. Secondary sources only ([Contreras slides](https://share.ansi.org/Shared%20Documents/Meetings%20and%20Events/2026%20ANSI%20Innovation%20Summit/Legal_Issues_Forum_2026_Presentations/Contreras%20-%20AI%20Copyright%202026-07-30.pdf); [Traverse Legal](https://www.traverselegal.com/blog/ai-copyright-litigation-where-the-key-cases-stand/)). The argument date given in one slide deck could not be confirmed.

[FAST-MOVING, March 2026] New York Times v OpenAI and Microsoft (S.D.N.Y., filed December 2023, now in MDL No. 3143): no fair-use ruling on the merits, in discovery as of March 2026, and an order requiring OpenAI to hand over 20 million anonymised ChatGPT logs was upheld. Secondary sources only ([Traverse Legal](https://www.traverselegal.com/blog/ai-copyright-litigation-where-the-key-cases-stand/); [Contreras slides](https://share.ansi.org/Shared%20Documents/Meetings%20and%20Events/2026%20ANSI%20Innovation%20Summit/Legal_Issues_Forum_2026_Presentations/Contreras%20-%20AI%20Copyright%202026-07-30.pdf)). For security purposes this shows that provider-held conversation logs can be reached by court order.

[FAST-MOVING, 24 Jul 2026] India, ANI v OpenAI, Delhi High Court, 24 July 2026: interim injunction refused. Secondary source only ([Herzog](https://herzoglaw.co.il/en/news-and-insights/recent-developments-in-ip-case-law-on-generative-ai-8th-update/)).
