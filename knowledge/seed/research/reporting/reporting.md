## 8. Reporting: answer first, likelihood on a named scale, confidence apart

### 8.1 Probability language

[DURABLE] Express likelihood with a fixed vocabulary tied to published percentage ranges, and name the scale in the report. Two official scales exist and they differ. The US ICD 203 bands, confirmed on the PDF text, are: almost no chance or remote, 1 to 5%; very unlikely or highly improbable, 5 to 20%; unlikely or improbable, 20 to 45%; roughly even chance or roughly even odds, 45 to 55%; likely or probable, 55 to 80%; very likely or highly probable, 80 to 95%; almost certain or nearly certain, 95 to 99% ([ICD 203](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)).

[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, 20 Jul 2026] The UK probability yardstick, read on the official page (first published 24 March 2025 per page metadata, updated 20 July 2026), is: remote chance, above 0% to about 5%; highly unlikely, about 10 to 20%; unlikely, about 25 to 35%; realistic possibility, about 40% to under 50%; likely or probable, about 55 to 75%; highly likely, about 80 to 90%; almost certain, about 95% to under 100%. The gaps between bands are deliberate ([GOV.UK, Explaining Uncertainty in UK Intelligence Assessment](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment)). An earlier Defence Intelligence note of 17 February 2023 explains that the non-contiguous ranges avoid a false impression of accuracy and were informed by research on how readers understand the terms ([GOV.UK, Defence Intelligence](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/defence-intelligence-communicating-probability)).

[DURABLE] The table sets the two official likelihood scales side by side, with two older scales for comparison.

[DURABLE] Do not mix synonym sets or scales. ICD 203 discourages mixing terms from its two synonym sets, and a product that does must state that they mean the same probability ([ICD 203](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)).

[DURABLE] Keep likelihood and confidence in separate sentences. Under ICD 203, likelihood describes the event and confidence describes the analyst's basis for the judgment, and the two must not be combined in one sentence ([ICD 203](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)). The UK page likewise treats analytical confidence as a separate high, moderate or low rating reflecting how sound and stable the basis for the likelihood judgment is ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment)).

[DURABLE] Give numbers where possible. Tetlock's advice is to express judgments as specific numeric probabilities and to practise distinguishing fine gradations ([Good Judgment, 10 commandments](https://goodjudgment.com/philip-tetlocks-10-commandments-of-superforecasting/)).

[INFERENCE] The same word means different things on the two scales: "unlikely" is about 25 to 35% on the UK scale and 20 to 45% on the US scale, "likely" is 55 to 75% against 55 to 80%, and "realistic possibility" has no US twin. The researchers recommend that the department pick one scale, print its table in every report template, never mix scales, and use the UK yardstick wording with the number in brackets, for example "likely (about 60%)".

[INFERENCE] Proposed house definitions of confidence: high means multiple independent high-quality sources or a primary source read and found to support the claim, with minimal assumptions; moderate means credible but limited or partly corroborated sources, such as one reputable secondary source or two that agree; low means sparse, uncorroborated or conflicting sources, or inference. These are the researchers' proposals modelled on intelligence usage; neither ICD 203 nor the UK page as read defines the levels.

### 8.2 What a trustworthy report contains

[DURABLE] Meet ICD 203's standards. Its five analytic standards are that analysis is objective, independent of political consideration, timely, based on all available sources, and implements the tradecraft standards. The nine tradecraft standards are: source quality and credibility described; uncertainties expressed and explained; underlying information distinguished from assumptions and judgments; analysis of alternatives; customer relevance and implications; clear and logical argumentation; change from or consistency with prior judgments explained; accurate judgments; and effective visual information where appropriate ([ICD 203](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)).

[DURABLE] Communicate assumptions, limits and uncertainty to each audience. The Aqua Book requires communicating assumptions (logged with source, ownership and reliability), limitations (what the analysis can and cannot be used for) and the effect of uncertainty, tailoring communication to each audience, and setting review dates or a sunset date ([Aqua Book revision, Delivery, communication and sign-off](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/delivery_and_communication.html)).

[DURABLE] Structure uncertainty work with the UK government Uncertainty Toolkit for Analysts: jointly agree how uncertainty will be used; define and identify it; mitigate; understand and measure; present and communicate, with a one-page decision-maker summary and an emphasis on tailoring to audience and decision ([Uncertainty Toolkit for Analysts in Government](https://analystsuncertaintytoolkit.github.io/UncertaintyWeb/index.html)). The page showed no date or version.

[FAST-MOVING, 2025] Disclose AI and synthetic data. ICC/ESOMAR Article 7 requires research to be fit for purpose with limitations stated, and clients to be told when AI, emerging technologies, synthetic data or synthetic personas are used, the extent of human oversight, and that client material is kept in secure environments. Article 9 requires published findings to carry enough method and source information to assess the conclusions, to disclose any significant role of AI or synthetic data and the extent of human oversight, and not to spread conclusions the data do not support ([ESOMAR, ICC/ESOMAR Code](https://esomar.org/representation/icc-esomar-code-of-conduct)).

[FAST-MOVING, 2025] Check outputs for intellectual-property problems before release. ICC/ESOMAR Article 11 requires compliance with law and local codes, applying the Code where it is stricter, and checking results for privacy and IP infringement, including copyright issues from AI training data ([ESOMAR, ICC/ESOMAR Code](https://esomar.org/representation/icc-esomar-code-of-conduct)). The researchers read this as a concrete review step: an AI-generated deliverable needs an originality and quotation check.

[DURABLE] Report honestly. SCIP's code requires truthful, realistic recommendations and conclusions ([SCIP Code of Ethics](https://www.scip.org/page/CodeofEthics)), and PRISMA 2020 requires reviews to publish the full search strategy, the screening process, any automation tools and whether data and code are available ([BMJ 2021;372:n71](https://www.bmj.com/content/372/bmj.n71)).

[INFERENCE] [GAP] BLUF (bottom line up front) and Minto's pyramid principle are unsourced in this knowledge base; neither page could be opened. As background practice: lead with the answer or recommendation, support it with three to five grouped arguments that do not overlap and together cover the case, each backed by evidence, with introductions following situation, complication, question, answer.

[INFERENCE] Recommended executive-summary template for agents, combining ICD 203 and Aqua Book requirements: the decision and recommendation in one sentence; three to five key findings, each with a likelihood term from the chosen scale and, in a separate sentence, a confidence level with its reason; what would change the assessment; limits of use and sunset date; and next steps.

[INFERENCE] Keep findings, judgments and recommendations in separate labelled sections. A finding is what the evidence shows and is traceable to sources; a judgment is the analyst's interpretation and carries likelihood and confidence; a recommendation is an action that also depends on the organisation's goals, costs and risk appetite. A reader should be able to accept the findings and reject the recommendation.

### 8.3 Charts

[DURABLE] Follow the UK Government Analysis Function chart guidance, published 19 May 2022. Give each chart a headline title stating the message plus a statistical subtitle, kept in body text and not inside the image; start bar charts at zero and never break the bar axis, while a line chart may break the y-axis with a clear symbol mentioned in the description ([Analysis Function, Data visualisation: charts](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/data-visualisation-charts/)).

[DURABLE] Respect the Analysis Function's limits (19 May 2022): at most about ten light-grey gridlines; direct labels in place of legends; never colour alone; at most four lines on a line chart, with small multiples beyond that; four categories per stack or cluster; pie charts only with five or fewer categories that sum to a meaningful whole; no 3D; and generally no dual axes ([Analysis Function, charts](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/data-visualisation-charts/)).

[DURABLE] Further Analysis Function rules (19 May 2022): rank bars by value unless there is a natural order; use horizontal bars for long labels; do not join line points across missing data; meet WCAG 2.2 A and AA, with a text alternative that gives the takeaway, or a data table, or both; and give a specific source with a direct link in body text and offer the data as a download ([Analysis Function, charts](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/data-visualisation-charts/)).

[INFERENCE] The chart limits translate into automatic output checks: reject any generated chart with a truncated bar axis, more than four lines, a dual axis, 3D, or no source line. Show uncertainty as intervals or ranges wherever an estimate is plotted, and give n, source and date with each chart.

### 8.4 Audit trail

[DURABLE] Keep an audit trail for every study. The Aqua Book lists specification and design documents; logs of data, assumptions and decisions; user and technical documentation; advice on uncertainty and limits of use; records of methods, dependencies, process maps, version control and error reporting; the code base if published; test plans and results; a statement of assurance; and a statement that ethical concerns were addressed ([Aqua Book revision, Delivery, communication and sign-off](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/delivery_and_communication.html)).
