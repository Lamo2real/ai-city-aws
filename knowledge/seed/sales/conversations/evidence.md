**When to use this:** When tuning how calls and meetings are run: talk ratio, questions, pitch length, discovery, closing and indecision.

**Source section:** 15. The evidence on sales conversations

### How to read conversation-intelligence findings

**[VENDOR]** Almost all conversation-analytics findings come from Gong. They are large-sample but correlational, skewed to SaaS video calls, often republished without their original dates, and several date from 2016 to 2018. **[INFERENCE]** Directionally robust and repeated across datasets: interactivity over monologue, next-step discipline, multi-threading, pricing late in the conversation and early competitor discussion. Fragile: exact ratios and minute marks, which reflect roughly 45-minute SaaS video calls of 2017. These metrics were measured on human voice and video calls; transfer to AI-agent chat or email conversations is untested. **[GAP]** No Chorus, ZoomInfo, Clari or independent academic conversation-analytics findings were retrieved.

### Discovery: aim for interactivity, not a magic talk ratio

**[VENDOR; CONTESTED]** Gong's 2016 to 2017 analysis of 25,537 B2B SaaS first calls found the highest-converting talk-to-listen ratio was 43:57, with talking above 65% linked to lower conversion ([Gong, Mar 2017](https://www.gong.io/blog/turn-sales-conversations-to-exact-science)). Its 2025 re-analysis of 326,000 calls of ten minutes or more found average rep talk time of 60%, with won deals at 57% and lost deals at 62%; high performers hold a steady ratio win or lose while low performers swing from 54% on won deals to 64% on lost ones ([Gong talk-ratio analysis, Mar 2025](https://www.gong.io/blog/talk-to-listen-conversion-ratio); [Gong, Jan 2026](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-best-sales-insights-of-2025)). **[INFERENCE]** The two figures conflict on the surface and come from different samples and call types. The stable signal is direction and consistency: lost deals have more rep talk and longer monologues. Agents target frequent turn-taking and not a fixed percentage. The house-style claim that top closers listen about twice as much as they talk points the same way.

### Discovery: about fifteen purposeful questions, weighted to problems and implications

**[VENDOR]** In Gong's 2025 data won deals averaged 15 to 16 questions per call and lost deals about 20, so more questions was associated with losing ([Gong talk-ratio analysis, Mar 2025](https://www.gong.io/blog/talk-to-listen-conversion-ratio)). **[DURABLE]** Rackham found that question count correlated with success, that open or closed format did not matter, and that problem, implication and need-payoff questions distinguish successful large-sale calls ([Sales Society, Aug 2022](https://salessociety.substack.com/p/the-fascinating-science-of-spin-selling)). **[INFERENCE]** A working cap is roughly 15 purposeful questions in a live discovery call, weighted to problem, implication and need-payoff, with situation questions answered by pre-call research. **[GAP]** The often-quoted figures of 11 to 14 questions per discovery call and three to four problems discussed could not be confirmed on any Gong page fetched and must not be cited.

### Buying groups are large, and procurement is in from the start

**[FAST-MOVING, Jan 2026]** Forrester's survey of nearly 18,000 buyers reports an average of 13 internal and 9 external influencers per purchase; purchases with generative-AI features average 14 members against 7 without; 94% of buyers with groups of six or more report benefits; and procurement is a decision-maker in 53% of buying cycles, involved from the start, scrutinising features and not only price ([Forrester blog, Jan 2026](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/state-of-business-buying-2026); [Forrester press release, Jan 2026](https://www.forrester.com/press-newsroom/forrester-2026-the-state-of-business-buying/)). **[VENDOR]** 6sense puts typical purchases at about 10 people (relayed by a secondary roundup, [Corporate Visions statistics roundup](https://corporatevisions.com/blog/b2b-buying-behavior-statistics-trends)); LinkedIn and Ipsos report that 40% of buyers see larger committees ([LinkedIn/Ipsos Trust Advantage, 2025](https://business.linkedin.com/sales-solutions/resources/the-trust-advantage)); LinkedIn's 2025 research on 750 senior buyers says hidden influencers such as legal, finance and procurement hold about half of decision power and that peer recommendation was about three times as influential as lower price or claimed performance ([The Drum, Jun 2025](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/06/18/linkedin-s-new-b2b-playbook-shows-ability-defend-bad-decisions-drives-b2b-buying)). **[GAP]** No Gartner 2025 to 2026 buying-group-size figure was retrieved.

### Multi-threading and team selling

**[VENDOR]** Gong's 2025 analysis of 1.8 million opportunities found 77% of deals involve multiple contacts, won deals have twice as many buyer contacts as lost ones, strategic enterprise deals average 17 contacts, multi-threading lifts win rate 130% in deals over $50,000, won deals have selling teams 67% larger, and enterprise win rates are up to 30% higher when a sales engineer joins ([Gong, Jan 2026](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-best-sales-insights-of-2025)). Older Gong data from 2021 on 10,332 deals showed win rates of about 15% solo against about 50% with three or more seller-side roles, with uplift by role of 204% for an executive, 119% for sales leadership, 98% for customer success and 32% for sales engineers ([Gong Labs, Sep 2021](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/how-to-close-more-6-figure-deals-according-to-data/)). In the same 2021 data closed-won deal value rose from about $10,000 to about $200,000 as buyer contacts went from 3 to 25 ([Gong Labs, Sep 2021](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/how-to-close-more-6-figure-deals-according-to-data/)). LinkedIn reports top performers are three times more likely to be connected to eight or more stakeholders per account and that 57% of sellers do not start multi-threading until after the first meeting ([LinkedIn/Ipsos Trust Advantage, 2025](https://business.linkedin.com/sales-solutions/resources/the-trust-advantage)). **[INFERENCE]** These effects are correlational, since larger deals naturally involve more contacts. The agent rule: begin stakeholder mapping in the first conversation and flag a single-contact deal above a size threshold as at risk.

### Champions, coaches and mutual plans

**[VENDOR]** MEDDIC's Champion drives the deal internally; Miller Heiman's Coach wants the seller to win and shares how decisions are really made, including bad news ([MEDDICC.com history page](https://meddicc.com/resources/who-created-meddic); [Rework on Miller Heiman](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/pipeline-management/miller-heiman-strategic-selling)). Sandler's up-front contract is described as functionally similar to a mutual action plan at meeting level ([Highspot on Sandler](https://www.highspot.com/blog/sandler-sales-methodology/)). **[GAP]** No quantitative evidence was found on mutual action plans or on champion-building tactics; guidance is practitioner opinion.

### Demos: lead with the buyer's top priority and keep segments short

**[VENDOR]** Gong's 2017 study of 67,149 recorded demos with CRM outcomes found winning demos averaged 47 minutes against 36 for unsuccessful ones; talk ratio barely differed (65:35 won, 66:34 lost) but interactivity did, with 21% more speaker switches per minute, rising 36% in the second half; the longest uninterrupted pitch reached 106 seconds in losing demos and never exceeded 76 seconds in winning ones; winning demos opened with the topic the buyer spent most time on in discovery; company overview stayed under about two minutes; pricing came between minutes 38 and 46; and winning demos spent 12.7% more time on next steps, about four minutes ([Gong Labs demo study, Sep 2017](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/sales-demos/); [Gong, Apr 2017](https://www.gong.io/blog/winning-sales-conversations)). The author notes this is correlation. **[GAP]** The dataset is from 2017 and SaaS-heavy; no 2025 to 2026 replication was found, and no evidence was found on written proposals or slide design.

### Demos: avoid feature-dumping

**[VENDOR]** In Gong's 2021 analysis of 8,382 deals, win rate fell from 26% to 5% as feature-dump duration rose from 0 to 500 seconds, and value topics drove more engagement than features ([Gong Labs, Jul 2021](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/driving-next-steps-isnt-enough-this-is-what-really-moves-deals-forward/)). **[INFERENCE]** A defensible demo template: a recap of discovery and agenda check under two minutes; the highest-priority problem first; segments under roughly 75 seconds each followed by a check-in question; pricing late; about four minutes reserved for concrete next steps. Because 67% of buyers prefer rep-free experiences and trial use is heavy, agents should offer a self-guided trial alongside or before a demo.

### Demos and proof: what sellers and buyers say helps

**[VENDOR; FAST-MOVING, 2025]** In HubSpot's 2025 survey of 1,000 sellers, market research (35%) and product demos (32%) were the enablement content sellers said most helps close ([HubSpot State of Sales, Sep 2025](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/hubspot-sales-strategy-report)); this is seller perception. **[FAST-MOVING, Jan 2026]** Forrester advises designing trials to prove long-term business value, since more than 60% of buyers use them ([Forrester press release, Jan 2026](https://www.forrester.com/press-newsroom/forrester-2026-the-state-of-business-buying/)).

### Pricing conversations: late, and buyers bring it up three to four times

**[VENDOR]** Top reps discuss price around minutes 40 to 49 of a call while weaker reps scatter it, and three to four buyer pricing mentions correlate with the highest win rates (April 2017, [Gong, Apr 2017](https://www.gong.io/blog/winning-sales-conversations)). Pricing conversations have lengthened 62% since 2020 and pricing mentions rose 18% since 2022 (January 2025, [Gong, Jan 2025](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-best-sales-insights-of-2024)). **[INFERENCE]** The house-style rule of surfacing obstacles before price and presenting price after the outcome is consistent with this; the house-style setter rule of not quoting price early must give way when a buyer asks directly (section 11).

### Negotiation: anchor credibly, and consider a bolstering range

**[DURABLE]** Anchoring research (Tversky and Kahneman) shows an initial number pulls later estimates toward it, and negotiators who make the first offer often do better if the anchor is credible. Ames and Mason (2015) found across five experiments that bolstering range offers, with the seller's target as the floor and stretching upward, beat single-point offers and bracketing ranges; recipients inferred a higher reservation price, saw the offerer as more cooperative and conceded more. Limits: ranges that are too wide or start from an extreme lose effect, spans of roughly 5 to 20% above the base work best, and the approach suits price-focused one-off deals more than integrative, trust-building ones (academic summary, March 2026, [Harvard Program on Negotiation, Mar 2026](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/negotiation-skills-daily/anchoring-bias-negotiation-get-ahead-range-offer/)).

### Negotiation: interests, options, criteria and your alternative

**[DURABLE]** Getting to Yes (Fisher and Ury) advises separating people from the problem, focusing on interests and not positions, generating multiple options, insisting on objective criteria, and knowing your best alternative to a negotiated agreement so that anything worse is rejected. Against hard tactics it advises naming the tactic, treating extreme demands as proposals to be judged on merit, verifying claims in writing and negotiating the ground rules (third-party summary, [Beyond Intractability summary of Getting to Yes](https://www.beyondintractability.org/bksum/fisher-getting)). **[GAP]** The primary Harvard page on principled negotiation was not retrieved; unverified background knowledge puts first publication in 1981.

### Negotiation: most sellers concede on price although buyers could pay more

**[VENDOR]** RAIN Group's study of 713 buyers and sellers, including 202 procurement professionals, reports that top negotiators open high and negotiate down, that their top differentiator is understanding each side's power and leverage, that most sellers give pure price concessions while most buyers admit they could pay more, and that procurement cites cost as a success metric 12 times more often than quality ([RAIN Group negotiation research](https://www.rainsalestraining.com/sales-research/negotiation)). The study date and detailed figures sit behind a download form. **[INFERENCE]** Discount discipline for agents: never concede on price alone; trade for term, volume, scope, payment timing or reference rights. Use a bolstering range only for simple price quotes; for multi-issue enterprise deals prefer multiple options built on the buyer's own numbers, prepared before the proposal because procurement is present from the start.

### Negotiation techniques that are not evidence-based here

**[GAP]** Chris Voss's techniques (mirroring, labelling, calibrated questions and staged offers) were not retrieved; as unverified background knowledge they derive from hostage-negotiation practice and lack controlled evidence in commercial sales, so they must not be described as evidence-based. No rigorous evidence was found on proposal format, number of pricing options or optimal discount ceilings.

### Contract friction is a controllable loss factor

**[VENDOR]** A smooth contracting experience makes about seven in ten buyers more likely to repurchase, according to 6sense's 2024 research as relayed by a secondary roundup ([Corporate Visions statistics roundup](https://corporatevisions.com/blog/b2b-buying-behavior-statistics-trends)). **[INFERENCE]** Together with the Paper Process field in MEDDPICC, this argues for mapping signature steps, legal review and procurement requirements early in the deal.

### Objections: pause, clarify, answer briefly, check

**[VENDOR]** In Gong's 2018 analysis of 67,149 demo recordings, top reps paused longer after an objection than in normal conversation, held a steady pace, responded with clarifying questions, kept speaker-switching up and checked understanding more often; average reps sped up and delivered a monologue of about 21 seconds ([Gong Labs, Mar 2018](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/handling-sales-objections/)). **[DURABLE]** Rackham found that in large sales objection-handling skill did not separate successful from unsuccessful calls ([Sales Society, Aug 2022](https://salessociety.substack.com/p/the-fascinating-science-of-spin-selling)). **[INFERENCE]** The agent protocol: pause, acknowledge, one clarifying question, a short answer (well under 20 seconds of speech or a few sentences of text), then check it landed. In text channels the pause means not replying with a wall of text. Frequent objections are a discovery-quality signal for the manager agent, because prevention through better discovery matters more than rebuttal skill. **[GAP]** Gong's article gives no pause length in seconds; circulating figures on pause multiples and question-response rates were not confirmed, and no data-backed frequency ranking of objection categories was found.

### Status quo versus indecision: diagnose before responding

**[INFERENCE]** Decision rule: if the buyer disputes the problem or its size, the issue is status quo, so build the gap and quantify the cost of inaction. If the buyer agrees but stalls, repeatedly asks for more information or cannot choose between options, the issue is indecision, so apply recommend, narrow and de-risk and do not add urgency or discount. **[VENDOR]** Supporting data: Miller Heiman's Even Keel and Overconfident modes describe stakeholders who see no reason to change ([Rework on Miller Heiman](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/pipeline-management/miller-heiman-strategic-selling)); LinkedIn's 2025 study found fear of being unable to defend a purchase the most decisive buying factor ([The Drum, Jun 2025](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/06/18/linkedin-s-new-b2b-playbook-shows-ability-defend-bad-decisions-drives-b2b-buying)); Forrester's 2024 data as relayed by a roundup says 86% of B2B purchases stall and 81% of buyers end up dissatisfied with the chosen provider ([Corporate Visions statistics roundup](https://corporatevisions.com/blog/b2b-buying-behavior-statistics-trends)); sellers surveyed by HubSpot in 2025 named no product fit (37%) and poor value for money (35%) as top deal-killers ([HubSpot State of Sales, Sep 2025](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/hubspot-sales-strategy-report)).

### Competitor objections: discuss competitors early

**[VENDOR]** Early discussion of competitors was associated with a 49% higher chance of closing than none in Gong's 2017 data, while late-stage competitor discussion came with slightly lower odds and smaller deals ([Gong Labs, May 2017](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/win-competitive-deals/)). The 2024 update found an early competitive mention raised enterprise win odds 32% and that competitive mentions rose 57% since 2022 ([Gong, Jan 2025](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-best-sales-insights-of-2024)). **[INFERENCE]** Agents ask about alternatives being considered, including doing nothing, during discovery, within the comparative-claims rule in section 11.

### Closing: next steps predict progress; closing techniques do not

**[VENDOR]** Deals where next steps were never discussed closed at 5% against 20% where they were, and value-based discussion precedes and fuels that momentum (2021, 8,382 deals, [Gong Labs, Jul 2021](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/driving-next-steps-isnt-enough-this-is-what-really-moves-deals-forward/)). Risk-reversal language at close was associated with a 32% higher win likelihood ([Gong, Mar 2017](https://www.gong.io/blog/turn-sales-conversations-to-exact-science)). At the decision point the JOLT guidance is a clear recommendation, limited further exploration and safety nets, not urgency ([Challenger Inc., Feb 2024](https://challengerinc.com/losing-to-customer-indecision/)). **[INFERENCE]** Agent rule: every interaction ends with a dated, mutual next step that requires a buyer action; a meeting that ends without one is flagged by the manager agent. Unverified background knowledge: Rackham's alternative to closing is the "advance", a specific buyer action that moves the sale forward. **[GAP]** No independent evidence was found on assumptive or now-or-never closes in modern B2B beyond Rackham and JOLT.

### Follow-up: add a call attempt and a voicemail to email

**[VENDOR]** In Gong's 2024 data on more than 300 million cold calls, a call attempt nearly doubled email reply rate (3.44% against 1.81%) even without a live connection, and leaving a voicemail lifted reply rate from 2.73% to 5.87% ([Gong, Jan 2025](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-best-sales-insights-of-2024)). LinkedIn reports top sellers are six times more likely to respond to buying signals within one hour ([LinkedIn/Ipsos Trust Advantage, 2025](https://business.linkedin.com/sales-solutions/resources/the-trust-advantage)). **[GAP]** No data was found on optimal follow-up cadence, number of touches or content for mid-deal follow-up, and the applicability of the call-plus-email finding to AI-agent outreach is untested.

### Sales psychology: what has support

**[DURABLE]** Anchoring is robust in negotiation research ([Harvard Program on Negotiation, Mar 2026](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/negotiation-skills-daily/anchoring-bias-negotiation-get-ahead-range-offer/)). Loss aversion appears in buying as omission bias: people prefer the error of not acting to the error of acting, which drives no-decision outcomes ([CustomerThink interview with Matt Dixon](https://customerthink.com/the-problem-of-customer-indecision-and-how-to-get-over-it-interview-with-matt-dixon/)). **[VENDOR]** Social proof from peers has support in buyer surveys: peer recommendation about three times as influential as price or claimed performance ([The Drum, Jun 2025](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/06/18/linkedin-s-new-b2b-playbook-shows-ability-defend-bad-decisions-drives-b2b-buying)), and 56% of buyers, 71% in enterprise, consult existing users before buying (TrustRadius 2024, relayed by [Corporate Visions statistics roundup](https://corporatevisions.com/blog/b2b-buying-behavior-statistics-trends)). Risk reversal is associated with higher win rates, consistent with loss aversion ([Gong, Apr 2017](https://www.gong.io/blog/winning-sales-conversations)).

### Sales psychology: field effects are smaller than popular accounts imply

**[VENDOR]** One practitioner's 72 A/B tests of behavioural principles won only 29% of the time (anchoring 7 of 19, loss framing 2 of 13, scarcity and urgency 2 of 10, defaults 5 of 16), and the winning scarcity and loss-framing cases all involved genuine scarcity or a real imminent loss (March 2026, [Atticus Li, Mar 2026](https://www.atticusli.com/blog/posts/behavioral-science-claims-vs-experiment-reality/)). This is a single practitioner in a consumer conversion context with no raw data, who sells consulting. **[GAP]** No peer-reviewed meta-analysis of persuasion principles in B2B sales was retrieved; as unverified background knowledge, Cialdini's principles rest on a mix of field and lab studies and the wider behavioural-science literature has had replication problems.

### Ethical operating rules that the evidence supports

**[INFERENCE]** Five rules follow from the evidence and from the law in sections 28 and 29: scarcity and deadlines only when true; social proof only from real, attributable, relevant customers; loss framing limited to the buyer's own quantified cost of inaction during the case-for-change stage and never at the decision stage; no fabricated urgency, fake competitor claims or hidden terms; and disclosure of AI identity with no impersonation of a human reference. These are also the tactics the available data shows working.
