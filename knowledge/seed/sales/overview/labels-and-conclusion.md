# House style sells; law and evidence govern

As of November 2026 the foundational knowledge for an AI sales department is a three-layer stack: compliance rules that always win, independent evidence that tunes tactics, and Alex Hormozi's sales system as the default structure underneath both. The house style supplies the working skeleton: answer leads within about a minute, run a question-led conversation (Clarify, Label, Overview, Sell the outcome, Explain away concerns, Reinforce), sort resistance into five buckets (time, money, fit, authority, avoidance), step down through payment terms instead of discounting the same thing, sequence attraction, upsell, downsell and continuity offers, and measure every stage through to cash collected; all of his performance numbers are his own unaudited claims. The evidence layer corrects it where deals get larger or buyers hesitate: classic closing techniques hurt large sales in Rackham's research, adding fear or discounts to an indecisive buyer made a loss more likely in 84% of cases in the JOLT dataset, the vendor a buying group prefers before contact wins roughly 77 to 80% of deals, and 67% of B2B buyers say they prefer a rep-free experience while 69% still want a rep to validate what AI told them. The compliance layer removes several house-style moves outright for a Sweden-based seller: a Swedish consumer contract from a trader-initiated call is invalid unless the consumer accepts in writing after the call, false urgency and scarcity are blacklisted under EU consumer law, cold email needs consent in Germany, Denmark and the Netherlands even for business recipients, AI systems must disclose themselves at first interaction under AI Act Article 50 (applicable since 2 August 2026), and a new Swedish consumer credit act enters into force on 20 November 2026. Current operating benchmarks are sobering and mostly vendor-sourced: new-business win rates near 19% with 91-day cycles against 45% and 52 days for expansion, median cold-email reply of 0.74% per contact, about one dial in ten connecting, only 32% of inbound demo forms receiving any reply, and no independent evidence that autonomous AI SDRs outperform humans. The practical conclusion is that agents win on speed, research depth, discipline and truthfulness, not on volume or pressure, and that pricing, discounts, contract terms, gifts and anything touching Swedish consumers by phone need hard gates outside the model. Where the research found no fetched source, this document says so instead of filling the hole from memory.

## How to read and split this document

This document is written to be cut into atomic knowledge-base entries. Every paragraph is meant to stand alone: it opens with one or more labels, names its subject in full, and keeps each number next to its date and source link. Section numbers group domains; sub-headings name the single topic of the paragraphs beneath them. Tables repeat their source link in every row so that a row can be split out as its own entry. When splitting, keep the label, the date and the link with the claim.

The labels mean the following. **[HOUSE STYLE]** marks Alex Hormozi's frameworks and rules, which the owner has chosen as the department's default style; it is a preference and not independent evidence, and every number attached to it is his own self-reported, unaudited claim unless stated otherwise. **[DURABLE]** marks a principle, definition, formula or long-standing legal rule that has held for years and is unlikely to age quickly. **[FAST-MOVING, date]** marks a figure, platform rule, survey result or legal status that will age, with the date of the source. **[VENDOR]** marks a figure or recommendation from a company with a commercial interest in the conclusion (tool vendors, training firms, agencies), including large platform datasets from such companies; treat it as correlational and self-selected. **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** marks a platform's or company's own statement about itself that nobody has independently verified. **[CONTESTED]** marks a point where sources disagree or where house style and evidence pull in different directions. **[INFERENCE]** marks a synthesis or convention written by the researchers that no single fetched source states; adopt it as a default and tune it against your own data. **[GAP]** marks something the research did not verify or could not find.

Three qualifiers are added inside labels where they matter. For house-style material, "primary transcript" means the point was read on a transcript of Hormozi's own video or on acquisition.com, and "second-hand summary" means it comes from a third-party summary (Shortform, SuperSummary, Deciphr, Summify, tubegraph, BAM) and was not checked against the original. For legal material, "checked on primary source" means the statute or regulator page was read during the research, "secondary source only" means a law firm, trade or vendor page was read and the primary text was not, and "unverified, not fetched" means the point comes from the researchers' background knowledge with a link to where it should be confirmed. For sales material, "unverified background knowledge" has the same meaning. Agents must not gate a live decision on an unverified legal point without confirmation.

Six caveats apply to the whole document. First, legal content is orientation as of 11 November 2026 and not legal advice. Second, the Hormozi transcripts were read through a third-party host of auto-generated transcripts and a summarising tool, so all of his material here is paraphrase, never his wording. Third, almost all benchmarks are US-weighted B2B SaaS; the only Nordic dataset with numbers is the Monterro benchmark, and no robust Nordic outbound benchmark exists. Fourth, conversation-intelligence numbers are large-sample but correlational, mostly from Gong, and several famous ones date from 2016 to 2018. Fifth, reply-rate figures from different email vendors use different denominators (per email, per contact, per sequence) and cannot be compared directly. Sixth, the research found essentially nothing research-grade on B2C high-ticket selling or B2C lead handling outside Hormozi's own material and the consumer-law rules, so consumer-sales guidance here is house style bounded by law.

## Conclusion

The research changes the starting assumption in one respect that matters for design: the house style and the best independent evidence agree far more often than they collide. Both say diagnose before pitching, talk less than the buyer, keep pitches short, raise obstacles before price, protect price by trading terms, always leave with a next step, respond fast and keep selling after the sale. The collisions are concentrated in a narrow band of tactics (repeated asking, pressure reframes, manufactured urgency, taking payment on the call, penalty trials and card-on-file charges) and in a narrow set of situations (multi-stakeholder deals, hesitant buyers, Swedish and EU consumers). That makes the department buildable: the conversation structures, objection taxonomy, payment ladder, offer sequence and stage metrics can be loaded as defaults, and a small number of hard gates outside the model remove the risky band entirely.

Three things should be revisited on a schedule, because the knowledge base will be wrong about them first. The legal layer is moving: the Swedish telephone-sales inquiry reports on 15 January 2027, the Consumer Credit Act applies from 20 November 2026, the AI Act marking deadline falls on 2 December 2026 and US consent-revocation rules change through January 2027. The benchmarks are vendor-sourced and US-weighted, so the department's own trailing data should replace them within two or three quarters. And the central open question for an AI sales team has no evidence yet in either direction: nobody has published whether buyers respond to a disclosed AI agent as they do to a human when the agent is fast, specific and truthful. The department is positioned to measure that itself, and held meetings that convert to opportunities is the metric that will answer it.
