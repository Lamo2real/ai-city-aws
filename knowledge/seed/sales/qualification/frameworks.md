**When to use this:** When choosing how to qualify a deal and how to record what is still unknown.

**Source section:** 14. Qualification frameworks: which one, when, and how to record unknowns

### Choose the framework by deal complexity

**[VENDOR]** Practitioner guidance from July 2026 gives five selection rules: define the job (short cycle and clear budget suits BANT or ANUM; a complex committee suits MEDDIC; challenge-first discovery suits CHAMP or SPICED); map complexity (customisation, stakeholder count, cycle length) and ignore fashion; pick what managers will actually coach and score; accept hybrids if the rules are written down; and record empty fields as unknown, especially the champion ([Demodesk, Jul 2026](https://demodesk.ai/blog/sales-qualification-frameworks-how-to-choose)). A common pairing is BANT at the top of the funnel and MEDDPICC from discovery to close ([MEDDICC.com framework comparison](https://meddicc.com/resources/meddicc-versus-other-qualification-frameworks-like-bant)). **[GAP]** No empirical study comparing win rates or forecast accuracy between frameworks was found; all comparisons are practitioner opinion.

### BANT and its gaps

**[VENDOR]** BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timing) originated at IBM and fits clear procurement paths and shorter cycles; it is weak when buying is collaborative and budget is created late. Its stated gaps are that authority is one checkbox although enterprise deals have many stakeholders, that it says nothing on decision criteria or process, has no champion concept and ignores competition including the status quo. It maps to MEDDPICC as Budget to Metrics, Authority to Economic Buyer plus Champion, Need to Implicate Pain, and Timing to Decision Process plus Paper Process; Decision Criteria and Competition have no BANT equivalent ([Demodesk, Jul 2026](https://demodesk.ai/blog/sales-qualification-frameworks-how-to-choose); [MEDDICC.com framework comparison](https://meddicc.com/resources/meddicc-versus-other-qualification-frameworks-like-bant)).

### The in-between frameworks: CHAMP, GPCTBA/C&I, ANUM, FAINT, SPICED

**[VENDOR]** CHAMP (Challenges, Authority, Money, Prioritization) suits SaaS deals where the return-on-investment conversation matters more than a pre-set budget. HubSpot's GPCTBA/C&I (Goals, Plans, Challenges, Timeline, Budget, Authority, Consequences, Implications) is the most thorough and is heavy for transactional motions. ANUM (Authority, Need, Urgency, Money) reorders BANT. FAINT (Funds, Authority, Interest, Need, Timing) targets buyers with no predefined budget. SPICED (Situation, Pain, Impact, Critical Event, Decision; Winning by Design) ties outcomes to a deadline and works across stages ([Demodesk, Jul 2026](https://demodesk.ai/blog/sales-qualification-frameworks-how-to-choose)).

### A two-tier default schema for the agents

**[INFERENCE]** A reasonable default is a four-field quick qualifier (need, impact, access to authority, timeline) for inbound and small deals, expanding to the eight MEDDPICC fields once a deal passes a size or stakeholder-count threshold set from the department's own data. Budget as a gate is increasingly unreliable because buying groups are large and budgets are created during the process; pain, impact and decision process are better early gates. **[VENDOR]** Platform data supports three specific gates: a decision-maker involved in the first two stages is associated with a 50 to 55% higher win rate (the report is internally inconsistent on the figure), top performers are 24% more likely to disqualify deals outside the ideal customer profile early, and 76% of average-performer deals lack a critical event (2024 data on 655,000 opportunities, [Ebsta/Pavilion 2025 GTM Benchmarks](https://benchmarks.ebsta.com/hubfs/V3%202025%20Benchmark%20Report/gtm_benchmarks_digital_report.pdf?hsLang=en)).
