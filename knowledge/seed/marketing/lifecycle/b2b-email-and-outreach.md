**When to use this:** When planning B2B newsletters, nurture or cold email.

## B2B email, nurture and cold outreach

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026 survey]** B2B evidence points to owned media gaining budget as AI search erodes web traffic: 40% of B2B marketers are spending more on owned media and it is the top 2027 investment priority at 42% ([Content Marketing Institute](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/articles/b2b-content-marketing-trends-research)). MailerLite's consulting, software and media medians above serve as B2B newsletter proxies. **[INFERENCE]** For nurture, the thought-leadership evidence in section 16 supports writing for non-obvious stakeholders (finance, legal, IT, procurement) and measuring by account-level engagement instead of individual lead score. **[VENDOR practitioner guidance, 2026]** Cold-outreach tooling advice is to send from a separate domain aged 2 to 4 weeks, with 2 to 5 mailboxes per domain, 20 to 40 cold emails per mailbox per day, and each mailbox warmed for 2 to 4 weeks, keeping Postmaster spam rate below 0.1% ([Inboxlogy](https://www.inboxlogy.com/blog/cold-email-deliverability-rules-2026)). **[DURABLE; Google]** Google's primary rules cut against this tooling: do not buy email addresses, do not send to people who did not sign up, and do not impersonate threaded replies ([Google Email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126?hl=en)). **[INFERENCE]** Lookalike domains and mailbox rotation are deliverability workarounds, not compliance. An agent should treat cold outreach as a human-approved activity with jurisdiction checks: the US is opt-out under CAN-SPAM, Denmark requires consent even for B2B, and Sweden, Norway and Finland allow opt-out only to role-relevant or generic business addresses (section 19).

## US email law

**[DURABLE, US law]** CAN-SPAM requires truthful headers and subject lines, identification as an ad, a postal address, and opt-outs honoured within 10 business days; it applies to B2B email and liability cannot be contracted away ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)). EU, Nordic and Swedish email and SMS consent rules are in section 19.
