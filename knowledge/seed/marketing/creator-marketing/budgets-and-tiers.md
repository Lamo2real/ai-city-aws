## Budgets are rising and moving to smaller creators

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026; mostly vendor and secondary]** eMarketer expects US influencer marketing spending to grow 15.7% in 2026 and reach $13.7 billion by 2027 ([EMARKETER](https://www.emarketer.com/content/faq-on-influencer-marketing--what-how-brands-use)); a secondary source puts 2026 US spend at $12.17 billion and says marketers put a median 26% of social budgets into creators, up from 18% in 2024 ([Gigapay, 6 Jul 2026](https://www.gigapay.com/blog/the-state-of-us-influencer-marketing-spend-in-2026)). The IAB's broader measure of US creator ad spend including paid amplification is about $37 billion in 2025 and about $44 billion forecast for 2026 ([FirstPromoter roundup, Oct 2026](https://firstpromoter.com/blog/influencer-marketing-statistics)). CreatorIQ reports that 71% of organisations increased creator investment year on year and that nearly two-thirds of new spend was reallocated from other paid and digital channels ([CreatorIQ](https://www.creatoriq.com/white-papers/state-of-creator-marketing-trends-2026)). **[VENDOR survey, 4 May 2026, 600+ marketers]** Influencer Marketing Hub finds 87.49% expect budgets to rise and 66.3% run programmes in-house. Marketers are expanding nano (51.43% expanding, 10.00% contracting), micro (52.83%, 7.55%) and UGC creators (50.00%, none contracting) far more than macro creators (20.59% expanding, 20.58% contracting). The top reported problems are rising creator costs (35.4%) and fake or bot followers (56.5% of fraud issues); measurement leans on promo codes (45.9%), affiliate links (26.0%) and native shop features (25.0%), and the top KPI is brand awareness (55.1%). Formats rated most effective are long-form video (83%), short-form video (80%) and live shopping (62%) ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-marketing-benchmark-report/?Sid=1)).
