**When to use this:** When choosing what benefit to lead with, writing messaging, or planning customer interviews.

## Use jobs-to-be-done to find the message, and the four forces to structure it

**[DURABLE]** Customers "hire" products to do a job, and nearly every job has functional, social and emotional dimensions. In the milkshake case, attribute research on a demographic segment did not lift sales; observation showed morning commuters hiring the shake to make a dull drive interesting and stave off hunger one-handed, competing with bagels, bananas and doughnuts, while parents hired it for a different job. Job-defined markets are usually larger than product-category markets ([HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/what-customers-want-from-your-products)). **[DURABLE; Ulwick/Strategyn]** Outcome-Driven Innovation writes each need as a desired outcome statement (direction of improvement + unit of measure + object of control + contextual clarifier) and scores it with two survey questions, importance and satisfaction. The opportunity algorithm, first published in Harvard Business Review in January 2002, is Opportunity = Importance + max(Importance − Satisfaction, 0), with Strategyn's bands at over 10 for a solid opportunity, over 12 high and over 15 extreme ([Strategyn white paper](https://innovationroundtable.com/summit/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/Strategyn_what_is_Outcome_Driven_Innovation.pdf); [Strategyn template](https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done-template/)). **[DURABLE; Moesta]** The four forces of progress are the push of the situation and the pull of the new solution, which drive a switch, and the anxiety of the new and the habit of the present, which restrain it; a switch happens only when push plus pull exceed anxiety plus habit. Pull is the customer's imagined better life, not a feature list; anxiety is reduced with guarantees, social proof, onboarding and risk removal, not features; and teams over-invest in pull while neglecting anxiety and habit ([jobstobedone.org](https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/)). The switch-interview method is to interview recent buyers, reconstruct the purchase timeline, ask what changed, and push vague words down to specific examples ([Business of Software](https://businessofsoftware.org/talks/understanding-your-customer-jtbd/)). **[Practitioner claim, not a study]** Moesta reports that about 10 deep interviews of 30 to 60 minutes with recent buyers typically reveal 3 to 5 buying patterns, with little new after 6 or 7, and cites a mattress ad built on the struggling moment that lifted sales 37%; both are podcast claims ([SaaS Club podcast](https://saasclub.io/podcast/jobs-to-be-done-bob-moesta-423/)). **[INFERENCE]** A messaging map from the four forces: the hook states the push (the struggling moment); the promise states the pull (the progress); proof, guarantee and trial address anxiety; migration help and "works with what you already use" address habit. Ulwick's opportunity scores give a defensible order for which benefits to lead with.
