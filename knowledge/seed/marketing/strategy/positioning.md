**When to use this:** When a product or brand has no written positioning, before briefs, messaging or channel plans.

## Build positioning bottom-up, then compress it into one sentence

**[DURABLE; secondary summary of April Dunford's *Obviously Awesome*, 2019]** Dunford frames positioning as context setting: it lets customers quickly grasp what the product is, why it is special and why it matters to them. Her components are competitive alternatives (what customers would do if the product did not exist), unique attributes, value and proof, target market characteristics (who cares most about that value), market category, and optionally relevant trends. Her ten steps in order are: understand the customers who love the product; form a positioning team; align vocabulary and drop positioning baggage; list true competitive alternatives; isolate unique attributes; map attributes to value themes; determine who cares a lot; find a market frame of reference that puts strengths at the centre; layer on a trend carefully; and capture the positioning so it can be shared. She describes three market-category styles: head to head (win an existing category), big fish in a small pond (dominate a sub-segment the leader under-serves) and create a new game (build a new category) ([Nat Eliason notes](https://nateliason.com/notes/obviously-awesome-april-dunford)). **[DURABLE; secondary reproductions]** Geoffrey Moore's template from *Crossing the Chasm* (1991) compresses the result: "For (target customer) who (statement of the need or opportunity), the (product name) is a (product category) that (statement of key benefit). Unlike (primary competitive alternative), our product (statement of primary differentiation)" ([MistyWest worksheet](https://www.mistywest.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/MistyWest-Building-a-Positioning-Statement.pdf); [Tailor Brands](https://www.tailorbrands.com/blog/positioning-statement)). **[INFERENCE]** A workable agent procedure is to run Dunford's steps four to eight to generate the raw positioning with evidence for each slot, then render it in Moore's sentence for briefs; Moore's template forces a single alternative and a single benefit.
