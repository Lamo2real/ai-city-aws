**When to use this:** When building a messaging hierarchy or writing copy from positioning.

## Value proposition and messaging hierarchy are organising devices, not proven formulas

**[DURABLE; vendor-owned frameworks]** Strategyzer's Value Proposition Canvas pairs a Customer Profile (jobs, pains, gains) with a Value Map (products and services, pain relievers, gain creators). Pains are negative emotions, undesired costs, situations and risks before, during or after the job; gains are benefits expected, desired or surprising; each is ranked by relevance and frequency ([Strategyzer](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/achieve-product-market-fit-with-our-brand-new-value-proposition-designer-canvas); [Strategyzer](https://www.strategyzer.com/value-proposition)). Strategyzer's listed mistakes are mixing several segments in one profile (for example payers and users), listing only the jobs, pains and gains the product already solves, confusing pains with pain relievers, and trying to address every pain and gain instead of the highest-priority ones; social and emotional jobs often carry the most deeply felt pains ([Strategyzer](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/5-common-mistakes-to-avoid-when-using-the-value-proposition-canvas)). Its book summary says strong fit typically means addressing 50 to 70% of the customer's most significant pains and gains, a heuristic with no data shown ([Strategyzer](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/value-proposition-design-book-summary)). **[VENDOR]** StoryBrand's SB7 (Donald Miller, 2017) orders a message as: a character (the customer) has a problem, meets a guide (the brand) who gives them a plan and calls them to action, which ends in success and helps them avoid failure. The brand is the guide and never the hero, showing empathy plus authority, and a simple plan of about three steps reduces perceived purchase risk ([StoryBrand workbook](https://storybrand.com/downloads/StoryBrand-Online-Marketing-Course-Workbook.pdf)). The problem is commonly taught in three layers (external, internal, philosophical) and calls to action in two kinds (direct and transitional) ([Gravity Global, secondary](https://www.gravityglobal.com/blog/complete-guide-storybrand-framework)). A message house is a claim-and-proof hierarchy with a roof (umbrella message), pillars (key messages) and evidence (proof points) ([Umbrex, secondary](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/storybrand-sb7-framework/)). **[INFERENCE]** A usable hierarchy for an agent is positioning, then value proposition (canvas fit), then a message house with one umbrella claim, three pillars and proof under each, then channel copy in a narrative order such as SB7.
