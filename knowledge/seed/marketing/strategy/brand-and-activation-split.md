**When to use this:** When splitting a budget between brand building and performance activation.

## Treat 60/40 as a starting prior, and about half-and-half in B2B

**[DURABLE, CONTESTED]** Les Binet's suggested split is about 60% brand building and 40% activation, on the argument that sales effects account for only 40% of advertising payback while 60% comes from long-term brand effects; the common mistake is trying to do both jobs in one execution, and a strong brand makes people more responsive to performance marketing ([Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/les-binet-brand-performance/)). The critique, from Neil Krikul of the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute on 13 August 2026, is that the split came from IPA Databank cases submitted for awards and so may not be representative, and that Binet and Field themselves said it varies by category. Krikul recommends combining four budgeting methods instead of a "magic split": objective-and-task (zero-based) budgeting; performance spend taken to the point of diminishing returns with CPA capped at break-even; brand spend planned on reach, frequency and continuity adjusted for purchase cycle; and share-of-voice benchmarking against competitors. Smaller brands can lean more to short-term activation to grow fast ([B&T, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.bandt.com.au/the-magic-split-between-brand-performance-spending-needs-to-end/)).

**[DURABLE in direction; small sample]** Binet and Field's B2B study for the LinkedIn B2B Institute (2019, *The 5 Principles of Growth in B2B Marketing*) concluded that B2B best practice is "mainly the same as in B2C" ([The Drum, 2019](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/2019/05/23/exclusive-look-binet-and-field-s-new-b2b-marketing-research)). Its five principles are: invest in share of voice; balance brand and activation; expand the customer base; maximise mental availability; and harness the power of emotion ([IPA](https://ipa.co.uk/effworks/marketing-marketing-v2/five-principles-of-growth-in-b2b-marketing/)). The B2B optimum in the data is **46% brand and 54% activation**, against roughly 60/40 for B2C, described as a guiding principle and not a precise rule. Measured as the average number of very large business effects per campaign: loyalty strategies scored 0.0, acquisition 1.0 and reach strategies aimed at both 1.6; by primary objective, activation goals scored 0.7, other brand goals 0.9, awareness 1.6 and fame 2.2; emotional campaigns scored 1.4 against 0.2 for rational campaigns, and fame campaigns 2.4. A foreword survey found only 4% of B2B marketers measure impact beyond six months. The evidence base is IPA Databank B2B cases from 1998 to 2018, **fewer than 50 cases** out of almost 1,500 campaigns, biased to award entrants and skewed to the UK ([LinkedIn B2B Institute PDF](https://news.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/news/en-us/linkedin-pressroom-page/LIN_B2B-Marketing-Report-Digital.pdf)). **[INFERENCE]** With so few cases, quote the B2B split as "about half and half, slightly activation-heavy", not as a precise target; the acquisition, fame and emotion findings agree with the Double Jeopardy evidence from an independent dataset.
