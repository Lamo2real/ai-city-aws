**When to use this:** When setting growth targets or loyalty KPIs, or when someone proposes growing mainly through retention or loyalty programmes.

## Growth comes from penetration, mental availability and physical availability

**[DURABLE]** The Double Jeopardy law says smaller-share brands have fewer buyers who are also slightly less loyal; loyalty is largely a predictable function of market share, so sustained growth through loyalty alone is not possible. The Ehrenberg-Bass Institute shows the law in UK business banking, US business insurance, concrete and aircraft markets and summarises it as "You have more loyalty because you are big, rather than you are big because you have more loyalty." Loyalty metrics have natural ceilings, so loyalty KPIs should be set against the Double Jeopardy norm for a brand of that size. To test the law in a category, survey a cross-section of category buyers (not only customers), ask which brands they use from a prompted list plus a loyalty measure (number of products held, share solely loyal, share naming the brand as main supplier), and order brands by penetration. Mental availability is being thought of by more customers in more buying situations, a function of media, branding and message quality; physical availability is being easy to find and buy, through presence and prominence in channels, sales coverage and portfolio; each amplifies the other ([Ehrenberg-Bass](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/the-double-jeopardy-law-in-b2b-shows-the-way-to-grow)). **[DURABLE]** Byron Sharp adds that most of a brand's buyers are very light buyers; growth nudges everyone's propensity up and shows mostly as non-buyers becoming once-buyers. Hitting even modest share targets typically needs quarterly penetration to double or triple while average purchase rate rises by a fraction of one occasion, and changes in sales and profit are largely due to acquisition performance, not retention ([Byron Sharp, 2016](https://byronsharp.wordpress.com/2016/09/18/answering-critics/)).
