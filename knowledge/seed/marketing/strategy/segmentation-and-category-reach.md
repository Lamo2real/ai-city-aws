**When to use this:** When setting a media target, deciding how narrow targeting should be, or setting short-term sales expectations.

## Segment for relevance, but define the reachable target broadly

**[DURABLE, CONTESTED in emphasis]** Christensen, Cook and Hall argued in Harvard Business Review (2005) that demographic and product segmentation yields "one-size-fits-none" products and that the job, not the customer, should be the unit of analysis; demographic targeting works mainly where the job coincides with the demographic group ([HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/what-customers-want-from-your-products)). The Ehrenberg-Bass counterpoint is that growth requires reaching all category buyers and that a brand with "excess loyalty" usually has a penetration deficit caused by restricted targeting or distribution ([Ehrenberg-Bass](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/the-double-jeopardy-law-in-b2b-shows-the-way-to-grow)). **[INFERENCE]** A common reconciliation is to segment for message and offer relevance while defining the reachable media target as broadly as the category allows; this is the researchers' synthesis, not a sourced rule.

## Reach all category buyers, because about 95% are not in market

**[DURABLE]** The 95-5 rule from the LinkedIn B2B Institute and Professor John Dawes of Ehrenberg-Bass holds that only about 5% of B2B buyers are in market at any time; if 80% of companies switch bank about once every five years, about 20% are in market in a year, 2% in a month and 1% in two weeks. Marketers cannot push buyers into market, so advertising works mainly by building memory for a future purchase. The practical guidance is to target the whole category (current and future buyers) instead of hyper-targeting in-market signals, to aim creative at memorability over hard persuasion, to spread media continuously, and to set realistic short-term sales targets ([Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/peter-weinberg-jon-lombardo-95-5-rule)). **[INFERENCE]** An agent can apply this arithmetically: estimate purchase-cycle length, derive the share of the category in market per month, fund always-on broad reach for future buyers, and aim activation at the in-market slice.
