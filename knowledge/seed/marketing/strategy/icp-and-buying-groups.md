**When to use this:** When targeting B2B accounts, building account lists, or writing personas for B2B campaigns.

## Describe accounts with an ICP and people with personas mapped to buying jobs

**[DURABLE; Gartner, 10 July 2019]** An ideal customer profile (ICP) is the set of firmographic, environmental and behavioural attributes of accounts expected to become a company's most valuable customers, developed through qualitative and quantitative analysis and optionally predictive analytics. It is narrower than the total addressable market and narrower than "target customer": it covers only the most valuable customers and prospects that are also most likely to buy. Gartner reports that in some firms the ICP is poorly defined or used only in briefs and onboarding instead of in strategy and execution ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/the-framework-for-ideal-customer-profile-development)). **[VENDOR]** Salesforce distinguishes the ICP (the type of company that is an ideal fit) from the buyer persona (a semi-fictional representation of an ideal individual customer based on research and data) and lists environmental attributes such as industry trends, geography, economic conditions and regulation ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/sales/ideal-customer-profile/)); N.Rich names "ICP based on gut feeling" as the most common mistake and derives the profile from analysis of a defined set of best accounts on firmographic, environmental and technographic parameters ([N.Rich](https://nrich.io/blog/how-to-create-an-ideal-customer-profile-that-drives-pipeline)). **[INFERENCE]** Minimum ICP fields implied by these sources are firmographics (industry, size, geography), environmental attributes (regulation, economic conditions, technology stack), behavioural attributes (buying triggers, usage, engagement) and a value criterion (retention, LTV, win rate) used to pick the seed accounts.

**[DURABLE in direction; figures dated]** Complex B2B purchases involve many people. Gartner's long-standing figure is 6 to 10 decision makers, each holding 4 or 5 independently gathered pieces of information; buyers spend 17% of purchase time meeting potential suppliers (5 to 6% with any one supplier when comparing several), and 77% called their latest purchase very complex or difficult ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/b2b-buying-journey)). The 17% figure traces to Gartner's 2017 Digital B2B Buyer Survey (n = 750): 27% of time researching independently online, 18% researching offline, 22% meeting with the buying group, 17% meeting suppliers and 16% other ([Gartner e-book via SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/gartner-buyer-enablement-winmoreb2bsalesdealspdf/258207352)). Gartner's six "buying jobs" are problem identification, solution exploration, requirements building, supplier selection, validation and consensus creation; buyers who found supplier information helpful were 2.8 times more likely to report high purchase ease and 3 times more likely to buy a bigger deal with less regret ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/b2b-buying-journey)). **[FAST-MOVING, Jan 2026]** Forrester's State of Business Buying 2026 (nearly 18,000 global business buyers surveyed in 2025) puts the average at **13 internal stakeholders and 9 external participants** per decision; 94% of buyers use AI during the buying process, procurement is a decision-maker 53% of the time, and over 60% have bought some form of trial. The same post is summarised as saying purchases with generative-AI features involve buying groups twice as large (14 versus 7 members), which sits oddly beside the 13-person average and should be checked against the report before quoting ([Forrester, 21 Jan 2026](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/state-of-business-buying-2026)). **[INFERENCE]** B2B persona work should map to buying-group roles and the six buying jobs, not to a single "decision maker".
