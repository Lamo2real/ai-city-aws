**When to use this:** Before any creative or campaign work starts, and when a manager agent approves a worker's brief.

## Write briefs with one objective, a real insight and agreed direction

**[DURABLE; survey, Oct 2021]** The BetterBriefs Project with Flood + Partners, supported by the IPA, surveyed over 1,700 marketers and agency staff in more than 70 countries. **80% of marketers think they write good briefs, and 10% of creative agencies agree.** On clear strategic direction the split is 78% of marketers against 5% of agencies; on clear, concise language 83% against 7%. Respondents estimate that 33% of marketing budget is wasted through poor briefs and misdirected work; 69% of marketers and 73% of agencies say rebriefs happen too often; 90% and 92% call the brief valuable yet neglected; and 89% and 86% say good creative is hard without a good brief ([IPA](https://ipa.co.uk/news/betterbriefs)). **[CONTESTED]** Marketing Week's report of the same study says "only 1 in 5 agencies" agree briefs are good, which conflicts with the IPA's 10% for creative agencies; prefer the IPA figure. Practitioner guidance in that article: pick one focused goal and do not use the brief as a shopping list; show where the brand and the business want to go; structure the brief as intent, insight, inspiration; agree the brief before anyone starts creating; and brief in person with time for questions instead of by email ([Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/clarity-direction-marketing-briefs/)). **[INFERENCE]** Minimum brief fields supported by these sources: business and marketing objective (one primary, measurable), the target and the insight about them, the positioning or single-minded proposition, strategic direction, mandatory brand assets, budget, timing, success measures, stakeholders and sign-off. For agent teams the "agree before creating" rule translates into the manager agent approving the brief before a worker agent generates assets.
