**When to use this:** When setting objectives and KPIs for a plan, campaign or brief.

## Objectives and KPIs: SMART for one objective, OKRs for alignment, a north star with inputs

**[DURABLE]** SMART originates in George T. Doran's November 1981 Management Review article; in Doran's version the A was "assignable", and later variants substitute attainable, relevant and so on. Per John Doerr's What Matters site, SMART shapes a single objective and is a guideline, whereas OKRs tie measurable key results to the objective so that hitting the key results means the objective is met ([What Matters](https://www.whatmatters.com/resources/okrs-smart-goals-difference-between)). OKRs were introduced by Andy Grove at Intel and taken to Google by John Doerr, and they are meant to be aspirational ([15Five, secondary](https://www.15five.com/blog/okrs-vs-smart-goals-which-model-is-right-for-your-employees)). **[VENDOR framework]** Amplitude's North Star Framework uses one North Star Metric (a single rate, count or ratio representing product strategy and customer value) plus three to five complementary input metrics that together produce it; its templates are "Our North Star Metric is called X, which we define as Y" and "I believe [the metric] is a function of [X, Y and Z]". John Cutler's test is "If you can move your North Star directly, it's probably not a good North Star"; the grocery-delivery example is "total monthly items received on time" with inputs such as orders placed, orders fulfilled and on-time delivery ([Amplitude playbook](https://info.amplitude.com/rs/138-CDN-550/images/Amplitude-The-North-Star-Playbook.pdf); [Amplitude](https://amplitude.com/books/north-star/defining-your-North-Star); [Amplitude blog](https://amplitude.com/blog/product-north-star-metric)). **[DURABLE]** AARRR (Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue) comes from Dave McClure's "Startup Metrics for Pirates" (video dated 25 August 2007), which assigns each stage a conversion step ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=irjgfW0BIrw); [SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/startup-metrics-for-pirates-long-version/89026)); RARRA reorders the stages to put retention first ([Koji, secondary](https://www.koji.so/docs/aarrr-pirate-metrics-framework)). Growth loops (Reforge, 31 July 2018) are closed systems where inputs, through a process, generate output that can be reinvested as input; funnels run one way and silo functions, while loops change how teams are organised and goaled ([Reforge](https://www.reforge.com/blog/growth-loops)). **[DURABLE; Ritson]** For an annual marketing plan, Ritson says "you need four or five objectives" and that beyond that objectives become "dreams that never happen"; he does not cite the research behind the number ([Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/ritson-ten-traits-successful-marketer/)). **[INFERENCE]** A sensible stack for a marketing team is OKR objective, then north star or primary KPI, then three to five input KPIs, then per-channel diagnostics such as AARRR stage rates; a KPI tree is the same construct as the north-star-plus-inputs model, with the unit-economics formulas in section 2 supplying the financial branch. A single campaign brief should carry fewer objectives than the annual plan.
