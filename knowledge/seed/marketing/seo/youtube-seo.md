**When to use this:** When optimising organic YouTube videos, titles, thumbnails or chapters.

## YouTube SEO: relevance, engagement and quality, with watch time deciding tests

**[DURABLE; YouTube Help, fetched 11 Nov 2026]** YouTube says search "prioritizes three key elements": relevance, engagement and quality. Relevance considers how well the title, tags, description and video content match the query; engagement signals such as watch time of a video for a particular query are used to judge relevance; and quality signals identify channels showing expertise, authoritativeness and trustworthiness on a topic. Results can be personalised by search and watch history, and YouTube takes no payment for organic placement ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16090438)). Recommendations use viewing behaviour, likes, dislikes, subscriptions and feedback including satisfaction surveys, and YouTube elevates high-quality sources for news, personal finance and medical or scientific topics ([How YouTube Works](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/product-features/recommendations/)). The native A/B test lets creators test up to 3 titles, thumbnails or combinations, and **the winner is the variant with the highest watch time share, not CTR**; tests can take up to two weeks, are unavailable for Shorts, and stop if the title or thumbnail is edited mid-test; thumbnails under 1280 x 720 cause all variants to be downscaled ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714?hl=en)). Manual chapters need a first timestamp of 00:00, at least three timestamps in ascending order and chapters of at least 10 seconds ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579)). **[INFERENCE]** Because the native test optimises for watch time share, a high-CTR thumbnail that attracts the wrong viewers can lose; packaging should promise what the video delivers. YouTube mentions are also the strongest measured correlate of AI brand visibility (section 9).
