**When to use this:** When rankings move, when a Google update is announced, or before producing content at scale with AI.

## Update cadence and how to respond: a core update is rolling out now

**[FAST-MOVING, status 11 Nov 2026]** Google's Search Status Dashboard lists eight ranking incidents in 2026: the February Discover update (5 February, about 21 days); the March spam update (24 March, 19.5 hours); the March core update (27 March, about 12 days); the May core update (21 May, about 12 days); the June spam update (24 June, about 2 days); the August spam update (18 August, about 2.7 days); the September spam update (24 September, 13 days 16 hours, the longest spam rollout of the year, finishing about 8 October with a second phase reported on 30 September); and the **November 2026 core update, which started on 10 November 2026 and was still in progress on 11 November** ([Google Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history); [Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/november-2026-google-webmaster-report-42431.html)). That makes three core updates and four spam updates in 2026 to date, after three core updates in 2025 ([PPC Land, 27 Mar 2026](https://ppc.land/googles-march-2026-core-update-is-here-and-it-follows-spam-by-just-3-days/); [TechWyse, 25 Jun 2026](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/google-june-2026-spam-update)). No core update occurred in October 2026; unconfirmed volatility was reported on 16, 20 to 21, 24 and 27 October ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/category/google-updates)). **[INFERENCE]** Treat ranking data from 10 November 2026 onward as unstable until the dashboard shows the update complete; the two earlier 2026 core updates each took about 12 days. **[DURABLE]** Core updates do not target specific sites; substantial recovery typically needs another core update; avoid changing content that still performs after small drops; deletion is a last resort; wait at least a full week after rollout completes before comparing Search Console data ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/googles-march-2026-core-update-is-here-and-it-follows-spam-by-just-3-days/)). Spam-update recovery can take many months ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-september-2026-spam-update-42163.html)). Volatility is large: SE Ranking found nearly 15% of top-10 pages before the December 2025 core update had left the top 100 by 5 January 2026 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/googles-march-2026-core-update-is-here-and-it-follows-spam-by-just-3-days/)).

## Spam policies now name AI-answer manipulation

**[DURABLE list; FAST-MOVING addition, 15 May 2026]** Google's spam policies cover scaled content abuse (many pages generated primarily to manipulate rankings, whatever the method), site reputation abuse ("parasite SEO"), expired domain abuse, link spam, thin affiliation, doorway pages, scraping and keyword stuffing, and since 15 May 2026 also "attempting to manipulate generative AI responses in Google Search" ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies); [TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/google-june-2026-spam-update)). Creating pages for every query variation or fan-out query violates the scaled content abuse policy ([Google AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)). AI-generated content is permitted when it meets Search Essentials, but Google says it is "critical to manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content", including titles, meta descriptions, structured data and alt text. In Merchant Center, AI-generated images must carry IPTC TrainedAlgorithmicMedia metadata and AI-generated titles and descriptions must be labelled ([Google, Using generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)).
