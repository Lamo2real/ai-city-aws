**When to use this:** Before publishing influencer, endorsement or review content aimed at UK or US audiences.

## UK: "#Ad" at the start; "Sponsored" and "Gifted" are not enough

**[FAST-MOVING; law-firm summary dated 23 Oct 2026]** Revised ASA, CAP and CMA influencer guidance is grounded in the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, under which the CMA can impose fines directly. Acceptable labels are #Ad, #Advert, #Advertising, #Advertisement and #AdvertisementFeature; "Sponsored", "Spon" and "Sp" are insufficient and the firm advises avoiding "Gifted"; platform tools are not always enough; gifts, trips and commission all count as payment; brands, agencies and creators are each responsible; and AI-generated content is subject to the same rules ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/10/23/new-influencer-guidance-from-the-asa-cap-and-cma-what-has-changed-and-what-to-d-102o6ar)).

## US: endorsements, reviews, California and New York

**[DURABLE]** The FTC requires clear disclosure of any material connection (financial, employment, personal or family, including free or discounted products) placed with the endorsement, not in a bio, after "more" or buried in hashtags; in video it must be said and shown in the video itself and repeated during live streams; "#ad" or "Sponsored by" work, while "#partner", "#ambassador", "sp", "spon", "collab" or "Gifted" alone do not; and platform tools alone are insufficient ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking); [FTC, Disclosures 101](https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers/)). Note the cross-market difference: the FTC accepts "sponsored", the UK does not, and Sweden wants "reklam" or "betalt samarbete". The FTC reviews rule, effective 21 October 2024, bans fake reviews and testimonials, incentives conditioned on sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews and buying fake social influence indicators; AI avatars cannot convey fake testimonials ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)). **[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** California regulations effective 1 January 2026 require businesses to show whether an opt-out preference signal was processed ([BakerHostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/california-privacy-in-2026-regulations-enforcement-ai-and-more/)). **[FAST-MOVING, 2026; trade summary, verify against primary texts]** New York's synthetic-performer disclosure law has been in force since 9 June 2026 ($1,000 for a first violation, $5,000 for subsequent ones), and California's AI Transparency Act has been in force since 2 August 2026 and binds large AI providers, not advertisers ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-ad-labels-cut-click-through-31-5-iab-framework-cites-nyu-study/)). US email and SMS rules are in section 15.
