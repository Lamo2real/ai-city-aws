**When to use this:** Before publishing any environmental or sustainability claim.

## Green claims: generic environmental claims have been restricted since 27 September 2026

**[FAST-MOVING; law-firm summary, Aug 2026]** Directive (EU) 2024/825 has applied in full since 27 September 2026. It restricts sustainability labels that are not based on a certification scheme or set up by a public authority, generic environmental claims such as "green", "environmentally friendly" or "sustainable" without recognised excellent environmental performance, and claims about a whole product when only one aspect is concerned ([Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/08/european-union-final-call-the-eu-empco-directive)). **[FAST-MOVING, Sweden]** Swedish amendments to the Marketing Act were proposed in SOU 2025:124 and are reported as expected to enter into force on 1 January 2027; Konsumentverket's position is that the directive is fully harmonising and that ongoing marketing falls under the new rules; and business groups report no clear transition rule for products already on shelves ([Konsumentverket remissvar, 24 Mar 2026](https://regeringen.se/contentassets/13ef70e74acc4c0d8f4f064aae104d52/konsumentverket.pdf); [Företagarna, Jul 2026](https://www.foretagarna.se/nyheter/riks/2026/juli/otydliga-regler-kan-leda-till-kasserade-produkter/)). Until then environmental claims fall under section 10 of the Marketing Act. **[INFERENCE]** Plan as if the directive's bans already apply: an agent should block generic claims such as "miljövänlig" and self-made sustainability labels and route any environmental claim to substantiation review.
