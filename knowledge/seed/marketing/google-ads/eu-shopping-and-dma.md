## Consent Mode and the Digital Markets Act make consent a performance input in the EEA

**[FAST-MOVING, 23 July 2026; secondary reading of the decision]** The European Commission adopted a non-compliance decision against Alphabet under Article 6(5) of the Digital Markets Act (Case DMA.100193) with a **EUR 460 million fine** for self-preferencing in Search across nine services, including product ads and free product units, flights, trains, lodging, places, Google Maps, translation, sports information and games; a separate EUR 430 million Google Play steering fine brings the total to EUR 890 million. Alphabet must end the non-compliance within 60 days of notification (about 21 September 2026 if counted from adoption), either by giving similar third-party services equal prominence or by removing the extra prominence of its own units, with periodic penalties of up to 5% of average daily worldwide turnover possible. The decision records that in the EU product ads unit only comparison shopping services (CSS) can bid, so merchants must supply inventory through a CSS, of which Google Shopping is one. Google was reported to be weighing an appeal, which does not suspend the obligation; AI Overviews and AI Mode are outside this decision ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-faces-cease-and-desist-orders-on-nine-services-in-eu-search-decision/)). **[INFERENCE]** EU Shopping advertisers should expect layout and auction changes in the product unit during late 2026 and 2027 and keep CSS arrangements under review.
