### Sustainability reporting: outside scope for a typical SaaS company

**[FAST-MOVING, 2025-2026; checked on primary source]** Under Årsredovisningslagen 6 kap. 10 § an aktiebolag must include a sustainability report if it exceeds more than one of the following in each of the two latest years: more than 250 employees, a balance sheet total above SEK 280 million, net turnover above SEK 550 million (with lower thresholds of 10 employees, SEK 5 million and SEK 10 million for companies with securities on a regulated market); a subsidiary can be exempt if covered by a group report. By Lag (2025:1297), other large companies start with the first financial year beginning after the end of 2026, and listed small and medium companies with the first year beginning after the end of 2027 ([Årsredovisningslag (1995:1554), riksdagen.se, consolidated through SFS 2026:780](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/arsredovisningslag-19951554_sfs-1995-1554/)).

### The EU Omnibus narrows CSRD

**[FAST-MOVING, Feb 2025 to July 2026; checked on primary source]** At EU level the February 2025 Omnibus proposed limiting CSRD to companies with more than 1,000 employees; an April 2025 directive postponed waves two and three; a July 2025 "quick fix" froze wave-one requirements for 2025 to 2026; Parliament and Council reached political agreement on Omnibus I on 9 December 2025; on 3 July 2026 the Commission adopted a delegated act with voluntary standards for companies protected by the value-chain cap; and a July 2025 Recommendation sets a voluntary SME standard (VSME) ([European Commission, corporate sustainability reporting](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/capital-markets-union-and-financial-markets/company-reporting-and-auditing/company-reporting/corporate-sustainability-reporting_en)). **[INFERENCE]** A typical growing SaaS company is outside mandatory sustainability reporting; its practical exposure is customer questionnaires, for which the VSME and value-chain-cap standards are the reference. **[GAP]** Formal adoption, final thresholds and the transposition deadline of the Omnibus directive were not confirmed.

### UK VAT on digital services to consumers

**[DURABLE; checked on primary source]** Digital services supplied to UK consumers are liable to UK VAT, and a business outside the UK making such supplies needs to register for UK VAT. Where the customer gives a valid VAT number the supply is treated as business-to-business and the customer accounts for the VAT. Location is where the consumer usually lives, evidenced by billing address, IP address, bank details or similar ([GOV.UK, VAT rules for digital services to private consumers](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-vat-rules-if-you-supply-digital-services-to-private-consumers)). **[INFERENCE]** The text implies no small-seller threshold for overseas suppliers, meaning registration from the first sale, though the page did not say so in those words. **[GAP]** The UK rate and the exact threshold position were not on the page read.

### DAC7, Pillar Two, US sales tax and other non-EU regimes

**[GAP; unverified background]** None of these was researched. The unverified recollection is: DAC7 obliges platform operators connecting sellers with buyers to report sellers' income annually, so a SaaS vendor selling its own software is normally not in scope unless it adds a marketplace feature; Pillar Two sets a 15% minimum effective tax for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million, implemented in Sweden by the top-up tax act, and matters to a small SaaS company only as a subsidiary of a large group; US states impose economic nexus for sales tax since a 2018 Supreme Court decision, commonly at USD 100,000 of sales into a state, with SaaS taxability varying by state; and Norway, Switzerland and other non-EU digital-services VAT regimes exist. Each needs a specialist check before the first sale into the jurisdiction.

## 22. Conclusion: what this changes about running finance with agents

**[INFERENCE]** The material turns the house style from a slogan into a design discipline with a boundary. Hormozi's models tell the department what to ask of every offer: when the cash arrives, whether it covers the cost of winning and serving the customer, and which constraint the next krona should go to. Swedish accounting rules then decide how the answer is recorded, and the benchmarks decide whether the answer is good for a SaaS company of this size and funding model. The most useful single artefact is the two-view unit-economics report: cash payback by day 30, 90 and 365 next to accrual payback, with deferred revenue shown between them. A department that produces it monthly can follow the house style at full strength without ever misstating revenue.

**[INFERENCE]** The second shift is that an AI finance department's reliability rests less on the model than on what surrounds it: a statutory calendar, code-level reconciliation, logs kept as accounting documentation, and a strict split between who configures, who prepares and who approves. The research also shows where the department is still blind. Transfer pricing, permanent establishment, pensions, treasury, classic control design and consumer-credit law were not verified, and several 2027 rules hang on a budget bill presented the day after this document's status date. The honest operating posture is to run the verified parts with confidence, treat every [GAP] as a question owed to a named human or to Research, and re-date the fast-moving figures at each half-year.
