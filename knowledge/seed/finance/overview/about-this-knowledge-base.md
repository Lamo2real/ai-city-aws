# Law rules the books; cash-first designs them

As of 11 November 2026 the foundational knowledge for an AI finance department serving a Swedish SaaS aktiebolag is a three-layer stack: law and accounting rules that always win, independent evidence and SaaS benchmarks that tune targets, and Alex Hormozi's cash-first money models as the default design style underneath both. The legal layer is concrete and mostly verified: book cash events by the next working day, keep records seven years, recognise subscription revenue straight-line over the service period under K3 point 23.26 or K2 point 6.11 however the customer pays, hold the AGM within six months and file within seven or pay SEK 7,500, test equity against half the share capital at every close, charge 25% VAT at home, reverse-charge EU business customers after a VIES check, switch to destination VAT through OSS once EU consumer sales pass EUR 10,000 (SEK 99,680), pay 20.6% corporate tax and 31.42% employer contributions. The evidence layer says the median private B2B SaaS company grew about 25 to 26% in 2024 with gross revenue retention near 88 to 90%, net revenue retention near 101 to 104%, about $2.00 of sales and marketing per $1.00 of new-customer ARR, and a Nordic average CAC payback of 14 months, while AI product gross margins averaged 45% in 2025 and public SaaS traded near 3.2 times trailing revenue in Q2 2026. The house style supplies the design questions those benchmarks do not ask: does a new customer's first 30 days of cash cover the cost of winning and serving them, is lifetime gross profit at least three times CAC, is there an attraction, upsell, downsell and continuity sequence with a prepay option, is price raised before spend, and is effort aimed at the single constraint; every number he attaches is his own unaudited claim. The layers collide in predictable places: prepaid cash is a liability and not revenue, his 30-day payback sits far from typical SaaS payback, a 10% prepay discount is expensive financing at roughly 26% annualised by the researchers' calculation, and consumer payment plans and money-back offers need a legal check that the research did not perform. For agents, the COSO 2026 generative-AI guidance points to a simple division of labour: agents prepare, reconcile, forecast and recommend; named humans approve payments, filings, prices and master-data changes; configuration rights stay separate from approval rights; everything is logged. Large areas remain unverified, including transfer pricing, permanent establishment, pensions, the 13-week cash forecast method, treasury and FX policy, consumer-credit rules and the 2027 budget bill due on 12 November 2026, and this document says so where it applies instead of filling the holes from memory.

## How to read and split this document

This document is written to be cut into atomic knowledge-base entries. Every body paragraph is meant to stand alone: it opens with one or more labels, names its subject in full, and keeps each number next to its date and source link. Section numbers group domains; sub-headings name the single topic of the paragraphs beneath them. Tables repeat their source link in every row so that a row can be split out as its own entry. When splitting, keep the label, the date and the link with the claim.

The labels mean the following. **[DURABLE]** marks a principle, definition, formula or long-standing legal rule that has held for years. **[FAST-MOVING, date]** marks a rate, amount, deadline, survey result or legal status that will age, with the date of the source or the period it covers. **[VENDOR]** marks a figure from a party with a commercial interest in the conclusion (investors, lenders, software vendors, brokers, benchmark publishers that sell to the segment); treat it as self-reported, self-selected and correlational. **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** marks a statement a platform or tool maker makes about its own product. **[CONTESTED]** marks a point where sources disagree, or where house style and evidence pull in different directions. **[INFERENCE]** marks a synthesis, calculation or convention written by the researchers that no single source states; adopt it as a default and tune it against the company's own data. **[GAP]** marks something the research did not verify or could not find. **[HOUSE STYLE]** marks Alex Hormozi's published frameworks, which the owner has chosen as the department's default style where they exist; it is a preference and not independent evidence, and every number attached to it is his own self-reported, unaudited claim.

Three qualifiers are added inside labels where they matter. For legal, tax and regulatory material, "checked on primary source" means the statute, Skatteverket page, Riksbank page, government document, European Commission page or standard-setter page was read during the research; "secondary source only" means an audit firm, FAR Online's reference series, a software vendor, a trade body or the press was read and the primary text was not; "unverified background" means the point comes from the researchers' recollection, was not checked, and is given only with a pointer to where it should be confirmed. For house-style material, "own site" means Acquisition.com was read directly, "transcript" means a third-party auto-generated transcript of Hormozi's own video or podcast was read (his words, with possible transcription errors), and "second-hand summary" means a third party's summary was read and not checked against the original. For finance method, "unverified working knowledge" means ordinary practice recalled by the researchers with no source opened.

Seven caveats apply to the whole document. First, this is orientation as of 11 November 2026 and not legal, tax, accounting or financial advice; a qualified accountant, auditor or tax adviser confirms anything that goes into a filing. Second, every source was read through a summarising fetch tool, so even a point marked "checked on primary source" should be re-read at the cited paragraph before it is relied on. Third, the research ran under search and fetch limits, so coverage is uneven: Swedish company law, VAT invoicing, employer contributions, SaaS benchmarks, Hormozi's models and the COSO guidance are well sourced, while FP&A statistics, cash and treasury method, classic controls detail, pensions, transfer pricing and funding terms other than Almi are thin. Fourth, the Swedish budget bill for 2027 is handed to the Riksdag on 12 November 2026, the day after this document's status date, with the framework vote on 9 December 2026 ([regeringen.se, press release 9 Nov 2026](https://www.regeringen.se/pressmeddelanden/2026/11/presentation-av-budgetpropositionen-for-2027/); [regeringen.se, Finance Minister's slides, 27 Oct 2026](https://www.regeringen.se/globalassets/regeringen/dokument/finansdepartementet/ppt-till-presstraffar/presentationsbilder-fran-presstraff-med-finansministern-den-27-oktober-2026.pdf)); everything marked "proposed for 2027" must be re-checked after those dates. Fifth, SaaS benchmarks are mostly US-weighted and self-reported; the only Nordic dataset with numbers is Monterro's, published by an investor in the segment. Sixth, all Hormozi material is paraphrase from transcripts and summaries; the 2025 book *$100M Money Models* was not read directly. Seventh, where a later research pass corrected an earlier one, only the corrected claim appears here; the corrections that matter most are the 2026 income base amount (SEK 83,400, not SEK 80,600), the income tax return dates (one date per year-end group), the continued existence of växa-stöd as a refund, and the confirmed late-filing fee scale.
