# Fix one constraint, let law govern

As of 11 November 2026 the foundational knowledge for an AI-run Operations department that also owns people operations is a three-layer stack applied in strict order: Swedish and EU law first, independent evidence second, and Alex and Leila Hormozi's published operating models as the default house style wherever the first two layers leave room. The house style supplies the working skeleton: place the company on a ten-level scaling roadmap with one named constraint per level, fix that one constraint before anything else, prefer doing more or better of what already works over anything new, keep the business simple, diagnose non-performance as a communication, training or motivation gap before blaming the person, train by showing, then observing, then releasing, give people written spending and approval limits, run hiring like a customer-acquisition funnel, and hold standards by acting on low performance without delay; every number attached to these models is the Hormozis' own unaudited claim. The evidence layer agrees with most of that skeleton and sharpens it: the constraint logic is the Theory of Constraints in plain language, the goal systems converge on a few quarterly priorities with a weekly scorecard and one named decider, structured interviews top the 2022 validity ranking, reference-class forecasting beats inside-view estimates, and only about 6% of companies in McKinsey's 2026 survey draw significant profit from AI, mostly those that redesigned workflows. The law layer overrides the house style in people matters: fast firing is US at-will practice, while Swedish LAS requires objective reasons, a redeployment check, a two-month window on known facts, written notice in a set form, and negotiation under MBL before redundancies even without a collective agreement. Work-environment duties (safety representative from five employees, written documentation from ten, safety committee from fifty), the annual pay survey, IMY's limits on monitoring and candidate data, GDPR Article 22 and the AI Act's high-risk regime for employment AI (now applying from 2 December 2027) bound what agents may do with people data. Agents therefore prepare, track and draft; a named human decides anything that is irreversible, external, above a spending limit or about a person. The research budget ran out several times, so the document also carries a long, honest gap list, and all legal content is orientation, not professional advice.

## How to read and split this document

This document is written to be cut into atomic knowledge-base entries. Every body paragraph is meant to stand alone: it opens with one or more labels, names its subject in full, and keeps each number next to its date and source link. Section numbers group domains; sub-headings name the single topic of the paragraphs beneath them. Tables repeat their source link in every row so that a row can be split out as its own entry. When splitting, keep the label, the date and the link with the claim.

The labels mean the following. **[HOUSE STYLE]** marks Alex or Leila Hormozi's published frameworks, which the owner has chosen as the department's default style where they exist; it is a preference and not independent evidence, and every number attached to it is their own self-reported, unaudited claim. **[DURABLE]** marks a principle, definition, framework or long-standing legal rule that has held for years. **[FAST-MOVING, date]** marks a figure, survey result, amount or legal status that will age, with the date of the source. **[VENDOR]** marks a figure or recommendation from a party with a commercial interest in the conclusion (tool vendors, consultancies, training firms). **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** marks a company's or tool maker's self-reported statement about its own practice or data. **[CONTESTED]** marks a point on which sources disagree or whose quality is disputed. **[INFERENCE]** marks synthesis by the researchers that no single source states, including every template, checklist, threshold and how-to that the notes built themselves. **[GAP]** marks something that was not verified; it must not be treated as a finding.

Two kinds of qualifier sit inside or next to the labels. For house-style material, "primary transcript" means the point was read on a third-party-hosted, caption-generated transcript of the Hormozis' own video or podcast (wording approximate), "primary site" means acquisition.com itself, and "second-hand summary" means a third party's summary or show notes (Shortform, Podcast Rex summaries, Metacast, Tubegraph, Margin Notes, workshop attendee notes) that was not checked against the original. For legal, tax and regulatory material, "checked on primary source" means the statute, EU text or authority page was read during the research, "secondary source only" means a law firm, trade body, encyclopaedia or vendor page was read and the primary text was not, and "unverified background" means the point comes from the researchers' background knowledge, with a link to where it should be confirmed. Agents must not gate a live decision on an unverified legal point without confirmation.

Seven caveats apply to the whole document. First, all legal, tax and regulatory content is orientation as of 11 November 2026 and not professional advice; a qualified Swedish employment lawyer, tax adviser or data-protection specialist confirms anything that drives a real decision. Second, the Swedish statutes were read through a summarising fetch tool, so section numbers and figures should be spot-checked against the linked text before they go into templates. Third, no Hormozi book, video or audio was consumed directly; all of their material is paraphrase from transcripts hosted by third parties or from summaries, the gated acquisition.com roadmap document itself was never read, and episode numbers and dates on aggregator sites are unreliable, so episodes are cited by title. Fourth, the operations benchmarks are US-weighted and large-company-weighted (Zylo, SHRM, McKinsey), and no Swedish or Nordic operations or recruiting benchmark was found. Fifth, the research ran out of search and page-fetch budget several times, so many standard topics are gaps and are listed as such in section 26 and not filled from memory. Sixth, where the gap-fill research corrected the earlier notes (pay-transparency timing, the VAB application deadline, which regulation replaced which, the status of the scaling roadmap and of the "document, demonstrate, duplicate" label), only the corrected claim appears here. Seventh, the report describes general practice that fits any company and then calls out what is specific to a Sweden-based SaaS company selling in the EU; the Swedish material assumes a private employer and notes where a rule depends on having a collective agreement.

## 1. Overview: three layers, one constraint, agents prepare and humans decide

### The three-layer rule that governs every agent decision

**[INFERENCE]** The Operations department runs on three layers in strict order. Layer one is law: Swedish employment, work-environment, discrimination and data-protection rules, and EU rules such as GDPR and the AI Act (sections 13 to 23). Layer two is independent evidence and recognised standards on processes, planning, projects, service levels, vendors, risk, organisation, AI operations and people practices (sections 4 to 16). Layer three is the house style, the Hormozis' operating models, which fills every space the first two layers leave open (section 2). A worker agent picks the house-style move by default, checks whether evidence changes it for this situation, and checks whether a legal gate blocks it. Section 3 lists the known collisions and says which rule wins in each.

### What the department is for

**[INFERENCE]** The department has two jobs that share one method. The business-operations job is to design processes, run the planning and execution rhythm, manage projects, vendors, risk and continuity, and keep systems of record clean. The people-operations job, which sits here because the company has no separate HR department, is to run hiring, onboarding, performance, pay and offboarding lawfully. The shared method is: name the one constraint, write the process down as a checklist with one owner, measure a baseline, improve the constraint step only, and record decisions with one named decider.

### The department's shape: one manager agent directing worker agents

**[INFERENCE]** The manager agent and its worker agents follow the orchestrator-workers pattern that Anthropic's engineering guidance describes, in which a central model decomposes work, delegates it and synthesises results ([Anthropic, Building effective agents, 19 Dec 2024](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)). In decision-rights terms the manager agent is the natural driver or recommender, and a named human is the approver or decider for anything irreversible, external, above a spending limit or about a person. That mapping is the researchers' synthesis and not something a source states about agent departments.

### What the house style and the standards have in common

**[INFERENCE]** The Hormozi constraint method is the Theory of Constraints in plain language: naming one constraint is the identify step, "more" is exploit, "better" is exploit and subordinate, and "new" is elevate and comes last ([TOC Institute, Five Focusing Steps](https://www.tocinstitute.org/five-focusing-steps.html); [second-hand summary of "More, Better, New", 15 Jul 2024](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/24931865-more-better-new)). The house style and the process standards in section 4 therefore do not conflict, and an agent can use either vocabulary for the same loop.

### Where the house style stops

**[INFERENCE]** The house style governs strategy, focus, management diagnosis, training method, delegation and the tone of feedback. It does not govern anything where Swedish or EU law prescribes a process: ending an employment, probation decisions, redundancy, monitoring, candidate data, pay equity and the use of AI in decisions about people. In those areas the house style contributes only its transferable parts, which are early, direct, behaviour-specific feedback, written expectations and not delaying the start of a proper process.

### Which roadmap levels matter for a small Swedish SaaS company

**[INFERENCE]** For a Sweden-based SaaS company of under 50 people, the relevant levels of Leila Hormozi's ten-level model are 4 to 6. Read together they give an ordered operations agenda: one customer type and one core offer with tracked metrics (level 4), delivery standards and retention (level 5), then formal onboarding, clean data, financial visibility and role clarity (level 6). Her model places formal HR systems, budgets and a director layer at 50 to 99 people (level 7), which coincides with Swedish thresholds at 50 employees for a safety committee and an internal whistleblowing channel ([Podcast Rex transcript of "Scale Your Business Like a Pro in 2026", 9 Apr 2026](https://podcastrex.com/shows/build-with-leila-hormozi/scale-your-business-like-a-pro-in-2026-ep-350/transcript); [Arbetsmiljölagen 6 kap. 8 §](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/arbetsmiljolag-19771160_sfs-1977-1160/); [Visselblåsarlagen 5 kap. 2 §](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2021890-om-skydd-for-personer-som-rapporterar_sfs-2021-890/)).

**[INFERENCE]** An AI-run Operations department reports process-level outcomes (cycle time, first-pass yield, cost per unit of work, error escapes) and not usage counts. ### Identify the constraint and fix one thing at a time

**[HOUSE STYLE]** Alex Hormozi's premise is that each business has one constraint limiting growth and that long hours without growth usually mean effort is going to the wrong thing. His diagnostic question is why the business cannot be ten times larger. His example is a media company with more than 40 million subscribers that believed it needed better production when the real limit was having no product to sell; improving the weak area was said to be worth roughly ten times more than polishing the strong one. The source is a timestamped transcript index whose source video and date were not identified, so this is second-hand and the figures are unaudited ([Tubegraph index of Alex Hormozi videos](https://tubegraph.vercel.app/alexhormozi/identifying_and_addressing_constraints_in_business_growth)).

**[HOUSE STYLE]** The same second-hand source adds diagnostic warnings: a lead-generation problem can really be a product or customer-experience problem, product quality is an often-missed constraint, a mediocre employee can absorb about 25% of an owner's bandwidth, and confronting an underperformer leads to improvement about one time in five and a clean exit otherwise. All figures are unaudited claims ([Tubegraph index](https://tubegraph.vercel.app/alexhormozi/identifying_and_addressing_constraints_in_business_growth)). The last point collides with Swedish law as described in section 3.

**[HOUSE STYLE]** A channel clip description says that to find the constraint one asks why the business cannot do more and works through four areas: market, metrics, model and money. The detail of that walk-through was not available, so only the four headings are sourced, second-hand ([Recapio digest of "You Need to Find Your Constraint"](https://recapio.com/digest/you-need-to-find-your-constraint-by-hormozi-highlights)).

**[HOUSE STYLE]** The "one thing" question, from Alex Hormozi's video "Less is More" dated 22 May 2025: name the single outcome that, if true in 12 months, would change everything for the business. His examples were an owned lead channel, a steady supply of technicians, and cutting cash collection from 90 days to 30 ([Rosetta transcript, primary transcript, 22 May 2025](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/less-is-more-the-magic-of-a-simple-business)).

**[HOUSE STYLE]** When two paths are possible, Alex credits Leila with the framing of comparing the problems that come with each path and choosing the set of problems the team is better equipped to solve. On the coaching call where this appears, the bottleneck named was the lack of a proven year-round acquisition channel ([Audioscrape excerpt of "Fix These Bottlenecks to Scale Past $1M", recorded 22 May 2025](https://audioscrape.com/podcast/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/episode/fix-these-bottlenecks-to-scale-past-1m--ep-891)).

### "More, better, new"

**[HOUSE STYLE]** The order of preference for growth work is more, then better, then new. More means doing more of what already works and is described as the fastest and lowest-effort option. Better means improving the efficiency or effectiveness of existing systems when no obvious "more" exists. New means new products or systems and comes only after the first two are exhausted, because new is slow and costly to start. The source is twice removed (a blogger summarising another writer who credits Hormozi), dated 15 July 2024 ([Goodreads blog post "More, Better, New"](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/24931865-more-better-new)).

**[HOUSE STYLE]** The reason given at the scaling workshop for preferring more or better is that any change costs performance, about 20% (the note-taker says arguably 40%), so a new initiative should clear at least that hurdle before it is worth starting. This is an unaudited rule of thumb from attendee notes ([Dickie Bush, 12 takeaways](https://dickiebush.substack.com/p/12-takeaways-from-alex-hormozis-scaling)).

### Simplicity and focus

**[HOUSE STYLE]** Alex Hormozi argues that simplicity takes more work than complexity, and he attributes complexity to a failure to prioritise. His test is how much can be removed while the result stays good. He prefers one large bet to many small ones, and gives the example of cutting a community's weekly calls from three to one because members were cancelling out of overwhelm ([Rosetta transcript of "Less is More", 22 May 2025](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/less-is-more-the-magic-of-a-simple-business)). His stated preference in an earlier video is "simple scales, fancy fails" ([Rosetta transcript of the What-Why-How video, 5 Jul 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/how-to-scale-yourself-the-what-why-how-framework)).

**[HOUSE STYLE]** Projects in the house style use few people, regular check-ins and a limited time box of about 12 weeks ([Rosetta transcript of "Less is More", 22 May 2025](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/less-is-more-the-magic-of-a-simple-business)). In the same video he remarks that staff typically sit at 70 to 80% utilisation, which leaves roughly 20 to 30% of capacity for one major bet; this is his unaudited observation, not a measured benchmark.

**[HOUSE STYLE]** Workshop notes report that he defines intelligence as speed of learning and links company size to iteration speed supported by fast, constant feedback, and that he advises consolidating the most important metrics into one dashboard judged by daily use and by whether people can connect their work to the numbers ([Dickie Bush, 12 takeaways, second-hand](https://dickiebush.substack.com/p/12-takeaways-from-alex-hormozis-scaling)). The same notes report advice to track customer lifetime value and acquisition cost constantly, tie every project to one of them, and treat a ratio of about 8 to 1 or higher as the level for aggressively scaling a service business; unaudited.

### Management diagnosis: what, how, why

**[HOUSE STYLE]** When someone does not do what is wanted, Alex Hormozi gives three possible causes: the person did not know it was expected (communication), did not know how (training), or did not want to (motivation). He credits the training and motivation pair to Andy Grove's book High Output Management and says he added communication. The sources are his videos dated 5 July 2021 and 5 July 2022 ([Rosetta transcript, "The SIMPLE Managerial Framework", 5 Jul 2022](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/the-simple-managerial-framework-that-changed-my-business); [Rosetta transcript, "What-Why-How Framework", 5 Jul 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/how-to-scale-yourself-the-what-why-how-framework)).

### The house-style operating loop for this department

**[INFERENCE]** The house-style operating loop for the Operations department has five steps. First, each quarter name the one constraint and the one outcome that would change everything. Second, classify every proposed project as more, better or new and require a higher hurdle (about 20% expected gain, per the unaudited workshop note) for anything new. Third, time-box work to about 12 weeks with few people. Fourth, review weekly the few numbers that define the constraint. Fifth, re-diagnose when the constraint moves. This loop is the researchers' synthesis of the sources in this section.

**[INFERENCE]** The what, how, why diagnosis applies before blaming a person or an agent: check the written expectation, then training and tools, then incentives and purpose. Paired with the training sequence, every standard operating procedure is the "document" step, a recorded walkthrough is the "demonstrate" step, and supervised runs with a sign-off count are the "duplicate" step. Applied to agents, the three causes become an unclear instruction (prompt or brief), a missing capability (tools, context, examples) or a misaligned incentive (evaluation criteria that reward the wrong thing). The agent adaptation is the researchers' own; the Hormozis have not published anything about managing AI agents.

## 3. Precedence: law first, evidence second, house style third

### The precedence rule

**[INFERENCE]** When sources collide, law wins over evidence and evidence wins over house style. The house style is the default only where neither of the other two layers speaks. An agent that finds a house-style instruction pointing one way and a legal rule pointing another follows the legal rule, records the collision in the decision record and escalates to a named human if the matter concerns a person's employment, pay, data or health. This precedence rule is the owner's instruction, expressed here as the researchers' synthesis.

### Why the firing advice does not transfer to Sweden

**[INFERENCE] [DURABLE]** The Hormozis' fast-removal advice reflects US at-will employment, where an employer can usually end employment without stating a reason. Swedish law works differently. Under LAS an employer needs objective reasons (sakliga skäl) for dismissal, which are either shortage of work or reasons tied to the individual, and objective reasons do not exist if the employer can reasonably offer the employee other work ([LAS 7 §, checked on primary source](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-198280-om-anstallningsskydd_sfs-1982-80/)). Under MBL an employer with no collective agreement at all must still negotiate with every affected union before redundancy dismissals ([MBL 13 §, checked on primary source](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/)). The transferable parts of the house style are early, direct, behaviour-specific feedback, written expectations, and not delaying the decision to start a proper process. This is orientation, not legal advice.

## 4. Process management: map, standardise, measure, improve the constraint

### Map at high level first with SIPOC

**[DURABLE]** SIPOC captures Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs and Customers; a variant adds Constraints and Measures. ASQ recommends it before drawing a flowchart, when first investigating a process and when communicating process boundaries. The procedure is to draw the blocks, name the process and fix its start and end points, fill each block while keeping the Process block to five to seven core activities at a very high level, and review the result with stakeholders and sponsors ([ASQ, SIPOC](https://asq.org/quality-resources/sipoc)).

### Standardized work is the baseline for improvement

**[DURABLE]** Standardized work sets precise procedures for each operator and rests on three elements: takt time (the pace needed to meet demand), work sequence and standard in-process inventory. The Lean Enterprise Institute lists the supporting documents: a process capacity sheet that finds bottlenecks, a standardized work combination table, a standardized work chart, a work standards sheet and a job instruction sheet whose job steps are used to train new operators. The benefits it names are a documented current process for every shift, lower variability, easier training, fewer injuries and a baseline for improvement ([LEI lexicon, Standardized Work](https://www.lean.org/lexicon-terms/standardized-work/)).

### Checklists work when they are really adopted

**[DURABLE]** The strongest checklist evidence is the 2009 WHO surgical safety trial (Haynes and colleagues, New England Journal of Medicine, published online 14 January 2009): eight hospitals in eight cities, 7,688 patients (3,733 before and 3,955 after), study period October 2007 to September 2008. Major complications fell from 11% to 7% and inpatient deaths after major operations fell from 1.5% to 0.8%. The checklist was a single page taking a few minutes, used at three pause points: before anaesthesia, before incision and before the patient leaves the operating room ([Harvard Gazette, 14 Jan 2009](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2009/01/surgical-safety-checklist-drops-deaths-and-complications-by-more-than-one-third/)).

**[CONTESTED] [GAP]** The 2009 checklist result came from a before-and-after study in eight hospitals with voluntary adoption, so it is the best-case result. A 2014 study of mandated adoption in Ontario is widely cited as finding no significant improvement, but the page failed to load and its figures were not confirmed. Treat "checklists work" as conditional on real adoption and not on the existence of a document.

**[INFERENCE]** Checklist design drawn from the WHO example: one page, minutes to run, tied to a small number of natural pause points, covering only the steps that are both critical and commonly skipped. A checklist that takes longer than the pause it sits in will be bypassed.

### SOP template for agent or human use

**[INFERENCE]** A standard operating procedure for this department has these parts: title and process ID; owner (one named role); purpose and trigger; scope with start point and end point taken from the SIPOC; inputs and required access; numbered steps written as a checklist with a "done" criterion per step; decision points with the rule or the escalation target; pause points (before an irreversible action, before sending anything external, before closing); output and where it is recorded; metrics (cycle time, first-pass yield); known exceptions; last reviewed date and review interval; change log. The template is the researchers' synthesis, not a sourced standard.

**[INFERENCE]** Process ownership rule: one owner per process, the owner approves changes to the procedure, and every KPI on a scorecard has one named owner. This is consistent with the single-owner guidance in the EOS and RAPID sources in section 5.

### PDCA and kaizen: improve in small cycles against a standard

**[DURABLE]** PDCA is an improvement cycle modelled on the scientific method: propose a change, carry it out, measure the result and decide the next step. Plan sets the goal for a process and works out the changes needed; Do carries them out; Check compares results with targets; Act standardises and stabilises the change if it worked and otherwise runs the cycle again. The Lean Enterprise Institute traces it to Walter Shewhart's three-step cycle and W. Edwards Deming's four-step version taught to Japanese executives in the 1950s, recast as PDCA by the Japanese Union of Scientists and Engineers in 1951; PDSA swaps "check" for "study". A gain is locked in by writing the new method into the work instructions ([LEI lexicon, PDCA](https://www.lean.org/lexicon-terms/pdca/)).

**[DURABLE]** Kaizen is continuous improvement through small, incremental changes that add up over time, involving everyone, with employees finding problems and applying practical fixes quickly. It runs on PDCA cycles. Kaizen events are typically five-day team efforts to make one significant process improvement, and the Lean Enterprise Institute cautions against relying on events alone, favouring daily kaizen that builds front-line problem-solving skill. Its rule, credited to Taiichi Ohno, is that improvement needs a standard first: stabilise, set standardized work as the baseline, improve against it, then update the standard ([LEI lexicon, Kaizen](https://www.lean.org/lexicon-terms/kaizen/)).

**[INFERENCE]** PDCA, kaizen and standardized work form one loop: standard, then PDCA experiment, then new standard. It is the same loop as the house-style practice of documenting, training and checking in section 2, which lets the house style and the lean vocabulary share one procedure lifecycle.

### DMAIC for larger improvement projects

**[DURABLE]** DMAIC has five phases. Define covers the problem, goals, customers, charter and team. Measure documents the actual process, validates measurement and sets a baseline, using tools such as a process map, capability analysis and Pareto chart. Analyze finds the critical inputs causing variation and defects. Improve evaluates and implements solutions, for example through designed experiments or a kaizen event. Control uses mistake-proofing, long-term measurement, reaction plans and standard operating procedures ([ASQ, DMAIC](https://asq.org/quality-resources/dmaic)).

**[DURABLE]** DMAIC targets incremental improvement of an existing process; for a new product or service or a total redesign ASQ points to DMADV. It can run as a part-time team project, often over months, or as a roughly one-week kaizen event in which Define and Measure are prepared beforehand and changes are piloted during the event ([ASQ, DMAIC](https://asq.org/quality-resources/dmaic)).

### Theory of Constraints: the five focusing steps

**[DURABLE]** The five focusing steps are: identify the constraint; exploit it, meaning get maximum output from it with existing resources before buying more; subordinate everything else, so that non-constraints do not outproduce the constraint and the constraint is never starved or fed poor-quality inputs, with a buffer kept in front of it; elevate, meaning add capacity only after the second and third steps; and do not let inertia become the constraint, because the constraint moves and the cycle returns to step one. The same source warns that investing in capacity too early adds unnecessary risk ([TOC Institute, Five Focusing Steps](https://www.tocinstitute.org/five-focusing-steps.html)).

**[INFERENCE]** Order of work for any process: SIPOC (about one hour), then a swimlane of the current state, then a baseline of three numbers (cycle time, throughput per week, first-pass yield), then identify the constraint, then exploit and subordinate before spending, and only then automate or add capacity.

### What to automate, and with what

**[VENDOR]** Anthropic's engineering guidance (19 December 2024; the page itself notes tooling has changed since) distinguishes workflows, in which models and tools are orchestrated through predefined code paths, from agents, in which models direct their own process and tool use. It advises looking for the simplest solution and adding complexity only when needed: workflows suit well-defined tasks, agents suit open-ended problems where steps cannot be predicted and the model's decisions can be trusted, and agents cost more and can compound errors ([Anthropic, Building effective agents, 19 Dec 2024](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)).

**[CONTESTED] [FAST-MOVING, 2025]** The MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide" report (2025), read only through a press summary, reported the biggest returns in back-office automation (eliminating outsourced process work, cutting agency costs, streamlining operations) while more than half of generative-AI budgets went to sales and marketing tools. Its sample is small and mixed and its quality is contested ([Fortune, 18 Aug 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/)).

**[INFERENCE]** What to automate first: automate at the constraint step only after it is standardised and measured; prefer deterministic workflow automation for well-defined steps and reserve agents for steps needing judgement. Automating a non-constraint step raises local efficiency without raising throughput. This combines the Theory of Constraints with the Anthropic guidance and is the researchers' synthesis.

### Process metrics

**[DURABLE]** Averages hide long-tail behaviour. Google's site reliability engineering book advises using percentiles (the median for the typical case, the 99th or 99.9th percentile for the plausible worst case) and not assuming normal distributions ([Google SRE Book, Service Level Objectives](https://sre.google/sre-book/service-level-objectives/)).

**[INFERENCE]** Metric definitions for the knowledge base, in standard usage and not retrieved from a source in this research: cycle time is start to finish of one unit of work; lead time is request to delivery including queue time; throughput is units completed per period; first-pass yield is units completed correctly without rework divided by units started.

**[GAP]** No benchmarks for first-pass yield, cycle time or throughput by function were found; these are company-specific. No general, non-AI standard for scoring automation candidates (volume, rule-based, stability) was retrieved. BPMN 2.0 and swimlane conventions, drum-buffer-rope and throughput accounting were not sourced.

## 5. Operating rhythm and decision rights

### The goal systems converge on the same mechanics

**[INFERENCE]** OKRs, EOS, and 4DX converge on the same mechanics: a few quarterly priorities with owners, a weekly numeric scorecard, a fixed-agenda weekly meeting that spends most of its time solving issues, and explicit single-person decision ownership. No controlled evidence comparing goal systems was found; the framework pages are the owners' own descriptions.

### OKRs

**[DURABLE]** In John Doerr's description, one Objective carries three to five Key Results; the typical cycle is a quarter with Key Results graded at period end, and Objectives may roll over for a year or more. Committed OKRs are expected to be achieved in the cycle; aspirational OKRs are stretch goals that may span cycles. Scoring options are Andy Grove's yes or no, red, yellow and green, or Google's 0.0 to 1.0 scale averaged across Key Results ([WhatMatters OKR FAQ](https://www.whatmatters.com/faqs/okr-meaning-definition-example)).

**[DURABLE]** OKRs are kept separate from compensation. The mistakes the same source lists are writing business-as-usual goals, confusing OKRs with KPIs (KPIs measure health, OKRs measure change) and sandbagging ([WhatMatters OKR FAQ](https://www.whatmatters.com/faqs/okr-meaning-definition-example)). The often-quoted convention that 0.7 is a good score was not on the page read and is unverified here.

### EOS: the weekly Level 10 meeting

**[VENDOR]** The EOS Level 10 Meeting is weekly, 90 minutes, on the same day and time with the same agenda, starting and ending on time. The agenda is: segue 5 minutes; scorecard 5; rock review 5; customer and employee headlines 5; to-do list 5; identify, discuss, solve (IDS) 60; conclude 5 ([EOS Worldwide, Level 10 Meeting](https://www.eosworldwide.com/blog/level-10-meeting)).

**[VENDOR]** In EOS, "rocks" are 90-day priorities and are reported only as on or off track; anything off track goes to the issues list and is not solved during the review. To-dos from the issue-solving block each have an owner and are due within seven days. Departmental versions can be shorter with the same structure ([EOS Worldwide, Level 10 Meeting](https://www.eosworldwide.com/blog/level-10-meeting)).

### 4DX

**[VENDOR]** FranklinCovey's four disciplines are: focus on the wildly important; act on lead measures, the highest-leverage activities; keep a compelling scoreboard; and create a cadence of accountability through a simple weekly team process that reviews successes, analyses failures and course-corrects ([FranklinCovey, The 4 Disciplines](https://www.franklincovey.com/the-4-disciplines/)). FranklinCovey's statement that more than 80% of strategies fail, and its client results such as an incremental $5.7M in 90 days at Whirlpool, are unsourced on the page and should be treated as marketing.

### Minimum viable operating rhythm

**[INFERENCE]** A minimum operating rhythm for a small SaaS company or an agent department has five tiers. Annually: set three to five company objectives and the budget, and refresh the risk register and the vendor renewals calendar. Quarterly: three to five priorities, each with one owner and a measurable result, plus a retrospective on last quarter's score. Monthly: KPI and financial review, vendor and SaaS spend review, risk register review. Weekly: a 60 to 90 minute fixed-agenda meeting with roughly two-thirds of the time on issue solving, as in the EOS agenda. Daily, optionally: a short written status. The rhythm is the researchers' synthesis.

**[INFERENCE]** KPI scorecard template: five to fifteen weekly numbers, with columns for metric, owner, target, red or green rule and the last 13 weeks. Off-target metrics become issues and are not discussed in the review slot.

**[INFERENCE]** Meeting design rules distilled from the EOS, GitLab and Atlassian sources: a fixed agenda; a pre-read in writing; decisions and to-dos recorded with owner and date, with seven days as the default; and the outcome posted where the work lives. The house style adds Leila Hormozi's five pre-meeting questions from section 2.

### Decision rights: RAPID

**[VENDOR]** In Bain's RAPID model, Recommend drives the process and develops the proposal; Agree confirms feasibility, typically for mandatory matters such as legal or regulatory compliance; Perform owns implementation; Input supplies expertise and surfaces risk; Decide makes the final call, commits the organisation, communicates the rationale and ensures resources ([Bain, RAPID decision making](https://www.bain.com/insights/rapid-decision-making)).

**[VENDOR]** Bain's rules of thumb: ideally one decider and one recommender per decision; if a group decides, define the tie-break up front; use the Agree role sparingly; assign Perform early; place the decider as close to implementation as possible; and start with high-value or high-frequency decisions, because not every decision needs explicit roles. If an Agree holder and the recommender cannot reconcile, the decider makes the call ([Bain, RAPID decision making](https://www.bain.com/insights/rapid-decision-making)).

### Decision rights and records: DACI

**[VENDOR]** In Atlassian's DACI play, the Driver gathers stakeholders and information and gets the decision made by the agreed date; the Approver is one person who makes the final decision; Contributors have a voice but no vote; the Informed are told the outcome ([Atlassian Team Playbook, DACI](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/daci)).

**[VENDOR]** The DACI decision record has these fields: a title phrased as the question; roles; status, impact, due date and outcome; one to two sentences of background; relevant data; decision factors such as cost, time and scalability; an options table with pros, cons and estimated cost; action items with owners; and the final outcome, recorded so later teams understand the reasoning ([Atlassian Team Playbook, DACI](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/daci)).

### RACI for recurring responsibility

**[DURABLE]** A RACI chart lists tasks or deliverables on one axis and roles on the other. Responsible are those who do the work, at least one per task. Accountable is the one person ultimately answerable, who delegates and signs off. Consulted are people whose input is sought, in two-way communication. Informed are people kept up to date, one way. The source is Wikipedia's entry citing the PMBOK Guide (7th edition, 2021, section 4.6.6); PMI itself was not read. The entry presents the single-accountable rule as the position of some project-management theories and not as universal, and lists variants including DACI, RASCI, RACIQ, RACI-VSL, RACIO, PARIS and RACI+F ([Wikipedia, Responsibility assignment matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Responsibility_assignment_matrix)).

**[INFERENCE]** The defensible house rule for RACI is exactly one Accountable per row and at least one Responsible, stated as the company's own convention with the PMBOK reference. Use RACI for recurring task responsibility and DACI or RAPID for one-off decisions.

### Decision record template

**[INFERENCE]** A decision record that merges DACI and RAPID has these fields: ID and date; the question; the decider (one name); the recommender or driver; agree roles, only legal, security or finance where mandatory; inputs consulted; options with cost and risk; the decision and rationale; who performs and by when; a review date; and status (proposed, decided, superseded). Decisions live in decision records, not in chat.

**[GAP]** Not verified in this research: Scaling Up (Verne Harnish, the Rockefeller Habits, the daily huddle and the one-page strategic plan); architecture decision record conventions; EOS quarterly and annual session formats; OKR scoring conventions beyond the page read; annual planning cycles; PMI's own RACI page and criticisms of RACI.

## 6. Project and programme management

### PMBOK Guide, eighth edition

**[FAST-MOVING, Nov 2025]** The PMBOK Guide, Eighth Edition is PMI's current reference, published in November 2025, with an errata sheet for the second printing last updated in April 2026. It has six principles (holistic view, focus on value, embedded quality, accountable leadership, integrated sustainability, empowered teams) and seven performance domains (governance, scope, schedule, finance, stakeholders, resources, risk). It keeps the principles-and-domains foundation of the seventh edition, simplifies it, reintroduces process guidance in a non-prescriptive form and expands coverage of AI, project management offices and procurement. PMI says it drew on more than 48,000 data points ([PMI, PMBOK Guide](https://www.pmi.org/pmbok-guide)).

### PRINCE2, seventh edition

**[DURABLE]** PRINCE2's seventh edition (2023) has seven principles: ensure continued business justification; learn from experience; define roles, responsibilities and relationships; manage by stages; manage by exception; focus on products; tailor to suit the project. It has seven practices (business case, organizing, quality, plans, risk, issues, progress) and seven processes (starting up, initiating, directing, controlling a stage, managing product delivery, managing stage boundaries, closing). The source is Wikipedia summarising the PeopleCert manual; the primary page was not opened ([Wikipedia, PRINCE2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PRINCE2)).

**[DURABLE]** PeopleCert has owned PRINCE2 since acquiring AXELOS in 2021. The seventh edition added a sustainability performance target, renamed themes to practices and "Change" to "Issues", added a digital and data management approach and a project log, and put more emphasis on people and change management ([Wikipedia, PRINCE2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PRINCE2)).

### Estimation: use the outside view

**[DURABLE]** Bent Flyvbjerg's database holds more than 16,000 public and private projects; only 1 in 200 (0.5%) came in on budget, on time and with the promised benefits. Major building projects averaged a 62% cost overrun, and 18% of IT projects had cost overruns above 50%. The source is a February 2023 Wall Street Journal review of Flyvbjerg and Gardner's book; the underlying papers were not read ([WSJ review reproduced at marclevinson.net, Feb 2023](https://marclevinson.net/HowBigThingsGetDone020723.pdf)).

**[DURABLE]** Reference-class forecasting, as described in that review: pick a category of similar completed projects, look at how they actually performed, and use those actual figures to test whether the proposal is realistic. The companion rule is to think slow and act fast: investigate thoroughly before committing, then deliver quickly, because low-probability shocks during delivery are the biggest source of risk and a short delivery window reduces exposure. The modularity example is the Madrid Metro, which built 76 stations and 81 miles of line from 1995 to 2003 with one repeated station design ([WSJ review, Feb 2023](https://marclevinson.net/HowBigThingsGetDone020723.pdf)).

**[INFERENCE]** Reference-class estimate procedure for an operations agent: classify the project (for example a CRM migration, a vendor onboarding or a security audit); pull actual duration and cost for at least five to ten comparable past projects, internal first and external second; use the median as the base estimate and the 80th percentile as the commitment date or budget; adjust only for documented, specific differences; and log estimate against actual at close to grow the reference class.

**[INFERENCE]** Because IT cost overruns are fat-tailed (18% exceed 50%, per the Flyvbjerg figures above), a single-point contingency such as 10% is not a defensible default. State a range and a threshold at which the project stops for re-approval.

**[HOUSE STYLE]** The house style adds a hurdle for new initiatives: workshop notes report that any change is expected to cost roughly 20% of performance (the note-taker says arguably 40%), so a new project should clear at least that expected gain. Unaudited rule of thumb ([Dickie Bush, 12 takeaways](https://dickiebush.substack.com/p/12-takeaways-from-alex-hormozis-scaling)).

### Project templates

**[INFERENCE]** Project brief outline: problem and business justification; outcome measures; in scope and out of scope; deliverables; stages with go or no-go gates; tolerances for time and cost that trigger escalation; roles including a single sponsor or decider; dependencies; top risks; and the reference-class estimate. The deliverables, stages and tolerances fields follow PRINCE2's focus on products, manage by stages and manage by exception.

**[INFERENCE]** Risk register columns: ID; risk statement written as cause, event and effect; category; likelihood from 1 to 5; impact from 1 to 5; score; owner; treatment (avoid, reduce, transfer or accept); action and due date; trigger or early-warning indicator; status; last reviewed.

**[INFERENCE]** Weekly status report outline: overall red, amber or green status with a one-sentence reason; progress against milestones with planned and actual dates; the top three risks and issues with owners; decisions needed, from whom and by when; dependencies due in the next two weeks; and forecast to complete against baseline.

**[INFERENCE]** Dependency management: keep a dependency log (provider, consumer, what, needed-by date, status), review it in the weekly meeting, and treat any cross-team dependency without a named provider owner as a risk.

**[INFERENCE]** Post-project review outline: goals against results; estimate against actual, which feeds the reference class; what went well; what failed and the root cause; changes to procedures and checklists with owners. Hold it within two weeks of close, following PRINCE2's learn-from-experience principle.

**[GAP]** Not verified: the PMBOK 7 principle list and PMBOK 8's process and AI content beyond the PMI landing page; the primary PRINCE2 page; agile at programme level (SAFe, LeSS, Disciplined Agile, MSP); Flyvbjerg's detailed figures and his IT fat-tail paper; Kahneman and Tversky's original planning-fallacy research.

## 7. Service levels and capacity

### SLI, SLO and SLA

**[DURABLE]** A service level indicator (SLI) is a defined quantitative measure of service such as latency, error rate, throughput or availability. A service level objective (SLO) is a target value or range for an indicator. A service level agreement (SLA) is a contract with consequences for missing objectives. The test is whether an explicit consequence exists: without one it is an objective, not an agreement ([Google SRE Book, Service Level Objectives](https://sre.google/sre-book/service-level-objectives/)).

**[DURABLE]** Guidance on setting targets from the same source: start from what users care about and work back to indicators, measuring on the client side where server metrics miss user-visible problems; do not set targets from current performance alone; avoid absolutes such as "always available"; use as few objectives as possible; start loose and tighten; keep an internal objective tighter than the advertised one; and avoid over-delivering so that users do not come to depend on performance beyond the objective ([Google SRE Book, Service Level Objectives](https://sre.google/sre-book/service-level-objectives/)).

**[DURABLE]** An error budget is the permitted rate of missing an objective, tracked daily or weekly and used to decide between reliability work and new releases ([Google SRE Book, Service Level Objectives](https://sre.google/sre-book/service-level-objectives/)).

**[INFERENCE]** Internal service-level template for an operations desk: request type; indicator (for example time to first response, time to resolution, first-pass yield); objective expressed as a percentile, such as 90% of access requests resolved within one business day; measurement source; owner; and what happens when the budget is burned, for example pausing new project intake.

### Queueing: why full utilisation produces long waits

**[DURABLE]** Kingman's approximation, due to John Kingman (1961), estimates mean waiting time in a single-server queue with general arrival and service distributions. Its inputs are utilisation, mean service time and the coefficients of variation of inter-arrival and service times, and it is described as generally very accurate, especially close to saturation. The source is Wikipedia; the equation itself did not render in the fetched text and is not reproduced here ([Wikipedia, Kingman's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingman%27s_formula)).

**[INFERENCE]** Kingman's inputs imply two levers for shortening waits at a fixed staffing level: lower planned utilisation at the bottleneck, and lower variability in arrivals or handling time, through standard work, intake templates, batching similar requests and limits on work in progress. The sharp rise in waiting as utilisation nears 100% is the textbook reading of the formula; it was not read on the page and stays an inference.

**[DURABLE]** The Theory of Constraints adds that non-constraint resources should not produce faster than the constraint can absorb, and that a buffer should protect the constraint from starvation ([TOC Institute, Five Focusing Steps](https://www.tocinstitute.org/five-focusing-steps.html)).

### Capacity planning

**[INFERENCE]** Planning rule of thumb, not a sourced benchmark: plan knowledge-work teams and agent queues at roughly 70 to 85% of capacity on the constraint step and keep the remainder as buffer for unplanned work; plan at 100% only for non-constraint, easily interruptible work. The house style's unaudited observation that staff typically sit at 70 to 80% utilisation points the same way ([Rosetta transcript of "Less is More", 22 May 2025](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/less-is-more-the-magic-of-a-simple-business)).

**[INFERENCE]** Capacity plan outline: demand forecast by work type (volume per week from 13 weeks of history); effort per unit (median and 85th percentile); available hours per role, or agent concurrency, after meetings and leave; utilisation at the constraint; the gap and the options (cut demand, exploit, subordinate, elevate); reviewed monthly.

**[GAP]** Not sourced: Kingman's formula as an equation, Erlang C staffing, Little's law, evidence on work-in-progress limits, the ITIL 4 service-level management practice, and any benchmark for target utilisation in knowledge work.

## 10. Organisation design and scaling

### Span of control depends on how standardised the work is

**[DURABLE]** McKinsey's 2017 framework gives five managerial archetypes with typical spans: player/coach 3 to 5 direct reports; coach 6 to 7; supervisor 8 to 10; facilitator 11 to 15; coordinator 15 or more. The criteria for choosing the archetype are the share of the manager's time spent on own work, how standardised the process is, how similar the direct reports' work is, and how much experience the work needs and how long it takes to become self-sufficient (several years for player/coach teams, a couple of weeks for coordinator teams). The source is a consultancy article dated 21 December 2017 ([McKinsey, How to identify the right spans of control](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/how-to-identify-the-right-spans-of-control-for-your-organization)).

**[INFERENCE]** When to add a management layer: when a manager's span exceeds the archetype range for the kind of work for more than a quarter, or when the manager is the constraint and decisions queue behind one person. For a founder-led SaaS team doing varied, non-standard work, the player/coach range of 3 to 5 is the relevant ceiling before a lead is needed.

**[INFERENCE]** A manager agent supervising worker agents maps to the same logic: highly standardised, checklist-driven worker tasks permit a wide span, while novel, judgement-heavy tasks need a narrow span and more human review. This is an analogy, not a sourced finding.

**[INFERENCE]** Functional structure suits small companies because expertise and ownership are clear; add cross-functional squads only around a specific outcome with one accountable owner. No evidence comparing functional and cross-functional structures was retrieved.

### Asynchronous work and documentation

**[PLATFORM CLAIM]** GitLab's handbook states its asynchronous principle as doing as much as possible with what one has, documenting everything, handing off ownership and moving on. Documentation makes asynchronous work possible because readers need a written source they can consult without waiting. Its pre-send checks for a message are the right format, a recipient who has the context, a clear and considerate text that includes resources or next steps, and documentation for later retrieval ([GitLab Handbook, Asynchronous work](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/company/culture/all-remote/asynchronous/)).

**[PLATFORM CLAIM]** GitLab lists the situations that justify a synchronous meeting: urgent incidents, troubleshooting, asynchronous discussion that has stalled or where people talk past each other, creative brainstorming and early design, highly technical topics, relationship building and periodic alignment. Outcomes of synchronous discussions are still documented in the related issue. The page sets no numeric thresholds; one contributor's heuristic is to move to a call after several back-and-forths ([GitLab Handbook, Asynchronous work](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/company/culture/all-remote/asynchronous/)).

**[INFERENCE]** Knowledge-management rules adapted from GitLab: one source of truth per topic; answer questions with a link and write the answer down if no link exists; every process document has an owner and a review date; decisions live in decision records, not chat.

**[INFERENCE]** Before a reorganisation, outsourcing or significant automation that changes roles, check whether MBL 11 § and 38 § negotiation applies, which it does where the employer is bound by a collective agreement, and schedule it before the decision is taken and not after.

## 12. Operating a company with AI agents in 2026

### Adoption is near-universal; profit impact is not

**[FAST-MOVING, Aug 2026]** McKinsey's "The state of AI in 2026" (published 25 August 2026; fielded 4 May to 8 June 2026; 1,719 respondents in 97 countries) finds that nearly nine in ten respondents' organisations use AI regularly in at least one function and 44% say AI is scaling across the enterprise, up from 38% the year before. About two in ten are scaling AI agents: 40% of large organisations (up from 27%) against 22% of smaller organisations (flat). **[FAST-MOVING, Aug 2026]** In the same survey 37% attribute at least some EBIT impact to AI, essentially unchanged from the year before. Cost reductions appear most often in supply chain, service operations and manufacturing, and revenue gains most often in marketing and sales. At the individual level 80% say AI improved their productivity and 50% say it helps them decide better ([McKinsey, The state of AI in 2026](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai)).

**[FAST-MOVING, Aug 2026]** McKinsey's high performers are about 6% of respondents (at least 5% of EBIT from AI plus "significant" value, unchanged from 2025). Nearly three-quarters of them fundamentally redesigned workflows, against 55% the previous year and about one-quarter among other respondents. **[CONTESTED] [FAST-MOVING, 2025]** The MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide" report of 2025, read through a press summary, found that about 5% of AI pilots achieved rapid revenue acceleration and the rest showed little or no measurable profit-and-loss impact. ### Design patterns for agent work

**[VENDOR]** Anthropic's guidance of 19 December 2024 describes five patterns: prompt chaining with programmatic checks between steps; routing; parallelisation by sectioning or voting; orchestrator-workers, in which a central model decomposes, delegates and synthesises; and evaluator-optimiser, in which one model generates and another critiques, which works best with clear criteria ([Anthropic, Building effective agents, 19 Dec 2024](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)).

**[VENDOR]** The same guidance says agents should check ground truth from the environment at each step through tool results, can pause for human feedback at checkpoints or when blocked, and should have stopping conditions such as a maximum number of iterations; they should be tested extensively in sandboxes with guardrails ([Anthropic, Building effective agents, 19 Dec 2024](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)).

### Workflow redesign, human review and agent supervision

**[INFERENCE]** The 2026 survey data separates three things that vendor claims merge: individual productivity (80% report gains), enterprise scaling (44%) and profit impact (37% any, about 6% significant) ([McKinsey, The state of AI in 2026](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai)). The separation is the reason this department reports process outcomes and not usage.

**[INFERENCE]** Workflow redesign procedure: map the current process with a SIPOC and a swimlane; remove steps that exist only because of human hand-offs; decide per step between deterministic automation, an agent or a human; define the acceptance check for each agent output; pilot against the baseline; and standardise the result as a procedure.

**[INFERENCE]** Human-in-the-loop design is tiered by reversibility and blast radius. Tier 0, read-only work and internal drafts, needs no approval and gets sampled review. Tier 1, reversible internal changes, gets post-hoc review with logging. Tier 2, external communication, spending, data deletion and contract or access changes, needs human approval before action. Tier 3, legal, people and security-critical decisions, is decided by a human with the agent only preparing. Section 24 turns these tiers into the department's guardrails.

**[INFERENCE]** Agent supervision and performance management under the manager-agent pattern: a task brief per worker with a definition of done; an evaluator check before hand-back; iteration and budget caps; an audit log of actions and tool calls; a weekly scorecard per agent workflow (volume, first-pass yield, rework rate, escalation rate, cost per task, incidents); and a human quality review of a 5 to 10% sample of outputs, with the sampling rate raised or lowered with the error rate. No source was found on formal performance management of agents; this paragraph is inference.

**[INFERENCE]** Cost control for agent work: with about 20% of firms reporting token costs as a constraint ([McKinsey, 2026](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai)) and 78% of IT leaders reporting surprise AI charges ([Zylo, 2026](https://zylo.com/reports/2026-saas-management-index/)), set a budget and an alert per workflow.

### Swedish legal constraints on the hiring process

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation, not legal advice. The Swedish Discrimination Act protects seven grounds: sex, transgender identity or expression, ethnicity, religion or other belief, disability, sexual orientation and age (1 kap. 1 and 5 §§). Recruitment and promotion is one of the five areas where an employer must run active anti-discrimination measures (3 kap. 4 to 5 §§) ([Diskrimineringslagen, riksdagen.se](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/diskrimineringslag-2008567_sfs-2008-567/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. An applicant who was not hired or not interviewed, and an employee passed over for promotion, can demand written information on the education, experience and other merits of the person chosen (Diskrimineringslagen 2 kap. 4 §). The process consequence is to keep a scorecard and merit record for each hire ([Diskrimineringslagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/diskrimineringslag-2008567_sfs-2008-567/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. The Parental Leave Act separately bans disadvantaging applicants and employees for reasons linked to parental leave, in hiring, promotion, pay and dismissal (16 §) ([Föräldraledighetslagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/foraldraledighetslag-1995584_sfs-1995-584/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source (the Swedish data protection authority, IMY); orientation only. For candidate data the usual legal basis is a balancing of interests and not consent. The employer collects only what is needed to judge fit for the specific role and deletes or anonymises applications, interview notes and references when the process ends, except that they may be kept for as long as the applicant could bring a legal claim. Keeping a candidate for future openings needs consent ([IMY, recruitment systems and competence databases](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/dataskydd-pa-olika-omraden/arbetsliv/rekryteringssystem-och-kompetensdatabaser/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. IMY treats personality-test results as privacy-sensitive and asks employers to weigh whether they are needed. Sensitive data and offence data are normally off limits in recruitment. IMY's own example of a prohibited solely automated decision is an online recruitment system that rejects a candidate with no human contact ([IMY, recruitment systems](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/dataskydd-pa-olika-omraden/arbetsliv/rekryteringssystem-och-kompetensdatabaser/)).

**[GAP]** The exact claim-limitation window for rejected applicants was read only in outline (the Discrimination Act refers to MBL time limits). IMY permits retention while a claim is possible but states no number of months on the page read. Many Swedish employers are said to use 24 months; the basis for that figure was not verified, and a lawyer should set the retention rule ([Diskrimineringslagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/diskrimineringslag-2008567_sfs-2008-567/)).

### A default hiring pipeline

**[INFERENCE]** A defensible default pipeline is: a role scorecard with four to six must-have outcomes and competencies; a structured screen; a work sample or job-knowledge test; a structured interview with anchored rating scales, each interviewer scoring independently before discussion; a reference check on the same criteria; and a written offer. It follows from the 2022 validity ranking and from the merit-information duty in Diskrimineringslagen 2 kap. 4 §.

**[HOUSE STYLE] [INFERENCE]** The house style fits on top of this pipeline where the law allows: treat the job description as a sales page and the interview as a sales call (Leila Hormozi), interview more candidates than feels necessary, prefer candidates who describe past work in metrics, and use the first good hire in a role as the pattern for later ones (Alex Hormozi) ([Podcast Rex summary, 21 May 2026](https://podcastrex.com/shows/build-with-leila-hormozi/the-hiring-system-that-lets-your-business-run-solo-ep-362); [Rosetta transcript, 4 Nov 2025](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/if-i-wanted-to-create-a-business-that-runs-itself-here-s-what-i-d-do)). Group interviews and interview recordings are used only inside the structured, equal-treatment and data-minimisation rules above.

**[INFERENCE]** Stop asking candidates about salary history now. It is not banned in Swedish law as of November 2026, but the EU Pay Transparency Directive contains such a ban (Article 5) and the question adds bias risk ([Directive (EU) 2023/970](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/970/oj)).

### Onboarding checklist and house-style training

**[INFERENCE]** Onboarding checklist for Operations. ## 15. Performance management

### What the evidence says

**[VENDOR]** Gallup's Q12 meta-analysis covers 183,806 business units across 736 studies; top-quartile units on engagement show 23% higher profit than bottom-quartile units, with lower absenteeism, turnover and safety incidents. **[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. ### A light performance cycle

**[INFERENCE]** A light cycle that fits a small company: quarterly goals, weekly or fortnightly one-to-ones, two written reviews a year, and a calibration meeting before the pay review so that the annual pay survey has documented criteria to analyse.

**[HOUSE STYLE] [INFERENCE]** The house-style contribution to performance management is the what, how, why diagnosis before any conclusion about the person, behaviour-specific feedback, telling people where they stand, and written expectations (section 2). **[INFERENCE]** A performance-improvement process in Sweden doubles as the evidence trail for dismissal on personal grounds: documented expectations, support, a written warning and a redeployment check all map onto the LAS 7 § test ([LAS](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-198280-om-anstallningsskydd_sfs-1982-80/)). ### What the directive contains, for preparation

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source for the article list (EUR-Lex), though the full article text was not read; orientation only. ## 17. Offboarding

### Ending employment under LAS

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source (text consolidated through SFS 2022:836; read through a summarising tool); orientation, not legal advice. Dismissal by the employer needs objective reasons (sakliga skäl), which can be redundancy or personal circumstances; no objective reasons exist if the employer can reasonably offer other work. A dismissal for personal circumstances may not rest only on facts the employer has known for more than two months (LAS 7 §) ([LAS, riksdagen.se](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-198280-om-anstallningsskydd_sfs-1982-80/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Before a dismissal on personal grounds the employee is notified at least two weeks ahead, one week for a summary dismissal; a union member's local union is alerted at the same time and consultation can be requested within one week (LAS 30 §) ([LAS](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-198280-om-anstallningsskydd_sfs-1982-80/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Notice of dismissal is written, says how to contest it and whether re-employment priority applies, and is handed over in person or sent by registered post; reasons are given on request (LAS 8 to 10 §§). Summary dismissal (avsked) is available only for gross breach of duty, in writing, and the two-month rule applies (LAS 18 to 19 §§) ([LAS](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-198280-om-anstallningsskydd_sfs-1982-80/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. The minimum notice period is one month for both sides. On notice from the employer the employee is entitled to two months after two years' service, three after four, four after six, five after eight and six after ten (LAS 11 §) ([LAS](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-198280-om-anstallningsskydd_sfs-1982-80/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. A dismissal without objective reasons can be declared invalid, but the employment does not continue beyond the notice period during the dispute (LAS 34 §). An employer that refuses to comply with an invalidity judgment pays 16 monthly salaries for under five years' service, 24 for five to under ten and 32 for ten or more (LAS 39 §). The employee must notify an invalidity claim within two weeks of the dismissal, or one month if the notice lacked the contest information, and damages claims within four months; missed deadlines extinguish the claim (LAS 40 to 42 §§) ([LAS](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-198280-om-anstallningsskydd_sfs-1982-80/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Fixed-term staff who meet the service threshold get written notice of non-renewal at least one month before the end (LAS 15 to 17 §§) ([LAS](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-198280-om-anstallningsskydd_sfs-1982-80/)).

### Redundancy

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. In a redundancy the order of dismissal is last in, first out by length of service with age as tiebreak. The employer may exempt up to three employees of particular importance before the order is set, with no further exemption within three months (LAS 22 §). Those made redundant with more than 12 months' service in three years (nine months for special fixed-term employment) have re-employment priority until nine months after the employment ended (LAS 25 §) ([LAS](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-198280-om-anstallningsskydd_sfs-1982-80/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. An employer with no collective agreement at all must still negotiate under MBL 11 § with every affected union before redundancy dismissals (MBL 13 §) ([MBL](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source (text consolidated through SFS 2017:361); orientation only. Written notice to the Public Employment Service (varsel) is required for a cutback affecting at least five employees, or when cutbacks are expected to lead to at least 20 redundancies within 90 days. The lead time before the first termination takes effect is at least two months for up to 25 employees, four months for 26 to 100 and six months for more than 100. The notice states reasons, the number and categories affected, the number and categories normally employed and the timing; names, information on negotiations and a copy of the notice given to the union follow as soon as possible and no later than one month before ([Lag 1974:13, 1 to 2 a §§](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-197413-om-vissa-anstallningsframjande_sfs-1974-13/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Under the same act a temporary lay-off needs one month's notice, or six weeks where fixed-term or seasonal staff cannot be kept on; an unforeseeable situation is notified as soon as possible; the employer may instead tell the Public Employment Service in writing, within the deadline, that MBL negotiations have been or will be requested; and failure by intent or gross negligence can cost a notification fee of SEK 100 to 500 per affected employee per started week of delay ([Lag 1974:13, 3, 4, 6 and 17 §§](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-197413-om-vissa-anstallningsframjande_sfs-1974-13/)).

**[INFERENCE]** Redundancy order of work for a company without a collective agreement: negotiate with the unions of affected members, notify the Public Employment Service if five or more are affected, apply the order of priority with the three-person exemption, then give written notice with the contest information. Hiring into a role within nine months of a redundancy needs a check against the priority list first.

### Final pay and continuing duties

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Holiday compensation for untaken earned holiday is due no later than one month after employment ends (Semesterlagen 28 to 30 §§) ([Semesterlagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/semesterlag-1977480_sfs-1977-480/)). Whistleblower protection continues for former staff ([Visselblåsarlagen 1 kap. 8 §](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2021890-om-skydd-for-personer-som-rapporterar_sfs-2021-890/)).

### Offboarding checklist

**[INFERENCE]** Offboarding checklist: written resignation or notice with the date received; notice period calculated from LAS 11 § or the contract if longer; handover plan; final pay with holiday compensation inside one month; access removal at the end of the last working day, or immediately on summary dismissal; equipment return; certificates issued; personal data deleted per the retention schedule; re-employment priority noted for nine months after a redundancy; and an exit interview by someone other than the line manager.

## 18. Swedish employment framework for day-to-day operations

### Contract forms

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. The permitted fixed-term forms are special fixed-term employment, substitute employment and seasonal employment (LAS 5 §). Special fixed-term employment converts to permanent after more than 12 months in a five-year period, and a substitute post after more than two years in five (5 a §). At age 69 the objective-reasons requirement and several other protections stop applying (33 §) ([LAS](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-198280-om-anstallningsskydd_sfs-1982-80/)).

### Unions and co-determination without a collective agreement

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Unions have a right to negotiate about their members (MBL 10 §). The duty to negotiate before major changes (11 §) binds employers with a collective agreement; an employer without one must negotiate when a matter specifically concerns a union's member, and before redundancies and certain business transfers (13 §) ([MBL](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1976580-om-medbestammande-i-arbetslivet_sfs-1976-580/)).

**[INFERENCE]** A company without a collective agreement still has MBL duties in three situations: redundancies, business transfers, and matters specific to a union member. Operations therefore asks whether the person is a union member before any individual adverse decision, and handles the answer as sensitive data.

### Working time

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source (text consolidated through SFS 2022:450); orientation only. Ordinary working time is 40 hours a week (Arbetstidslagen 5 §). General overtime is at most 48 hours per four weeks or 50 per month and 200 per year (8 §), with up to 150 more hours for special reasons (8 a §). Total working time is at most 48 hours per seven days on average over up to four months (10 b §) ([Arbetstidslagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/arbetstidslag-1982673_sfs-1982-673/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Daily rest is 11 consecutive hours including midnight to 05:00 (13 §); weekly rest is 36 consecutive hours (14 §); no one works more than five hours without a break (15 §); schedule changes are announced two weeks ahead (12 §). The employer keeps records of on-call time, overtime and additional part-time hours, open to employees and unions (11 §) ([Arbetstidslagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/arbetstidslag-1982673_sfs-1982-673/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. The Working Hours Act exempts work the employer cannot supervise, managerial staff and those who largely control their own time (2 §), and collective agreements may deviate from most rules (3 §). The Work Environment Authority supervises working time and can issue orders backed by fines (20 to 22 §§) ([Arbetstidslagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/arbetstidslag-1982673_sfs-1982-673/)).

**[FAST-MOVING, Sep 2026]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Unlawful overtime carries a strict-liability sanction fee of 1% of the price base amount per hour and employee (Arbetstidslagen 26 §), about SEK 596 per hour at the 2027 amount of SEK 59,600 ([Arbetstidslagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/arbetstidslag-1982673_sfs-1982-673/); [Regeringen, Sep 2026](https://regeringen.se/pressmeddelanden/2026/09/prisbasbeloppfor-2027-faststallt/)).

**[INFERENCE]** "Trust-based hours" clauses rely on the 2 § exemption and do not remove the employer's work-environment responsibility for workload.

### Holiday

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source (text consolidated through SFS 2014:424); orientation only. Statutory holiday is 25 days a year (Semesterlagen 4 §). The statutory holiday year runs 1 April to 31 March with the preceding year as the earning year (3 §). Four weeks fall in June to August unless otherwise agreed (12 §), and the employer gives two months' notice of placement when there is no agreement (11 §) ([Semesterlagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/semesterlag-1977480_sfs-1977-480/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Days above 20 can be saved for up to five years (18 and 20 §§). Holiday pay for monthly salaried staff is normal pay plus 0.43% of monthly pay per day, with 12% on variable pay (16 a §); otherwise it is 12% of earnings (16 b §). Absence that still earns holiday pay includes sickness up to 180 days per earning year and parental leave up to 120 days, or 180 for single parents (17 to 17 a §§) ([Semesterlagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/semesterlag-1977480_sfs-1977-480/)).

### Sick pay and rehabilitation

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source (text consolidated through SFS 2025:938); orientation only. The employer pays sick pay for 14 calendar days at 80% of lost pay, less a waiting deduction of 20% of one average week's sick pay, with at most ten deductions in twelve months; a relapse within five days continues the same period (Sjuklönelagen 6 to 7 §§). A medical certificate is needed from the seventh calendar day after the sick report, in practice day eight (8 §). Contracts shorter than a month give sick pay only after 14 days worked (3 §) ([Sjuklönelagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-19911047-om-sjuklon_sfs-1991-1047/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. The employer reports the case to the Social Insurance Agency from day 15 and by day 21 (the statute says within seven calendar days after the sick-pay period), and failure can be fined. By day 30 the employer must have a written return-to-work plan if the absence is expected to pass 60 days. The rehabilitation duty includes adapting tasks, hours or the workplace and keeping in contact ([Försäkringskassan, sick employee days 1 to 90](https://www.forsakringskassan.se/arbetsgivare/sjukdom-och-skada/sjuk-medarbetare-dag-1-90); [Sjuklönelagen 12 and 19 §§](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-19911047-om-sjuklon_sfs-1991-1047/)). The later rehabilitation checkpoints were not on the page read.

### Parental leave, parental benefit and care of a sick child

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source (text consolidated through SFS 2025:733); orientation only. A parent has a right to full leave until the child is 18 months old and beyond that while on full parental benefit (Föräldraledighetslagen 5 §). Working time may be cut by up to a quarter until the child turns eight (7 §), extended to the end of the second school year from 1 July 2028. The employee gives two months' notice of planned leave, and one week or as soon as possible for care of a sick child (13 §). Leave may be split into three periods a year (10 §), and the employer can postpone an early return by up to a month (15 §) ([Föräldraledighetslagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/foraldraledighetslag-1995584_sfs-1995-584/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. After six months' employment a parent of a child under eight may request flexible arrangements such as remote work; the employer answers in reasonable time and gives reasons for a refusal (Föräldraledighetslagen 15 a to 15 c §§) ([Föräldraledighetslagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/foraldraledighetslag-1995584_sfs-1995-584/)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 24 Aug 2026]** Legal status: checked on primary source (page last updated 24 August 2026; the page does not name the year its amounts apply to); orientation only. Parental benefit is 480 days per child, shared by the parents: 390 days at the income-based level and 90 days at the minimum level of SEK 180 a day. Ninety of the income-based days are reserved for each parent and cannot be transferred (60 for children born 2015 or earlier); other days can be transferred, including to someone other than a parent. An employee with regular income usually gets about 80% of pay, and the income-based day rate is shown as SEK 250 to 1,259 for a full day ([Försäkringskassan, parental benefit](https://www.forsakringskassan.se/privatperson/foralder/foraldrapenning)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 24 Aug 2026]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. To get the income-based level for the first 180 days a parent needs an income corresponding to at least SEK 7,083 a month for at least 240 days before the expected birth. Days can be used until the child turns 12 or finishes school year five, at most 96 days can be kept after the child's fourth birthday, and twins give 660 days ([Försäkringskassan, parental benefit, updated 24 Aug 2026](https://www.forsakringskassan.se/privatperson/foralder/foraldrapenning)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 10 Nov 2026]** Legal status: checked on primary source (page last updated 10 November 2026, covering children under 12); orientation only. Temporary parental benefit for care of a sick child (VAB) is 120 days per child per year, shared among those who care for the child. Once 60 days are used, it is no longer paid for days when the child's regular carer is ill, only when the child is ill or has a care visit. Compensation is a little under 80% of qualifying income, and income above 7.5 price base amounts is not covered. A certificate from a doctor or nurse is needed when the child is ill for more than seven days, weekends included. The application must reach the Social Insurance Agency within 30 days of the first day at home ([Försäkringskassan, VAB](https://www.forsakringskassan.se/privatperson/foralder/vard-av-barn-vab)).

### Discrimination: active measures and harassment

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Active measures follow a continuous four-step method (investigate risks, analyse causes, act, follow up) across five areas (Diskrimineringslagen 3 kap. 2 to 3 §§). Guidelines and routines against harassment, sexual harassment and reprisals are mandatory for every employer (6 §), and the work is done in cooperation with employees (11 §). On learning that an employee feels harassed, the employer must investigate and take reasonable measures (2 kap. 3 §) ([Diskrimineringslagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/diskrimineringslag-2008567_sfs-2008-567/)).

### Whistleblowing

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Internal whistleblowing channels are mandatory from 50 employees at the start of the year (lag 2021:890, 5 kap. 2 §), and private employers with 50 to 249 employees may share intake and investigation (3 §). The requirements are independent designated persons, written procedures, written and oral reporting and a meeting on request, receipt confirmed within seven days, feedback within three months, and records kept at most two years after the case closes ([Visselblåsarlagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2021890-om-skydd-for-personer-som-rapporterar_sfs-2021-890/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. The bans on hindering reports and on reprisals protect employees, applicants, interns, volunteers, self-employed contractors, board members and active shareholders, with a reversed burden of proof (1 kap. 8 §, 3 kap. 1 and 5 §§). These protections are not tied to the 50-employee threshold for channels ([Visselblåsarlagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2021890-om-skydd-for-personer-som-rapporterar_sfs-2021-890/)).

## 19. Swedish work-environment law

### The employer's core duties under the Work Environment Act

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source (Arbetsmiljölagen 1977:1160, text consolidated through SFS 2026:1001; what that 2026 amendment changed was not identified); orientation only. The employer takes every measure needed to prevent ill health and accidents and removes a risk at its source where possible (3 kap. 2 §). The employer systematically plans, directs and checks the work, investigates work injuries, assesses risks continuously, schedules measures that cannot be taken at once, documents the work environment and action plans to the extent the business requires, and runs an organised adaptation and rehabilitation activity (3 kap. 2 a §) ([Arbetsmiljölagen, riksdagen.se](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/arbetsmiljolag-19771160_sfs-1977-1160/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. The employer provides the occupational health service the working conditions call for, as an independent expert resource (3 kap. 2 c §). Employees get knowledge of conditions and risks, the training they need and work adapted to their individual circumstances (3 kap. 3 §). Deaths, serious injuries and serious incidents are reported to the authority without delay (3 kap. 3 a §). Employees take part in the work, follow rules, use protective equipment and report immediate serious danger (3 kap. 4 §) ([Arbetsmiljölagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/arbetsmiljolag-19771160_sfs-1977-1160/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Breach of an order or prohibition under the Work Environment Act can bring a fine or up to one year in prison (8 kap. 1 §), and sanction fees for breaches of regulations range from SEK 1,000 to SEK 1,000,000 (8 kap. 5 §) ([Arbetsmiljölagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/arbetsmiljolag-19771160_sfs-1977-1160/)).

### Safety representative from five employees, safety committee from fifty

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. One or more safety representatives (skyddsombud) are appointed at a workplace where at least five employees are regularly employed (Arbetsmiljölagen 6 kap. 2 §). A safety committee (skyddskommitté) is required where at least fifty employees are regularly employed, and at smaller workplaces if the employees ask for it (6 kap. 8 §) ([Arbetsmiljölagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/arbetsmiljolag-19771160_sfs-1977-1160/)). Who appoints the representative and the length of the term were not extracted.

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. The safety representative takes part in planning changes, is informed of changes affecting the work environment, gets the paid time off the role needs and has access to the documents and information needed (6 kap. 4 to 6 §§). The representative may demand measures or an investigation; the employer confirms receipt in writing and answers without delay, and without action in reasonable time the Work Environment Authority decides on an order or prohibition (6 a §). The representative may stop work that involves immediate and serious danger pending the authority's decision and is not liable for damage caused by doing so (7 §) ([Arbetsmiljölagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/arbetsmiljolag-19771160_sfs-1977-1160/)).

### Systematic work environment management: AFS 2023:1

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Since 1 January 2025 systematic work environment management (SAM) sits in the regulation AFS 2023:1, decided 15 September 2023, which replaced AFS 2001:1. It has 16 sections and one annex. SAM covers all physical, organisational and social conditions that matter for the work environment (5 §), is continuous, and is followed up every year and after reorganisations or serious incidents ([AFS 2023:1, av.se](https://www.av.se/arbetsmiljoarbete-och-inspektioner/publikationer/foreskrifter/afs-20231/); [Arbetsmiljöverket, SAM](https://www.av.se/arbetsmiljoarbete-och-inspektioner/arbeta-med-arbetsmiljon/systematiskt-arbetsmiljoarbete-sam/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Under AFS 2023:1 four things must be in writing from ten employees: the work-environment policy (7 §), the routines saying when, by whom and with whose participation the work is done (8 §), the allocation of work-environment tasks (9 §) and the result of the annual follow-up (14 §). Three things must be in writing regardless of size: risk assessments, which state which risks exist and whether they are serious (11 §); an action plan for measures not taken immediately (13 §); and written instructions where the work involves serious risks (10 §) ([AFS 2023:1](https://www.av.se/arbetsmiljoarbete-och-inspektioner/publikationer/foreskrifter/afs-20231/)).

### Organisational and social work environment: AFS 2023:2, chapter 2

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. The regulation AFS 2023:2 on planning and organising work-environment work was decided on 15 September 2023 and has been in force since 1 January 2025. It has nine chapters: general provisions; organisational and social work environment; work adaptation; first aid and crisis support; violence and threats; lone work; pregnant, recently delivered and breastfeeding employees; minors; and records of on-call time, overtime and additional hours ([AFS 2023:2, av.se](https://www.av.se/arbetsmiljoarbete-och-inspektioner/publikationer/foreskrifter/afs-20232/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Chapter 2 of AFS 2023:2 defines unhealthy workload as demands that exceed resources more than temporarily, which harms health when prolonged and recovery is insufficient, and defines victimisation (kränkande särbehandling) as hostile acts directed at one or more employees that can cause ill health or exclusion from the workplace community (2 kap. 3 §). A business that hires in agency staff is treated as their employer for this chapter (2 §) ([AFS 2023:2](https://www.av.se/arbetsmiljoarbete-och-inspektioner/publikationer/foreskrifter/afs-20232/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Under chapter 2 of AFS 2023:2 managers and supervisors must know how to prevent and handle unhealthy workload and victimisation and must be given the conditions to apply that knowledge (4 §). The employer sets goals for the organisational and social work environment with employee participation and makes them known; the goals are written where there are ten or more employees (5 §) ([AFS 2023:2](https://www.av.se/arbetsmiljoarbete-och-inspektioner/publikationer/foreskrifter/afs-20232/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Under chapter 2 of AFS 2023:2 tasks and authority must not create unhealthy workload, meaning resources are matched to demands. Employees must know their tasks, the expected result, which tasks to prioritise when time is short, whom to ask for help and their authority. The employer acts against severely psychologically straining tasks (6 to 8 §§) and prevents the scheduling of working time from causing ill health (9 §) ([AFS 2023:2](https://www.av.se/arbetsmiljoarbete-och-inspektioner/publikationer/foreskrifter/afs-20232/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Under chapter 2 of AFS 2023:2 the employer states that victimisation is not accepted and counters conditions that can cause it. Routines must say who receives a report, what the recipient does, how the report is handled and how those affected get help quickly, and the routines are made known to all employees. No headcount threshold applies (10 to 11 §§) ([AFS 2023:2](https://www.av.se/arbetsmiljoarbete-och-inspektioner/publikationer/foreskrifter/afs-20232/)).

**[INFERENCE]** Corrected claim: the regulation page for AFS 2023:1 names only AFS 2001:1 as replaced. That the older regulation on organisational and social work environment (AFS 2015:4) was absorbed into chapter 2 of AFS 2023:2 is an inference from the matching content (knowledge, goals, workload, working time, victimisation); the repeal list confirming it was not read.

**[INFERENCE]** The chapter 2 duty that employees know their tasks, the expected result, priorities when time is short and their authority is the legal twin of the house-style rule that expectations are written down. One written role document can serve both.

### Remote and hybrid work

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary sources (the statute and a ministerial answer); orientation only. The Work Environment Act has no section specific to work in the employee's home; its scope is every activity in which an employee works for an employer (1 kap. 2 §) ([Arbetsmiljölagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/arbetsmiljolag-19771160_sfs-1977-1160/)). In an answer to the Riksdag dated 7 April 2021, the employment minister stated that the employer's work-environment responsibility also holds when employees work remotely, stressing risk assessments and cooperation with safety representatives; the answer does not address employer access to the home ([Riksdagen, answer to question 2020/21:2350, 7 Apr 2021](https://data.riksdagen.se/dokument/H8122350.html)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 30 Jul 2026]** Legal status: checked on primary source. On 30 July 2026 the government instructed the Work Environment Authority to analyse remote and hybrid work in relation to employment of people with disabilities and to write practical guidance that also sets out employers' responsibility for work environment, rehabilitation and reasonable adjustments in remote and hybrid work, with a report due 31 July 2028. The decision does not itself state how responsibility applies in the home ([Government decision A2026/00752, 30 Jul 2026](https://regeringen.se/contentassets/e571be55fa834076a0d4d41d2b804f7a/uppdrag-till-arbetsmiljoverket-att-ta-fram-vagledning-om-distans--och-hybridarbete.pdf)).

**[INFERENCE] [GAP]** The statute's scope and the 2021 ministerial answer point the same way: the systematic duties (risk assessment, measures, follow-up) cover remote work. How far the duty reaches inside a private home is not settled by either source. The Work Environment Authority's own remote-work guidance was not opened, so whether the employer may enter the home and how to risk-assess a home office remain unverified.

### Minimum compliance pack by size

**[INFERENCE]** Minimum work-environment pack for a company of 10 to 49 people: written policy; written routines; written task allocation; documented annual follow-up; written goals for the organisational and social work environment; written risk assessments and action plans; a victimisation routine known to all; managers trained in workload and victimisation; an occupational health service contract as needed; and a safety representative (from five employees). At 50, add the safety committee. This list is the researchers' synthesis of the duties above.

**[INFERENCE]** Three different counting rules meet at ten employees: the SAM documents (AFS 2023:1), the written goals for the organisational and social work environment (AFS 2023:2) and the pay-survey documentation (Discrimination Act). One annual review cycle can cover all three.

## 20. Employee data and monitoring

### Consent is weak in employment

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source (IMY); orientation only. Consent carries little weight as a legal basis in employment because of the dependency relationship, and the employer must be able to show lawful handling ([IMY, working life](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/dataskydd-pa-olika-omraden/arbetsliv/)).

### Monitoring

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. The steps IMY expects before monitoring are a specific purpose, a documented balancing of interests, clear information in advance, proportionality (group level before individual level), consideration of a data protection impact assessment for systematic email or web monitoring, and prevention such as site blocking before after-the-fact checks ([IMY, monitoring of employees](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/dataskydd-pa-olika-omraden/arbetsliv/kontroll-och-overvakning-av-anstallda/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Not allowed as a rule, per IMY: reading private files or email (with an exception for serious suspicion of disloyalty or crime), reusing data for an incompatible purpose, and monitoring that leaves staff feeling constantly watched. Security motives weigh more than efficiency motives ([IMY, monitoring of employees](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/dataskydd-pa-olika-omraden/arbetsliv/kontroll-och-overvakning-av-anstallda/)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 1 Apr 2025]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Since 1 April 2025 workplace camera use needs no IMY permit but does need a documented balancing of interests ([IMY, monitoring of employees](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/dataskydd-pa-olika-omraden/arbetsliv/kontroll-och-overvakning-av-anstallda/)).

### Offence data and background checks

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Private employers may process offence data only with support in law, to handle legal claims, to meet a legal duty, under IMY's regulation IMYFS 2024:1, or with an IMY permit. Consent never works as a basis ([IMY, offence data](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/dataskydd-pa-olika-omraden/arbetsliv/tillaten-behandling--vilka-krav-galler/brottsuppgifter/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. On background checks, asking a question or glancing at a paper criminal-record extract without recording falls outside GDPR. Scanning, noting or structuring the content falls inside it and is not permitted without specific legal support; even logging that an extract was shown is processing and is generally not a relevant data point. Sector laws for schools and work with children give explicit authority ([IMY, offence data](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/dataskydd-pa-olika-omraden/arbetsliv/tillaten-behandling--vilka-krav-galler/brottsuppgifter/)).

### Retention points found

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. Whistleblowing case data may not be kept longer than two years after the case closes ([Visselblåsarlagen 7 kap. 8 §](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2021890-om-skydd-for-personer-som-rapporterar_sfs-2021-890/)). Candidate data is deleted or anonymised when the recruitment ends, subject to the claim window ([IMY, recruitment systems](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/dataskydd-pa-olika-omraden/arbetsliv/rekryteringssystem-och-kompetensdatabaser/)).

**[GAP]** IMY guidance on GPS tracking, drug testing and social-media screening of candidates was cut off or not on the pages read. Whether Sweden has enacted a general law on employer background checks was not searched further.

## 21. AI in people decisions

### GDPR Article 22 applies today

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source (IMY's wording); orientation only. Candidates and employees have a right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effect. IMY's own example of what individuals have a right not to face is automatic rejection by a recruitment system with no human involvement ([IMY, recruitment systems](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/dataskydd-pa-olika-omraden/arbetsliv/rekryteringssystem-och-kompetensdatabaser/)).

### Employment uses of AI are high-risk under the AI Act

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul 2026]** Legal status: read on a non-official reproduction of the AI Act; orientation only. Annex III point 4 lists as high-risk AI intended for recruitment or selection (targeted job advertisements, filtering applications, evaluating candidates) and AI for decisions on terms, promotion or termination, for task allocation based on behaviour or traits, and for monitoring and evaluating performance ([AI Act Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)).

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul 2026]** Legal status: read on a non-official reproduction of the AI Act; orientation only. Deployer duties under Article 26 are to follow the provider's instructions; assign human oversight to natural persons with competence, training, authority and support; keep input data relevant; monitor and suspend on risk; keep logs at least six months; tell workers' representatives and affected workers before a high-risk system is used at work (26(7)); tell individuals when such a system makes or assists decisions about them (26(11)); and use provider information for the GDPR impact assessment ([AI Act Art. 26](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/)).

### The dates after the Digital Omnibus

**[FAST-MOVING, 27 Jul 2026]** Legal status: application dates read on the consolidated text (Article 113); the Omnibus chronology rests on secondary sources only; orientation only. The consolidated AI Act now gives 2 December 2027 for Annex III high-risk systems and 2 August 2028 for Annex I product systems ([AI Act Art. 26 page showing Art. 113](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/)). A law firm gives the chronology of the amending "Digital Omnibus": Commission proposal 19 November 2025, Parliament endorsement 16 June 2026, Council approval 29 June 2026, Official Journal 24 July 2026 and in force 27 July 2026 ([DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act)).

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul 2026]** Legal status: secondary source only. Unchanged and already applying: prohibited practices and the AI-literacy duty for staff operating AI since 2 February 2025, and general-purpose model duties since 2 August 2025. The marking of synthetic content under Article 50(2) moved to 2 December 2026 ([VerifyWise](https://verifywise.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-omnibus-what-changed)).

**[CONTESTED]** The date of the provisional political agreement on the Omnibus is 7 May 2026 in one secondary source and 27 May 2026 in another; the difference does not affect the application dates ([VerifyWise](https://verifywise.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-omnibus-what-changed); [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act)).

### What this means for the department's agents

**[INFERENCE]** The deferral to December 2027 does not relax GDPR: Article 22, impact-assessment duties and IMY's monitoring limits apply today to any AI screening or performance tool.

**[INFERENCE]** An AI agent that ranks or filters candidates, allocates tasks by personal traits, or scores staff performance is itself an Annex III point 4 system. From 2 December 2027 the company is a deployer and, if it built or substantially modified the agent, possibly a provider. An agent that only schedules or formats is unlikely to be high-risk; one that scores or ranks people is. Classify each agent task against Annex III point 4 and record the reasoning.

**[INFERENCE]** The Article 26(7) duty to inform workers lines up with MBL information duties, so one combined notice and consultation routine is sensible.

**[GAP]** Not read: Article 5(1)(f) on emotion recognition at the workplace and Article 6(3) on the exemption for narrow procedural tasks, including whether the Omnibus changed them or the Article 4 AI-literacy duty. Whether the Article 6(3) exemption would cover a summarising or scheduling agent needs legal review. The Omnibus regulation number, the Swedish market-surveillance authority for employment AI, guidance from DO or IMY on AI in recruitment, and evidence on measured bias in AI screening tools were not found.

## 22. Contractors, remote and international teams

### Contractor or employee

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: secondary source only (an employers' organisation guide, undated; no statute or case was read); orientation only. Classification in Sweden is an overall weighing of objective criteria, and no single one decides. Pointing toward employment: working under the client's direction on method, hours and place; a duty to do the work personally; an ongoing relationship with a continuous flow of new tasks; being unable in practice to work for others; using the client's equipment; fixed monthly pay and expense reimbursement; and A-tax. Pointing toward a genuine contractor: independence over method, time and place; a result that matters more than who does it; a time-limited, defined assignment; several clients; own equipment; a fee per unit or fixed price; and F-tax approval ([Företagarna, employ or engage](https://www.foretagarna.se/driva-eget-foretag/hrpersonalfragor/anstalla-eller-uppdrag/)).

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: secondary source only; orientation, not tax advice. The client should check that the contractor holds F-tax approval for the whole engagement; losing it mid-assignment points toward employment, and without it the client must withhold preliminary tax. If the relationship is reclassified as employment, employer contributions and withheld tax become due, a tax surcharge is possible and VAT may need correcting, and employment-law claims can include holiday pay, overtime pay and damages for dismissal without objective grounds ([Företagarna, employ or engage](https://www.foretagarna.se/driva-eget-foretag/hrpersonalfragor/anstalla-eller-uppdrag/)).

### Working time, flexible work and permits for distributed teams

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source; orientation only. The Working Hours Act excludes work the employer cannot supervise and staff who largely set their own hours, which often covers home-based roles; total-hours and rest rules otherwise apply to all work for an employer ([Arbetstidslagen 1 to 2 §§](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/arbetstidslag-1982673_sfs-1982-673/)). Parents of children under eight have a statutory right to request remote or flexible work and to a reasoned answer ([Föräldraledighetslagen 15 a §](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/foraldraledighetslag-1995584_sfs-1995-584/)).

### Foreign staff working briefly in Sweden

**[DURABLE]** Legal status: checked on primary source for the definition only; orientation, not tax advice. Under the economic-employer rule, the Swedish Tax Agency defines "shorter work" as work lasting at most 15 days in a row, to the extent total work does not exceed 45 days in a calendar year ([Skatteverket FAQ](https://skatteverket.se/privat/etjansterochblanketter/svarpavanligafragor/sink/sink/vadarettkortarearbete.5.5b35a6251761e6914206973.html)). Everything else about the rule (start date, who withholds tax, registration duties, group-internal work) is unverified.

### Hiring abroad

**[INFERENCE]** For a hire abroad, decide in this order: can the person be an employee of the Swedish company working abroad (which triggers foreign payroll registration, social security and a possible permanent establishment), a contractor (misclassification risk under local law), or employed through an employer-of-record (cost premium, limited control over intellectual property and termination). Take local advice before the offer.

**[GAP]** Almost nothing in the international area was verified. Unverified: employer-of-record legality and limits, permanent-establishment tests for home offices, A1 certificates and the EU framework agreement on cross-border telework, the special income tax for non-residents, and the primary sources on contractor status (the Tax Agency's F-tax pages and a 2022 Supreme Administrative Court case). The secondary guide mentions a 24-month rule for hired-in consultants without explaining it.

## 24. Guardrails for the department's AI agents

### The principle: agents prepare, humans decide

## Conclusion

**[INFERENCE]** The research changes the picture of the house style in one useful way: it is less a rival to operations standards than a plain-language front end to them. One constraint at a time is the Theory of Constraints; show, observe, release is standardized work with job instruction; written expectations are what Swedish work-environment rules already demand of every employer. The department can therefore run one loop (standard, experiment, new standard) under two vocabularies, and spend its design effort where the layers truly collide, which is almost entirely in how employment ends and how people are measured. There the house style's valuable residue is speed in starting a fair, documented process, and Swedish law's two-month rule makes that speed a legal necessity as well as a cultural preference.

**[INFERENCE]** Three things should be done before this knowledge base drives live decisions. A Swedish employment lawyer should confirm the points marked secondary or unverified, above all contractor classification, candidate-data retention, remote-work duties and anything international. The fast-moving items (pay transparency after the September 2026 election, AI Act dates, work-permit pay floors, base amounts, ITP premiums) need a dated re-check at least each January and each time a decision depends on them. And the largest house-style gaps, the gated roadmap document and Leila Hormozi's cadences and accountability dial, are worth one more research pass, because they are exactly the parts an operations manager agent would use weekly.
