# Rights gate media; plain structure scales it

As of 11 November 2026 the foundational knowledge for an AI media department is a three-layer stack: rights and disclosure rules that always win, evidence and official platform guidance that tune the craft, and Alex Hormozi's published content-production system as the default structure underneath both. The house style supplies the working skeleton: record long-form, cut it into many platform-native short pieces that each carry their own hook, build every piece as hook, retain, reward, open long-form with proof, promise and plan, state the outcome plainly in titles and thumbnails, move effort from post-production to pre-work, treat organic posts as cheap hook tests for ads, and judge content by revenue-linked signals; every number attached to that system (about 35,000 pieces, about $4 million, 7.8 million followers) is his own unaudited claim. Official platform guidance mostly agrees with it: YouTube's own title and thumbnail test picks winners on watch-time share and not click-through, YouTube warns that clickbait earns clicks with short viewing, and half of all channels sit between 2% and 10% impressions click-through rate. The rights layer is where an AI department can do real damage. Article 50 of the EU AI Act has applied since 2 August 2026 and requires a visible disclosure at first exposure for realistic synthetic image, audio or video; Swedish law forbids using a person's name or picture in marketing without consent; purely prompted AI output has no copyright according to both the US Copyright Office and Sweden's PRV; and of the vendor terms read, only OpenAI (API and Enterprise) and Google Cloud (generally available models) give an output indemnity, with Getty's being a vendor statement, while Midjourney, Suno, ElevenLabs, Runway, Luma and HeyGen put the infringement risk on the customer. The tool list is unstable: Google shut down the Veo 3.1 preview API on 22 October 2026 and Sora was reported discontinued during 2026, so prompts, seeds and references must be stored so that a shot can be regenerated elsewhere. Audience tests point one way: AI that viewers cannot see does little harm, while visibly artificial or fully AI-made ads scored lower than human-made ones (14% on short-term and 17% on long-term effectiveness measures in the Ipsos and Syracuse test of May 2026). The research behind this document ran out of search and fetch budget, so production workflow, graphic and brand design, photography, asset-management standards, official safe zones, stock, font and music licences and several vendor terms have little or no sourced coverage; this document says so in each place and in a consolidated gaps section instead of filling the holes from memory.

## 1. Overview: what the Media department is for and how its knowledge is layered

### The three-layer rule that governs every media decision

**[INFERENCE]** The Media department runs on three layers in strict order. Layer one is rights and disclosure: consent for any real person's likeness or voice, copyright and licence scope for every component, AI disclosure under EU AI Act Article 50 and platform rules, advertising identification under Swedish marketing law, and data protection (sections 17 to 22). Layer two is evidence and official platform guidance: platform help pages, standards bodies and audience research (sections 5 to 12 and 16). Layer three is the house style, Hormozi's published content-production structures, which fill every space the first two layers leave open (section 2). A worker agent picks the house-style move by default, checks whether official guidance or evidence changes it for this platform and format, and checks whether a rights or disclosure gate blocks it. Section 3 lists the known collisions and says which rule wins.

### What the department produces and the one mechanism that drives it

**[INFERENCE]** The department produces video, audio, images and design for the company and delivers them packaged for each platform. The mechanism the notes support best is a single pipeline: one long-form recording is planned heavily in advance, recorded, cut into many platform-native short pieces, published on a steady cadence, measured, and the winning hooks are handed to Marketing for paid tests. This is a synthesis of Hormozi's primary statements and YouTube's analytics guidance, not a design any single source states ([Hormozi, "1.2M Followers in 6 Months", auto-transcript, 2 Aug 2022](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/1-2m-followers-in-6-months-my-content-marketing-strategy-revealed); [YouTube Help, audience retention, read 11 Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)).

### Map the manager agent and worker agents onto roles

**[INFERENCE]** The manager agent maps to a head of media plus a rights and quality gatekeeper: it classifies each brief by risk (people, claims, paid use, minors, regulated sector), picks tools by rights position first, enforces the review gates, maintains the rights log and the winners register, and reviews the approved tool list monthly. Worker agents map to scriptwriter and researcher, editor and clipper, designer and thumbnail maker, audio engineer, captioner and accessibility checker, generative-media operator, and packaging and delivery operator. A second-hand, vendor-sourced description of Hormozi's own team lists comparable roles (a social media producer per brand, short-form editors, a writer, a videographer, a chief media officer and an AI and automation specialist), with a headcount estimated from social posts at 15 to 25 or more as of April 2026 ([Clipflow, undated, covers to Apr 2026](https://clipflow-ig-analysis.pages.dev/hormozi/)). The mapping onto agents is the researchers' synthesis.

### Compete on clarity, proof and rights hygiene, not on effects

**[INFERENCE]** Three independent lines of material point the same way. Hormozi reports, as his own unaudited observation from April 2024, that his best-performing pieces stressed clear language and his worst stressed production ([Hormozi, "How I Gained 7.8 Million Followers In 40 Months", auto-transcript, 27 Apr 2024](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/how-i-gained-7-8-million-followers-in-40-months-6-key-lessons)). YouTube's own test tool rewards the title and thumbnail that earn the most watch time, which favours accurate packaging ([YouTube Help, A/B test titles and thumbnails, read 11 Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)). Kantar's November 2025 analysis found that ads where generative AI was obvious did worse on branded cut-through ([Kantar, 4 Nov 2025](https://www.kantar.com/Inspiration/Advertising-Media/Rethinking-AI-generated-advertising)). The department should therefore spend its effort on the idea, the proof and the script, keep visuals functional, and treat rights and disclosure as part of quality.

### Measure against the company's own recent output first

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** YouTube compares a video's typical retention against the channel's own 10 latest videos of similar length, and advises judging click-through rate over the long term and by traffic source instead of reacting to small or early movements ([YouTube Help, audience retention, read 11 Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en); [YouTube Help, impressions and click-through rate, read 11 Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en)). **[INFERENCE]** Every benchmark in this document is a starting reference for a new team. Once the department has its own data, the manager agent compares each piece against the company's own trailing output by platform and format and uses external figures only as a sanity check.

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript] [FAST-MOVING, Aug 2022]** Hormozi describes five phases of content operations: post something; post on a regular cadence; post reliably on all platforms; push each platform to its maximum volume; then deliberately create and capture content with repurposing in mind. **[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript] [FAST-MOVING, Aug 2022]** His stated platform tolerances in 2022 were that short-form feeds can take 5 to 10 posts a day while newsfeed-style audiences tire at about 1 to 2 a day, and he said he does all of his marketing work on one day a week ([Hormozi, "1.2M Followers in 6 Months", auto-transcript, 2 Aug 2022](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/1-2m-followers-in-6-months-my-content-marketing-strategy-revealed)). ### Long-form recorded first, then cut into many short pieces

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript] [FAST-MOVING, Aug 2022]** Hormozi's workflow is that content is created, captured and then packaged: the same idea is repackaged per platform, so that a text post becomes a TikTok, a Short and a Reel. He says that beyond a few platforms one person cannot do this alone and leverage is required, first through vendors and then in-house ([Hormozi, "1.2M Followers in 6 Months", auto-transcript, 2 Aug 2022](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/1-2m-followers-in-6-months-my-content-marketing-strategy-revealed)).

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript] [FAST-MOVING, May 2024]** He says short and long pieces do different jobs. In his own data long-form drove most book sales, opt-ins and applications, while shorts are kept at a similar volume for top-of-funnel awareness and face recognition, which he says pays off later through retargeting ads. He also reports finding no evidence that shorts viewers convert into long-form viewers ([Hormozi, "I Posted ONLY Business Content for 30 Days", auto-transcript, May 2024](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/i-posted-only-business-content-for-30-days-strategy-revealed)). These are his own unaudited observations.

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript] [FAST-MOVING, Oct 2025]** In October 2025 he argued that 480 shorts at about 15 seconds each equal roughly two hours of long-form exposure, and said he planned more live, interactive formats ([Hormozi, "My Actual Social Media Strategy For 2026", auto-transcript, 18 Oct 2025](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/my-actual-social-media-strategy-for-2026)). ### The hook-retain-reward content unit

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand]** Summaries of *$100M Leads* describe a content unit with three parts: a hook that earns the first seconds, a retain section that keeps attention through lists, steps or stories, and a reward that delivers what the hook promised ([Business Book Club summary of $100M Leads](https://businessbookclub.substack.com/p/free-book-summary-100m-leads-by-alex); [ADV Launch summary of $100M Leads](https://advlaunch.us/blog/hormozi-100m-leads-summary-community-operators)). A community post attributes the unit to a Hormozi video titled "The Content Unit" ([Skool, Accelerator University community post](https://www.skool.com/acceleratoruniversity/what-three-parts-make-up-an-effective-content-unit)), and his company's free course lists two modules named "Mozi Media Content Method" parts I and II, whose lesson content was not readable from the landing page ([acquisition.com/training/leads](https://www.acquisition.com/training/leads)). The book text was not read, so the details rest on summaries.

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand]** The same summaries report four supporting rules: spend as much time on the headline as on the rest of the piece; length is not the problem and boredom is; give roughly three pieces of value for every ask; and a first-person account of what you did is more credible than generic instruction ([ADV Launch summary of $100M Leads](https://advlaunch.us/blog/hormozi-100m-leads-summary-community-operators); [Business Book Club summary of $100M Leads](https://businessbookclub.substack.com/p/free-book-summary-100m-leads-by-alex)). The point that boring content, not long content, loses viewers also appears in his own August 2022 video ([Hormozi, "1.2M Followers in 6 Months", auto-transcript, 2 Aug 2022](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/1-2m-followers-in-6-months-my-content-marketing-strategy-revealed)).

**[INFERENCE]** How agents use the unit: write the reward first (the single thing the viewer will be able to do or know), then the retain structure (list, steps or story), then several candidate hooks drawn from the strongest true element of the piece. A hook that the body does not pay off fails the unit and also fails the accuracy rules in sections 3 and 22.

### Proof-promise-plan intros

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript] [FAST-MOVING, Apr 2024]** After rewatching what he says were his top 35 educational videos, Hormozi reports a shared intro pattern: proof (why the viewer should trust the speaker), promise (what the viewer will get) and plan (how the video delivers it). The order can vary and he usually leads with proof. He adds that the presenter should be introduced in every piece, that the viewer should be told why to listen, and that inside references should be avoided so that nothing is assumed about what the viewer already knows ([Hormozi, "How I Gained 7.8 Million Followers In 40 Months", auto-transcript, 27 Apr 2024](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/how-i-gained-7-8-million-followers-in-40-months-6-key-lessons)). In a May 2025 video he says proof outperforms promise ([Hormozi, "14 Years of Marketing Advice in 35 Minutes", auto-transcript, May 2025](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/14-years-of-marketing-advice-in-35-minutes)).

**[INFERENCE]** How agents use the pattern: the first lines of a long-form script state one verifiable proof point the company can substantiate, one promise that matches the title, and a one-sentence plan. The proof must be true and documented, because Swedish marketing law requires statements to be true and not misleading (section 22).

### Message over effects, and pre-work over post-production

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript] [FAST-MOVING, Apr 2024]** In a 30-day review of his own Reels, Hormozi says 9 of his top 12 leaned on clear language while 8 of his bottom 12 leaned on effects and production. He recommends fewer vlog-style cuts and more lists, steps and stories, says visuals should clarify (for example charts showing scale or change over time) and not decorate, and says one of his best long-form videos is on-screen text for roughly 70 to 80% of its runtime ([Hormozi, "How I Gained 7.8 Million Followers In 40 Months", auto-transcript, 27 Apr 2024](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/how-i-gained-7-8-million-followers-in-40-months-6-key-lessons)). These are his own unaudited observations.

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript] [FAST-MOVING, Apr-May 2024]** He claims that shifting effort from post-production to pre-production cut his post-edit work by roughly 90 to 95% and allowed about four times the output, and that less post-production also let him publish more ([Hormozi, "How I Gained 7.8 Million Followers In 40 Months", auto-transcript, 27 Apr 2024](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/how-i-gained-7-8-million-followers-in-40-months-6-key-lessons); [Hormozi, "I Posted ONLY Business Content for 30 Days", auto-transcript, May 2024](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/i-posted-only-business-content-for-30-days-strategy-revealed)). He reports that slides versus no slides made little difference and that talking to camera performed about the same as a stage presentation ([Hormozi, "I Posted ONLY Business Content for 30 Days", auto-transcript, May 2024](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/i-posted-only-business-content-for-30-days-strategy-revealed)). In 2022 he said each platform needs its own packaging and that production does not need to be fancy ([Hormozi, "1.2M Followers in 6 Months", auto-transcript, 2 Aug 2022](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/1-2m-followers-in-6-months-my-content-marketing-strategy-revealed)), and in May 2025 he argued that plain-looking pages and ads convert better than elaborate ones ([Hormozi, "14 Years of Marketing Advice in 35 Minutes", auto-transcript, May 2025](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/14-years-of-marketing-advice-in-35-minutes)). **[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand] [VENDOR]** Third-party descriptions of the look, which do not come from Hormozi: plain, direct-to-camera delivery with captions, a whiteboard and credibility drawn from specific numbers ([OutlierKit, 10 Jul 2026](https://outlierkit.com/resources/alex-hormozi-growth-strategy/)); and a talking-head edit with burned-in word-by-word captions, jump cuts on breaths, a payoff-first hook in the first 3 seconds, a pattern interrupt every 5 to 10 seconds, a clean background, good light and a decent microphone ([thinkdmg agency blog, 10 Mar 2025, updated Oct 2026](https://thinkdmg.com/the-alex-hormozi-content-strategy-how-to-repurpose-one-video-into-30-pieces/)). ### Education-only positioning: the six shifts

### How a small SaaS team should scale the house style

**[INFERENCE]** Nine working rules follow from his primary statements. Every piece teaches one thing to the target customer. The title and thumbnail state the outcome plainly. Research and outlining are heavy before the shoot and the edit is light after. Long-form is recorded first and cut into many shorts, each with its own hook. Winners are logged and top hooks go to paid tests. Content is ranked by revenue-linked signals and not by views.

**[INFERENCE]** His volume figures come from a multi-million-dollar team and several presenter brands. ## 3. Precedence rules: rights and disclosure first, evidence and official platform guidance second, house style third

### The order of precedence

**[INFERENCE]** When rules collide, rights and disclosure rules win over everything; evidence and official platform guidance win over house style; house style governs only what is left. A rights or disclosure rule is any requirement in sections 17 to 22: consent, copyright and licence scope, AI Act Article 50, platform AI-label rules, advertising identification, data protection and vendor terms. An evidence rule is a finding from a platform's own documentation, a standards body or audience research. The house style is section 2. The manager agent records which layer decided each contested choice.

### Agents apply his structures without imitating him or using his name or likeness

**[INFERENCE]** The house style is a set of structures (content unit, intro pattern, clear packaging, pre-work, long-to-short cutting, hook testing). It is not a persona. Agents must not imitate Hormozi's voice, catchphrases, appearance, on-screen look or delivery; must not use his name, image, voice or a lookalike or soundalike in any company media, prompt, thumbnail, title or ad; must not present his numbers as benchmarks or as the company's own results; and must not cite him in published content as an endorser. Internal documents may name him as the source of a framework.

**[DURABLE]** The legal ground for this rule in Sweden was checked on a primary source: under Lag (1978:800) a trader may not use another person's name or picture in marketing without that person's consent, and a designation that clearly points to a specific person counts as a name ([Riksdagen, Lag (1978:800) om namn och bild i reklam](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1978800-om-namn-och-bild-i-reklam_sfs-1978-800/)). **[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, Nov 2026]** Platform and vendor rules point the same way: TikTok bans AI content showing public figures endorsing or being endorsed, and OpenAI and ElevenLabs prohibit reproducing a person's likeness or voice without consent ([TikTok Support, AI-generated content, read Nov 2026](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content); [OpenAI Service Terms, updated 16 Oct 2026](https://openai.com/policies/service-terms/); [ElevenLabs Terms, updated 9 Oct 2026](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)). ### A proposed stage-gate sequence

**[INFERENCE]** A sensible stage-gate for an AI media department is: brief; script or outline; storyboard or shot list; pre-production checklist; record; rough cut; review round one for content; fine cut; review round two for polish, captions and legal; master; platform versions; delivery and archive. The researchers describe this as standard industry practice but did not source it. Use it as the default and revise it when the Frame.io guide has been read.

### Put approval effort on the brief and the script

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript] [FAST-MOVING, Apr 2024]** Hormozi claims that moving effort from post-production to pre-production cut his post-edit work by roughly 90 to 95% and allowed about four times the output ([Hormozi, "How I Gained 7.8 Million Followers In 40 Months", auto-transcript, 27 Apr 2024](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/how-i-gained-7-8-million-followers-in-40-months-6-key-lessons)). This is his own unaudited claim. **[DURABLE]** NN/g's research-based guidance from 2014 points the same way: keep web videos as short and tight as possible, and cut content and tighten scripts ([NN/g, Video Usability, 16 Nov 2014](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/video-usability/)). **[INFERENCE]** Approval effort therefore belongs on the brief and the script, where changes are cheap, and post-production review rounds should be capped. Two consolidated rounds is a common default in agency contracts according to the researchers' unverified recall.

### What a brief from Marketing to Media should carry

**[INFERENCE]** A marketing-to-media brief should carry: the audience and its problem, the single promise of the piece, the proof available and where it is documented, the required call to action, the platform list and aspect ratios, the deadline, the named approver, the metric the piece will be judged on, and a risk classification (real people, claims, paid use, minors, regulated sector, AI-generated elements). This is the researchers' proposal; no briefing template was sourced.

### Web video basics that have held since 2014

**[DURABLE]** NN/g advises identifying the source and topic within about 5 seconds, because repeated intros add up for people watching several videos; not autoplaying; giving users start, stop, restart, mute and volume control; giving a clear next step at the end, such as a call to action or related links; showing video length next to the title; and choosing a representative thumbnail that reads at small sizes ([NN/g, Video Usability, 16 Nov 2014](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/video-usability/)). The guidance dates from 16 November 2014 and is older than short-form feeds, so apply it to video on the company's own website and product pages.

**[DURABLE]** NN/g also advises not relying on video alone: present essential information as text as well, with captions and a full transcript ([NN/g, Video Usability, 16 Nov 2014](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/video-usability/)). This matches the accessibility requirements in section 10.

### Recording practice

**[GAP]** No source was read on lighting, framing or audio capture. The following are unverified background recall and must be checked before they are used as standards: dialogue recording peaks around -12 to -6 dBFS, capture at 48 kHz and 24-bit, three-point lighting, and the eye line on the upper-third line. A second-hand agency description of a Hormozi-style setup mentions only a clean background, good light and a decent microphone ([thinkdmg agency blog, 10 Mar 2025, updated Oct 2026](https://thinkdmg.com/the-alex-hormozi-content-strategy-how-to-repurpose-one-video-into-30-pieces/)).

### The delivery master for YouTube

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** YouTube's recommended upload settings are an MP4 container with the moov atom at the front (fast start) and no edit lists; H.264 video, progressive scan, High Profile, 2 consecutive B-frames, a closed GOP at half the frame rate, CABAC and 4:2:0 chroma subsampling; AAC-LC or Opus audio at 48 kHz; upload at the recorded frame rate; and deinterlace before upload ([YouTube Help, recommended upload encoding settings, read 11 Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171?hl=en)). **[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** The house-style opening for long-form is proof, promise, plan, as described in section 2 ([Hormozi, "How I Gained 7.8 Million Followers In 40 Months", auto-transcript, 27 Apr 2024](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/how-i-gained-7-8-million-followers-in-40-months-6-key-lessons)). **[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** YouTube measures the intro as the share of viewers still watching after the first 30 seconds, and its own advice for a weak intro is to align the title and thumbnail with the content or to experiment with the first 30 seconds ([YouTube Help, audience retention, read 11 Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)). **[INFERENCE]** The two fit together: the title and thumbnail make a promise, and the first 30 seconds must visibly keep it.

### Reading the YouTube retention report

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** YouTube's audience retention report is per video and data takes 1 to 2 days to process. "Top moments" are parts where almost nobody left; if they come late, YouTube advises moving strong material earlier. "Spikes" are parts that were rewatched or shared, which can signal interest or confusion. "Dips" are parts that were skipped or abandoned. A gradual decline is normal. Typical retention is compared against the channel's 10 latest videos of similar length. Key moments show only for videos at least 60 seconds long with at least 100 views ([YouTube Help, audience retention, read 11 Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)).

**[INFERENCE]** Use the long-form retention graph as the clip-selection tool: spikes and rewatch points become short-form candidates, and dips mark material to cut from the next script. This matches the outside reading that Hormozi's channel treats long-form retention spikes as pre-validated clip moments ([OutlierKit, 10 Jul 2026](https://outlierkit.com/resources/alex-hormozi-growth-strategy/)).

### Video chapters

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** Manual chapters on YouTube are entered in the description. The first timestamp must be 00:00, there must be at least three timestamps in ascending order, and each chapter must last at least 10 seconds. Manual chapters override automatic chapters. Automatic chapters are allowed by default on new uploads and can be switched off per video or in upload defaults; not every video is eligible. Chapters are unavailable if the channel has active strikes or the content may be inappropriate for some viewers ([YouTube Help, video chapters, read 11 Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579?hl=en)).

**[INFERENCE]** Chapter checklist for the packaging agent: the description starts chapters at 00:00; three or more entries; none shorter than 10 seconds; ascending order; and a check after publishing that the chapters render.

## 8. Loudness and audio standards

### How loudness normalisation works and why louder gains nothing

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** Spotify adjusts tracks to -14 LUFS (measured per ITU 1770) at playback and does not change the uploaded file. Louder masters are turned down. Quieter masters are turned up only as far as headroom allows, leaving 1 dB: Spotify's example is a -20 LUFS track with a -5 dBFS true peak that is raised only to -16 LUFS. Premium listeners can choose Loud at -11 LUFS (with a limiter), Normal at -14 or Quiet at -19 ([Spotify for Artists, loudness normalization, undated, read 11 Nov 2026](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/loudness-normalization/)). The page is undated and addresses music delivery; it does not state a target for podcasts. **[INFERENCE]** A master louder than the platform target gains nothing because it is simply turned down, so mixing for dynamics is safe.

### Apple Podcasts loudness

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** Apple Podcasts specifies a target of about -16 LKFS with a tolerance of ±1 dB and a true peak no higher than -1 dBFS, measured per ITU-R BS.1770-5, and says loudness processing should be applied before encoding because encoding can cause clipping ([Apple Podcasts for Creators, audio requirements, read 11 Nov 2026](https://podcasters.apple.com/support/893-audio-requirements)).

### EBU R 128 for broadcast

**[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, Nov 2023]** EBU R 128, first issued in 2010 and at version 5 since November 2023, sets a target programme loudness of -23.0 LUFS, with a tolerance of ±1.0 LU where exact normalisation is impractical (for example live programmes) and a measurement tolerance of ±0.2 LU in quality-control workflows. The maximum permitted true peak in production is -1 dBTP. Measurement follows ITU-R BS.1770 with gating; metering follows EBU Tech 3341; loudness range follows Tech 3342 and is not recommended for programmes under one minute. Supplement s1 covers short-form content such as adverts and promos, s2 covers normalisation for streaming, s3 radio and s4 cinematic content ([EBU R 128, version 5, Nov 2023](https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/r/r128.pdf)). The s2 streaming supplement was not read.

### Mono and stereo measurement

**[FAST-MOVING, 9 Oct 2026]** The trade publication Podnews notes that a mono file at -19 LUFS sounds as loud as a stereo file at -16 LUFS and that stereo-oriented meters can misreport mono. It also points out that -23 LUFS (Europe) and -24 LUFS (United States) are broadcast standards and not podcast targets ([Podnews, page modified 9 Oct 2026](https://podnews.net/article/lufs-lkfs-for-podcasters)).

### Proposed house targets

**[INFERENCE] [CONTESTED]** The two research passes propose slightly different defaults, and both are inference. For podcasts, a single master at -16 LUFS integrated and -1 dBTP meets Apple's published target and sits within 2 dB of the commonly quoted -14 figure. For music-led and social masters, -14 LUFS integrated and -1 dBTP matches Spotify's published normalisation level. Deliver at -23 LUFS and -1 dBTP only to European broadcasters. ### Web image formats

**[DURABLE]** AVIF is based on the AV1 codec, supports transparency and animation, and testing cited by web.dev from Netflix, Cloudinary and the Chrome codecs team shows significant file-size reductions against JPEG or WebP. Browser support arrived in Chrome and Opera in 2020, Firefox in 2021 and Safari in 2022. ### Alt text

**[DURABLE]** The W3C alt decision tree sets what alt text an image needs. An image containing text that is not available elsewhere gets that text as its alt. An image inside a link or button gets a description of the destination or action. A meaningful simple image gets a brief description of its meaning. A graph or complex image has its information given elsewhere on the page. A decorative or redundant image gets an empty alt ([W3C WAI, alt decision tree](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/decision-tree/)). **[INFERENCE]** For charts and screenshots on marketing pages, the alt text states the point of the image and the page carries the data in text.

### Swedish moral rights limit how licensed images may be edited or placed

**[DURABLE]** Checked on a primary source: under 3 § of the Swedish Copyright Act the author must be named as good practice requires, and a work may not be changed, or made available in a form or context, that offends the author's reputation or individuality; the right can be waived only to a limited extent ([Riksdagen, upphovsrättslagen SFS 1960:729, read 11 Nov 2026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1960729-om-upphovsratt-till-litterara-och_sfs-1960-729/)). Under 49 a § whoever made a photographic picture has exclusive rights to copy it and make it available for 50 years after the year it was made, whether or not it is an original work ([Riksdagen, upphovsrättslagen SFS 1960:729, read 11 Nov 2026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1960729-om-upphovsratt-till-litterara-och_sfs-1960-729/)). This is orientation and not legal advice.

**[INFERENCE]** Three design rules follow for a Sweden-based company. Templates should carry a photographer or illustrator credit field by default, because naming is a statutory right and not a courtesy. Heavy retouching, AI restyling or use of a stock photo in a sensitive context can breach moral rights even when the economic licence permits edits, so check the licence and, for commissioned work, get a written and specific waiver. Every photo found online, including plain snapshots and screenshots of photos, should be treated as protected for at least 50 years from creation.

### Subtitle quality benchmarks

**[DURABLE]** The Netflix English Timed Text Style Guide is an industry standard and not law. It sets 42 characters per line and a maximum of 2 lines; a reading speed of up to 20 characters per second for adult programmes and 17 for children's; line breaks after punctuation and before conjunctions or prepositions; and no splitting of an article from its noun, a first name from a last name, or a verb from its subject pronoun. For subtitles for the deaf and hard of hearing, speaker IDs and sound effects go in square brackets in lowercase, speakers are identified only when not visually obvious, and plot-relevant sounds are described ([Netflix English Timed Text Style Guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-Timed-Text-Style-Guide)).

**[GAP]** No sourced caption accuracy target, such as 99%, was found. The BBC Subtitle Guidelines and the W3C captions and audio-description pages could not be read.

### Proposed house standard for every published video

**[INFERENCE]** Every published video gets human-reviewed closed captions as .srt and .vtt with speaker IDs and meaningful sounds, a transcript on the hosting page, and a script written so that the narration says what the visuals show, which reduces the need for a separate audio-description track. Burned-in social captions help sound-off viewers but do not replace a closed-caption file, because users cannot resize, restyle or translate them.

### The European Accessibility Act: what the directive covers

**[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** Checked on a primary source: Directive (EU) 2019/882 applies to listed services provided to consumers after 28 June 2025, including services providing access to audiovisual media services and e-commerce services (Article 2(2)). Services providing access to audiovisual media services are services transmitted by electronic communications networks that are used to identify, select, receive information on and view audiovisual media services (Article 3(6)). E-commerce services are provided at a distance through websites and mobile services, at a consumer's individual request, to conclude a consumer contract (Article 3(30)) ([EUR-Lex, Directive (EU) 2019/882, read 11 Nov 2026](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0882)). This is orientation and not legal advice.

**[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** Checked on a primary source: the directive excludes pre-recorded time-based media published before 28 June 2025, third-party content that the operator neither funds, develops nor controls, and archives not updated after 28 June 2025 (Article 2(4)). Microenterprises providing services are exempt (Article 4(5)). Article 14 covers fundamental alteration and disproportionate burden, and Article 32 gives a transitional period to 28 June 2030. Under Annex I, websites, online applications and mobile apps of covered services must be perceivable, operable, understandable and robust, and access services for audiovisual media must provide accessible electronic programme guides and fully transmit subtitles for the deaf and hard of hearing, audio description, spoken subtitles and sign language, synchronised and user-controllable ([EUR-Lex, Directive (EU) 2019/882, read 11 Nov 2026](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0882)).

### The Swedish accessibility act

**[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** Checked on a primary source: Sweden's Lag (2023:254) on the accessibility of certain products and services has been in force since 28 June 2025. Covered consumer services (4 §) include electronic communications, services giving access to audiovisual media services, parts of passenger transport, consumer banking, e-books and e-commerce services. Third-party content on websites and apps that the operator neither funds, develops nor controls is exempt (5 §). No requirement applies where it would fundamentally alter the service or impose a disproportionate burden (7 §), but an operator that invokes an exemption must document the assessment with reasons, keep it and notify the supervisory authority (8 §). Microenterprises, meaning fewer than ten employees and a turnover or balance sheet total not above EUR 2 million, need not meet the requirements for services (10 §) ([Riksdagen, Lag (2023:254), read 11 Nov 2026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2023254-om-vissa-produkters-och-tjansters_sfs-2023-254/)). This is orientation and not legal advice.

**[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** Checked on a primary source: under the Swedish act's transitional rules, the act does not apply to pre-recorded time-based media or archived content published before entry into force (archives only if not updated since), and contracts made before then may run until expiry, at the latest 27 June 2030. Enforcement is through corrective orders, conditional fines and sanction fees of SEK 10,000 to SEK 10,000,000 (37 to 40 §§). Supervisory authorities are designated by the government (30 §) and the act itself does not name them. The definitions in 2 § are amended by Lag (2026:1026) with effect from 20 November 2026; the content of that amendment was not analysed ([Riksdagen, Lag (2023:254), read 11 Nov 2026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2023254-om-vissa-produkters-och-tjansters_sfs-2023-254/)).

### Does the act reach a SaaS company's video?

**[INFERENCE]** This is the researchers' reading and not legal advice. The directive and the Swedish act regulate listed consumer services. ## 12. Measuring media performance and creative testing loops

### YouTube's native title and thumbnail test

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** YouTube Studio's A/B test for titles and thumbnails runs on desktop only, requires advanced features, and compares up to 3 titles, thumbnails or both. The winner is decided by watch-time share and not by click-through rate, and YouTube says titles and thumbnails should set accurate expectations. Results are reported as a winner (statistically significant), performed the same, or inconclusive, in which case the first uploaded option becomes the default. Tests can take a few days and should finish within two weeks. A small control group is excluded from the calculation, and low impressions make a clear result less likely ([YouTube Help, A/B test titles and thumbnails, read 11 Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)).

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** Shorts and scheduled live streams cannot be tested; made-for-kids, mature and private videos are excluded; and editing the title or thumbnail during a test stops the test. YouTube's own tips are to test older videos first, to make variants meaningfully different, and to upload at high resolution, because if any test thumbnail is below 1280×720 all of them are downscaled to 854×480 ([YouTube Help, A/B test titles and thumbnails, read 11 Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)).

**[INFERENCE]** Because YouTube optimises its own test for watch-time share, a thumbnail that raises click-through but disappoints viewers will lose. Brief thumbnails and titles as a promise that the first 30 seconds must visibly keep. YouTube's help text advises against reacting to small CTR movements, and traffic mix confounds manual swaps, so use the native test and not hand-swapping when a test is wanted.

### Impressions click-through rate on YouTube

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** YouTube states that half of all channels and videos have an impressions click-through rate between 2% and 10%. New videos and channels, and videos under 100 views, show a wider range. Videos with many impressions, for example on Home, naturally show lower CTR, and videos seen mostly from the channel page show higher. YouTube warns that clickbait tends to produce high CTR with low average view duration and fewer recommendations, and advises judging CTR over the long term and by traffic source ([YouTube Help, impressions and click-through rate, read 11 Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en)).

**[INFERENCE]** Treat the 30-second intro retention measure and the 2 to 10% CTR band as orientation only. Compare a video against the channel's own recent videos and not against a fixed target.

### Judge by business outcomes, with paired metrics

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** Hormozi's published approach is to judge content by downstream business outcomes and to pair metrics, as described in section 2 ([Hormozi, "How I Gained 7.8 Million Followers In 40 Months", auto-transcript, 27 Apr 2024](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/how-i-gained-7-8-million-followers-in-40-months-6-key-lessons); [Hormozi, "I Posted ONLY Business Content for 30 Days", auto-transcript, May 2024](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/i-posted-only-business-content-for-30-days-strategy-revealed)). **[INFERENCE]** For a SaaS company the equivalent of his revenue-per-thousand-views metric is a paired metric such as qualified sign-ups or demo requests per 1,000 views, reported next to retention. Views alone should not rank content.

**[INFERENCE]** The proposed loop is: Media ships variants; platform data is read at 48 hours and at 7 days; winners are logged in the register; Marketing turns the top organic hooks into paid tests against a control; results feed the next brief. The 48-hour and 7-day checkpoints are the researchers' proposal. ## 15. Generative media craft and quality control

### Prompting video models: what official guidance says

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read Nov 2026]** Google's Gemini Omni Flash documentation is the only official prompt guidance the research could read. It says prompts work best when they describe the scene, camera movement, lighting and mood; that wanted audio and music should be described explicitly; that a single continuous shot or no scene cuts should be requested when one take is wanted; that timing can be controlled with natural language or timecodes such as `[0-3s]`; that exact on-screen text should be put in quotes; and that negatives must be stated in the prompt text, such as "No dialogue", because there is no negative-prompt field ([Google, Gemini Omni docs, read Nov 2026](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)).

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read Nov 2026]** For image-to-video, the same documentation says higher-resolution source images and specific motion descriptions work better; that two images are used for first and last frame interpolation; that media is bound to roles with tags (`<FIRST_FRAME>`, `<LAST_FRAME>`, `<IMAGE_REF_N>`, `<VIDEO_REF_N>`); that edits work best with simple prompts plus an instruction to keep everything else the same; and that extension continues from the last 10 seconds and cannot prepend ([Google, Gemini Omni docs, read Nov 2026](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)). The end of the guide was truncated when read.

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read Nov 2026]** OpenAI's image documentation says the higher complexity setting helps with dense text, complex layouts and object relationships but can add latency and unwanted changes, so results should be compared per task ([OpenAI image generation guide, read Nov 2026](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)).

### A prompt template for video

**[INFERENCE]** A video prompt template built on Google's guidance: subject and action; setting; camera (shot size, movement, lens feel); lighting; mood and style; audio (ambience, music, dialogue in quotes); timing cues; exclusions; and "single continuous shot" if needed. Write one shot per prompt. Consistency comes from reference images and first and last frames, not from longer prompts.

### A storyboard-first workflow

**[INFERENCE]** The proposed workflow has six steps. Write a script and shot list with a duration per shot, planning shots of 3 to 10 seconds. Generate or shoot key stills per shot. Lock a character and product reference sheet (front, three-quarter and profile views; product pack shots from the brand's own photography). Run image-to-video from the approved still, using first and last frames for controlled moves. Extend only when needed. Edit, grade, add sound and titles in an editor, adding the real logo and text as graphics instead of generating them. This is the researchers' synthesis and is consistent with the house-style preference for pre-work over post-production.

### Consistency across shots

**[FAST-MOVING, 5 Nov 2026]** A trade guide reports that Kling supports multi-shot storyboards of up to 6 shots, that Seedance 2.0 keeps character identity across multi-shot sequences, that Runway is rated strongest for character and location consistency, and that identity drift still occurs on longer sequences ([Pixflow, last modified 5 Nov 2026](https://pixflow.net/blog/best-ai-video-generator/)).

### Product accuracy

**[INFERENCE]** Never let a model invent the product. Use real product photography as the reference or composite the real product in post, and compare every frame against the approved pack shot for shape, label text, colour, ports, quantities and claims. **[FAST-MOVING, 2025-2026]** The reason is legal as well as aesthetic: according to a Swedish law firm's May 2026 briefing, the advertiser is responsible for false AI-generated content, as shown by a Reklamombudsmannen matter decided 13 August 2025 and a Patent and Market Court judgment of 1 April 2026 on a chatbot's false statement (secondary source only; [CMS Sweden, 28 May 2026](https://cms.law/en/swe/legal-updates/ai-in-advertising-key-aspects-for-advertisers-in-sweden)).

### Voice, text-to-speech and dubbing

**[INFERENCE]** For synthetic voice, write for the ear, mark pauses and emphasis, spell out numbers and brand pronunciations, generate in short blocks and keep a pronunciation list. Clone only voices with recorded written consent (section 20). For dubbing, have a native speaker review the translation and the lip-sync, and check names, units and legal disclaimers; treat a dubbed real person as a manipulated likeness that needs that person's consent and, in the EU, a deepfake-labelling assessment (section 17). **[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, 9 Oct 2026]** ElevenLabs' terms require the user to review output before use ([ElevenLabs Terms, updated 9 Oct 2026](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)).

### Mixing AI and filmed footage

**[INFERENCE]** Match frame rate, grain, lens and colour; keep AI shots short; and record in the rights log which shots are synthetic, because the labelling duty attaches to the published whole when a realistic synthetic element could be taken as authentic.

### AI tends to skip storytelling, so humans direct the idea

**[FAST-MOVING, 18 May 2026]** In the Ipsos and Syracuse Newhouse test, where one AI model wrote the brief and shot list and another generated the video, the AI chose storytelling structures in only 30% of ads against 49% in Ipsos' wider ad database, and some AI ads needed several iterations ([Ipsos, 18 May 2026](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/ai-ads-are-good-enough-and-thats-problem)). **[INFERENCE]** Keep human creative direction on the idea and the story, and use the house-style content unit to force a structure onto generated pieces.

### The QC checklist before human approval

## 16. Audience response to AI-generated media

### The pattern across the tests

**[INFERENCE]** The best available tests point the same way: AI use that viewers cannot see does little harm, but visibly artificial or fully AI-made ads underperform human-made ones on effectiveness and emotion, and a large share of consumers say they want disclosure. No study found measured the effect of a disclosure label itself.

### Kantar: seamless AI does well, obvious AI does worse

**[FAST-MOVING, 4 Nov 2025]** Kantar's analysis of ads using generative AI in its LINK database, using a survey plus facial coding and published 4 November 2025, found that over 40% of ads with seamless generative AI landed in the top tier for branded cut-through, while ads with obvious AI, often with distracting or unnatural visuals, did worse. AI ads drew stronger reactions but lower net positivity, and branding was lower on average mainly where AI was obvious. From Kantar's Media Reactions 2024 survey, 41% of consumers against 29% of marketers say AI-generated ads bother them ([Kantar, 4 Nov 2025](https://www.kantar.com/Inspiration/Advertising-Media/Rethinking-AI-generated-advertising)). Kantar sells ad testing, so its commercial interest should be kept in mind.

### Ipsos and Syracuse: human-made ads were stronger

**[FAST-MOVING, 18 May 2026]** Ipsos and Syracuse University's Newhouse School tested 20 ads in 10 brand pairs with 3,000 US consumers, with the AI versions made end to end by AI models. Human-made ads were 14% stronger on short-term and 17% stronger on long-term effectiveness measures. Viewers rated human ads more entertaining, unique and imaginative without knowing which were AI; 40% of viewers were unsure either way; and 79% said companies using AI should have to disclose it ([Ipsos, 18 May 2026](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/ai-ads-are-good-enough-and-thats-problem)). Syracuse's account adds that human ads over-indexed by 11 points against benchmark while AI ads under-indexed by 5, that the brands tested were large US consumer brands, and that the human originals predate 2021 ([Syracuse University, 18 May 2026](https://news.syr.edu/2026/05/18/newhouse-research-finds-ai-ads-fall-short-on-sales-impact/)).

**[CONTESTED] [FAST-MOVING, 18 May 2026]** The two accounts of the same study differ on detection. Ipsos' page was read as saying 25% of AI-ad viewers were at least somewhat confident the ad was AI-made; Syracuse's page says 13%, and that 13% also suspected the human ads. Both agree on 40% uncertain. Check the original report before quoting either figure ([Ipsos, 18 May 2026](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/ai-ads-are-good-enough-and-thats-problem); [Syracuse University, 18 May 2026](https://news.syr.edu/2026/05/18/newhouse-research-finds-ai-ads-fall-short-on-sales-impact/)).

### How platforms treat disclosed and undisclosed AI content

**[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read Nov 2026]** YouTube says disclosure alone does not limit a video's audience or its monetisation eligibility, while repeated non-disclosure can lead to forced labels, removal or suspension from the Partner Programme ([YouTube Help, disclosing altered or synthetic content, read Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). TikTok says unlabelled realistic AI content may be removed and that labelling real content as AI is also a violation ([TikTok Support, AI-generated content, read Nov 2026](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content)). Meta says AI content is generally left up and labelled and not removed, while fact-checked false or altered content is demoted and ads with debunked content are rejected ([Meta Newsroom, labelling AI content, post last modified 23 Oct 2025](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/); post last modified 23 October 2025).

### Practical reading for the department

**[INFERENCE]** Use AI where it is invisible or clearly stylised, keep human creative direction and storytelling, and pre-test fully synthetic hero ads against a human-made control before spending media budget. Since most viewers cannot tell and many want disclosure, honest labelling is unlikely to be the main driver of weaker results; visible artefacts and weak ideas are. This is an inference, because no cited study isolated the label effect.

## 17. Disclosure and labelling

### Article 50 in one paragraph

**[FAST-MOVING, since 2 Aug 2026]** Article 50 of the EU AI Act has applied since 2 August 2026, which the European Commission's own pages confirm (checked on a primary source; [European Commission, Code of Practice page, updated 19 Oct 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content); [European Commission, guidelines page, updated 6 Aug 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-transparency-ai-generated-content)). A company that publishes realistic AI-generated or AI-manipulated image, audio or video that could be taken as authentic must disclose that clearly at first exposure, with a lighter duty for evidently artistic, creative, satirical or fictional work. The wording of Article 50 in this document comes from a reference site's consolidated text and law-firm notes and not from EUR-Lex (secondary source only; [Article 50, artificialintelligenceact.eu reference text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)). This is orientation and not legal advice; verify at [EUR-Lex, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, unread](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) or [AI Act Service Desk, Article 50, unread](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50).

### The deployer's duty for deepfakes

**[FAST-MOVING, since 2 Aug 2026]** Secondary source only: Article 50(4) requires deployers to disclose that image, audio or video content constituting a deepfake has been artificially generated or manipulated. Where the content is part of an evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous work, the duty is limited to disclosing the existence of such content in a way that does not hamper display or enjoyment of the work ([Article 50, artificialintelligenceact.eu reference text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)). Checked on a primary source: the Commission's icon page summarises a deepfake as AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video resembling real people, objects, places, entities or events that could falsely appear authentic ([European Commission, EU icons page, updated 13 Oct 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-icons-labelling-ai-generated-content)).

### The text rule and why ordinary marketing text is generally outside it

**[FAST-MOVING, since 2 Aug 2026]** Secondary source only: Article 50(4) also requires deployers to disclose AI-generated or manipulated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest, unless it has undergone human review or editorial control and a natural or legal person holds editorial responsibility ([Article 50, artificialintelligenceact.eu reference text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)). A Swedish law firm reads the text rule as not covering ordinary marketing, and notes that the AI literacy duty in Article 4 has applied to deployers since 2 February 2025 ([CMS Sweden, 28 May 2026](https://cms.law/en/swe/legal-updates/ai-in-advertising-key-aspects-for-advertisers-in-sweden)). The guidelines, as summarised by a law firm, set the human-review bar as a named person with relevant expertise who can approve, change or reject, and say a skim read is not enough ([Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/102nfqm/you-talkin-to-me-operationalising-the-eu-ai-acts-transparency-obligations)).

### Provider marking and the form of disclosure

**[FAST-MOVING, since 2 Aug 2026]** Secondary source only: Article 50(2) requires providers of generative systems to mark outputs in a machine-readable, detectable way, with exemptions for systems that perform an assistive function for standard editing or do not substantially alter the input or its semantics. Article 50(5) requires information to be clear, distinguishable, given at the latest at first interaction or exposure, and accessible ([Article 50, artificialintelligenceact.eu reference text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)).

### Timing changes from the Digital Omnibus

**[FAST-MOVING, 27 Jul 2026]** Secondary source only: law firms report that the AI Omnibus, cited as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, entered into force on 27 July 2026. It gives providers of generative systems already on the market before 2 August 2026 until 2 December 2026 to meet the Article 50(2) marking duty. Article 50(1), (3) and (4) were not delayed. Content published before 2 August 2026 need not be labelled retroactively ([Goodwin, Aug 2026](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/102nfqm/you-talkin-to-me-operationalising-the-eu-ai-acts-transparency-obligations); [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content)). The Omnibus also bans AI nudification apps and systems generating child sexual abuse material from 2 December 2026, with fines for the nudification ban reported at up to EUR 35 million or 7% of turnover ([Goodwin, Aug 2026](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force)).

### Penalties

**[FAST-MOVING, Aug 2026]** Secondary source only: breaches of Article 50 carry penalties of up to EUR 15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, enforced by national market surveillance authorities ([Goodwin, Aug 2026](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force)). **[GAP]** Which Swedish authority supervises Article 50, and any Swedish supplementary law, was not found.

### The Code of Practice and the guidelines

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** Checked on a primary source: the Commission's Code of Practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content went through a first draft on 17 December 2025 and a second on 3 March 2026, and the final Code was published on 10 June 2026. It had about 190 signatories by the end of July 2026. The Commission and the AI Board confirmed it as an adequate voluntary tool, and non-signatories must show that their own measures are adequate ([European Commission, Code of Practice page, updated 19 Oct 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content)). Secondary source only: the final guidelines on Article 50 were published on 20 July 2026 and are non-binding ([Goodwin, Aug 2026](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force); [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** Secondary source only: the deployer section of the Code, as summarised by a law firm, says labels must be noticeable without user interaction, visible long enough, embedded in the content where possible and perceivable at first exposure; that audible disclaimers are used where no visual is possible; that deployers need proportionate internal processes, staff awareness and feedback routes; and that reliance on the editorial exemption needs documented workflows ([Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** Secondary source only: the provider section, as summarised, calls for at least two machine-readable marking techniques such as signed metadata plus an imperceptible watermark, for users to be barred from stripping metadata, for a free detection mechanism, for watermark-detection interoperability by 2 February 2027, and exempts text under about 150 words from watermarking ([Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content); [Jones Day, June 2026](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/european-commission-publishes-final-code-of-practice-on-marking-and-labelling-aigenerated-content)).

### The EU icons

**[FAST-MOVING, 13 Oct 2026]** Checked on a primary source: the Commission provides three free icons in SVG and PNG, with no attribution needed and optional use: a basic icon meaning AI was involved, to be paired with a text label or an interactive second layer; a "fully AI-generated" icon; and a "partially AI-modified" icon. There are four colour variants, and user testing found that the basic icon works better with a text label ([European Commission, EU icons page, updated 13 Oct 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-icons-labelling-ai-generated-content)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 13 Oct 2026]** Checked on a primary source: the Commission's placement summary says the icon must be perceivable at first view, not obstructed, embedded in the content unless an equivalent such as an interface overlay is used (creative works are exempt from direct embedding), and still visible when the content is reshared or downloaded. Accessibility advice includes alt text and plain wording. Using the icon does not by itself prove compliance. The Commission's examples of "partially AI-modified" include a real photo of an empty flat furnished with AI ([European Commission, EU icons page, updated 13 Oct 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-icons-labelling-ai-generated-content)).

### A labelling decision sequence for agents

**[INFERENCE]** The researchers propose five questions in order. First, does the asset contain AI-generated or AI-manipulated image, audio or video? If not, stop, but check platform rules for edits. Second, could a reasonable viewer take it as a real person, place, object or event? If so, treat it as a deepfake: a visible label at first exposure, embedded in the asset, plus the platform toggle, using "fully AI-generated" or "partially AI-modified" wording or icon. Third, is it evidently fictional, artistic or stylised, such as a cartoon or obvious fantasy? A discreet disclosure such as a credit line or caption is likely enough under the artistic limb, and platform labels are still set where the platform asks. Fourth, is it only standard editing such as colour, crop, noise removal or upscaling? No Article 50 label is needed; keep the metadata. Fifth, for voice: a cloned or synthetic voice that sounds like a real identifiable person needs consent plus disclosure, while a generic synthetic narrator is lower risk but should be disclosed in audio-only formats when realistic.

**[INFERENCE]** Realistic AI "photography" in ads, such as synthetic models, synthetic product-in-use scenes and AI-furnished rooms, falls within the Commission's own deepfake examples, so marketing teams should assume a label is required. This is an inference from the icon-page examples; the guidelines' text on advertising was not read.

## 18. Copyright in AI output, vendor terms and indemnities

### Purely prompted output is not protected

**[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, 29 Jan 2025]** Checked on a primary source: the US Copyright Office's Part 2 report of 29 January 2025 concludes that prompts alone do not give enough control to make the user an author; that using AI as an assistive tool does not remove protection; that human-authored input that stays perceptible in the output is protected; that creative selection, arrangement or modification of AI material can be protected; and that no new legislation is needed ([US Copyright Office, Part 2 report, 29 Jan 2025](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)). The Office's series also includes Part 1 on digital replicas (31 July 2024), which recommends a federal digital-replica law, and Part 3 on training, released only as a pre-publication version on 9 May 2025 ([US Copyright Office AI page](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/)).

**[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, 22 Nov 2024]** Checked on a primary source: Sweden's Intellectual Property Office, PRV, takes the position that an image created wholly by an AI tool from a text instruction has no copyright because the creator must be a human. Copyright may arise where a human made enough free, creative choices in an AI-assisted work, and a court decides. The user is responsible for not infringing others' works even when the image came from an AI tool, and copying a known character into an AI image is not allowed. PRV says claiming copyright in a fully AI-generated image may amount to fraud, says the lawfulness of training on internet material is unresolved, and advises reading the tool's terms because they decide what you may do with the image ([PRV, copyright on AI images, updated 22 Nov 2024](https://www.prv.se/sv/upphovsratt/for-anvandare/upphovsratt-pa-ai-bilder/)). The page was last updated 22 November 2024 and predates 2025 and 2026 case law. This is orientation and not legal advice.

**[INFERENCE]** A company should assume it cannot stop others copying raw AI output and should add documented human authorship. To strengthen ownership of AI-assisted work, keep evidence of human contribution (sketches, own photography used as input, edit history, selection and arrangement decisions) in the rights log. Never claim copyright in a fully AI-generated asset.

### Infringement risk sits with whoever publishes the output

**[FAST-MOVING, 31 Jul 2026]** Secondary source only: in GEMA v Suno (Landgericht München I, case 42 O 763/25, 31 July 2026, not final) the court held that training copies obtained by stream-ripping were not covered by an exception; that the trained models contained the works in reproducible form, which was a separate reproduction; that outputs with recognisable protected musical elements infringed even though they were not identical copies, and were attributed to Suno and not the user on simple open prompts; and that the German text-and-data-mining exception did not cover permanent storage in model parameters. Responsibility for an output could shift to the user where the user steers it with repeated or suggestive prompts. The same chamber had earlier found a making-available infringement in GEMA v OpenAI ([CMS Germany on the Suno judgment](https://cms.law/de/deu/legal-updates/ki-musikgeneratoren-suno-urteil-staerkt-rechteinhaber)).

**[INFERENCE]** What the cases mean for a user: the legality of training is the vendor's fight, but an output that reproduces recognisable protected expression (a character, melody, lyric, photograph or logo) is a risk for whoever publishes it. The Munich ruling suggests that a user who deliberately steers a model toward a specific work or artist carries more of the responsibility. Style alone is generally not protected by copyright, but naming a living artist or a brand in a prompt creates evidence of intent and raises moral-right, passing-off and marketing-law risk, so it is banned (section 23). Run a reverse-image and audio-similarity check on hero assets before paid distribution.

**[INFERENCE] [FAST-MOVING, Nov 2026]** Four rules follow from the terms read. ### The Swedish statutory backdrop

**[DURABLE]** Checked on a primary source: under the Swedish Copyright Act photographic pictures are protected for 50 years from creation without an originality threshold (49 a §), works generally for 70 years after the author's death (43 §), and the creator must be named as good practice requires (3 §) ([Riksdagen, upphovsrättslagen SFS 1960:729, read 11 Nov 2026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1960729-om-upphovsratt-till-litterara-och_sfs-1960-729/)). **[INFERENCE]** Keep credit lines even for licensed stock unless the licence waives them.

### Stock images and footage

**[INFERENCE]** For each stock asset record the licence type (royalty-free standard or extended, or rights-managed), permitted media, territory, term, print-run or impression caps, seat count, and whether the file is editorial use only. Editorial-only files, which typically show newsworthy people, events, unreleased property or trade marks, must not be used in ads or branded content. Check whether the licence allows use as AI input or reference; the researchers believe many do not, which is unverified.

### Fonts

**[INFERENCE]** A desktop font licence rarely covers web embedding, apps, video broadcast or logo use. Record the licence per use and never ship font files to third parties. This is standard practice as the researchers understand it and was not sourced.

### Music

**[DURABLE]** Checked on a primary source: the Swedish collecting society Stim describes music in film and TV as licensed through a one-off synchronisation payment, and promotes a service called Cora Music for using well-known music in social media videos with rights cleared ([Stim, music in radio, TV, film and advertising, read 11 Nov 2026](https://www.stim.se/sv/musik-i-radio-tv-film-och-reklam)). **[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, read Nov 2026]** YouTube treats AI-generated music that is the main focus of a video as content requiring disclosure ([YouTube Help, disclosing altered or synthetic content, read Nov 2026](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)), and Suno ties commercial use of AI music to paid-tier downloads ([Suno Terms, effective 3 Sep 2026](https://suno.com/terms)).

Consumer music libraries inside social apps are generally not licensed for business accounts or ads; use the platform's commercial library or a production-music or subscription licence, and keep the licence certificate per track. ## 20. Likeness and voice

### Sweden: no name or picture in marketing without consent

**[DURABLE]** Checked on a primary source: under 1 § of Lag (1978:800) on names and pictures in advertising, a trader may not, when marketing goods, services or other utilities, use a presentation in which another person's name or picture is used without that person's consent; a designation clearly pointing to a specific person equals a name. Under 2 § an intentional or grossly negligent breach is punishable by fines. Under 3 § the person is owed reasonable compensation, plus damages if the breach was intentional or negligent, with regard to suffering, and the employer pays for employees' acts. Under 4 § materials can be ordered altered or destroyed. Under 5 § prosecution takes place only on the person's complaint or in the public interest. The statute text read does not mention protection after death ([Riksdagen, Lag (1978:800) om namn och bild i reklam](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1978800-om-namn-och-bild-i-reklam_sfs-1978-800/)). This is orientation and not legal advice.

**[INFERENCE]** The statute's wording is technology-neutral, so an AI-generated lookalike picture, or a designation that clearly points to a real person in an ad, plausibly falls within it. Voice is not mentioned in the statute, so a cloned voice is more likely handled through marketing law on misleading practices, GDPR and, where applicable, performers' rights. No case law was read.

### Three separate things are needed to show a person in Swedish marketing

**[INFERENCE]** For people in marketing material in Sweden, three separate things are needed: consent under Lag 1978:800 for use of name or picture in advertising; a GDPR legal basis and information to the person; and, for employees, a genuinely voluntary arrangement with an objection route.

### GDPR: images, film and voice are personal data

**[DURABLE]** Checked on a primary source: IMY's guidance, last updated 14 April 2021, says photos, film and an audio file with a person's voice are personal data when they identify a living person, and that publishing needs a defined purpose and a legal basis. Consent is often impractical for event photos. Companies can rely on legitimate interest if it outweighs the individuals' privacy interest, and the assessment must be documented. Event attendees should be told that images may be published, that they can object, and whom to contact ([IMY, publishing images, film and audio, last updated 14 Apr 2021](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/vi-guidar-dig/publicera-bilder-filmer-och-ljud-pa-internet/)). IMY's FAQ adds that a business may publish images of people on social media without consent if another basis such as legitimate interest applies, that asking first is still good practice, and that children's images deserve extra protection ([IMY FAQ, images on social media without consent](https://www.imy.se/vanliga-fragor-och-svar/ar-det-tillatet-for-en-verksamhet-att-publicera-bilder-pa-personer-i-sociala-medier-utan-samtycke/)). The GDPR basis does not replace the consent that Lag 1978:800 requires for marketing use.

### Employees on camera

**[DURABLE]** Checked on a primary source: for employees IMY says to assess case by case and always ask before publishing. Consent is often unsuitable because of the dependent relationship. Employers may have a strong enough interest for staff in outward-facing roles, and individuals can object to processing based on legitimate interest ([IMY, publishing images, film and audio, last updated 14 Apr 2021](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/vi-guidar-dig/publicera-bilder-filmer-och-ljud-pa-internet/)).

**[INFERENCE]** The default for employee photos and video is: ask first; rely on a documented legitimate interest for outward-facing roles; give an easy objection route; remove on objection or when employment ends unless otherwise agreed; and never make appearing on camera a condition of employment.

### What a consent or release should contain

**[INFERENCE]** The proposed minimum content of a consent or release is: the person's identity; the exact assets; purposes and channels (organic, paid, print); territory; duration and end date; whether AI editing, a synthetic avatar or a voice clone is permitted and for which scripts; an approval right over synthetic output; the fee; the withdrawal route; and, for employees, a statement that refusal has no consequences. Store the signed document's ID in the rights log.

## 21. Trade marks, products, locations and third-party material in media

### Generated images can carry other parties' marks

### Competitors' products

**[INFERENCE]** Depicting a competitor's product or mark is acceptable only within the comparative-advertising rules, which the researchers summarise from general EU and Swedish marketing-law principles as objective, verifiable, not denigrating and not taking unfair advantage; this was not verified. An AI-generated depiction of a competitor's product is likely to be inaccurate and therefore misleading, so use real, verified imagery or none, and get legal sign-off.

### Buildings and outdoor art in Sweden

**[DURABLE]** Checked on a primary source: under 24 § of the Swedish Copyright Act artworks permanently placed outdoors in a public place may be depicted, and buildings may be freely depicted. Artworks may also be depicted to advertise an exhibition or sale and in catalogues ([Riksdagen, upphovsrättslagen SFS 1960:729, read 11 Nov 2026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1960729-om-upphovsratt-till-litterara-och_sfs-1960-729/)). **[GAP]** The fetched text indicates that some of these permissions do not extend to digital form; that limit comes from a machine summary and must be re-read in the statute before it is relied on.

### Real locations

**[INFERENCE]** Private property and distinctive interiors need a property release. Altering a real, identifiable place with AI can trigger the deepfake label, because the Commission's summary of the definition covers places ([European Commission, EU icons page, updated 13 Oct 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-icons-labelling-ai-generated-content)).

### What was not verified here

**[GAP]** Not verified: Marknadsföringslagen 18 § on comparative advertising; Directive 2006/114/EC; Swedish case law on buildings and artworks in advertising; trade-mark use in imagery under the Swedish Trade Marks Act and the EU Trade Mark Regulation; and design-right or trade-dress risks from AI imagery.

## 22. Swedish media law for a company's media department

### Scope and status

**[INFERENCE]** This section gathers the Swedish rules a media department meets daily. Each paragraph says whether the point was checked on a primary source. All of it is orientation as of 11 November 2026 and not legal advice. Likeness is in section 20, accessibility in section 10 and AI-image copyright in section 18.

### Copyright basics: who owns a work and for how long

**[DURABLE]** Checked on a primary source: under the Swedish Copyright Act (1960:729), consolidated through SFS 2026:1120, copyright belongs to the person who created a literary or artistic work, including film, photography, visual art and computer programs (1 §). The author has the exclusive right to make copies, including temporary copies, and to make the work available to the public in original or altered form (2 §). Protection lasts 70 years after the author's death (43 §), and photographic pictures are protected for 50 years from creation regardless of originality (49 a §) ([Riksdagen, upphovsrättslagen SFS 1960:729, read 11 Nov 2026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1960729-om-upphovsratt-till-litterara-och_sfs-1960-729/)).

### Moral rights

**[DURABLE]** Checked on a primary source: under 3 § the author must be named as good practice requires, and a work may not be changed, or presented in a form or context, that offends the author's reputation or individuality; the right can be waived only to a limited extent ([Riksdagen, upphovsrättslagen SFS 1960:729, read 11 Nov 2026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1960729-om-upphovsratt-till-litterara-och_sfs-1960-729/)). The practical consequences for editing and crediting are in section 9.

### Adaptations and new independent works

**[DURABLE]** Checked on a primary source: under 4 § a translator or adapter holds copyright in the adaptation but cannot exploit it against the original's copyright, and a new independent work created in free connection with another has its own copyright ([Riksdagen, upphovsrättslagen SFS 1960:729, read 11 Nov 2026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1960729-om-upphovsratt-till-litterara-och_sfs-1960-729/)).

### Quotation is narrow, and picture quotation does not cover marketing

**[DURABLE]** Checked on a primary source: under 22 § anyone may quote from published works in accordance with good practice and to the extent justified by the purpose. Under 23 § published artworks may be reproduced alongside text in a non-commercial scholarly presentation, in a critical presentation, and in a newspaper or periodical reporting a current event, with good practice and justified extent applying ([Riksdagen, upphovsrättslagen SFS 1960:729, read 11 Nov 2026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1960729-om-upphovsratt-till-litterara-och_sfs-1960-729/)). Under 52 p § users of certain online content-sharing services may make works available for quotation, criticism, review, caricature, parody or pastiche ([Riksdagen, upphovsrättslagen SFS 1960:729, read 11 Nov 2026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-1960729-om-upphovsratt-till-litterara-och_sfs-1960-729/)).

**[INFERENCE]** The quotation right in 22 § is narrow for marketing use: quoting must serve a purpose such as illustration or criticism within your own content. 23 § does not cover commercial use of images, so picture quotation is not a basis for using others' images in marketing. Using clips, screenshots or music from third parties as a quote is therefore high risk in commercial content. **[GAP]** Whether the act now contains a general parody or pastiche exception beyond 52 p § was not seen, because the fetched text was cut at about two thirds of the act.

### Marketing must be identifiable as marketing

**[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, reviewed 4 Sep 2026]** Checked on a primary source: Konsumentverket, the Swedish Consumer Agency, states that posts a company publishes about its own products to promote sales fall under the ad-identification requirement. It must be clear at once, even on fleeting contact, that the content is marketing (court reference PMT 2054-18). Ad posts should be clearly marked with the word "reklam", and the assessment looks at word choice, graphic design (placement, size, colour and contrast) and whether vulnerable consumers are reached ([Konsumentverket, hidden marketing in social media, reviewed 4 Sep 2026](https://www.konsumentverket.se/marknadsratt-foretag/dold-marknadsforing-i-sociala-medier-regler-for-foretag/)).

**[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, reviewed 4 Sep 2026]** Checked on a primary source: the company bears the main responsibility for influencers' posts about its products, and the influencer can be liable in parallel (PMT 5923-20). Liability cannot be contracted away (PMT 5093-23). Agreements can be oral or implied (PMT 2479-20, PMT 10572-20). A breach cannot be undone by editing the post afterwards. Companies should have a working ad-review function, and a platform's ad-label tool failing is no excuse (PMB 5086-23). Sanctions are prohibition, a conditional fine, and a market disruption fee of up to four percent of annual turnover ([Konsumentverket, hidden marketing in social media, reviewed 4 Sep 2026](https://www.konsumentverket.se/marknadsratt-foretag/dold-marknadsforing-i-sociala-medier-regler-for-foretag/)).

**[GAP]** The Konsumentverket page read does not rule on wording such as "annons" or "i samarbete med" and does not address employees' own posts. Further Konsumentverket material, including the Nordic consumer ombudsmen's position, was not read ([Konsumentverket publication, Reklamidentifiering i sociala medier, unread](https://publikationer.konsumentverket.se/produkter-och-tjanster/reklam-och-marknadsforing/reklamidentifiering-i-sociala-medier)).

**[INFERENCE]** Company posts on its own channels count as marketing that must be identifiable. On a clearly branded company account this is normally evident from the sender, while paid partnerships, employee advocacy and influencer content need the explicit word "reklam" at the start.

### Marketing statements must be true, and AI is no excuse

**[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, 28 May 2026]** Secondary source only: a Swedish law firm summarises Marknadsföringslagen (2008:486) as requiring good marketing practice (5 §), commercial communication that is clearly identifiable as marketing and shows who is responsible (9 §), and statements that are true and not misleading (10 §), with contributory liability for others such as agencies (23 §). Enforcement is by Konsumentverket and the Patent and Market Court, with injunctions and market disruption fees ([CMS Sweden, 28 May 2026](https://cms.law/en/swe/legal-updates/ai-in-advertising-key-aspects-for-advertisers-in-sweden)). The statute itself was not read.

**[FAST-MOVING, 2025-2026]** Secondary source only: in PMT 13029-25 (1 April 2026) the Patent and Market Court held that false information given by a mobile operator's AI chatbot was misleading marketing and that chatbot answers count as marketing. In matter 2503-36 (13 August 2025) Reklamombudsmannen held an education company responsible for false AI-generated information in its marketing; the ombudsman's decisions are not legally binding ([CMS Sweden, 28 May 2026](https://cms.law/en/swe/legal-updates/ai-in-advertising-key-aspects-for-advertisers-in-sweden)).

### GDPR where AI tools process personal data

**[DURABLE]** Secondary source only: the same briefing notes that GDPR applies where AI tools process personal data in advertising, so a legal basis, transparency and safeguards for third-party AI providers and transfers outside the EEA are needed ([CMS Sweden, 28 May 2026](https://cms.law/en/swe/legal-updates/ai-in-advertising-key-aspects-for-advertisers-in-sweden)). **[INFERENCE]** Uploading footage of identifiable people to a generative tool is a processing operation that needs its own assessment, in addition to the vendor-terms checks in section 18.

### Utgivningsbevis: what the publishing certificate does and does not do

**[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** Checked on a primary source: the Swedish Agency for the Media, Mediemyndigheten, describes the utgivningsbevis as a voluntary certificate for websites and databases that gives the constitutional protection mass media get automatically. Individuals, companies and organisations can apply. Conditions include that the database is publicly available and actively sought out by the visitor; is a clearly delimited, coherent product; can be changed only by the editorial staff, with comment fields clearly separated; has a name containing a domain name that is not confusable with another registered database; has a connection to Sweden; has a responsible publisher; and starts within six months. It is valid for ten years and renewable, an application fee applies, and it can be revoked. Its effects are special liability rules with the publisher responsible, source anonymity and no prior censorship ([Mediemyndigheten, utgivningsbevis och databaser, read 11 Nov 2026](https://mediemyndigheten.se/ansokan-och-registrering/utgivningsbevis-och-databaser/)).

**[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, read 11 Nov 2026]** Checked on a primary source: Mediemyndigheten states that the certificate does not in itself give a right to publish personal data and that each publication must be assessed by its purpose; it refers to a judgment of the Court of Justice of the EU from July 2026 holding that member states may not exempt processing from GDPR where the purpose is not journalistic, academic, artistic or literary. The authority has not issued certificates for Facebook or YouTube pages, because they are not the applicant's own databases ([Mediemyndigheten, utgivningsbevis och databaser, read 11 Nov 2026](https://mediemyndigheten.se/ansokan-och-registrering/utgivningsbevis-och-databaser/)). The judgment was not identified by case number and the fee amount was not found.

**[INFERENCE]** An utgivningsbevis is of little use to a SaaS marketing site: it does not cover social accounts and does not by itself lift GDPR. A company web or social channel is normally ordinary marketing and not constitutionally protected publishing, so marketing law, GDPR and Lag 1978:800 apply in full. That marketing law still applies to a certified database is the researchers' inference and is not stated on the page read.

## Conclusion

**[INFERENCE]** The research changes the usual picture of a media department in one respect: for an AI-staffed team the scarce resource is not production capacity but clearance. Agents can produce variants without limit, while each published asset needs a human approver, a consent trail, a licence trail, a disclosure decision and intact provenance metadata. The house style's volume claims therefore translate, for this company, into volume of drafts and hook tests upstream of a deliberately narrow publishing gate. The three layers also reinforce each other more than they collide: clear packaging, proof that can be documented and light post-production are what the house style asks for, what YouTube's own test rewards, and what marketing law requires.

**[INFERENCE]** Two things should happen before this knowledge base is treated as complete. A follow-up research pass should close the unsourced domains (workflow, design, asset-management standards, official safe zones, stock, font and music licences, the remaining vendor terms), because agents will otherwise act on proposals that look like standards. And the owner's counsel should confirm the handful of points on which daily decisions will hang: whether the accessibility act reaches the company's video, how Article 50 labels apply to realistic AI imagery in its ads, and the wording of the consent and release documents. Until then the dated facts here expire monthly, and the safe defaults are the ones the notes support from several directions: caption everything, label realistic synthetic media, never show a real person without consent on record, and keep the record of how each asset was made.
