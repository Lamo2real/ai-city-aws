# Research agents earn trust by verifying claims

As of November 2026 the foundational knowledge for an AI research department is that a working link is not verification, a signed-off question comes before any method, and lawful access comes before any collection. The strongest independent evidence on AI-assisted research shows that frontier models now return working links more than 94% of the time yet the cited page actually supports the attached claim only about 39 to 77% of the time, and that support falls as an agent run gets longer (May 2026, [arXiv 2605.06635](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06635)); the department therefore needs claim-by-claim checking against the opened source, a liveness check, a separate verifier with fresh context, and a mandatory "not verified" list in every deliverable. The method layer is stable and well documented: scope with a written specification that the commissioner signs (UK Aqua Book, 2025 edition), check whether existing evidence already answers the question (AAPOR), read sources laterally and trace claims to their origin, start forecasts from a reference class, report likelihood on a published scale and confidence in a separate sentence, and give every analysis a sunset date. The fast-moving layer is sample integrity and law: industry estimates put fraud at 15 to 30% of market-research responses (NORC review, April 2026) while a vendor-funded preprint reports under 1% on vetted panels, so survey work needs layered, pre-specified and disclosed defences; EU and Swedish copyright law allows text and data mining only with lawful access and no rights reservation; the Advocate General's opinion of 3 November 2026 in CJEU case C-250/25 points toward liability for answers that recognisably reuse press wording; and EU data-protection bodies treat robots.txt, login walls and CAPTCHAs as objections that defeat a legitimate-interest basis for scraping. For a Sweden-based SaaS company the free official registers come first (Bolagsverket and SCB company data free since 3 February 2025, Vinnova project data under CC0, Eurostat reusable commercially with attribution), and three Swedish statutes set hard edges: the trade-secrets act makes obtaining a secret unlawfully a crime with up to two years' prison, the ethics-review act applies to companies and carries prison for intentional breach, and the data-protection act puts criminal-offence data largely out of reach for private actors. Much practice material could not be sourced because the researchers' fetch budgets ran out, including TAM/SAM/SOM definitions, conjoint and MaxDiff sample rules, battlecards, research-operations frameworks and provider data-retention terms; this document marks those holes instead of filling them from memory. The practical conclusion is that the manager agent's main job is delegation discipline and assurance, the workers' main job is reading what they cite, and anything involving a technical barrier, a named individual, a confidential-looking document, a competitor contact or external publication stops for a human.

## How to read and split this document

[DURABLE] This document is written to be cut into atomic knowledge-base entries for a vector index and a graph database. Every body paragraph is meant to stand alone: it opens with one or more labels, names its subject in full, and keeps each number next to its date and its source link. Section numbers group domains, and sub-headings name the single topic of the paragraphs beneath them. Tables carry a label and a source link in every row so that a row can be split out as its own entry. When splitting, keep the label, the date and the link together with the claim.

[DURABLE] The labels mean the following. **[DURABLE]** marks a principle, definition, formula, standard or long-standing legal rule that has held for years. **[FAST-MOVING, date]** marks a figure, platform rule, study result, API limit or legal status that will age, with the date of the source. **[VENDOR]** marks a figure or recommendation from a party with a commercial interest in the conclusion. **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** marks a platform's or tool maker's own statement about itself that nobody has independently verified. **[CONTESTED]** marks a point where sources disagree. **[INFERENCE]** marks a synthesis, rule of thumb or operating rule written by the researchers that no single fetched source states; adopt it as a default and tune it. **[GAP]** marks something the research did not verify or could not find. **[HOUSE STYLE]** is reserved for Alex or Leila Hormozi's published frameworks, which the owner has chosen as the default style where they exist; the research notes for this department contain no Hormozi material on research method, so no paragraph below carries that label, and that absence is itself recorded in the gaps section.

[DURABLE] Legal, tax and regulatory points carry one of three verification qualifiers. "Checked on primary source" means the statute, regulation, court press release or regulator page was read during the research; where the text was read on an unofficial mirror (lagen.nu, gdpr-info.eu) or through a summarising extraction tool, the paragraph says so. "Secondary source only" means a law-firm briefing, tracker, slide deck, trade press or encyclopaedia page was read and the primary text was not. "Unverified background" means the point was not opened at all and comes with a pointer to what should be checked. Agents must not gate a live decision on a secondary or unverified legal point without confirmation from Legal.

## 1. Research design: scope the decision before choosing a method

### 1.1 The Aqua Book specification as the scoping standard

[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, 2025] Use the UK government's Aqua Book as the scoping standard for any research request. The 2025 edition replaces the 2015 edition; the text read was the final pre-publication draft, with the official version on GOV.UK ([Aqua Book revision, Introduction](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/intro.html)). Its quality principles are proportionate assurance (effort matched to the risk of the intended use and to complexity), assurance throughout the life cycle, verification (built correctly to specification) and validation (right for its purpose), uncertainty treated in every phase, and the RIGOUR acronym: repeatable, independent, grounded in reality, objective, uncertainty-managed, robust ([Aqua Book revision, Introduction](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/intro.html)).

[DURABLE] Run every study through the Aqua Book's four-stage analytical lifecycle: engagement and scoping, design, analysis, then delivery and communication. The lifecycle is iterative and stages can repeat ([Aqua Book revision, Introduction](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/intro.html)). The manager agent should name the stage a study is in whenever it reports status.

[DURABLE] At scoping, the commissioner and the analyst should agree the analytical question with its context and boundaries, deadlines and resources, assumptions, risks and known uncertainties, how business-critical the work is, the acceptable accuracy, precision and margin of error, and the data sources and their quality ([Aqua Book revision, Engagement and scoping](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/engagement_and_scoping.html)). A request that arrives without these items is not ready to assign to a worker.

[DURABLE] Write the agreement into a specification, which serves as the research brief. It contains scope and constraints, the acceptable level of risk, the required level of assurance, optionally the degree of uncertainty the decision can tolerate, and the identified sources of uncertainty. The analyst drafts it, the commissioner signs it off, and it later becomes the reference for validation and approval ([Aqua Book revision, Engagement and scoping](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/engagement_and_scoping.html)).

[DURABLE] Make the cost of shortcuts explicit to the requester. The Aqua Book says the commissioner should understand the risk created when time or resource pressure limits the approach, and can state how consequences vary with different degrees of uncertainty so that the analyst pitches effort proportionately ([Aqua Book revision, Engagement and scoping](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/engagement_and_scoping.html)).

[DURABLE] When the problem itself is ill-defined, structure it before researching it. The Aqua Book points to problem-structuring methods: Strategic Choice Approach, Rich Pictures and Systems Thinking, and SODA or Soft Systems Methodology where user groups conflict ([Aqua Book revision, Engagement and scoping](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/engagement_and_scoping.html)).

[DURABLE] Separate four roles on every study: the commissioner owns the need and uses the output within its stated limits; the analyst does and documents the work; the assurer reports whether the work was properly scoped, executed, validated, verified and documented and whether uncertainty was handled; the approver reviews the assurance evidence and signs off ([Aqua Book revision, Delivery, communication and sign-off](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/delivery_and_communication.html)). In a one-manager, many-worker agent department, the worker is the analyst and must not be its own assurer.

### 1.2 Gates before new research starts

[DURABLE] Check whether new primary research is needed at all. AAPOR's best practices say that before fielding a survey the researcher should check whether existing surveys, focus groups, content analyses or administrative records already answer the question, and that surveys must not be used to produce predetermined results, which breaches the AAPOR Code ([AAPOR Best Practices for Survey Research](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/)).

[DURABLE] Triage questions by tractability. Tetlock's first rule, as published by Good Judgment, is to spend effort on questions that are neither trivially easy nor hopelessly unpredictable, to break problems into knowable parts, and to state assumptions explicitly ([Good Judgment, Tetlock's 10 commandments](https://goodjudgment.com/philip-tetlocks-10-commandments-of-superforecasting/)).

[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, 15 May 2026] Design evaluation into an intervention from its earliest stages. This is the HM Treasury Magenta Book principle; its main guidance and Annex A were replaced on 14 May 2026 with a new TIGER transparency annex and a Test and Learn annex, and the page was last updated 15 May 2026 ([GOV.UK, The Magenta Book](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-magenta-book)). For the Research department this means agreeing how a recommendation's outcome will be checked before the recommendation is delivered.

[INFERENCE] Translate a business question into a research plan in six written items: the decision and its owner; the two to five research questions whose answers would change the decision; a falsifiable hypothesis for each, with the evidence that would refute it; the required precision (good enough to choose between A and B, or a stated margin); deadline and budget; and the assurance level. This is the researchers' synthesis of the Aqua Book specification and Tetlock's advice to decide beforehand what would change your mind; it is not a quoted standard.

[INFERENCE] For a Sweden-based SaaS company, attach the business-critical flag to research that feeds pricing changes, market-entry decisions, fundraising figures such as market-size claims to investors, and anything published externally. Business-critical studies get independent assurance and human approval.

### 1.3 Choosing a method

[DURABLE] Sequence qualitative before quantitative when the answer categories are unknown. Pew recommends piloting with open questions to discover the common answers and then building closed options from them ([Pew Research Center, Writing survey questions](https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions/)).

[DURABLE] Choose survey mode by balancing data quality against budget and time. AAPOR notes that the choice between online, phone, in-person and mail trades these off and that mixed-mode designs commonly start with the cheapest mode ([AAPOR Best Practices](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/)).

[INFERENCE] Default method-choice heuristic: secondary research first because it is cheapest and fastest; qualitative research when the categories are unknown (why, how, vocabulary, unmet needs); quantitative research when categories are known and the decision needs magnitude, incidence or ranking; mixed methods when a number needs explaining or a theme needs sizing. Only the qual-then-quant ordering is sourced (Pew); the rest is common practice that the researchers could not source, and no sourced decision tree for qualitative versus quantitative was found.

### 1.4 Stopping rules

[DURABLE] Stop qualitative interviewing by a stated saturation rule, not by feel. Guest, Namey and Chen (2020) propose choosing a base size of 4 to 6 interviews, a run length of 2 to 3 further interviews, and a new-information threshold of 5% or less, or 0%; the saturation ratio is new themes in the run divided by unique themes in the base. In their datasets the 5% threshold was typically reached at about 6 to 7 interviews in homogeneous samples and up to 11 to 12 in a more varied sample, while 0% took 11 to 16 ([PLOS ONE, 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0232076)).

[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, 6 Feb 2024] Time-box evidence reviews and declare every shortcut. Cochrane caps its rapid reviews at six months and requires the authors to define explicitly every simplification of method ([BMJ 2024, Updated Cochrane rapid review methods guidance](https://www.bmj.com/content/384/bmj-2023-076335)).

[INFERENCE] Suggested stopping rule for agent desk research: stop when two consecutive search rounds add no new independent source that changes the estimate, or when each key claim has two independent sources or one primary source, or when the time-box in the specification is reached, whichever comes first, and report which rule triggered. The rule is modelled on Guest's saturation logic and Aqua Book proportionality; it is not a published standard.

## 2. Desk research: judge sources by leaving them and tracing claims to origin

### 2.1 Evaluating a source

[DURABLE] Read laterally. A 2017 Stanford History Education Group study of 45 people (10 professional fact-checkers, 10 PhD historians and 25 Stanford undergraduates) found that fact-checkers left the site quickly, opened new tabs and used other sources to judge it, and reached more warranted conclusions in less time, while historians and students stayed on the page and were misled by logos and domain names ([SSRN, Lateral Reading, 2017](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3048994)). An agent should judge an unfamiliar site by what others say about it, not by how it presents itself.

[DURABLE] Apply the four SIFT moves (Caulfield, 2019) to any unfamiliar source: stop and ask whether the source and its reputation are known and what the purpose is; investigate the source, meaning who publishes it and what they know and want, in about a minute; find better coverage by setting the source aside and comparing trusted reporting on the claim; and trace claims, quotes and media back to their original context to see what was cut or reframed ([Mike Caulfield, SIFT](https://hapgood.us/2019/06/19/sift-the-four-moves/)). This is a practitioner method, not a tested standard.

[DURABLE] Rate the source and the item separately. The Admiralty (NATO) system grades source reliability A to F (A completely reliable, E unreliable, F cannot be judged) and information credibility 1 to 6 (1 confirmed by independent sources, 2 probably true, 3 possibly true, 4 doubtful, 5 improbable, 6 cannot be judged), and a source's grade must not determine the grade of a given item ([Wikipedia, Admiralty code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiralty_code)). The description was read on a tertiary source.

[DURABLE] Follow the ICD 203 tradecraft standards that bear on desk research: describe the quality and credibility of sources, data and methods; distinguish underlying information from assumptions and judgments; include analysis of alternatives; and explain change from, or consistency with, earlier judgments ([ICD 203, Analytic Standards](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)). The directive is dated 2 January 2015 and carries later technical amendments; the extracted text showed amendment dates in 2022 and 12 June 2023, which the researchers could not disambiguate, and the PDF is now served from archive.dni.gov.

[FAST-MOVING, 2025] Honour the secondary-data duties of the ICC/ESOMAR Code, 2025 revision. Article 5 requires that use of secondary data be compatible with the original collection purpose, respect original exclusions and intellectual-property rights, cause no direct harm, and honour data subjects' requests against re-use ([ESOMAR, ICC/ESOMAR Code](https://esomar.org/representation/icc-esomar-code-of-conduct)). A secondary source reports that the revision was approved in June 2025 and replaced the 2016 version ([Research World](https://researchworld.com/articles/why-the-icc-esomar-code-will-matter-more-than-ever-in-2026)); the month was not on ESOMAR's own page.

[FAST-MOVING, April 2026] Treat third-party "a survey says" statistics from non-probability samples as lower-reliability unless fraud controls are disclosed. NORC's April 2026 literature review reports industry estimates of fraud at 15 to 30% of market-research responses and up to 45% on some platforms, and several case studies of social-media-recruited, incentivised open surveys with over 90% fraudulent responses ([NORC, April 2026](https://www.norc.org/content/dam/norc-org/pdf2026/cpss-research-brief-fraud-lit-review.pdf)).

[INFERENCE] Working reliability hierarchy for business desk research, highest first: official statistics and registers, audited annual reports and regulatory filings; peer-reviewed research and standards bodies; a company's primary statements about itself such as press releases, pricing pages and job ads, which are reliable for what the company says and not for what is true; reputable trade press and analyst firms with a disclosed method; vendor white papers and surveys, where sample and method must be checked; and last, aggregators, SEO blogs, unsourced statistics and AI-generated summaries. The ranking is the researchers' synthesis applying the Admiralty logic and SIFT's trace-to-origin move.

[INFERENCE] Primary versus secondary source: a primary source is the originator of the data or statement (the register, the dataset, the filing, the paper), and a secondary source reports or interprets it. Agent rule: cite the primary; if only a secondary is reachable, write "as reported by" the secondary and lower confidence. The definition was not sourced in the research; it is consistent with SIFT's fourth move.

[INFERENCE] Triangulation rule: two sources count as independent only if they do not share an upstream origin, so five articles quoting one vendor report are one source. When sources conflict, first check definitions, units, geography and date; then prefer the source closest to the origin with a disclosed method; and if the conflict is unresolved, report the range and both sources instead of averaging silently. This applies ICD 203's analysis of alternatives.

[GAP] The CRAAP test was not verified. Its criteria are commonly given as currency, relevance, authority, accuracy and purpose, but the wording was not checked; verify at https://library.csuchico.edu/sites/default/files/craap-test.pdf before using it. ICD 206 on sourcing requirements returned no readable text, so its citation rules are not captured.

### 2.2 Search strategy and review discipline

[DURABLE] Use only search operators whose behaviour is documented, and never treat an empty result as proof of absence. Google documents `site:` (domain, URL or prefix) and `filetype:` (content type or extension), plus `imagesize:` and `src:` for Google Images only, and states that operators are bound by indexing and retrieval limits ([Google Search Central, Search operators](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-operators)). No fetched source covered other operators such as `intitle:`, quoted phrases or exclusion terms.

[DURABLE] Report searches so that they can be repeated. PRISMA 2020, published in the BMJ on 29 March 2021, is a 27-item checklist in seven sections that requires the full search strategy for every database, register and website searched, how many reviewers screened each record and whether independently, any automation tools used, and whether data, code and materials are public ([BMJ 2021;372:n71](https://www.bmj.com/content/372/bmj.n71)). The PRISMA site lists the statement, an explanation paper, an abstract checklist and flow-diagram templates and announces no newer version ([PRISMA 2020](https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020)).

[FAST-MOVING, 6 Feb 2024] For rapid reviews, apply Cochrane's search rules: search at least two bibliographic databases, limit grey-literature and supplementary searching, peer-review the main search or at least double-check it, justify any date limits, and apply language limits at selection and not in the search ([BMJ 2024, Cochrane rapid review guidance](https://www.bmj.com/content/384/bmj-2023-076335)).

[INFERENCE] Search-strategy checklist for agents: define concepts and synonyms in Swedish and English; combine `site:` with official domains (scb.se, bolagsverket.se, ec.europa.eu, vinnova.se, riksdagen.se) and `filetype:pdf` for reports and grey literature; log every query; and search at least two independent indexes for anything important, borrowing the Cochrane two-database minimum. Keep queries short, vary phrasing, search in the source language (Swedish for Swedish law), go to the issuing body's own domain, and fetch the page instead of trusting the snippet.

[INFERENCE] Citation record for agents: every claim carries URL, publisher, publication or last-updated date, access date and a one-word source class. For Eurostat and SCB pulls, also store the dataset code, the query and the retrieval timestamp, because Eurostat keeps no version history.

[GAP] No source was retrieved on Swedish grey-literature sources (government inquiries in the SOU series, Tillväxtanalys, Tillväxtverket), on search-engine coverage limits or ranking bias, or on contamination of search results by AI-generated content.

## 3. Public data sources: free official registers first

### 3.1 Sweden

[FAST-MOVING, 3 Feb 2025] Build Swedish company universes from the free official register data. Bolagsverket and SCB "värdefulla datamängder" (EU high-value datasets) have been free and available without an agreement, by REST API or bulk file, since 3 February 2025; the EU implementing regulation entered into force on 9 February 2023 and Sweden had a two-year derogation ([Bolagsverket, Värdefulla datamängder](https://bolagsverket.se/apierochoppnadata/hamtaforetagsinformation/vardefulladatamangder.5294.html)). Checked on the authority's own page.

[FAST-MOVING, 3 Feb 2025] The free Bolagsverket and SCB fields are organisation name and form, legal form (SCB), identity number, postal address, registration date, deregistration and its cause, ongoing liquidation or restructuring proceedings, business description, SNI industry codes (SCB), active status for F-tax, VAT and employer registration (SCB), advertising block (SCB), and all digitally filed annual reports ([Bolagsverket, Värdefulla datamängder](https://bolagsverket.se/apierochoppnadata/hamtaforetagsinformation/vardefulladatamangder.5294.html)).

[FAST-MOVING, 3 Feb 2025] Re-use of the free Bolagsverket and SCB data is allowed for commercial and non-commercial purposes, including modification and combination with other sources, subject to personal-data and secrecy law and any licence terms such as source attribution; more detailed company data remain behind Bolagsverket's paid APIs ([Bolagsverket, Värdefulla datamängder](https://bolagsverket.se/apierochoppnadata/hamtaforetagsinformation/vardefulladatamangder.5294.html)). Bolagsverket's open-data overview, updated 2 July 2026, says some open data is free and some requires an agreement ([Bolagsverket, API:er och öppna data](https://bolagsverket.se/apierochoppnadata.2531.html)).

[FAST-MOVING, October 2025] Pull Swedish official statistics through SCB's PxWebApi v2, released for the Statistical Database in October 2025 and due to replace v1. Limits are 150,000 data cells per extraction and 30 calls per 10 seconds per IP address, a limit shared by everyone behind the same office IP ([SCB, PxWebApi](https://www.scb.se/en/services/open-data-api/pxwebapi/)). Agents running in parallel from one address must share that budget.

[FAST-MOVING, 10 Nov 2026] Use Vinnova's open data for Swedish technology scouting and funding signals. The data cover funding calls and funded R&D projects, the licence is CC0 and the data are free to use (page updated 10 November 2026, [Vinnova, Öppna data](https://www.vinnova.se/om-oss/om-webbplatsen/oppna-data/)).

[FAST-MOVING, 10 Nov 2026] Vinnova recommends the shared GDP API (Gemensamma data-projektet), which has three endpoints for calls, applications and financed activities, with applications and financed activities from decision dates of 1 January 2010 onward. It needs an account and API key through gdphub.se, most data are also available as CSV without an account, and documentation sits at github.com/Gemensamma-data-projektet-GDP/Dokumentation ([Vinnova, Öppna data](https://www.vinnova.se/om-oss/om-webbplatsen/oppna-data/)).

[FAST-MOVING, 10 Nov 2026] Older Vinnova endpoints on data.vinnova.se are `/api/program/`, `/api/utlysningar/`, `/api/ansokningsomgangar/` and `/api/projekt/`; adding a diarienummer returns one record and adding a date returns records changed since then, and bulk sets sit on vinnova.entryscape.net. The GDP data name only the project leader and lack employer, ORCID, English organisation names and application keywords ([Vinnova, Öppna data](https://www.vinnova.se/om-oss/om-webbplatsen/oppna-data/)).

[INFERENCE] The Vinnova project leader's name is personal data even though the dataset is CC0, so GDPR still applies when an agent profiles individuals from it. Use the data at organisation and project level by default.

[INFERENCE] For a Swedish SaaS company the free Bolagsverket data are enough to build a competitor or customer universe by SNI code and to read digitally filed annual reports for revenue and headcount. Commercial register sites of the allabolag type repackage the same registers with convenience features; their terms and coverage were not checked.

### 3.2 EU and international

[DURABLE] Pull EU statistics through Eurostat's free APIs: a Statistics API returning JSON-stat 2.0, SDMX 2.1 and SDMX 3.0 APIs (SDMX-ML, SDMX-CSV, JSON-stat, TSV), a Catalogue API and an asynchronous API for large queries. The base is `https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/dissemination`, and Comext and PRODCOM "DS-" datasets use `https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/comext/dissemination` ([Eurostat, API introduction](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/user-guides/data-browser/api-data-access/api-introduction)).

[DURABLE] Snapshot every Eurostat extract. Eurostat datasets update twice daily, at 11:00 and 23:00 CET, and only the latest version is kept, so a figure pulled today can differ from the one pulled last quarter with no history to consult ([Eurostat, API introduction](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/user-guides/data-browser/api-data-access/api-introduction)).

[DURABLE] Eurostat data can be reused commercially with attribution. Reuse of statistical data, metadata and publications is authorised, including commercially, if the source is acknowledged and changes are indicated; editorial content is CC BY 4.0; the basis is the Commission Decision of 12 December 2011 ([Eurostat copyright notice](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/help/copyright-notice)). Checked on Eurostat's own page.

[DURABLE] Check Eurostat's exceptions per dataset before commercial reuse. Not covered are data marked as from sources other than Eurostat, publications partly owned by other organisations, data for countries outside the EU, EFTA and official candidate countries, trade data declared by Liechtenstein and Switzerland from 1995 in the listed classifications, and Austrian trade data at CN 8-digit level; third-party material and logos are excluded ([Eurostat copyright notice](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/help/copyright-notice)).

[FAST-MOVING, 1 July 2024] OECD content published from 1 July 2024 is mostly CC BY 4.0 and should be cited as OECD (year), title, URL; third-party-owned data can carry extra restrictions to be checked in the metadata or source tab ([OECD Terms and Conditions](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/terms-conditions.html)). The OECD publishes an explainer on programmatic retrieval from its Data Explorer ([OECD, API explainer](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/insights/data-explainers/2024/09/api.html)); endpoint details were not captured.

[DURABLE] OECD APIs are provided as-is with no stated numeric rate limit; the OECD may limit or suspend an IP address without notice if usage strains the service, and users must not interfere with usage monitoring ([OECD Terms and Conditions](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/terms-conditions.html)).

[DURABLE] The World Bank Indicators API offers nearly 16,000 time-series indicators, many going back more than 50 years, at base `https://api.worldbank.org/v2/` with no API key; version 1 is retired ([World Bank Data Help Desk](https://datahelpdesk.worldbank.org/knowledgebase/articles/889392-about-the-indicators-api-documentation)).

[DURABLE] Use Espacenet for free patent research: worldwide coverage from 1782 to today, DOCDB simple families, INPADOC extended families and INPADOC legal-event data, with CPC classification guidance ([EPO, Espacenet](https://www.epo.org/en/searching-for-patents/technical/espacenet)). WIPO provides Guidelines for Preparing Patent Landscape Reports, a Patent Analytics Handbook, a Manual on Open Source Patent Analytics and the PATENTSCOPE database ([WIPO, Patent analytics](https://www.wipo.int/web/patent-analytics)).

[INFERENCE] Store an attribution line with each extract: "Source: Eurostat, [dataset code], extracted [date], changes: [none or description]" and "OECD (year), Title, URL". This turns the licence conditions into a field the repository can check.

[GAP] Not verified: EUIPO TMview and DesignView, data.europa.eu, EU CORDIS, OECD API endpoints, and the European Single Access Point timeline. ## 4. Market analysis and forecasting: start from the reference class

### 4.1 Market sizing

[GAP] [INFERENCE] TAM, SAM and SOM definitions are unsourced in this knowledge base; two attempts to open a source failed. The widely used definitions, given here as unverified background, are: TAM is total demand if every possible buyer bought; SAM is the part reachable with the current product, geography and channel; SOM is the share realistically winnable in the planning horizon given competition and capacity. Verify at https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/market-sizing before quoting a definition externally.

[INFERENCE] Top-down sizing starts from a published market total and applies filters for geography, segment and product fit. Bottom-up sizing multiplies the number of target accounts by an adoption rate and an annual contract value; for SaaS, market value in SEK is roughly the sum over segments of companies in the segment, times seats or units per company, times price per seat per year. Unsourced common practice.

[INFERENCE] Compute both top-down and bottom-up estimates and reconcile them. A gap larger than roughly two to three times means an assumption is wrong and should be found before reporting; that threshold is the researchers' rule of thumb and not a standard. Report sizing as a range with the three most sensitive assumptions exposed, in line with the Aqua Book's assumptions log and sensitivity requirement.

[FAST-MOVING, 3 Feb 2025] For a Sweden-based SaaS company, the inputs for bottom-up sizing are free: organisation counts, SNI codes, legal form, active status and filed annual reports through the Bolagsverket and SCB high-value-dataset API ([Bolagsverket, Värdefulla datamängder](https://bolagsverket.se/apierochoppnadata/hamtaforetagsinformation/vardefulladatamangder.5294.html)).

[GAP] Segmentation methods (firmographic, needs-based, jobs-to-be-done) and trend-analysis techniques were not researched. No source was sought on Swedish or Nordic SaaS market size or benchmarks.

### 4.2 Industry structure: Porter's five forces and their limits

[DURABLE] Use Porter's five forces to check industry structure: threat of new entrants, bargaining power of suppliers, bargaining power of buyers, threat of substitutes, and rivalry among existing competitors. The purpose is to explain how economic value is divided among industry actors and to assess industry attractiveness and how trends will change competition ([Harvard Business School, The Five Forces](https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/the-five-forces.aspx)).

[DURABLE] Respect the limits Porter's own institute states: industry structure changes over time; structure is only one of two profitability drivers, the other being the firm's relative position; and the threat of entry depends on likely entrants' capabilities as well as on barriers ([HBS, The Five Forces](https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/the-five-forces.aspx)).

[DURABLE] Avoid three errors Porter named in 2008: defining competition too narrowly as today's direct rivals; assuming a fast-growing industry is a profitable one; and treating government policy or complements as forces in themselves when they act through the five forces ([HBR, January 2008](https://hbr.org/2008/01/the-five-competitive-forces-that-shape-strategy)). The full article is paywalled and was only partly captured.

[INFERENCE] For SaaS, five forces is weak on network effects, platforms and complements, fast-moving market boundaries and ecosystem partners; use it as a structure check and not as a forecast. The SaaS application is the researchers' own, partly supported by Porter's caveats on change and complements.

### 4.3 External drivers and scenarios

[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, 29 Aug 2024] Use the UK Government Office for Science Futures Toolkit as the sourced method for driver mapping and scenarios. It was first published 8 July 2014, an updated edition (edition 2, 117 pages) was uploaded 11 July 2024, and the page was last updated 29 August 2024 ([GOV.UK Futures Toolkit](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/futures-toolkit-for-policy-makers-and-analysts)).

[DURABLE] The Futures Toolkit groups twelve tools by question. For what is changing: Delphi, Seven Questions (structured stakeholder interviews), Horizon Scanning, Three Horizons and Driver Mapping. For what it means: SWOT, Scenarios, Visioning and Futures Wheels. For what to do: Policy Stress-testing, Roadmapping and Backcasting ([Futures Toolkit HTML](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/futures-toolkit-for-policy-makers-and-analysts/the-futures-toolkit-html)).

[DURABLE] Map drivers with PESTLE headings and an importance-versus-certainty matrix. The toolkit's driver-mapping method is to brainstorm drivers under PESTLE headings (STEEP and STEEPV are named variants), write each as a short phrase with a direction of change, place each on the matrix and pick the three to five most important. Important and certain means act now; important and uncertain means research further or use for scenarios; unimportant and uncertain means track; unimportant and certain means park. The exercise takes about two hours ([Futures Toolkit HTML](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/futures-toolkit-for-policy-makers-and-analysts/the-futures-toolkit-html)).

[DURABLE] Choose scenario axes from critical uncertainties. Start from at least 10 critical uncertainties, state each as a continuum between two opposite outcomes, cluster them into two independent groups (for example technological versus social), and take the two dominant ones as the axes of a two-by-two ([Futures Toolkit HTML](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/futures-toolkit-for-policy-makers-and-analysts/the-futures-toolkit-html)).

[DURABLE] Build four scenarios from the two-by-two: build the axes, describe each world, name each scenario, discuss in plenary and write up. The toolkit specifies a workshop of at least 4 to 5 hours (6 hours if virtual), a minimum of 8 to 12 participants, and a horizon of at least five years and usually 10 to 20, and it states that scenarios are not plans or predictions ([Futures Toolkit HTML](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/futures-toolkit-for-policy-makers-and-analysts/the-futures-toolkit-html)). The page also showed a conflicting 45 to 60 minute duration in a header, which the researchers could not resolve.

[DURABLE] Call the step of testing options against scenarios "policy stress-testing", which is the toolkit's term ([Futures Toolkit HTML](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/futures-toolkit-for-policy-makers-and-analysts/the-futures-toolkit-html)). The steps for policy stress-testing and backcasting were in a part of the page that was not read.

[INFERENCE] For a SaaS company the toolkit's 10 to 20 year horizon is too long for product planning; a three to five year horizon with the same mechanics is a reasonable adaptation. Convert every PESTLE item into driver, mechanism, effect on demand or cost, evidence and confidence, because a list of headings is a prompt and not an analysis. Scenarios should not be given a single probability. These are the researchers' judgments.

### 4.4 Forecasting evidence and practice

[DURABLE] Follow Tetlock's ten points as published by Good Judgment: triage questions; break problems into parts and expose assumptions; use comparison classes by asking how often similar things happened in similar situations, even for events that seem unique; update as information arrives; weigh the strongest counter-case and pre-commit to what would change your mind; use numeric probabilities and practise fine distinctions; balance under- and over-confidence; run post-mortems on successes and failures; manage teams for constructive disagreement; and practise with clear feedback ([Good Judgment, 10 commandments](https://goodjudgment.com/philip-tetlocks-10-commandments-of-superforecasting/)).

[DURABLE] The evidence base is the Good Judgment Project, which won IARPA's 2011 to 2015 forecasting tournament of six teams, hundreds of geopolitical questions with horizons of months to a year, and thousands of volunteer forecasters. All figures in this sub-section come through one secondary review, published 7 February 2019 and modified 17 July 2020; Tetlock's and Mellers' primary papers were not opened ([AI Impacts](https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/)).

[DURABLE] Write the reference class first. In the Good Judgment Project, forecasts whose rationale was tagged as using comparison classes (the outside view) had an average Brier score of 0.17, against 0.26 for the next-best tag, where lower is better (review dated 2019 to 2020, [AI Impacts](https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/)). The review did not state which Brier scale was used.

[DURABLE] Aggregation and teams beat individuals. The Good Judgment Project beat the control group, a plain average of ordinary forecasts, by more than 60%, and beat a prediction market of intelligence analysts by about 25 to 30% (review dated 2019 to 2020, [AI Impacts](https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/)).

[DURABLE] Forecasting skill is persistent and trainable. Superforecasters were the top 2% of forecasters, about 70% kept that status the following year, and year-to-year performance correlated at 0.65; a one-hour training module improved accuracy for at least a year; and superforecasters' average gap between stated probability and observed frequency was 0.01 (review dated 2019 to 2020, [AI Impacts](https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/)).

[DURABLE] Correlates of accuracy in the Mellers et al. 2015 model (multiple correlation 0.64) were domain knowledge, intelligence, active open-mindedness, team membership, more deliberation time and more updates per question (review dated 2019 to 2020, [AI Impacts](https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/)). For agents the transferable levers are deliberation, team review and frequent updating.

[CONTESTED] The project's aggregation weighted better forecasters more heavily and then "extremized" the pooled forecast toward 0 or 1; the review calls the value of extremizing disputed and notes that aggregation helped ordinary forecasters most (review dated 2019 to 2020, [AI Impacts](https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/)). Do not extremize pooled agent forecasts by default.

[DURABLE] Keep forecast horizons short. Superforecasters looking 300 days out beat regular forecasters looking 100 days out, expert accuracy on geopolitics fell toward chance at five years, and forecasts are described as reasonable to about two years (review dated 2019 to 2020, [AI Impacts](https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/)). Beyond about two years, switch from point forecasts to scenarios.

[VENDOR] Good Judgment Inc., describing its own research, attributes superforecasters' accuracy gain over regular forecasters roughly half to noise reduction, a quarter to better information and a quarter to bias reduction, says teams outperform individuals and prediction markets, and states that "the best LLM lags Superforecasters by 19%" without defining the metric or giving a date ([Good Judgment, The science of superforecasting](https://goodjudgment.com/about/the-science-of-superforecasting/)). Treat this as vendor-reported.

[DURABLE] Do not read small movements as signal. Pew's 2016 explainer shows that a 3-point move in a lead between two polls carries a margin of about plus or minus 8 points, and that consistent movement across several independent readings is more informative than one pair ([Pew Research Center, 2016](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2016/09/08/understanding-the-margin-of-error-in-election-polls/)).

[INFERENCE] Brier score, standard definition from general knowledge and not from a fetched page: the mean of squared differences between forecast probability and outcome, with the outcome coded 1 or 0. In this single-outcome form it runs from 0 (perfect) to 1 and always answering 50% scores 0.25; Brier's original form, used in the Good Judgment Project papers, sums over both outcomes and runs 0 to 2, so 50% scores 0.5. State which form is used whenever a score is quoted.

[INFERENCE] Forecast hygiene for agents: state each forecast as a dated, resolvable question with a numeric probability; write the reference class and its base rate first, then the adjusted figure, and record both in the forecast log; score later. For market forecasts, look up the reference class (for example growth and survival of comparable Swedish SaaS firms from filed annual reports) before using the company's own plan.

## 5. Competitive intelligence: public sources, honest methods, hard stops

### 5.1 Ethical baseline

[DURABLE] Hold competitive-intelligence work to the SCIP Code of Ethics as a minimum. Its seven points are to elevate the profession; comply with all applicable domestic and international laws; be transparent by disclosing identity, organisation and all relevant information before any interview; avoid conflicts of interest; give honest, realistic recommendations and conclusions; act as an ambassador for the code inside the company and with contractors; and stay aligned with company policies, objectives and guidelines ([SCIP, Code of Ethics](https://www.scip.org/page/CodeofEthics)). The page gave no date for the current code.

[CONTESTED] The SCIP code is criticised as vague at the points agents will hit. A CI consultancy blog argues that "all relevant information" and "interview" are undefined, that the code is unclear on visiting competitor sites, viewing LinkedIn profiles or registering for gated downloads with a throwaway address, and that disclosure does not settle the issue of obtaining information a competitor expects to keep confidential ([Aqute](https://www.aqute.com/blog/is-it-impossible-to-stick-to-scips-code-of-ethics)). This is opinion from an undated blog; it is the reason the department needs its own explicit rules.

[FAST-MOVING, 2025] The ICC/ESOMAR Code's fifth principle keeps overall responsibility and oversight with the researcher whatever method or technology is used, with contributors responsible in line with their activity, expertise and control; Article 10 requires fair competition and declared conflicts of interest; Article 4 requires researchers to identify themselves in primary data collection ([ESOMAR, ICC/ESOMAR Code](https://esomar.org/representation/icc-esomar-code-of-conduct)). Outsourcing or automating collection does not move the responsibility.

[DURABLE] Apply intelligence tradecraft to competitor assessments: state source quality, separate facts from assumptions and judgments, include analysis of alternatives, explain changes from earlier judgments and express uncertainty ([ICD 203](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)). Add the forecasting habit of acknowledging the strongest opposing case and deciding beforehand what evidence would change the assessment ([Good Judgment, 10 commandments](https://goodjudgment.com/philip-tetlocks-10-commandments-of-superforecasting/)).

### 5.2 Trade-secret law (EU and Sweden)

[DURABLE] EU Trade Secrets Directive 2016/943, checked on primary source: a trade secret is information that is secret, commercially valuable because it is secret, and subject to reasonable protective steps (Article 2(1)). Lawful acquisition includes independent discovery, studying or reverse-engineering a product that is publicly available or lawfully held, and other honest commercial practices (Article 3). Unlawful acquisition includes unauthorised access to or copying of documents or files and conduct contrary to honest commercial practices, and use is also unlawful for a recipient who knew or should have known the secret came from an unlawful source (Article 4) ([EUR-Lex 32016L0943](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016L0943)).

[DURABLE] Swedish lag (2018:558) om företagshemligheter, consolidated to SFS 2025:1281, checked on primary source: under 2 § a trade secret is information on a trader's or research institution's business or operating conditions that is not generally known or readily accessible to those who normally handle such information, that the holder has taken reasonable steps to keep secret, and whose disclosure is likely to cause competitive harm. An employee's ordinary experience and skills are excluded, and information about a crime or other serious wrongdoing is not a trade secret ([riksdagen.se SFS 2018:558](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2018558-om-foretagshemligheter_sfs-2018-558/)).

[DURABLE] Under the Swedish trade-secrets act, checked on primary source, an attack is acquiring, using or disclosing the secret without the holder's consent (3 §), and only unauthorised attacks are covered; disclosure to publish or report suspected crime punishable by prison, or other wrongdoing in the public interest, is never unauthorised (4 §) ([riksdagen.se SFS 2018:558](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2018558-om-foretagshemligheter_sfs-2018-558/)).

[DURABLE] Criminal liability under the Swedish trade-secrets act, checked on primary source: företagsspioneri, intentionally and unlawfully obtaining access to a trade secret, carries a fine or prison up to 2 years, and 6 months to 6 years if gross (26 §); unlawful use or disclosure of a technical trade secret obtained through employment or a business relationship carries a fine or up to 2 years, 6 months to 6 years if gross, with no liability in minor cases (26 a §); olovlig befattning, intentionally acquiring a secret knowing that the provider or someone earlier in the chain obtained it by företagsspioneri, carries a fine or up to 2 years, up to 4 years if gross (27 §) ([riksdagen.se SFS 2018:558](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2018558-om-foretagshemligheter_sfs-2018-558/)).

[DURABLE] Damages under the Swedish trade-secrets act, checked on primary source: 5 to 11 §§ provide for damages, including 9 § liability for someone who knows or should know that an earlier party attacked the secret, and 24 § limits damages to harm in the five years before the claim ([riksdagen.se SFS 2018:558](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2018558-om-foretagshemligheter_sfs-2018-558/)).

[INFERENCE] The Swedish act puts criminal liability on two points an agent can hit: getting access (26 §) and taking delivery of something already stolen (27 §). Because 9 § uses a knows-or-should-know test, an agent log showing that a document looked confidential and was still used would be evidence against the company. If a leaked or confidential-looking competitor document surfaces, do not open it further, summarise it or store it; stop and report to Legal.

[INFERENCE] Lawful competitive intelligence in Sweden follows from 2 §: information that is generally known or readily accessible is not a trade secret, so public pricing pages, filings, patents, job ads and marketing material are safe on trade-secret grounds. Copyright, database right, contract terms and GDPR still apply to how that material is collected and reused.

### 5.3 Competition law and inside information

[DURABLE] Swedish competition guidance, checked on a primary source but thinly: Konkurrensverket's 2015 brochure tells companies not to exchange strategically important information and not to exchange information on prices or other sensitive, strategic information with competitors; only these two lines were extracted ([Konkurrensverket, 2015](https://www.konkurrensverket.se/globalassets/dokument/informationsmaterial/rapporter-och-broschyrer/informationsmaterial/konkurrera_pa_ratt_satt2015.pdf)).

[INFERENCE] [GAP] Competition-law line, unverified background: collecting a competitor's public information unilaterally is permitted, while receiving or swapping non-public, forward-looking commercial information with a competitor, directly or through a customer, trade body or shared tool, is the risk. The EU Horizontal Guidelines 2023 chapter on information exchange (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:52023XC0721(01)) and konkurrenslagen (2008:579) 2 kap. 1 § were not read. Agents must never contact a competitor to ask for pricing or plans and must not relay information between competitors.

[DURABLE] EU Market Abuse Regulation 596/2014, checked on primary source: inside information is precise, non-public, related to an issuer or instrument and likely to affect prices significantly (Article 7); trading on it, recommending on its basis and disclosing it outside normal duties are prohibited (Articles 8, 10 and 14); issuers and those acting for them keep insider lists, retained five years (Article 18) ([EUR-Lex 32014R0596](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014R0596)).

[DURABLE] Research built on public data is not in itself inside information, per recital 28 of the Market Abuse Regulation, checked on primary source, but it can become so where the market routinely expects its publication or it comes from a recognised commentator able to move prices ([EUR-Lex 32014R0596](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014R0596)).

[FAST-MOVING, 22 Oct 2025] A Danish court in BoligPortal v ReData (22 October 2025) treated scraping a competitor's database as contrary to good marketing practice as well as an infringement of database right; secondary source only ([Taylor Wessing tracker](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/campaigns/de/2025/ai-and-copyright-tracker)). This is the only Nordic ruling in the research and shows that scraping a competitor carries marketing-law risk in a neighbouring jurisdiction.

[INFERENCE] For a listed employer or client, internal non-public research material may itself need insider-list handling; agents should not mix it into outputs meant for wider circulation, and non-public information about any listed company is a stop-and-escalate trigger.

### 5.4 Competitor profiles, tracking and win/loss

[FAST-MOVING, 3 Feb 2025] Free inputs for a Swedish competitor profile are registration data, status, SNI codes and digitally filed annual reports from Bolagsverket and SCB, free since 3 February 2025 ([Bolagsverket](https://bolagsverket.se/apierochoppnadata/hamtaforetagsinformation/vardefulladatamangder.5294.html)), patent families and legal-event data from Espacenet ([EPO](https://www.epo.org/en/searching-for-patents/technical/espacenet)), and public funding records from Vinnova ([Vinnova](https://www.vinnova.se/om-oss/om-webbplatsen/oppna-data/)).

[INFERENCE] Competitor profile template, unsourced common practice: identity and ownership; financials from filings; product and feature matrix; pricing and packaging with dated screenshots; target segments and positioning; go-to-market and partners; hiring signals from job ads; technology and intellectual property; customer evidence from reviews and case studies; strategy hypotheses with confidence; indicators of what would change the view; and a last-verified date per field.

[INFERENCE] Pricing and feature tracking: capture dated snapshots of public pricing pages, changelogs, release notes and documentation on a fixed schedule; record changes as events with date, before, after and source URL; and never infer discounting from list prices alone. Unsourced common practice.

[VENDOR] [FAST-MOVING, 20 Jul 2026] Klue, a competitive-intelligence software vendor, defines win/loss analysis as the systematic practice of finding out why a buyer chose you or a competitor and using it for positioning, messaging and product decisions; its data sources are structured buyer interviews, seller interviews, surveys, sales-call analysis and the CRM record ([Klue, 20 Jul 2026](https://klue.com/topics/what-is-win-loss-analysis-how-to-build-a-program)).

[VENDOR] [FAST-MOVING, 20 Jul 2026] Klue's four programme steps for win/loss are to define coverage first (every closed competitive deal, a sample, or only escalated deals, with programmes tending to slide to the last); match method to deal stakes (human interviews for strategic accounts, AI-led interviews for breadth, automated call and CRM analysis for patterns); name an owner who can update battlecards, brief sellers and flag product issues; and set a cadence that revisits top competitors at least quarterly and sooner after a major competitor move ([Klue, 20 Jul 2026](https://klue.com/topics/what-is-win-loss-analysis-how-to-build-a-program)).

[VENDOR] [FAST-MOVING, 20 Jul 2026] Klue names three win/loss pitfalls: measuring activity such as interviews done and reports delivered instead of outcomes, having no route from finding to update, and limiting coverage to the deals sellers remember to flag ([Klue, 20 Jul 2026](https://klue.com/topics/what-is-win-loss-analysis-how-to-build-a-program)).

[INFERENCE] Win/loss practice for the department, unsourced: interview buyers from both won and lost deals soon after the decision, ideally by someone outside the sales team, with a consistent guide covering trigger, alternatives considered, decision criteria and weights, perceived strengths and weaknesses, the role of price and the decision process; code reasons thematically; report rates by segment and competitor with counts; and avoid percentages on fewer than about 30 deals, which is the researchers' rule of thumb. Coverage bias is a sampling problem, so report which deals were eligible, which were covered and how they were selected.

[INFERENCE] A win/loss or customer interview must not turn into a sales pitch without fresh consent, because ICC/ESOMAR Article 1 requires research to be kept clearly separate from non-research activity such as selling ([ESOMAR](https://esomar.org/representation/icc-esomar-code-of-conduct)); interviews run by sales staff sit close to this line.

[INFERENCE] [GAP] Battlecards, war-gaming and monitoring cadence have no retrieved source. As unsourced defaults: a battlecard is one page per competitor covering where we win, where we lose, objection responses, proof points, pricing position, last-updated date and owner, with every claim linked to evidence and a review date; a war-game role-plays the company, two or three competitors and the customer over two or three move rounds and yields likely responses and early-warning indicators, not a prediction; a suggested cadence is continuous alerts for pricing, product, funding and leadership events, a weekly digest, a monthly battlecard review, a quarterly profile refresh and win/loss synthesis, and an annual war-game.

## 6. Survey and interview research: stable craft, unstable samples

### 6.1 Questionnaire design

[DURABLE] Write survey questions about one concept at a time in short, simple language; avoid biased wording; watch for social-desirability and yes-saying; put general questions before specific ones on a topic; and make response options mutually exclusive, exhaustive and logically ordered, with "don't know" or neutral options where appropriate ([AAPOR Best Practices](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/)).

[DURABLE] To measure change, do not change the measure. Hold wording, framing and mode constant, and if a change is unavoidable run a split-ballot experiment to quantify its effect ([AAPOR Best Practices](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/)). Random assignment to split forms is the way to attribute a difference to wording itself ([Pew, Writing survey questions](https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions/)).

[DURABLE] Wording moves results by tens of points. In a Pew example from January 2003, support for military action in Iraq fell from 68% to 43% when a clause about casualties was added ([Pew, Writing survey questions](https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions/)).

[DURABLE] Closed and open questions give different answers. In a 2008 Pew poll, 58% picked "the economy" when it was listed against 35% who volunteered it unprompted, and only 8% gave an unlisted answer in the closed form against 43% in the open form ([Pew, Writing survey questions](https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions/)).

[DURABLE] Keep opinion response lists to about four or five options, while factual and demographic items can carry more; a 2019 Pew study found forced-choice items more accurate than select-all-that-apply, especially on sensitive topics ([Pew, Writing survey questions](https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions/)).

[DURABLE] Control response-order effects. Phone respondents favour later options and self-administered respondents favour earlier ones, so randomise unordered lists; never randomise an ordinal scale, but reverse it for a random half of respondents ([Pew, Writing survey questions](https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions/)).

[DURABLE] Control question-order effects. Pew documents a contrast effect (45% versus 37% support for civil unions in 2003, depending on whether a marriage question came first) and an assimilation effect (81% versus 66% on a 2008 bipartisanship item), and notes that closed questions placed before an open one seed its answers ([Pew, Writing survey questions](https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions/)).

[DURABLE] Replace agree/disagree items with a forced choice between two statements, because agree/disagree formats attract more agreement from less-educated and less-informed respondents ([Pew, Writing survey questions](https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions/)).

[DURABLE] Expect social-desirability bias: respondents understate drug and alcohol use and tax evasion and overstate voting, giving and attendance, more so with an interviewer than in web or paper modes; mitigate with face-saving wording ([Pew, Writing survey questions](https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions/)).

[DURABLE] Pretest every questionnaire with cognitive interviews among people like the target sample, then pilot every procedure from recruitment to data cleaning, and use experiments to choose between wordings ([AAPOR Best Practices](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/)).

[DURABLE] Order the questionnaire for flow: group by topic, open with engaging items, do not stack difficult questions, and put demographics late unless they are needed for screening ([Pew, Writing survey questions](https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions/)). Disclose sensitive content in advance, allow skipping, start with easy questions and keep it short ([AAPOR Best Practices](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/)).

[INFERENCE] Scale advice that the researchers could not source: use 5- or 7-point fully labelled scales for attitudes and keep direction consistent.

### 6.2 Sampling, sample size and margin of error

[DURABLE] Probability samples remain the traditional standard; non-probability samples such as opt-in panels need special statistical techniques and careful transparency, and the sampling frame should match the target population ([AAPOR Best Practices](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/)).

[DURABLE] Report the margin of sampling error for probability surveys and a credibility interval for non-probability surveys, and be transparent about weighting ([AAPOR Best Practices](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/)).

[DURABLE] Benchmarks for margin of error at 95% confidence: a sample of 1,067 gives about plus or minus 3 points and a subgroup of about 160 gives about plus or minus 8 ([Pew, 2016](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2016/09/08/understanding-the-margin-of-error-in-election-polls/)).

[DURABLE] The margin on the gap between two shares in the same sample is roughly double the single-share margin, so plus or minus 3 on each share becomes about plus or minus 6 on the lead, and plus or minus 8 becomes about plus or minus 16 in the subgroup ([Pew, 2016](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2016/09/08/understanding-the-margin-of-error-in-election-polls/)).

[DURABLE] Weighting corrects non-response bias but increases variance through the design effect and shrinks the effective sample size, and the reported margin should include it. Margin of error covers sampling error only and not coverage error, mode effects, misreporting or question wording ([Pew, 2016](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2016/09/08/understanding-the-margin-of-error-in-election-polls/)).

[DURABLE] Size samples for the difference you need to detect. The NIST handbook gives, for a two-sided test of a change in a proportion, N at least [(z for 1 minus alpha over 2, times the root of p0(1 − p0), plus z for 1 minus beta, times the root of p1(1 − p1)), divided by delta] squared, where delta is the absolute difference between p1 and p0, with a continuity correction adding 1/delta. Its worked example, detecting a move from 10% to 20% with one-sided alpha of 0.05 and 90% power, needs about 102 observations, or 112 with the correction ([NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc242.htm)).

[DURABLE] Reduce non-response with incentives, multiple contacts at different times and by different methods, and varied messages ([AAPOR Best Practices](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/)).

[INFERENCE] Standard margin-of-error formula, not fetched in the research but numerically consistent with Pew's 1,067 figure: at 95% confidence the margin for a proportion is 1.96 times the square root of p(1 − p)/n, worst case at p = 0.5, which gives required samples of about 96 for plus or minus 10 points, 384 for 5, 600 for 4, 1,067 for 3 and 2,401 for 2. Multiply by the design effect when weighting, and for small populations apply the finite population correction, adjusted n = n / (1 + (n − 1)/N).

[INFERENCE] B2B SaaS reality: the population of target accounts is often a few hundred to a few thousand, response rates are low and respondents are not a random sample, so margin-of-error arithmetic overstates precision. Report counts alongside percentages and label findings from fewer than about 30 respondents per cell as directional; the cut-off is the researchers' rule of thumb.

### 6.3 Fraud and AI-generated responses, 2025 to 2026

[FAST-MOVING, April 2026] Treat online sample integrity as the main data-quality risk. NORC's April 2026 literature review reports industry estimates of fraud at 15 to 30% of market-research responses and up to 45% on some platforms, one case where usable responses fell from about 75% to 10%, and openly recruited, incentivised surveys with 94 to 95% or more fraudulent or bot responses ([NORC, April 2026](https://www.norc.org/content/dam/norc-org/pdf2026/cpss-research-brief-fraud-lit-review.pdf)).

[FAST-MOVING, 2025] Standard quality checks no longer stop AI respondents. NORC reports that Westwood (2025, PNAS) built an LLM-based synthetic respondent that passed 99.8% of standard data-quality checks ([NORC, April 2026](https://www.norc.org/content/dam/norc-org/pdf2026/cpss-research-brief-fraud-lit-review.pdf)). The figure is cited through NORC; the original paper could not be opened.

[FAST-MOVING, 2025] Real participants also use AI. In one 2025 study reported by NORC, 34% of active online survey participants said they used LLMs to help answer open-ended questions; LLM answers are more homogeneous and can suppress real variation, and response-time signals are less reliable when AI assists ([NORC, April 2026](https://www.norc.org/content/dam/norc-org/pdf2026/cpss-research-brief-fraud-lit-review.pdf)).

[FAST-MOVING, April 2026] Fraud types named by NORC are identity falsification and repeat entries, bots that mimic human timing and evade honeypots, CAPTCHA and IP checks, use of VPNs and CAPTCHA-solving services, and hybrid attacks where a human starts and a bot finishes. Question-specific transformer classifiers reached up to 100% accuracy on the question they were tuned for but transferred poorly across questions (Claassen et al., 2026, via [NORC, April 2026](https://www.norc.org/content/dam/norc-org/pdf2026/cpss-research-brief-fraud-lit-review.pdf)).

[FAST-MOVING, April 2026] NORC's recommended defence is layered, multi-stage checking: reviews tailored to the instrument and population because generic AI detectors and static rules often fail; care not to over-screen legitimate respondents with low digital literacy; collected paradata; pre-specified and disclosed exclusion rules; targeted human review; "reverse shibboleth" questions that the real target population should get wrong; the CATCH framework (configure, assess, triage, corroborate, hone; Stemmer et al., 2026); and probability-based panels with verified contact and PIN entry, which lower but do not remove risk ([NORC, April 2026](https://www.norc.org/content/dam/norc-org/pdf2026/cpss-research-brief-fraud-lit-review.pdf)).

[CONTESTED] [VENDOR] [FAST-MOVING, April 2026] A PsyArXiv preprint from early April 2026, with its lead author at Prolific and funded by Prolific and not peer-reviewed, found fewer than 1% of about 4,800 responses from 12 sample providers flagged as likely non-human text, against about 16% of roughly 400 from a thirteenth provider, Amazon Mechanical Turk. Detection used Prolific's proprietary checker, which outside researchers cannot use; Westwood said the claims cannot be verified externally, and another researcher argued that human fraud remains the larger problem ([Retraction Watch, 30 Apr 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/04/30/are-ai-chatbots-infiltrating-online-survey-data-not-yet-says-new-study/)).

[INFERENCE] The under-1% figure and the 15 to 30% figure are not directly comparable: they cover different populations (vetted panellists versus the whole market), use different definitions (AI-written text versus any fraud), and the lower figure comes from an interested party using an unverifiable tool. Agents should cite both and not pick one.

[FAST-MOVING, 30 Apr 2026] A VU Amsterdam expert panel reported on 30 April 2026 that CAPTCHA is becoming obsolete as a bot filter; that bot networks farming paid panels and ordinary participants pasting open-ends into chatbots are two distinct contaminations needing different countermeasures; that AI text tends to inflate effect sizes, over-confirm hypotheses and misrepresent minorities; that embedded "poison pill" instructions, keystroke and response-time metadata and copy-paste blocking are partial and temporary; and that panel providers should be responsible for verifying respondents ([VU Amsterdam, 30 Apr 2026](https://vu.nl/en/news/2026/ai-and-the-integrity-of-survey-responses)). This is expert opinion.

[DURABLE] Data cleaning is part of the analysis. NORC reports a 2005 study in which removing an 11% share of fraudulent responses changed which results were statistically significant, and notes that LLM-generated answers reduce natural variance ([NORC, April 2026](https://www.norc.org/content/dam/norc-org/pdf2026/cpss-research-brief-fraud-lit-review.pdf)).

[INFERENCE] Minimum 2026 data-quality protocol for any online survey the department commissions, derived from NORC's recommendations: pre-register exclusion rules; prefer the company's own customer lists or verified or probability panels over open links, and never recruit incentivised surveys through open social posts; run layered checks for device and IP duplication, speeding, straight-lining, consistency pairs, at least one open-end reviewed for templated or LLM-style text, and a reverse-shibboleth item; have a human review flagged cases; and report the number screened, the number removed and why.

[GAP] No reliable source was found on the validity of synthetic respondents or LLM-simulated panels as a substitute for human samples, beyond the ICC/ESOMAR disclosure duty. Until one exists, synthetic respondents must not be reported as survey evidence.

### 6.4 Disclosure and respondent duties

[DURABLE] Every survey report discloses sample size, margin of error or credibility interval, weighting, full question text and response options, mode, target population, and sample construction and recruitment, following the AAPOR Transparency Initiative list as summarised on the best-practices page ([AAPOR Best Practices](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/)).

[FAST-MOVING, 2025] ICC/ESOMAR Article 4 on primary data collection requires researchers to identify themselves, to disclose at the start any synthetic persona used in an interaction, to keep participation voluntary, to limit re-contact, to give a retention statement, and to honour the right to withdraw and have data deleted ([ESOMAR, ICC/ESOMAR Code](https://esomar.org/representation/icc-esomar-code-of-conduct)).

[FAST-MOVING, 2025] ICC/ESOMAR Article 1 (duty of care) gives participants the right to decline, requires that participation cause no harm, and requires research to be kept clearly separate from non-research activity such as selling, with separate consent. Article 2 requires parental or guardian consent for children and special care for vulnerable people, and Article 3 limits collection to personal data relevant to the research purpose ([ESOMAR, ICC/ESOMAR Code](https://esomar.org/representation/icc-esomar-code-of-conduct)).

[INFERENCE] An AI interviewer or AI-moderated chat must say it is an AI at the start of every interview, on the basis of ICC/ESOMAR Article 4 on synthetic personas. The researchers also point to the EU AI Act Article 50 transparency duty, but its text was not read in this research, so treat that legal basis as unverified and confirm it with Legal.

[INFERENCE] The ICC/ESOMAR Code binds ESOMAR members and those who sign up to it; for a SaaS company's internal research function it is a voluntary benchmark unless an agency it hires is a member. The membership effect was not checked on the page.

### 6.5 Interviews, focus groups and qualitative sample size

[DURABLE] Size interview studies by saturation. At a new-information threshold of 5% or less, saturation was typically reached at 6 to 7 interviews in homogeneous samples and up to 11 to 12 in a more varied one, at which point roughly 78 to 82% (homogeneous) or 62 to 71% (varied) of all themes in the full dataset had been found ([PLOS ONE, Guest, Namey and Chen 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0232076)). Report saturation as base size, run length and threshold, for example "5% or less new information at 6+2".

[DURABLE] For expert and stakeholder input, the Futures Toolkit offers two structured techniques: "Seven Questions", a structured stakeholder interview, and Delphi, which gathers expert opinion over anonymous rounds ([Futures Toolkit HTML](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/futures-toolkit-for-policy-makers-and-analysts/the-futures-toolkit-html)).

[INFERENCE] [GAP] Interview and focus-group method has no retrieved source. As unsourced defaults: use a semi-structured guide of 6 to 10 open questions, ask about past concrete behaviour and not hypotheticals, avoid leading questions, record and transcribe with consent, and debrief immediately. Focus groups, commonly described as 6 to 8 people for 60 to 90 minutes, suit idea generation and language discovery but are prone to dominance and conformity effects and should not be used to estimate incidence. A lead to open next time is https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-interviews/.

### 6.6 Pricing research and choice methods

[DURABLE] Van Westendorp's Price Sensitivity Meter asks four questions: at what price the product is too expensive to consider, so cheap that quality is doubted, starting to seem expensive but still worth considering, and a bargain. The cumulative curves intersect at the point of marginal cheapness (too cheap crossing expensive, the lower bound), the point of marginal expensiveness (too expensive crossing cheap, the upper bound), the indifference price point (expensive crossing cheap) and the optimal price point (too cheap crossing too expensive); the acceptable range runs between the two marginal points ([Wikipedia, Van Westendorp's Price Sensitivity Meter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Westendorp%27s_Price_Sensitivity_Meter)). Read on a tertiary source.

[DURABLE] Van Westendorp measures price perception and not demand; interpretations of the intersections vary and are advocates' claims, not established results; and the method assumes respondents can imagine the price landscape. The Newton-Miller-Smith extension adds purchase-likelihood questions on a 1 to 5 scale at the "expensive" and "cheap" prices to derive a revenue curve ([Wikipedia, Van Westendorp's Price Sensitivity Meter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Westendorp%27s_Price_Sensitivity_Meter)).

[INFERENCE] [GAP] Method selector, unsourced: MaxDiff (best-worst scaling) to rank many features or messages; choice-based conjoint to estimate trade-offs and willingness to pay across feature-and-price bundles; Gabor-Granger for a simple demand curve on a defined product; Van Westendorp for an early acceptable-price range on a new concept; and monadic or sequential-monadic concept tests to compare ideas against a benchmark. All stated-preference methods should be checked against behavioural data such as trials, price tests and win/loss. Conjoint and MaxDiff sample-size rules (Sawtooth), Gabor-Granger and concept testing were not opened; do not quote rules of thumb for them from memory.

## 7. Analysis: effect sizes, honest causal claims and logged assumptions

### 7.1 Statistical inference

[DURABLE] Follow the American Statistical Association's 2016 statement on p-values: a p-value indicates how incompatible the data are with a specified model; it does not measure the probability that the hypothesis is true or that the data arose by chance alone; conclusions and business or policy decisions should not rest only on whether p crosses a threshold; proper inference needs full reporting and transparency; a p-value does not measure effect size or importance; and by itself it is not a good measure of evidence ([ASA, 7 March 2016](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/p-valuestatement.pdf)).

[DURABLE] Screen every analysis, the department's own and those in papers it reads, against the ten common statistical mistakes in eLife (9 October 2019): no adequate control group; claiming two effects differ without comparing them directly; inflated units of analysis; spurious correlations; underpowered groups; circular analysis; analytic flexibility or p-hacking; uncorrected multiple comparisons; over-reading non-significant results; and correlation read as causation ([eLife, Makin and Orban de Xivry, 2019](https://elifesciences.org/articles/48175)).

[DURABLE] The table lists the ten common statistical mistakes with the remedy the eLife authors give for each.

| Label | Mistake | Remedy | Date | Link |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [DURABLE] | No adequate control group | Treat conclusions as tentative | 9 Oct 2019 | [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/48175) |
| [DURABLE] | Claiming two effects differ without comparing them | Test the difference directly | 9 Oct 2019 | [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/48175) |
| [DURABLE] | Inflated units of analysis | Mixed-effects models | 9 Oct 2019 | [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/48175) |
| [DURABLE] | Spurious correlations | Robust correlation methods; check outliers | 9 Oct 2019 | [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/48175) |

| [DURABLE] | Circular analysis | Define criteria in advance, independent of the data | 9 Oct 2019 | [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/48175) |

| [DURABLE] | Uncorrected multiple comparisons | Disclose all measures and correct | 9 Oct 2019 | [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/48175) |

| [DURABLE] | Correlation read as causation | Test third variables and competing models, or randomise | 9 Oct 2019 | [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/48175) |

[DURABLE] Give differences their own error margin and expect chance outliers. The margin on a difference between two shares is about double that of each share, subgroup margins are far wider (about plus or minus 8 at n near 160), and with 95% intervals about 1 in 20 estimates miss by more than the margin through sampling alone; Pew's example is roughly 30 of 590 polls ([Pew, 2016](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2016/09/08/understanding-the-margin-of-error-in-election-polls/)).

[DURABLE] Follow AAPOR's coding and tabulation hygiene: record partial responses, separate unanswered items from substantive "none" or "don't know" answers, and show counts or percentages in cross-tabs with columns totalling 100% ([AAPOR Best Practices](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/)).

[INFERENCE] Descriptive and inferential minimum, textbook practice not sourced in the research: report n, centre (mean, plus median when skewed), spread and distribution shape; for B2B revenue-type data use medians and show top-customer concentration; and always give an interval estimate and an effect size alongside any p-value, which is the practical consequence of ASA principles 3, 5 and 6.

[GAP] [INFERENCE] Effect-size conventions commonly attributed to Cohen (d of about 0.2 small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large; r of about 0.1, 0.3, 0.5) were not verified; the intended source is https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3444174/. They are context-free rules of thumb; in business, translate an effect into money or customers. Simpson's paradox, base-rate neglect, survivorship bias and regression to the mean were not sourced either.

[INFERENCE] Causal-claim ladder for agents: rung one, an association is observed; rung two, it survives controls for obvious confounders and is temporally ordered; rung three, a natural experiment or quasi-experimental design; rung four, a randomised experiment. State which rung the evidence reaches and never write "drives", "causes" or "leads to" below rung three without a hedge. Consistent with the eLife list's tenth mistake.

### 7.2 Qualitative analysis and mixed methods

[DURABLE] Use Braun and Clarke's reflexive thematic analysis as the reference process for qualitative data: familiarise with the dataset; code; generate initial themes; develop and review themes; refine, define and name themes; write up. The phases are guidance and not rigid steps, the process is recursive, a theme is a pattern of shared meaning organised around a central concept, and the authors describe analysis as skilled, situated and subjective ([Braun and Clarke, Doing reflexive TA](https://www.thematicanalysis.net/doing-reflexive-ta/)).

[INFERENCE] Qualitative coding workflow for agent teams: two workers code a shared subset independently, reconcile a codebook and then code the rest; keep verbatim quotes linked to participant IDs; count how many participants, not mentions, support each theme; and report disconfirming cases. This codebook-and-agreement style differs from Braun and Clarke's reflexive approach, which treats researcher subjectivity as a resource; that contrast comes from the researchers' background knowledge and not from the fetched page.

[GAP] No source was retrieved on the reliability of AI-assisted qualitative coding as of 2026, which is a real hole for an AI research department. Until it is filled, LLM-coded themes need human spot-checks against the transcripts and a note in the methods section.

[INFERENCE] [GAP] Mixed-methods designs, unsourced: exploratory sequential (interviews, then a survey to size themes), explanatory sequential (survey, then interviews to explain a number) and convergent (both in parallel, then compared). Integrate at the conclusion level by saying where qualitative and quantitative evidence agree, where they diverge and what explains the divergence.

### 7.3 Assumptions, sensitivity and model health

[DURABLE] Keep an assumptions log with source, owner and reliability for every analysis, together with any sensitivity analysis; document what the analysis can and cannot be used for; explain the effect of uncertainty on outputs; and set a review schedule or a sunset date after which the analysis should not be used ([Aqua Book revision, Delivery, communication and sign-off](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/delivery_and_communication.html)).

[DURABLE] For black-box and AI models used in analysis, monitor for data drift and report model health, and have the approver confirm that risks and ethics were addressed ([Aqua Book revision, Delivery, communication and sign-off](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/delivery_and_communication.html)).

[INFERENCE] Sensitivity-analysis minimum: vary each key assumption one at a time across its plausible range and show which ones flip the recommendation; add best, base and worst cases for correlated assumptions; and report the break-even value of the decisive variable. Only the requirement that sensitivity analysis accompany the assumptions log is sourced, to the Aqua Book; the specifics are common practice.

## 8. Reporting: answer first, likelihood on a named scale, confidence apart

### 8.1 Probability language

[DURABLE] Express likelihood with a fixed vocabulary tied to published percentage ranges, and name the scale in the report. Two official scales exist and they differ. The US ICD 203 bands, confirmed on the PDF text, are: almost no chance or remote, 1 to 5%; very unlikely or highly improbable, 5 to 20%; unlikely or improbable, 20 to 45%; roughly even chance or roughly even odds, 45 to 55%; likely or probable, 55 to 80%; very likely or highly probable, 80 to 95%; almost certain or nearly certain, 95 to 99% ([ICD 203](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)).

[DURABLE] [FAST-MOVING, 20 Jul 2026] The UK probability yardstick, read on the official page (first published 24 March 2025 per page metadata, updated 20 July 2026), is: remote chance, above 0% to about 5%; highly unlikely, about 10 to 20%; unlikely, about 25 to 35%; realistic possibility, about 40% to under 50%; likely or probable, about 55 to 75%; highly likely, about 80 to 90%; almost certain, about 95% to under 100%. The gaps between bands are deliberate ([GOV.UK, Explaining Uncertainty in UK Intelligence Assessment](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment)). An earlier Defence Intelligence note of 17 February 2023 explains that the non-contiguous ranges avoid a false impression of accuracy and were informed by research on how readers understand the terms ([GOV.UK, Defence Intelligence](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/defence-intelligence-communicating-probability)).

[DURABLE] Keep likelihood and confidence in separate sentences. Under ICD 203, likelihood describes the event and confidence describes the analyst's basis for the judgment, and the two must not be combined in one sentence ([ICD 203](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)). The UK page likewise treats analytical confidence as a separate high, moderate or low rating reflecting how sound and stable the basis for the likelihood judgment is ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment)).

[DURABLE] Give numbers where possible. Tetlock's advice is to express judgments as specific numeric probabilities and to practise distinguishing fine gradations ([Good Judgment, 10 commandments](https://goodjudgment.com/philip-tetlocks-10-commandments-of-superforecasting/)).

[INFERENCE] The same word means different things on the two scales: "unlikely" is about 25 to 35% on the UK scale and 20 to 45% on the US scale, "likely" is 55 to 75% against 55 to 80%, and "realistic possibility" has no US twin. The researchers recommend that the department pick one scale, print its table in every report template, never mix scales, and use the UK yardstick wording with the number in brackets, for example "likely (about 60%)".

[INFERENCE] Proposed house definitions of confidence: high means multiple independent high-quality sources or a primary source read and found to support the claim, with minimal assumptions; moderate means credible but limited or partly corroborated sources, such as one reputable secondary source or two that agree; low means sparse, uncorroborated or conflicting sources, or inference. These are the researchers' proposals modelled on intelligence usage; neither ICD 203 nor the UK page as read defines the levels.

### 8.2 What a trustworthy report contains

[DURABLE] Meet ICD 203's standards. Its five analytic standards are that analysis is objective, independent of political consideration, timely, based on all available sources, and implements the tradecraft standards. The nine tradecraft standards are: source quality and credibility described; uncertainties expressed and explained; underlying information distinguished from assumptions and judgments; analysis of alternatives; customer relevance and implications; clear and logical argumentation; change from or consistency with prior judgments explained; accurate judgments; and effective visual information where appropriate ([ICD 203](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)).

[DURABLE] Communicate assumptions, limits and uncertainty to each audience. The Aqua Book requires communicating assumptions (logged with source, ownership and reliability), limitations (what the analysis can and cannot be used for) and the effect of uncertainty, tailoring communication to each audience, and setting review dates or a sunset date ([Aqua Book revision, Delivery, communication and sign-off](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/delivery_and_communication.html)).

[DURABLE] Structure uncertainty work with the UK government Uncertainty Toolkit for Analysts: jointly agree how uncertainty will be used; define and identify it; mitigate; understand and measure; present and communicate, with a one-page decision-maker summary and an emphasis on tailoring to audience and decision ([Uncertainty Toolkit for Analysts in Government](https://analystsuncertaintytoolkit.github.io/UncertaintyWeb/index.html)). The page showed no date or version.

[FAST-MOVING, 2025] Disclose AI and synthetic data. ICC/ESOMAR Article 7 requires research to be fit for purpose with limitations stated, and clients to be told when AI, emerging technologies, synthetic data or synthetic personas are used, the extent of human oversight, and that client material is kept in secure environments. Article 9 requires published findings to carry enough method and source information to assess the conclusions, to disclose any significant role of AI or synthetic data and the extent of human oversight, and not to spread conclusions the data do not support ([ESOMAR, ICC/ESOMAR Code](https://esomar.org/representation/icc-esomar-code-of-conduct)).

[FAST-MOVING, 2025] Check outputs for intellectual-property problems before release. ICC/ESOMAR Article 11 requires compliance with law and local codes, applying the Code where it is stricter, and checking results for privacy and IP infringement, including copyright issues from AI training data ([ESOMAR, ICC/ESOMAR Code](https://esomar.org/representation/icc-esomar-code-of-conduct)). The researchers read this as a concrete review step: an AI-generated deliverable needs an originality and quotation check.

[DURABLE] Report honestly. SCIP's code requires truthful, realistic recommendations and conclusions ([SCIP Code of Ethics](https://www.scip.org/page/CodeofEthics)), and PRISMA 2020 requires reviews to publish the full search strategy, the screening process, any automation tools and whether data and code are available ([BMJ 2021;372:n71](https://www.bmj.com/content/372/bmj.n71)).

[INFERENCE] [GAP] BLUF (bottom line up front) and Minto's pyramid principle are unsourced in this knowledge base; neither page could be opened. As background practice: lead with the answer or recommendation, support it with three to five grouped arguments that do not overlap and together cover the case, each backed by evidence, with introductions following situation, complication, question, answer.

[INFERENCE] Recommended executive-summary template for agents, combining ICD 203 and Aqua Book requirements: the decision and recommendation in one sentence; three to five key findings, each with a likelihood term from the chosen scale and, in a separate sentence, a confidence level with its reason; what would change the assessment; limits of use and sunset date; and next steps.

[INFERENCE] Keep findings, judgments and recommendations in separate labelled sections. A finding is what the evidence shows and is traceable to sources; a judgment is the analyst's interpretation and carries likelihood and confidence; a recommendation is an action that also depends on the organisation's goals, costs and risk appetite. A reader should be able to accept the findings and reject the recommendation.

### 8.3 Charts

[DURABLE] Follow the UK Government Analysis Function chart guidance, published 19 May 2022. Give each chart a headline title stating the message plus a statistical subtitle, kept in body text and not inside the image; start bar charts at zero and never break the bar axis, while a line chart may break the y-axis with a clear symbol mentioned in the description ([Analysis Function, Data visualisation: charts](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/data-visualisation-charts/)).

[DURABLE] Respect the Analysis Function's limits (19 May 2022): at most about ten light-grey gridlines; direct labels in place of legends; never colour alone; at most four lines on a line chart, with small multiples beyond that; four categories per stack or cluster; pie charts only with five or fewer categories that sum to a meaningful whole; no 3D; and generally no dual axes ([Analysis Function, charts](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/data-visualisation-charts/)).

[DURABLE] Further Analysis Function rules (19 May 2022): rank bars by value unless there is a natural order; use horizontal bars for long labels; do not join line points across missing data; meet WCAG 2.2 A and AA, with a text alternative that gives the takeaway, or a data table, or both; and give a specific source with a direct link in body text and offer the data as a download ([Analysis Function, charts](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/data-visualisation-charts/)).

[INFERENCE] The chart limits translate into automatic output checks: reject any generated chart with a truncated bar axis, more than four lines, a dual axis, 3D, or no source line. Show uncertainty as intervals or ranges wherever an estimate is plotted, and give n, source and date with each chart.

### 8.4 Audit trail

[DURABLE] Keep an audit trail for every study. The Aqua Book lists specification and design documents; logs of data, assumptions and decisions; user and technical documentation; advice on uncertainty and limits of use; records of methods, dependencies, process maps, version control and error reporting; the code base if published; test plans and results; a statement of assurance; and a statement that ethical concerns were addressed ([Aqua Book revision, Delivery, communication and sign-off](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/delivery_and_communication.html)).

## 9. Academic and technology research: label the evidence grade

### 9.1 Systematic and rapid reviews

[DURABLE] Report systematic reviews to PRISMA 2020: a 27-item checklist in seven sections plus an abstract checklist. Compared with the 2009 version it adds full search strategies for all sources, reporting of reviewers, independence and automation at screening, certainty-of-evidence items, competing interests, and data and code availability; protocol and registration information sits in an "Other" section; and flow-diagram templates exist for original and updated reviews ([BMJ 2021;372:n71](https://www.bmj.com/content/372/bmj.n71)).

[FAST-MOVING, 6 Feb 2024] A rapid review, in Cochrane's 2024 definition, is an evidence synthesis that accelerates the systematic-review process by streamlining or omitting methods that the authors must clearly define; scope and methods vary by need ([BMJ 2024, Cochrane rapid review guidance](https://www.bmj.com/content/384/bmj-2023-076335)).

[FAST-MOVING, 6 Feb 2024] Cochrane's rapid-review shortcuts for screening: pilot on 50 to 100 records, have two reviewers screen at least 20% of records and check agreement, and let a single reviewer continue if kappa is at least 0.8; the guidance acknowledges that the 20% figure rests on experience and not evidence ([BMJ 2024, Cochrane rapid review guidance](https://www.bmj.com/content/384/bmj-2023-076335)).

[FAST-MOVING, 6 Feb 2024] Cochrane's rapid-review shortcuts for extraction and synthesis: extract only the most important fields, with one extractor and a second verifying critical data; reuse good existing systematic reviews instead of re-extracting primary studies; do not skip risk-of-bias assessment, but use one assessor with verification focused on the most important outcomes; write a narrative synthesis around the question's elements, with meta-analysis only if the data allow; and limit certainty grading to the main comparison and critical outcomes if time is short ([BMJ 2024, Cochrane rapid review guidance](https://www.bmj.com/content/384/bmj-2023-076335)).

[INFERENCE] Rapid-review protocol for a business question, adapted from Cochrane: write the question and inclusion criteria first; search two or more indexes plus targeted grey literature; log queries; calibrate two worker agents on 50 to 100 records, dual-screen 20% and proceed singly only at high agreement; use single extraction with second-agent verification of key numbers; rate each source's quality; write a narrative synthesis; and list every shortcut taken. Time-box to days or weeks, far under Cochrane's six-month ceiling; that duration is the researchers' estimate.

### 9.2 Reading papers and handling preprints

[DURABLE] When reading an empirical paper, look for the weaknesses the eLife list names: a missing control group, underpowered samples, flexible analysis, uncorrected multiple comparisons, circular analysis and causal language from correlations ([eLife, 2019](https://elifesciences.org/articles/48175)).

[FAST-MOVING, 30 Apr 2026] Use the April 2026 Prolific survey-bot study as the worked example of why preprints need labels: it was not peer-reviewed, was funded by and partly authored at the sample vendor concerned, used a proprietary detector unavailable to outside researchers, and was reported as news while an academic whose peer-reviewed paper it disputes said its claims cannot be externally verified ([Retraction Watch, 30 Apr 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/04/30/are-ai-chatbots-infiltrating-online-survey-data-not-yet-says-new-study/)).

[FAST-MOVING, December 2024] When assessing claims about AI tools, note that the UK government added a supplementary guide on impact evaluation of AI interventions to the Magenta Book set in December 2024 ([GOV.UK, The Magenta Book](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-magenta-book)). Its content was not read.

[INFERENCE] Paper triage for agents, background practice: read title, abstract, figures and conclusion first; then check venue and peer-review status, funding and conflicts, sample size and design, whether data and code are available, and whether the headline claim matches the results; only then read the methods in full. Keshav's three-pass method was not retrieved.

[INFERENCE] Preprint rule: cite a preprint as "[preprint, not peer-reviewed]", check whether a published version exists, never use one as the sole support for a decision-relevant claim, and lower confidence one level. Peer review is a filter and not a guarantee, so apply the eLife checklist either way. Several key sources in this knowledge base are themselves arXiv preprints and are labelled as such in section 11.

### 9.3 Technology readiness, scouting and patents

[DURABLE] Grade technology maturity on the EU Technology Readiness Level scale (Horizon 2020 General Annex G, 2014): TRL 1 basic principles observed; 2 technology concept formulated; 3 experimental proof of concept; 4 technology validated in lab; 5 technology validated in relevant environment; 6 technology demonstrated in relevant environment; 7 system prototype demonstration in operational environment; 8 system complete and qualified; 9 actual system proven in operational environment ([European Commission, H2020 Annex G](https://ec.europa.eu/research/participants/data/ref/h2020/wp/2014_2015/annexes/h2020-wp1415-annex-g-trl_en.pdf)). The current Horizon Europe wording was not checked.

[DURABLE] A patent landscape report gives an overview of patenting activity in a technology field in a country, a region or worldwide, and patent analytics has three stages: obtain the data, evaluate it for patterns, present the results ([WIPO, Patent analytics](https://www.wipo.int/web/patent-analytics)). Espacenet's families and legal-event data, with CPC classification as the search backbone, are the free working tools ([EPO, Espacenet](https://www.epo.org/en/searching-for-patents/technical/espacenet)).

[INFERENCE] [GAP] Patent-landscape cautions, not sourced in the research: applications normally publish about 18 months after filing, so the most recent period is under-counted; count patent families and not individual documents; patent volume is not commercial strength; combine CPC classes with keywords; and check legal status before treating a patent as a barrier. Verify the 18-month point before quoting it.

[INFERENCE] Technology-scouting sources for a Swedish SaaS company: scholarly indexes and preprint servers, Espacenet and PATENTSCOPE, funded-project databases (Vinnova, verified in section 3; EU CORDIS, not verified), standards bodies, open-source repositories, job ads and conference programmes. Give each candidate a TRL with a note of the evidence for it; because TRL was designed for hardware-style technologies, state what "operational environment" means for software, for example production use at paying customers. No source was retrieved on scouting process standards or hype-cycle-type maturity frameworks.

## 10. Research operations: a repository, four roles and sunset dates

### 10.1 The research repository

[FAST-MOVING, 5 Jul 2024] Keep a central research repository to prevent scattered insights and duplicated work. Nielsen Norman Group (5 July 2024) lists its contents as reports with themes and recommendations; individual insights as standalone entries; research plans, screeners, recordings, clips, transcripts and raw notes; and optionally non-research inputs such as support emails, advisory-board notes or market research ([NN/g, Research Repositories 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/research-repositories/)).

[FAST-MOVING, 5 Jul 2024] Combine the two repository types. NN/g distinguishes document libraries, which are easy to maintain but hard to search for insights, from insight databases, which make atomic insights findable but lose context and cost more to maintain, and notes they can be combined by linking each insight to its full study ([NN/g, Research Repositories 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/research-repositories/)).

[FAST-MOVING, 5 Jul 2024] Required repository attributes per NN/g: easy to access and learn; flexible permissions with participant data protected; intuitive navigation or a small set of global tags; keyword search; and export, sharing and chat-tool integrations. For adoption, get buy-in, study current sharing habits first, start with a few broad tags and common item types, test labels and iterate, then onboard and keep advocating ([NN/g, Research Repositories 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/research-repositories/)).

[VENDOR] [FAST-MOVING, 5 Jul 2024] In an NN/g survey whose sample size and date were not captured, repository tools reported were general collaboration tools (Confluence, SharePoint) at 43%, dedicated research tools (Dovetail, EnjoyHQ, Condens, Great Question) at 32% and database tools (Notion, Airtable) at 14% ([NN/g, Research Repositories 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/research-repositories/)). Treat as indicative; NN/g is a consultancy describing practitioner habits.

[DURABLE] Store the extracted data snapshot and the query with each study, because Eurostat keeps only the latest version of each dataset ([Eurostat, API introduction](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/user-guides/data-browser/api-data-access/api-introduction)), and keep a forecast and judgment log whose hits and misses are both reviewed to separate skill from luck ([Good Judgment, 10 commandments](https://goodjudgment.com/philip-tetlocks-10-commandments-of-superforecasting/)).

[INFERENCE] Repository record schema for an AI research department, mapping the Aqua Book audit trail onto NN/g's combined model: study ID; requester and decision supported; question and hypotheses; method; dates; sources with access dates and reliability class; data snapshots and queries; findings, each with likelihood, confidence and evidence links; recommendations; limits of use; sunset or review date; assurer and approver; model and tool versions; and the outcome of the decision.

[INFERENCE] Knowledge-management rule: before starting any request, the manager agent searches the repository for prior work and unexpired findings, applying AAPOR's is-new-research-needed gate internally; expired findings may be reused only after re-verification.

### 10.2 Governance, intake and impact

[DURABLE] Register and assure projects. Under the Aqua Book the approver registers each new project, confirms that resources and an assurance plan exist and secures a suitably qualified assurer ([Aqua Book revision, Engagement and scoping](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/engagement_and_scoping.html)); the organisation maintains a register of business-critical analyses and models with thresholds aligned to its risk framework and gives each analysis a review schedule or sunset date ([Aqua Book revision, Delivery, communication and sign-off](https://best-practice-and-impact.github.io/aqua_book_revision/delivery_and_communication.html)).

[VENDOR] [FAST-MOVING, 20 Jul 2026] Measure outcomes, not activity. Klue warns that research programmes fail when they count outputs such as interviews and reports and have no owner or route to turn findings into changes ([Klue, 20 Jul 2026](https://klue.com/topics/what-is-win-loss-analysis-how-to-build-a-program)).

[INFERENCE] [GAP] No sourced framework was found for research intake, prioritisation or impact measurement, and no cited definition of ResearchOps. As the researchers' proposal: score each request on the value of the decision at stake, how much the research could change the decision (if the answer would not change the action, do not do it), urgency, cost and feasibility, and reuse potential; then assign a proportionate tier such as a quick answer in hours, a standard study in days, or a business-critical study with independent assurance.

[INFERENCE] Proposed impact measures: for each delivered study, log the decision it was meant to inform, whether a decision was taken, whether it cited the study, and the time from request to delivery, and review quarterly. Add forecast accuracy from the forecast log, the reuse rate of repository items, stakeholder satisfaction, and errors caught at assurance against errors found after delivery.

[GAP] No source was retrieved on retention periods and GDPR handling for research recordings and transcripts in Sweden or the EU. Retention for each study must be set with Legal until this is filled.

## 11. Reliable AI-assisted research: links resolve, claims often do not

### 11.1 Citation-fidelity evidence

[FAST-MOVING, 7 May 2026] The central measured fact: a working, on-topic link is not evidence that the source supports the claim. A preprint by PwC authors (arXiv 2605.06635, 7 May 2026) tested 14 LLMs on 130 queries from DeepResearch Bench and BrowseComp and found, for frontier models, link validity above 94% and topical relevance above 80% but factual support of only about 39 to 77%. The best was Claude Opus 4.5 at 76.8%, and GPT-5.4 had 100% working links with 47.7% factual support ([arXiv 2605.06635](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06635)). Scoring used an LLM judge calibrated against humans; the paper is a preprint.

[FAST-MOVING, 7 May 2026] Accuracy falls with depth. In the same May 2026 preprint, factual support fell from 79% at 2 tool calls to 17% at 150 for GPT-5.4 and from 80% to 58% for Claude Opus 4.6, and providers that generated more citations tended to score lower ([arXiv 2605.06635](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06635)).

[FAST-MOVING, 3 Apr 2026] Fabricated and dead URLs remain common without tooling. A preprint by Rao, Wong and Callison-Burch (arXiv 2604.03173, 3 April 2026) covering 10 models and agents on DRBench (53,090 URLs) and 3 on ExpertQA (168,021 URLs) found 3 to 13% of cited URLs were hallucinated, with no Wayback record, and 5 to 18% did not resolve; deep research agents cite more per query and hallucinate URLs at higher rates than search-augmented LLMs ([arXiv 2604.03173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03173)).

[FAST-MOVING, 3 Apr 2026] A mechanical liveness check works. Giving the agent a URL-liveness tool cut non-resolving citations by a factor of 6 to 79, to under 1%, depending on the model's tool-use skill (April 2026 preprint, [arXiv 2604.03173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03173)).

[FAST-MOVING, 14 Aug 2025] ReportBench (14 August 2025, preprint) found commercial deep research agents more reliable than plain LLMs with search, while factual consistency still had "substantial room for improvement"; only the abstract was read and no figures were retrieved ([arXiv 2508.15804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15804)).

[FAST-MOVING, 6 Mar 2025] Consumer AI search tools misattribute news. The Tow Center study (published 6 March 2025, tests in February 2025) ran 1,600 queries across 8 AI search tools asking them to identify the source article of a news excerpt: over 60% of answers were wrong overall, Perplexity was wrong 37% of the time and Grok 3 94%, more than half of Gemini and Grok 3 responses cited fabricated or broken URLs, ChatGPT signalled uncertainty in 15 of 200 answers, paid tiers answered more but were more often confidently wrong, and several tools returned content from publishers that blocked their crawlers. The study used one run per excerpt ([CJR Tow Center](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/we-compared-eight-ai-search-engines-theyre-all-bad-at-citing-news.php)).

[FAST-MOVING, 22 Oct 2025] An EBU and BBC study (22 October 2025) of 22 public broadcasters in 18 countries and 14 languages had journalists judge over 3,000 answers from ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini and Perplexity: 45% had at least one significant issue, 31% had serious sourcing problems, 20% had major accuracy issues including hallucinated and outdated details, and Gemini had significant issues in 76% of answers ([EBU](https://www.ebu.ch/news/2025/10/ai-s-systemic-distortion-of-news-is-consistent-across-languages-and-territories-international-study-by-public-service-broadcaste)).

[INFERENCE] The two news studies test consumer assistants on news tasks and not enterprise research agents, so their percentages show direction and are not the error rate of any internal agent. No November 2026 benchmark was found; the newest evidence is from May 2026 and the tested model versions may already be superseded.

[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, 10 Feb 2026] OpenAI describes its deep research product (published 2 February 2025, page updated to 10 February 2026) as running multi-step web research over text, images and PDFs in typically 5 to 30 minutes, and itself states that it can hallucinate facts, may struggle to tell authoritative information from rumour, and is weak at conveying uncertainty ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-deep-research/)).

### 11.2 Failure modes

[INFERENCE] Eight failure modes for the knowledge base, each tied to evidence in this section: fabricated or dead URLs (3 to 13% hallucinated in the April 2026 preprint); a real, on-topic source that does not support the claim, the largest measured problem; misattribution to syndicated copies in place of the original; outdated information; confident wording without uncertainty; low-quality or SEO sources; injected instructions in fetched pages; and quality loss on long runs with many citations.

[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, 13 Jun 2025] Source-quality failure is real and hard to catch automatically. Anthropic reports that its early research agents preferred SEO content farms to authoritative sources, and that human testers and not automated evaluations caught this ([Anthropic Engineering, 13 Jun 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). The Tow Center study adds that chatbots often cite syndicated or copied versions of an article instead of the original publisher ([CJR Tow Center, 6 Mar 2025](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/we-compared-eight-ai-search-engines-theyre-all-bad-at-citing-news.php)).

[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, 24 Nov 2025] Every page a browsing agent reads is a possible attack vector. Anthropic reported on 24 November 2025 that measured prompt-injection attack success with safeguards was about 1.4 to 2.4% per model against an adaptive attacker on its internal benchmark, and that no browser agent is immune ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/prompt-injection-defenses)).

[FAST-MOVING, 7 May 2026] Web citations are unstable over time: 404s, paywalls and bot blocks are a routine cause of citations that can no longer be verified ([arXiv 2605.06635](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06635)).

[GAP] No independent measurement was found of AI-generated content contaminating search results, or of over-reliance on a single source; both are plausible and unquantified.

### 11.3 Verification practice

[FAST-MOVING, 7 May 2026] Run three separate checks on every citation: the link works, the content is relevant, and the fact is actually supported. Evaluators found the first two stay above 92% even when the third collapses ([arXiv 2605.06635](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06635)).

[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, 13 Jun 2025] Anthropic's judge rubric for research output scores factual accuracy, citation accuracy, completeness, source quality and tool efficiency; a single LLM call producing a 0.0 to 1.0 score plus pass or fail tracked human judgment best; about 20 representative queries sufficed for early evaluation; and human review still caught what automation missed ([Anthropic Engineering, 13 Jun 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). This is a vendor practice report.

[DURABLE] Selective or inaccurate citation is an integrity breach. The ALLEA European Code of Conduct (2023) classes citing selectively or inaccurately and padding a bibliography as unacceptable practices, and defines fabrication as making up data or results and recording them as real ([ALLEA Code 2023](https://allea.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/European-Code-of-Conduct-Revised-Edition-2023.pdf)). Agents must therefore report contrary evidence they find and not only supporting sources.

[INFERENCE] Claim ledger: keep one row per substantive claim with the claim text, source URL, access date, publication date, the location of the supporting passage, support status (supported, partly, not found), source type (primary, secondary, vendor, opinion) and confidence. This is the researchers' proposed operating rule.

[INFERENCE] Reading rule: never cite a page that was not opened in the current run; search snippets are leads only. Because support falls with depth and citation count, a long run needs more verification per claim and not less, and citations should be capped to sources actually read.

[INFERENCE] Number, quote and date rules: copy each figure with its unit, period, population and denominator; cross-check any decision-relevant number against a second independent source or the primary dataset and flag when two sources share an origin; use verbatim quotes only when checked character by character against the source, and otherwise paraphrase and say so; date-stamp every claim with "as of" and the source's own date; and treat anything older than 12 months in a fast-moving field as needing a recheck. The 12-month figure is a suggested default.

[INFERENCE] Liveness and archiving: every URL passes a liveness check before delivery, and key sources get an archive copy or internal snapshot reference at time of use because pages change; snapshots stay internal for copyright reasons.

[INFERENCE] Adversarial review: a second agent with fresh context re-opens a sample of sources and tries to refute the claims. The suggested sample is 100% of numbers, legal statements and quotes and at least 20% of other claims. No published standard sets these thresholds, and no controlled study was found measuring how much second-agent review improves accuracy.

[INFERENCE] Human-review triggers, suggested: legal conclusions; personal data about named individuals; claims about listed companies; health or safety claims; anything going to external publication; and any report where the reviewer finds more than 1 in 10 sampled claims unsupported.

[INFERENCE] Every deliverable carries a mandatory "not verified" section and a reproducible log: queries run, URLs fetched, timestamps, tool errors, model and version, and what was skipped and why.

### 11.4 Multi-agent workflows

[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, 13 Jun 2025] The best-documented orchestrator-worker design is Anthropic's: a lead agent plans and spawns parallel subagents, and a separate citation agent attaches citations. Anthropic reports that an Opus 4 lead with Sonnet 4 subagents beat single-agent Opus 4 by 90.2% on an internal research evaluation ([Anthropic Engineering, 13 Jun 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). The figure is internal and unreplicated.

[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, 13 Jun 2025] Multi-agent research is expensive. Anthropic reports that token use explained about 80% of performance variance on BrowseComp, and 95% together with tool-call count and model choice, and that agents use about 4 times the tokens of chat and multi-agent systems about 15 times ([Anthropic Engineering, 13 Jun 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). Use multi-agent runs for broad, parallelisable questions whose value justifies the cost.

[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, 13 Jun 2025] Delegation quality drives output quality. Anthropic found each subagent needs an objective, an output format, tool and source guidance and clear boundaries, and that vague briefs caused duplicated and misdirected work ([Anthropic Engineering, 13 Jun 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).

[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, 13 Jun 2025] Scale effort to the question. Anthropic's rule is 1 agent with 3 to 10 tool calls for simple fact-finding, 2 to 4 subagents with 10 to 15 calls each for comparisons, and more than 10 subagents for complex research; early versions wrongly spawned 50 subagents for simple queries ([Anthropic Engineering, 13 Jun 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).

[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, 13 Jun 2025] Parallelism and checkpoints: a lead launching 3 to 5 subagents in parallel, with subagents calling 3 or more tools in parallel, cut research time by up to 90%; the lead saves its plan to memory because context beyond 200,000 tokens is truncated; and because errors compound, runs should resume from checkpoints ([Anthropic Engineering, 13 Jun 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).

[CONTESTED] More effort is not monotonically better. Anthropic's vendor data link more tokens and tool calls to better performance ([Anthropic Engineering, 13 Jun 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)), while the independent May 2026 preprint found that more tool calls and more citations correlated with lower factual support ([arXiv 2605.06635](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06635)). The measures differ (task success against citation support), so both can hold.

[INFERENCE] The two findings combine into one rule: spend tokens on breadth through separate workers with small contexts, and keep a separate verification pass, instead of one very long run.

[INFERENCE] Worker brief template for the manager agent: objective, key questions, scope exclusions, preferred sources, output schema (finding, URL, date, verification label), stop conditions and tool-call budget. The synthesising agent must not add facts that are absent from worker notes, and unsourced worker claims go to the gaps list.

[GAP] OpenAI and Google multi-agent guidance and academic comparisons of multi-agent against single-agent research were not retrieved.

### 11.6 Retrieval: robots.txt, AI preferences and lawful access

[DURABLE] robots.txt is a signal, not access control. RFC 9309 (September 2022, Standards Track) says its rules are not access authorisation and not a security measure; compliant crawlers must follow parseable rules, treat an unreachable file (5xx) as full disallow, and should not cache it beyond 24 hours ([RFC 9309](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309.html)). In the EU the signal still carries legal weight as a rights reservation and a data-protection objection; see sections 12 and 13.

[FAST-MOVING, 31 Aug 2026 milestone] The IETF AI Preferences working group is standardising a vocabulary and attachment methods (robots.txt, HTTP headers) for AI-use preferences, with milestones to submit both drafts to the IESG set for 31 August 2026; enforcement is out of scope and the final status was not confirmed ([IETF aipref](https://datatracker.ietf.org/wg/aipref/about/)).

[INFERENCE] Lawful-access checklist: use open-access versions, licensed subscriptions, library access or author copies; never bypass paywalls, logins, CAPTCHAs or rate limits; and record the access route for each source. Large pages are truncated by fetch tools (seen on EUR-Lex, lagen.nu and the Futures Toolkit in this research), so request specific offsets and record which part was read.

## 12. Copyright and text-and-data-mining law: lawful access, no reservation, no reproduction

### 12.1 EU and Swedish statute

[DURABLE] EU DSM Directive 2019/790, checked on primary source: text and data mining is any automated analytical technique analysing digital text and data to generate information (Article 2(2)). Under Article 3, research organisations and cultural heritage institutions may copy lawfully accessed works for scientific-research mining, with secure storage. Under Article 4, anyone may copy lawfully accessible works for mining, keeping copies only as long as needed, unless the rightsholder has expressly reserved rights in an appropriate manner, with machine-readable means as the example for online content. Article 7(1) stops contract terms overriding Article 3; Article 4 is not listed ([EUR-Lex 32019L0790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790)).

[DURABLE] Recital 18 of the DSM Directive, checked on primary source, says that for online content a reservation by machine-readable means, including metadata and a site's terms, is appropriate ([EUR-Lex 32019L0790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790)).

[DURABLE] Swedish upphovsrättslagen (1960:729), checked on the consolidated text at lagen.nu, an unofficial mirror: under 15 a § a person with lawful access may make copies for text and data mining, kept only as long as needed and not used for other purposes, unless the rightsholder has appropriately reserved the right; 15 b § covers research organisations, libraries, museums and archives, requires secure storage and voids contrary contract terms; 15 c § holds the definitions. The provisions came in through SFS 2022:1712 ([lagen.nu 1960:729](https://lagen.nu/1960:729); [SFS 2022:1712](https://svenskforfattningssamling.se/sites/default/files/sfs/2022-12/SFS2022-1712.pdf)).

[DURABLE] Swedish quotation and attribution rules, checked via lagen.nu: 22 § allows quoting from published works in line with good practice and only to the extent the purpose requires, and 11 § requires the source to be credited as good practice requires ([lagen.nu 1960:729](https://lagen.nu/1960:729)).

[DURABLE] Swedish database (catalogue) right, checked via lagen.nu on a truncated page: 49 § protects compilations of a large number of data or resulting from substantial investment, gives an exclusive right to copy and make available for 15 years, and applies 13 to 16 §§, and so the mining rules ([lagen.nu 1960:729](https://lagen.nu/1960:729)). Later amendments after SFS 2022:1712 cannot be excluded.

[FAST-MOVING, May 2026] Swedish rightsholders are reserving rights in natural language. Sveriges Författarförbund published a reservation against mining for language-model training under 15 a § and calls it a disputed question whether the exception covers such training ([Författarförbundet, 2026](https://forfattarforbundet.se/app/uploads/2026/05/FORBEHALL-MOT-TDU.pdf)).

[INFERENCE] A company's research use falls under Article 4 and 15 a §, where the opt-out applies and contracts can restrict use, and not under the research-organisation exception unless the company meets the 15 c § definition. Practical rule: reading and analysing is permitted with lawful access and no reservation; check robots.txt, site terms and metadata for reservations before bulk collection; delete working copies when the task ends; and deliver facts, figures and own-words summaries with attribution, with short quotes only.

[INFERENCE] Facts and ideas are not protected, but a summary that tracks a source's structure and wording closely can still reproduce expression, and systematic extraction from a database can infringe 49 § even if each item is a bare fact. Purchased market reports are governed by their licence, so do not paste them into external tools or redistribute them beyond licensed users without checking the terms.

### 12.2 European case law, 2024 to November 2026

[FAST-MOVING, 3 Nov 2026] The CJEU has not yet ruled in C-250/25, Like Company v Google Ireland. [DURABLE] Ryanair v PR Aviation, CJEU C-30/14, 15 January 2015, checked on primary source: the Database Directive 96/9 does not apply to a database protected neither by copyright nor by the sui generis right, so its user rights and its rule voiding contrary contract terms do not apply, and the owner may set contractual limits on use, subject to national law ([EUR-Lex 62014CJ0030](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62014CJ0030)).

## 13. Web scraping and data access: any technical barrier is a stop sign

### 13.1 Data-protection regulators' position

[INFERENCE] No single rule makes scraping lawful or unlawful; the answer is the combined result of contract, database right, copyright, GDPR and computer-misuse law. This framing is the researchers' synthesis of the sources in this section; no single source states it.

[FAST-MOVING, 5 Jan 2026] CNIL's focus sheet dated 5 January 2026, checked on primary source, says that scraping under legitimate interest requires defining collection criteria in advance, filtering out unneeded categories, deleting irrelevant data at once, and excluding sites that oppose scraping through robots.txt or CAPTCHA, because collection from such sites cannot fall within people's reasonable expectations. Recommended extras are an exclusion list of sensitive sites, collecting only freely accessible data, public notice, a prior objection route and prompt pseudonymisation ([CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/en/legal-basis-legitimate-interests-focus-sheet-measures-implement-case-data-collection-web-scraping)). CNIL is the French authority; its sheet is persuasive and not binding in Sweden.

[DURABLE] EDPB Opinion 28/2024 (adopted 18 December 2024), checked on primary source: legitimate interest needs a three-step test of a lawful, specific interest, necessity and balancing; a mention in a privacy policy alone does not create reasonable expectations; balancing weighs the nature of the data, context, scale, consequences and reasonable expectations; public availability alone is not decisive; the Article 21 right to object applies; and a deployer should check, in proportion to risk, that a model it uses was not built on unlawful processing ([EDPB Opinion 28/2024](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-12/edpb_opinion_202428_ai-models_en.pdf)).

[FAST-MOVING, 7 Jul 2026] EDPB Guidelines 03/2026 on web scraping in the context of generative AI, version 1.0 adopted 7 July 2026 as a consultation version, cover scraping of internet sources by private entities, directly or through a contractor, for training generative AI; other scraping purposes are outside the stated scope. Checked on the primary document through an extraction tool ([EDPB Guidelines 03/2026](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpb_guidelines_2020603_webscraping_v1_en_0.pdf)). Whether a final version has since been adopted was not found.

[FAST-MOVING, 7 Jul 2026] Main points of EDPB Guidelines 03/2026 on lawfulness: personal data being public online is not consent, and a missing robots.txt is not consent; legitimate interest needs the three cumulative steps; and before collecting, a controller should exclude sites that clearly oppose scraping through technical measures such as login walls, robots.txt or ai.txt files, or CAPTCHA ([EDPB Guidelines 03/2026](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpb_guidelines_2020603_webscraping_v1_en_0.pdf)).

[FAST-MOVING, 7 Jul 2026] Main points of EDPB Guidelines 03/2026 on minimisation: define collection criteria, map sources, filter out unneeded categories such as financial and location data, exclude sites that structurally hold data on minors or vulnerable people, apply pattern filters after collection, and pseudonymise or anonymise. Special-category data needs an Article 9(2) derogation as well as a legal basis, and incidental residual collection is assessed in light of the controller's preventive measures ([EDPB Guidelines 03/2026](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpb_guidelines_2020603_webscraping_v1_en_0.pdf)). CNIL adds that incidental residual sensitive data is not unlawful if filtering was applied but must be deleted once noticed ([CNIL, 5 Jan 2026](https://www.cnil.fr/en/legal-basis-legitimate-interests-focus-sheet-measures-implement-case-data-collection-web-scraping)).

[FAST-MOVING, 7 Jul 2026] Main points of EDPB Guidelines 03/2026 on transparency and responsibility: where individual notice is impossible or disproportionate under Article 14(5)(b), publish the information, including sources and crawler characteristics; offer a prior right to object or an opt-out list; a scraper and a later reuser are in principle each responsible for their own processing, and a buyer of scraped datasets should name and link to the original controller; and a published DPIA counts as a protective measure ([EDPB Guidelines 03/2026](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpb_guidelines_2020603_webscraping_v1_en_0.pdf)).

[FAST-MOVING, 26 May 2026] The Swedish authority IMY publishes guidance on GDPR and AI (page updated 26 May 2026) with sections on legal basis and AI training, and states that GDPR applies whenever personal data is processed in developing or using AI. Only the overview page was read ([IMY](https://www.imy.se/verksamhet/dataskydd/innovationsportalen/vagledning-om-gdpr-och-ai/)). IMY's detailed position on scraping was not opened.

[INFERENCE] The EDPB guidelines formally cover only generative-AI training, so a research department scraping for market analysis is outside their stated scope; but their legitimate-interest, opt-out-signal, minimisation and Article 14 points restate general GDPR logic and match the CNIL sheet, so the department should apply them by analogy.

### 13.2 Swedish criminal law on access

[DURABLE] Swedish brottsbalken 4 kap. 9 c § (dataintrång), checked on lagen.nu, an unofficial mirror, reading the first part of the page: unlawfully obtaining access to data intended for automated processing, or unlawfully altering, deleting, blocking or entering such data, or seriously disrupting its use, carries a fine or prison up to 2 years; gross dataintrång carries 1 to 8 years, weighing serious damage, a large volume of data or particular danger ([lagen.nu 1962:700](https://lagen.nu/1962:700)).

[INFERENCE] Dataintrång turns on acting without permission. Using credentials not issued to the company, or getting past a login, is the obvious risk; reading openly published pages is not access without permission in the ordinary sense. This is the researchers' reading, and no Swedish case law on scraping was checked.

### 13.3 United States decisions and why they do not transfer

[FAST-MOVING, 23 Jan 2024] Meta v Bright Data (N.D. Cal., 23 January 2024): logged-off scraping of public data did not breach Meta's terms because the scraper was not using the service as a user, a ruling limited to logged-off public data. Secondary source only ([Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/technology/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/)).

[FAST-MOVING, May 2024] X Corp v Bright Data (N.D. Cal., May 2024): contract and tort claims over scraping public user content were dismissed as pre-empted by the Copyright Act. Secondary source only ([Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/recent-district-court-decision-casts-doubt-terms-use-barring-data-scraping)).

[DURABLE] hiQ Labs v LinkedIn: hiQ won a preliminary injunction in 2017, the Ninth Circuit affirmed on 9 September 2019, the Supreme Court vacated and remanded on 14 June 2021, the Ninth Circuit reaffirmed in April 2022, and in November 2022 the district court held that hiQ had breached LinkedIn's User Agreement and the parties settled. Read on a tertiary source ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HiQ_Labs_v._LinkedIn)).

[FAST-MOVING, February 2026] The US litigation trend as of February 2026 is a shift to DMCA section 1201 anti-circumvention claims aimed at bypassing rate limits, CAPTCHAs and identity masking: Reddit v SerpApi and Perplexity (filed 22 October 2025), Google v SerpApi, and Reddit v Anthropic (filed 4 June 2025, pending). Secondary source only ([ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/alternative-data/how-artificial-intelligence-shaping-web-scraping-litigation/)).

[INFERENCE] US rulings favouring scrapers of public data do not transfer to the EU, where database right and GDPR apply independently. hiQ ended in a contract loss for the scraper despite its win on the computer-crime point; combined with Ryanair, both jurisdictions point to contract as the live risk when scraping public pages.

[INFERENCE] Circumventing a technical barrier is the common trigger across regimes: the EU's lawful-access condition, US anti-circumvention theories, CNIL's and the EDPB's reasonable-expectations reasoning, and Swedish dataintrång. Treat any barrier (login, paywall, CAPTCHA, rate limit, block) as a stop sign that needs human sign-off.

[INFERENCE] Scraping rules for agents: stay logged out unless the account's terms allow automated use; obey robots.txt and AI-preference signals; no CAPTCHA solving, IP rotation to evade blocks, or fake accounts; rate-limit requests; collect no personal data unless the task needs it; and prefer official APIs and bulk downloads.

## 14. Personal data in research: minimise, inform, and stay out of offence data

### 14.1 GDPR provisions that bear on research

[DURABLE] GDPR Article 89, checked on gdpr-info.eu, an unofficial mirror: processing for scientific or historical research or statistics needs safeguards, in particular data minimisation, with pseudonymisation where the purpose allows and non-identifying processing where possible; Union or Member State law may derogate from Articles 15, 16, 18 and 21 for research and statistics where those rights would make the purpose impossible or seriously impair it; and derogations cover only the research part of mixed-purpose processing ([gdpr-info.eu Art. 89](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-89-gdpr/)).

[DURABLE] GDPR Article 14, checked on gdpr-info.eu, an unofficial mirror: where data are not collected from the person, the controller must give its identity, purposes and legal basis, data categories, recipients, retention, the legitimate interest relied on, rights, and the source including whether it was publicly accessible, within a reasonable period of at most one month or at first communication or first disclosure. Article 14(5)(b) lifts the duty where informing is impossible or a disproportionate effort, in particular for research and statistics under Article 89(1) safeguards, but the controller must then take protective measures including making the information public ([gdpr-info.eu Art. 14](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-14-gdpr/)).

[INFERENCE] Article 89 derogations need Union or national law, and scientific research is not the same as commercial market research. A SaaS company should assume that Articles 15, 16, 18 and 21 apply in full to its research data and should use Article 14(5)(b) only with a published notice and a documented effort assessment.

[FAST-MOVING, 21 Oct 2026] GDPR reform is not law. The Commission proposed the Digital Omnibus in November 2025, a Council position was reported on 21 October 2026, Parliament has no position, and trilogue is not expected before March 2027; the Council deleted the proposed explicit AI-training legitimate-interest basis and the changed personal-data definition. Secondary source only ([Addleshaw Goddard](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/en/insights/insights-briefings/2026/data-protection/eu-digital-omnibus-european-council-adopts-its-position/)). An earlier briefing describes a 10 June 2026 Council compromise and numbers the pseudonymisation article differently ([Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/digital-omnibus-ai-is-nearing-the-finish-line-is-eu-data-law-heading-down-the-wrong-track)), so article numbers in commentary on the reform are unstable.

### 14.2 Swedish data-protection act

[DURABLE] Swedish dataskyddslagen (2018:218), consolidated through SFS 2026:1589 and checked on primary source: GDPR yields to the press and expression constitutions, and most GDPR articles do not apply to processing for journalistic purposes or academic, artistic or literary creation (1 kap. 7 §) ([riksdagen.se SFS 2018:218](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2018218-med-kompletterande-bestammelser_sfs-2018-218/)). The researchers' reading is that this exemption is not available to routine corporate research.

[DURABLE] Under dataskyddslagen, checked on primary source, criminal-offence data may be processed by authorities and by others only in narrow cases or under specific rules (3 kap. 8 to 9 §§); personal identity numbers may be processed without consent only when clearly justified (3 kap. 10 §); data processed only for research may be used against the person only for vital-interest reasons (4 kap. 3 §); and the age of consent for children is 13 (2 kap. 4 §) ([riksdagen.se SFS 2018:218](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2018218-med-kompletterande-bestammelser_sfs-2018-218/)).

[INFERENCE] Offence data on named individuals is close to off-limits for a private company in Sweden under 3 kap. 8 §, so agents should not compile criminal-record-style profiles, even from public court records or commercial background sites.

[INFERENCE] Rules for researching named individuals: stick to the professional role, minimise, make no special-category inference, do not aggregate into profiles, record purpose and legal basis, set retention, and escalate to a human before storing anything. No guidance from IMY on profiling named individuals at competitors was researched.

### 14.3 Participants, recordings and consent

[DURABLE] Tell research participants how their data will be used, stored and deleted, as the ALLEA Code (2023) requires ([ALLEA Code 2023](https://allea.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/European-Code-of-Conduct-Revised-Edition-2023.pdf)). ICC/ESOMAR Article 6 adds that no personal data goes to clients without specific consent, that deductive disclosure must be prevented including through advanced analytics or AI, that retention is limited, that recipients provide equivalent security, and that breaches are notified ([ESOMAR, ICC/ESOMAR Code](https://esomar.org/representation/icc-esomar-code-of-conduct)).

[DURABLE] Swedish brottsbalken 4 kap. 9 a § (olovlig avlyssning), checked on lagen.nu, an unofficial mirror: secretly and unlawfully using a technical aid to listen to or record speech in private, a conversation between others, or a closed meeting that the person does not take part in or has entered without right, carries a fine or up to 2 years; the provision does not cover a conversation in which the recorder is a participant. 4 kap. 8 § covers unlawfully accessing a message carried by a postal or telecom operator, and 4 kap. 9 b § makes installing equipment for these offences punishable as preparation ([lagen.nu 1962:700](https://lagen.nu/1962:700)).

[INFERENCE] Recording in Sweden: a participant may record their own call without committing olovlig avlyssning, but for a company that recording is still personal-data processing under GDPR, so participants should be told and a legal basis recorded, and the ICC/ESOMAR code requires transparency in any case. An agent must never record or transcribe a meeting it was not invited to.

[INFERENCE] For surveys and interviews, give information first, get documented consent, state that there will be recording and AI processing, and allow withdrawal. This mirrors the consent rules in the Swedish ethics-review act and is good practice even where that act does not apply.

## 15. Swedish ethics review: the act applies to companies

[DURABLE] Swedish etikprövningslagen (2003:460), consolidated through SFS 2026:1229 and checked on primary source: research means scientific work to gain new knowledge plus science-based development (2 §); the act covers research that processes GDPR Article 9 special-category data or offence data (3 §) and research involving physical or psychological influence on a person (4 §); such research requires prior approval (6 §); consent must be voluntary, explicit, specific and documented after information (16 to 17 §§); and intentional breach carries fines or up to two years' prison (38 §) ([riksdagen.se SFS 2003:460](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-lagar/dokument/_sfs-2003-460)).

[DURABLE] The research principal under the Swedish ethics-review act can be a company; the act does not distinguish public and private actors. Checked on government bill material ([Lagrådsremiss, 17 Mar 2026](https://www.lagradet.se/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Lagradsremiss-Etikprovning-av-forskning-SLUTLIG-260317_tryck.pdf)).

[FAST-MOVING, effective 1 July 2027] From 1 July 2027 the Swedish ethics-review act gains exemptions (a new 6 a §) and a duty on the research principal to assess and document for each project whether review is needed (6 b §). Checked on primary source ([riksdagen.se SFS 2003:460](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-lagar/dokument/_sfs-2003-460)).

[FAST-MOVING, 11 Jun 2026] The content of the 2027 exemptions, per the parliamentary committee report of 11 June 2026 on Prop. 2025/26:260, checked on primary source: adults aged 18 or over giving explicit, documented consent; data already widely spread, such as in media, blogs and podcasts; data in public judgments and decisions; each only where the risk to health, safety and privacy is slight; voluntary review remains available; and the supervisory body is no longer obliged to report to prosecutors ([Betänkande 2025/26:UbU31](https://data.riksdagen.se/dokument/HD01UBU31); [Regeringen, 30 Apr 2026](https://regeringen.se/pressmeddelanden/2026/04/regeringen-gar-vidare-med-ny-etikprovningslag/)).

[INFERENCE] Ordinary commercial market or competitor research is usually not research in the sense of 2 §, but a company project that uses scientific method to produce new knowledge about people's health, politics, religion, ethnicity, union membership, sex life or offences is likely within the act. Route any such project to Legal before any data is collected; until 1 July 2027 there is no low-risk exemption to rely on.

## 16. Research integrity standards: disclose AI, sources, limits and interests

[DURABLE] The ALLEA European Code of Conduct for Research Integrity, revised edition published 23 June 2023 and checked on primary source, rests on reliability, honesty, respect and accountability. Methods reporting should include use of external services or AI and automated tools; hiding the use of AI in creating content is listed as an unacceptable practice; authors disclose financial and non-financial conflicts and funding; and fabrication, falsification and plagiarism are defined ([ALLEA Code 2023](https://allea.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/European-Code-of-Conduct-Revised-Edition-2023.pdf)). ALLEA states that the code underpins the ERA Forum living guidelines on responsible generative-AI use in research ([ALLEA](https://allea.org/code-of-conduct/)); those guidelines were not read.

[FAST-MOVING, 2025] The ICC/ESOMAR Code, 2025 revision, read in outline on ESOMAR's own page, has five fundamental principles: research is legal, honest, transparent and truthful; due care with fair and respectful interaction that avoids harm to data subjects; clear communication on how personal data is collected and used, with protection against unauthorised access; ethical behaviour that does not undermine public trust in research; and researchers keeping overall responsibility and oversight whatever method or technology is used ([ESOMAR, ICC/ESOMAR Code](https://esomar.org/representation/icc-esomar-code-of-conduct)).

[FAST-MOVING, 2025] The ICC/ESOMAR Code's thirteen articles are: 1 Duty of Care; 2 Children, Young Persons and Other Vulnerable Individuals; 3 Data Minimisation; 4 Primary Data Collection; 5 Use of Secondary Data; 6 Data Protection and Privacy; 7 Fit for Purpose; 8 Transparency, Confidentiality and Responsibility; 9 Publishing Findings; 10 Professional Responsibility; 11 Legal Responsibility; 12 Compliance; 13 Implementation. AI and synthetic-data duties sit in Articles 4, 6, 7, 9 and 11 ([ESOMAR, ICC/ESOMAR Code](https://esomar.org/representation/icc-esomar-code-of-conduct); [PDF](https://esocorpwebsitestg.blob.core.windows.net/strapi-uploads/uploads/ICC_ESOMAR_Code_db0815800d.pdf)).

[FAST-MOVING, 2 Oct 2024] Vetenskapsrådet published a reworked "God forskningssed" on 2 October 2024, replacing the 2017 edition and aimed at researchers and research principals. Its existence was checked on the agency's page; the content, including any text on AI, was not read ([Vetenskapsrådet](https://www.vr.se/analys/rapporter/vara-rapporter/2024-10-02-god-forskningssed-2024.html)).

[INFERENCE] Minimum disclosure block for every deliverable: the AI tools and models used, the date range of the research, the search scope, what humans reviewed, known limits, unverified items, and any interest the commissioning unit has in the conclusion. This combines ALLEA's methods-reporting duty with ICC/ESOMAR Articles 7 and 9.

## 17. Security of confidential information: queries are disclosures, pages are untrusted

[PLATFORM CLAIM] [FAST-MOVING, 24 Nov 2025] Treat all fetched content as untrusted data. Anthropic states that hidden instructions in web content can hijack an agent and that training, classifiers and red-teaming reduce the risk without removing it ([Anthropic, 24 Nov 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/prompt-injection-defenses)). Its engineering report adds that production tracing should avoid inspecting conversation contents, for privacy ([Anthropic Engineering, 13 Jun 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).

[DURABLE] Trade-secret protection depends on reasonable secrecy measures, under both Article 2(1) of the EU Trade Secrets Directive ([EUR-Lex 32016L0943](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016L0943)) and 2 § of the Swedish act ([riksdagen.se SFS 2018:558](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2018558-om-foretagshemligheter_sfs-2018-558/)), both checked on primary source. How the company's own secrets are handled in tools therefore has legal weight.

[DURABLE] Check the tools themselves. EDPB Opinion 28/2024, checked on primary source, says a deploying organisation should check, in proportion to risk, that an AI model it uses was not developed through unlawful personal-data processing ([EDPB Opinion 28/2024](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-12/edpb_opinion_202428_ai-models_en.pdf)). ICC/ESOMAR Article 7 requires telling clients when AI is used and keeping client data and collateral in secure environments, and Article 6 requires recipients of personal data to provide equivalent security ([ESOMAR, ICC/ESOMAR Code](https://esomar.org/representation/icc-esomar-code-of-conduct)).

[DURABLE] Queries to public services are observable. The OECD's API terms reserve the right to monitor usage ([OECD Terms and Conditions](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/terms-conditions.html)), which illustrates that what an agent searches for reveals what the company is researching.

[INFERENCE] Outbound-data rules: never place client names, deal names, unreleased product names, unreleased financials, personal data or secrets in search queries, URLs or third-party tool payloads; use only tools approved under a contract with no-training and retention terms; keep a list of approved tools with those terms; and log which tool handled which confidential input.

[INFERENCE] Architecture rules: separate the agent that reads untrusted web content from any agent holding confidential context or write-capable tools; ignore instructions found in fetched pages and log them as incidents; and restrict outbound domains where possible.

[INFERENCE] Sending trade secrets to a tool without contractual confidentiality terms could be argued to undermine the reasonable-measures element of trade-secret protection. This is the researchers' inference from Article 2(1) and 2 §, not a holding of any court read in this research.

[GAP] This is the weakest section of the knowledge base. No provider data-retention or enterprise-privacy terms (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, Perplexity), no agency guidance (NCSC, ENISA, MSB, IMY) and no documented leak incidents were retrieved. It needs a dedicated research pass before the tool list in the previous paragraph can be populated.

## Conclusion

[INFERENCE] The research changes the picture of what an AI research department is for. The scarce capability in November 2026 is not finding sources, which agents do cheaply and at volume, but establishing that a given source supports a given claim, which the best measured systems manage roughly three times in four and which degrades as runs lengthen. That inverts the usual instinct about effort: breadth should come from many small, tightly briefed workers, and the saved budget should go to a verifier that re-opens sources, to a claim ledger, and to an honest list of what was not checked. The same logic runs through the method standards, which converge from very different origins (government analysis, intelligence tradecraft, survey methodology, forecasting tournaments, research-integrity codes) on one design: agree the question in writing, separate the person who does the work from the person who assures it, state likelihood and confidence on a published scale, and put an expiry date on every finding.

[INFERENCE] The legal picture converges too, and on a rule simple enough for an agent to follow. Across EU copyright, the Advocate General's November 2026 opinion, the data-protection regulators' scraping guidance, Swedish computer-misuse and trade-secret law and the US anti-circumvention trend, the trigger for liability is the same act: getting past something that was put there to stop you, or keeping something you should have known was not yours to have. A department that treats every barrier and every confidential-looking document as a hard stop is on the right side of nearly every regime surveyed, whatever the pending judgments decide. The largest remaining risks are therefore the unfilled ones: provider data-retention terms, retention rules for research recordings, competition-law guidance on information exchange, and the validity of AI-coded qualitative analysis and synthetic respondents. Those four should be the first follow-up research the department commissions about itself.
