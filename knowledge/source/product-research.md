# Law gates, evidence steers, Hormozi frames product

As of 11 November 2026 the foundational knowledge for an AI Product department is a three-layer stack: compliance rules that always win, independent evidence that sets methods and thresholds, and Alex Hormozi's published models as the default framing wherever he has published one. The house style gives the manager agent and its workers a compact set of tests: score every feature on the four levers of the value equation (bigger outcome, higher perceived likelihood, less delay, less effort), screen markets on four indicators (pain, growth, reachability, money), generate delivery options with the six-part delivery cube, sequence attraction, upsell, downsell and continuity offers, refuse side projects, and fix activation and retention before buying growth; every number attached to these models is his own unaudited claim from gyms, coaching and community businesses, and several pieces (trim and stack, the next-problem cycle, quality before scaling) were verified only through second-hand summaries or not at all. The evidence layer supplies what he has not published: the SVPG product operating model and its four risks, Teresa Torres's opportunity solution tree and assumption tests, scoring models used as aids and not as deciders, activation defined as the earliest milestone that predicts retention, A/B testing reserved for cases with enough traffic (a 5% revenue lift can need about 147,000 users per arm), evaluation suites as the specification for AI features, and hybrid pricing with predictable bills. The same layer covers design: Nielsen's heuristics, one question per page, five users per round for finding problems and twenty or more for numbers, SUS with an average of 68, WCAG 2.2 AA as the build target, and human-AI guidance from Microsoft, Apple, Google and NN/g. The compliance layer is concrete for a Sweden-based SaaS company selling in the EU: the Swedish accessibility act has applied to consumer e-commerce services since 28 June 2025 with sanction fees up to SEK 10 million, the online withdrawal function has applied since 19 June 2026, AI Act Article 50 disclosure has applied since 2 August 2026, analytics cookies need opt-in consent, and the Digital Fairness Act was still a reported draft on the research date. The benchmarks agents most want are the weakest part of the record: the best retention figures date from 2020, activation from 2022, feature adoption from 2019, and no 2025 to 2026 replacement was retrieved. The practical conclusion is that agents should treat discovery, judgment and evaluation design as the scarce work now that delivery is cheap, measure against the company's own trailing data, and keep pricing, legal wording, customer-facing claims and irreversible actions behind human approval. Where the research found no fetched source, this document says so instead of filling the hole from memory.

## How to read and split this document

This document is written to be cut into atomic knowledge-base entries for a vector index and a graph database. Every body paragraph is meant to stand alone: it opens with one or more labels, names its subject in full, and keeps each number next to its date and source link. Section numbers group domains; sub-headings name the single topic of the paragraphs beneath them. Tables repeat their source link in every row so that a row can be split out as its own entry. When splitting, keep the label, the date and the link with the claim.

The labels mean the following. **[HOUSE STYLE]** marks Alex or Leila Hormozi's published frameworks, which the owner has chosen as the department's default style where they exist; it is a preference and not independent evidence, and every number attached to it is his own self-reported, unaudited claim. No Leila Hormozi material was retrieved in this research, so every house-style entry here is Alex Hormozi's. **[DURABLE]** marks a principle, definition, formula or long-standing standard that has held for years. **[FAST-MOVING, date]** marks a figure, survey result, tool capability or legal status that will age, with the date of the source. **[VENDOR]** marks a figure or recommendation from a party with a commercial interest in the conclusion (tool makers, training firms, consultancies that sell the method, research firms that sell their research). **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** marks a platform's or tool maker's statement about itself that nobody independently verified. **[CONTESTED]** marks a point where sources disagree or where house style and evidence pull in different directions. **[INFERENCE]** marks a synthesis, template or convention written by the researchers that no single fetched source states; adopt it as a default and tune it against your own data. **[GAP]** marks something the research did not verify or could not find.

Qualifiers are added inside labels where they matter. For house-style material, "primary transcript" means the point was read on a third-party-hosted transcript of Hormozi's own video or on acquisition.com, and "second-hand summary" means it comes from someone else's summary of his book, podcast or event (SuperSummary, Shortform, Dean Yeong's notes, jxnl.co, Skool community posts) and was not checked against the original. For other material, "practitioner opinion" means an expert's published view without a study behind it, "survey" means self-reported data, and "academic" means a peer-reviewed or pre-print paper. For legal, tax and regulatory material, "checked on primary source" means the statute, standard or authority page was read during the research, "secondary source only" means a law firm, trade press, trade body or unofficial reproduction was read and the official text was not, and "unverified background" means the point comes from the researchers' prior knowledge and was not fetched. Agents must not gate a live decision on a secondary or unverified legal point without confirmation from Legal.

Seven caveats apply to the whole document. First, legal, tax and regulatory content is orientation as of 11 November 2026 and is not professional advice. Second, none of Hormozi's books ($100M Offers, $100M Leads, $100M Money Models) was read directly; book content is second-hand, and his video material was read through auto-generated caption transcripts hosted by third parties, so everything here is paraphrase and may carry caption errors. Third, his three primary videos on market selection, the delivery cube and the red-dress focus principle date from 2021 and describe service businesses; they pre-date his 2025 book and may not match his current wording. Fourth, the research ran out of search and page-fetch budget several times, so a number of standard topics were never reached; they are listed as gaps and are not filled from memory. Fifth, most benchmarks are US-weighted, self-reported and venture-skewed, and the central ones are old (retention 2020, activation 2022, free-to-paid 2023, feature adoption 2019). Sixth, templates, checklists and decision rules labelled as inference are the researchers' synthesis and not published templates. Seventh, where a later gap-fill pass corrected or superseded an earlier research note, only the corrected claim appears here.

## 1. Overview: how the Product department decides and works

### The three-layer rule that governs every agent decision

**[INFERENCE]** The Product department runs on three layers in strict order. Layer one is compliance: accessibility law, consumer withdrawal rules, consent and deceptive-design rules, AI disclosure and data protection (sections 17, 18 and 19). Layer two is independent evidence and established practice on strategy, discovery, prioritisation, delivery, metrics, pricing, research and design (sections 5 to 16). Layer three is the house style, Alex Hormozi's published models, which is the default framing wherever he has published one (sections 2 and 3). A worker agent starts from the house-style test, checks whether an evidence rule changes it for software or for this product's scale, and checks whether a compliance gate blocks it for this market and customer type. Section 4 lists the known collisions and says which rule wins in each.

### The product operating model in three dimensions

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** The SVPG product operating model has three dimensions. How you build: small, frequent releases, at least every two weeks and ideally continuous. How you solve problems: teams receive problems and outcomes, not feature lists, and each solution must be valuable, usable, feasible and viable. How you decide which problems to solve: leaders set a customer-centric vision and an insight-driven strategy ([SVPG, The Product Operating Model, Feb 2023, modified Jul 2024](https://svpg.com/the-product-operating-model-an-introduction)). SVPG is a consultancy that sells coaching on this model, so treat the model as an influential practitioner position and not as tested evidence.

### The four risks every solution must clear

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** SVPG names four risks for any proposed solution: value (will customers buy it or choose to use it), usability (can they work out how to use it), feasibility (can it be built with the time, skills and technology available) and business viability (does it work for sales, marketing, finance, legal and compliance). In SVPG's model the product manager owns value and viability, the designer owns usability and the tech lead owns feasibility ([SVPG, The Product Operating Model, Feb 2023, modified Jul 2024](https://svpg.com/the-product-operating-model-an-introduction); [SVPG, Product vs Feature Teams, Aug 2019, modified Oct 2023](https://svpg.com/product-vs-feature-teams)). **[INFERENCE]** For an agent department, every specification should record evidence against each of the four risks, and Teresa Torres's fifth category, ethical risk (section 6), should be added as a standing line.

### Three kinds of product work need different methods

**[FAST-MOVING, Oct 2026; practitioner opinion]** SVPG separates product work into three types that need different strategy and experimentation: zero-to-one work that seeks product-market fit, innovation on existing products that creates new value, and optimisation that captures value ([SVPG, Experimentation, AI and the Product Model, Oct 2026](https://www.svpg.com/experimentation-ai-and-the-product-model/)). **[INFERENCE]** The manager agent should tag each piece of work with one of the three types before choosing methods: qualitative discovery and prototypes dominate zero-to-one work, mixed methods suit innovation, and controlled experiments belong mainly to optimisation where traffic allows (section 9).

### Mapping the manager agent and worker agents onto the product model

**[INFERENCE]** A Product department with one manager agent and several worker agents matches the empowered-team model if the manager assigns problems and outcome metrics to workers and holds them to evidence, and it matches a feature factory if the manager hands down feature lists and dates. SVPG's diagnostic questions work as a self-audit: is the team handed features and dates or problems and outcomes, and have its OKRs decayed into feature lists ([SVPG, Product vs Feature Teams, Aug 2019, modified Oct 2023](https://svpg.com/product-vs-feature-teams)). A worker agent can maintain an opportunity solution tree and draft assumption lists, but opportunities must trace to real customer stories, because synthetic interviews break the premise of the method (section 15).

### The state of practice in 2025 to 2026

**[FAST-MOVING, 2025-2026; survey]** Survey data shows most product teams fall short of the model they describe. In the PLA/ProductPlan survey of about 250 respondents (fielded Q4 2025, published May 2026), only 34% regularly collect insights and use them in prioritisation, and 27% blame missing discovery or customer insight for strategy-roadmap misalignment ([Product-Led Alliance statistics page citing the PLA/ProductPlan State of Product Management 2026, published May 2026](https://www.productledalliance.com/product-management-statistics/)). In Atlassian's State of Product 2026 (more than 1,000 product professionals in the US and Europe, reported September 2025), 49% say they lack time for strategic planning, 80% of teams do not involve engineers in ideation, problem definition or roadmap creation, only 31% of organisations prioritise rapid experimentation, and 84% worry their products will not succeed in market although 85% feel empowered to influence strategy ([Mind the Product summary of Atlassian's State of Product 2026, Sep 2025](https://www.mindtheproduct.com/inside-atlassians-state-of-product-2026-key-takeaways/)). These figures were read through secondary pages; Atlassian's primary report and the full ProductPlan PDF were not fetched.

### AI moved the bottleneck from delivery to discovery

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul-Nov 2026; practitioner opinion]** SVPG's 2026 position is that with AI, delivery is no longer the bottleneck, so the demand for discovery, product judgment and evaluation design rises ([SVPG, AI and the Future of the Product Role, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.svpg.com/ai-and-the-future-of-the-product-role/)). It argues that teams ship faster with AI without better outcomes when they only speed up output inside a project model, citing Atlassian's State of Teams 2026, in which 89% of executives say AI sped up work while 6% can show organisation-wide return ([SVPG, The AI Productivity Paradox, 23 Jul 2026](https://www.svpg.com/the-ai-productivity-paradox/)). SVPG's recommendation is to use AI for "build to learn" (throwaway prototypes that test ideas) before "build to earn" (production-quality delivery) ([SVPG, The AI Productivity Paradox, 23 Jul 2026](https://www.svpg.com/the-ai-productivity-paradox/)). **[INFERENCE]** For an AI Product department this is the central operating risk: agents can produce business cases, roadmaps, specifications and code quickly, and that speed is wasted if the underlying idea is unproven.

### Measure against your own trailing data first

**[INFERENCE]** Because the public benchmarks in this document are old, self-reported and skewed to venture-backed US companies (retention from 2020, activation from 2022, free-to-paid from 2023, feature adoption from 2019), the department should treat them as rough orientation and use the company's own trailing cohorts as the primary baseline. Each benchmark paragraph in sections 5, 9 and 10 carries its date so that agents can state its age when quoting it.

### The value equation

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** Hormozi's value equation is: value equals dream outcome multiplied by perceived likelihood of achievement, divided by time delay multiplied by effort and sacrifice. The instruction is to raise the top two and cut the bottom two, and driving delay and effort toward zero is presented as the strongest lever. He advises building early wins so that customers feel on track ([Dean Yeong, book notes on $100M Offers](https://www.deanyeong.com/book-notes/100m-offers); [SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/)). Perceived likelihood is raised by proof, credibility and guarantees ([SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/)). The equation is a framing device and not a measured formula; no source assigns units to its terms.

### The value equation as a product-design test

**[INFERENCE; adapts HOUSE STYLE]** The department uses the value equation as a design test on every proposed feature, flow or offer by asking which lever it moves. Dream outcome maps to the end result of the customer's job. Perceived likelihood maps to reliability, proof, evaluation pass rates for AI features, and guarantees. Time delay maps to time-to-first-value and time to activation. Effort and sacrifice map to setup steps, integrations, learning curve and form length. A feature that moves none of the four levers is a candidate to cut. The mapping is the researchers' adaptation; it lines up with mainstream activation practice (the earliest milestone that predicts retention, section 9) and with Torres's opportunities (section 6), and the levers are defined in the second-hand summaries cited above ([Dean Yeong, book notes on $100M Offers](https://www.deanyeong.com/book-notes/100m-offers); [SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/)).

**[INFERENCE]** The table gives the test in a form an agent can apply to a specification. The questions and measures are the researchers' synthesis.

| Lever | Question to ask of the feature | Software measure to attach |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome (raise) | Does this make the end result of the customer's job bigger or more complete? | Outcome metric of the opportunity; north-star input |
| Perceived likelihood (raise) | Does this make success more certain or more believable before and after purchase? | Task success rate, eval pass rate, proof shown, uptime |
| Time delay (cut) | Does this shorten the time to the first real result? | Time-to-first-value, activation window |
| Effort and sacrifice (cut) | Does this remove steps, decisions, learning or waiting from the customer? | Step count, form elements, Single Ease Question, support contacts |

### Market selection: four indicators, all required

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** In a video dated 21 January 2021 Hormozi gives four indicators for choosing a market and treats all four as required: the customer feels real pain (a need and not a nice-to-have), the market is stable or growing, prospects are easy to reach through places where they gather, and they have money to spend. Missing one is a dealbreaker. He names health, wealth and relationships as lasting needs. His examples: an unemployed audience fitted on paper but could not pay, and moving the same coaching offer from students to seniors improved the market. He ranks personal experience of a market above the four checks, because knowing its pains leads to a better product ([Hormozi, The Key To Finding a Profitable Market, third-party transcript, video dated 21 Jan 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/the-key-to-finding-a-profitable-market)). **[INFERENCE]** The four indicators work as a segment-screening checklist; "easy to reach" and "money to spend" correspond to the standard segment criteria of accessibility and ability to pay.

### Market first, then offer, then persuasion

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** Book notes on $100M Offers give his order of importance as market first, offer strength second and persuasion skill third. He advises picking a market with existing demand and staying in it long enough to learn, because switching audiences restarts the learning ([Dean Yeong, book notes on $100M Offers](https://www.deanyeong.com/book-notes/100m-offers)). **[GAP]** His claims about niche price multiples (a niche product priced at a multiple of a generic one) and the instruction to commit to the niche were not in any fetched source; they are believed to be in the book and are unverified.

### Offer creation in five steps

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** A study guide to $100M Offers describes five steps: define the dream outcome; list every obstacle the customer meets before, during and after; turn each obstacle into a "how to" solution; generate many delivery vehicles for each solution; then trim the high-cost, low-value items and stack the high-value remainder into one offer. One-to-many assets such as templates and systems are favoured because they carry high value at low ongoing cost ([SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/)). **[GAP]** "Trim and stack" has no primary source in this research: the delivery-cube video does not mention it, and his free course and audiobook episodes, the likely primary sources, were not reachable.

### The delivery cube: six ways to vary how a solution is delivered

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** In a video dated 17 May 2021 Hormozi breaks delivery into six parts. Ratio: one-to-one, small group or one-to-many. Client involvement: do it yourself, done with you or done for you. Support level: which channels the customer can use, from email or chat up to phone or video. Consumption: live or recorded, and written, audio or video. Speed and convenience: faster results or less friction. The ten-times and one-tenth test: what you would add at ten times the price, and how you would deliver more at a tenth of it ([Hormozi, I Broke Down Every High Margin Service Into 6 Buckets (Delivery Cube), third-party transcript, video dated 17 May 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/i-broke-down-every-high-margin-service-into-6-buckets-delivery-cube)). He observes that done-for-you is the easiest to sell and the hardest to run, and that the harder something is to fulfil the harder he finds it to sell at scale. Support channels can separate tiers, for example email for basic and phone or video for premium ([Hormozi, I Broke Down Every High Margin Service Into 6 Buckets (Delivery Cube), third-party transcript, video dated 17 May 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/i-broke-down-every-high-margin-service-into-6-buckets-delivery-cube)).

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** The stated aim of the delivery cube is to find value already inside the business so that revenue rises without much new cost; his analogy is a sawmill selling its sawdust, and he cites delivery and streaming brands to show that customers pay for speed and convenience ([Hormozi, I Broke Down Every High Margin Service Into 6 Buckets (Delivery Cube), third-party transcript, video dated 17 May 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/i-broke-down-every-high-margin-service-into-6-buckets-delivery-cube)).

### Over-deliver first, then make fulfilment efficient

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** The summaries of $100M Offers report his advice to over-deliver at first to create demand and cash, then make fulfilment more efficient without reducing customer value, balancing ease of selling against ease of fulfilling ([SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/); [Dean Yeong, book notes on $100M Offers](https://www.deanyeong.com/book-notes/100m-offers)). Components can be presented as stacked bonuses, which widens the gap between price and perceived value. Guarantees improve an offer but cannot rescue a weak product, and on a weak product they drive refunds ([Dean Yeong, book notes on $100M Offers](https://www.deanyeong.com/book-notes/100m-offers)).

### Pricing power and premium positioning

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** Hormozi advises charging a premium supported by higher perceived value, argues that a price that stings slightly makes customers more invested in getting results, and aims for a wide gap between cost and price. A well-built offer is meant to make buyers judge value instead of comparing prices ([Dean Yeong, book notes on $100M Offers](https://www.deanyeong.com/book-notes/100m-offers); [SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/)). A summary of a 12 May 2026 episode ties premium pricing to delivery: his claim is that a business with unmatched speed, reliability or quality can charge 20 to 40% more and that the higher price funds better talent and delivery ([Shortform summary of The Game Ep 969, 12 May 2026](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2026-05-12-episode-summary-solving-6-scaling-problems-ep-969)). The 20 to 40% figure is his own unaudited claim.

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** A summary of a 23 April 2026 episode reports his claim that cutting a coaching price from $3,000 to $2,500 a month did not change retention and cost roughly $500,000 a month in revenue, from which he concludes that retention in such businesses depends on activation and engagement more than on price ([Shortform summary of The Game Ep 964, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2026-04-23-episode-summary-before-you-run-ads-fix-this-one-thing-first-ep-964)). This is one unaudited case from a coaching business.

### The offer ladder: what a money model is

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** In the 2025 book $100M Money Models, as summarised, a money model is a sequence of offers in which the business finds each further customer problem and offers to solve it, with the aim of recovering customer acquisition cost within about 30 days ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/)). A write-up of the August 2025 launch event records his stated rule that 30-day gross profit should exceed twice the sum of acquisition cost and cost of goods, and gives Skool's own offer as an example: 14 days free, then $99 a month ([jxnl.co report on the Money Models launch, 20 Aug 2025](https://jxnl.co/writing/2025/08/20/the-100m-money-models-the-complete-report)). The business figures he cited at that event (his gym, Gym Launch, Prestige Labs, Skool user growth) are in the source and are unaudited.

### The offer ladder: attraction offers

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** The attraction offers listed in the summary of $100M Money Models are win-your-money-back, giveaway, decoy, buy-X-get-Y, and pay-less-now-or-pay-more-later ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/)). These are front-of-ladder offers meant to turn strangers into customers. Several of them need legal review before use with EU consumers (section 4).

### The offer ladder: upsell offers

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** The upsell types in the summary of $100M Money Models are the classic upsell (offer the next solution when the need appears), the menu upsell (un-sell what the customer does not need, prescribe what they do, give an A or B choice, keep a card on file), the anchor upsell, and the rollover upsell (credit past spend toward a bigger offer) ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/)).

### The offer ladder: downsell offers

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** The downsell types in the summary of $100M Money Models are a payment plan, a trial with a penalty, and a feature downsell. His rule is never to sell the same product for less: change the terms or the features instead ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/)).

### The offer ladder: continuity offers and sequencing

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** The continuity offers in the summary of $100M Money Models are a continuity bonus, a discount for commitment, and a waived fee. His sequencing advice is to build one stage at a time with continuity last, to launch new offers cheaply for feedback and then raise the price until revenue stops rising, and to prefer many ways of selling the same product over many products ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/)).

### Solving the next problem

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary] [GAP]** "Solving the next problem" as a named problem-solution cycle was not found in any primary source. It appears only as the definition of a money model (each offer solves the problem the previous one creates or reveals) and as the classic upsell, both via a second-hand summary ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/)). Agents may use the idea as a sequencing heuristic and must not attribute a named cycle or exact wording to him.

### Focus and saying no

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** In a video dated 16 August 2021 Hormozi borrows the woman in the red dress from The Matrix as an image for attractive side opportunities that pull an owner off the main goal, and says such temptations become more appealing as the business succeeds. Side deals look like free money but cost the focus and time the core business needs; he frames strategy as choosing what to refuse and describes declining consulting requests. He argues that simple businesses scale and elaborate ones fail, and that concentrating on the main revenue source and letting it compound produces the large results. By his own account, starting a second business too early stalled growth for about two years, and he advises that a business should run without the owner's daily work before the owner adds another ([Hormozi, The Biggest Mistake Everyone is Making, third-party transcript, video dated 16 Aug 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/the-biggest-mistake-everyone-is-making)). A second-hand community post from October 2024 likewise attributes to him the view that opportunities multiply with success and become harder to refuse ([Skool community post by Alexi Drouin, Oct 2024](https://www.skool.com/acceleratoruniversity/the-woman-in-the-red-dress-alex-hormozis-lesson-from-the-matrix)).

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** In a video dated 11 March 2026 he treats saying no as a discipline problem: the more capable a person becomes, the more opportunities arrive and the more must be declined; one thing done well for a long time can become large, while two things split attention so that neither does. He tells a founder that adding info products or software alongside the core business is a distraction, likening it to a juice seller adding newspapers, and tests new ideas by whether they serve the core ([Hormozi, You Have To Learn To Say "No", third-party transcript, 11 Mar 2026](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/you-have-to-learn-to-say-no)). A second-hand summary of a 12 May 2026 episode adds related advice: weigh opportunity cost across businesses, divest lower-potential ones even at a loss, pick one main sales channel and extend what works, and favour recurring revenue with switching costs ([Shortform summary of The Game Ep 969, 12 May 2026](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2026-05-12-episode-summary-solving-6-scaling-problems-ep-969)). **[GAP]** His keynote "New Distractions Kill" exists ([Global Player podcast listing, New Distractions Kill](https://globalplayer.com/podcasts/episodes/7DroGbD); [Podscripts listing, New Distractions Kill, not read](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/new-distractions-kill-keynote-speech-at-future-flipper-pt-2-ep-461)) but its transcript blocks automated access and was not read.

### Retention figures he cites for community businesses

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript] [PLATFORM CLAIM]** In a transcript of his retention video (transcribed August 2026; original upload date unknown; the transcript writes "School" where Skool is evidently meant) he gives what he presents as platform figures for communities on Skool: average monthly retention of 80%, the best communities under 10% monthly churn, and retention under 70% described as poor and as making retention the highest-return fix. He describes churn as front-loaded: over 20% in months one to three, about 10% or less by day 90, and about 2% by month six. He states that halving churn from 20% to 10% doubles lifetime value ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)). These are his own unaudited platform figures for paid community memberships and are far worse than acceptable B2B SaaS retention (section 3).

### Activation: a fast first win

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** His activation advice in the retention video is to put the most valued element of the product into onboarding so that a new customer gets a fast win, ideally within 24 hours; to set expectations accurately at the point of sale; and to aim for a real outcome by day 90 and customer-to-customer connection by month six ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)). A second-hand summary of a 23 April 2026 episode adds the method for finding activation steps: compare high performers with churned clients, identify the steps the retained ones took, and standardise them ([Shortform summary of The Game Ep 964, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2026-04-23-episode-summary-before-you-run-ads-fix-this-one-thing-first-ep-964)). A community post attributes to him a 14-item churn checklist that includes a cancellation video and finding the activation point, the first "aha" moment; only two of the items were visible ([Skool community post on the churn checklist, Nov 2024](https://www.skool.com/acceleratoruniversity/churn-checklist-alex-hormozis-14-strategies)).

### Subtract before adding

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** By his account in the retention video, overwhelm is the top churn driver after price. His method is to ask customers which single thing they would keep if everything else were deleted and which thing they would cut. He cites one case that he says cut monthly churn from 30% to 5% by deleting content ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)). The case is a single unaudited community example.

### Cancellation feedback as a product input

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** His feedback routine in the retention video is to ask every cancelling customer why, tag the reasons (price, overwhelm, not using, missing feature, wrong expectation), act on the top one or two reasons after roughly every 20 cancellations and never on a single reply, and ask the best customers why they stay and do more of that. He also recommends seating support leadership next to the chief executive so that problems reach product quickly ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)).

### Price as a filter, and annual plans

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** In the retention video he advises ignoring price complaints from non-ideal customers; if ideal customers cite price, add a lower tier with features held back for the higher tier (his example is about $9 against $99 a month). He recommends annual plans at roughly 16 to 17% off, framed as "buy 10, get two", on the reasoning that retention stabilises because the customer decides once a year ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)). The figures are his own.

### Delivery capacity before paid growth

**[HOUSE STYLE; second-hand summary]** The summary of the 23 April 2026 episode reports his view that paid advertising multiplies revenue only when delivery capacity exists, and that a one-off transformation and an ongoing service should be charged for separately ([Shortform summary of The Game Ep 964, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2026-04-23-episode-summary-before-you-run-ads-fix-this-one-thing-first-ep-964)). **[INFERENCE] [GAP]** "Product quality before scaling" is best presented as an inference from this point, his retention-first material and his own story of adding a second business too early ([Hormozi, The Biggest Mistake Everyone is Making, third-party transcript, video dated 16 Aug 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/the-biggest-mistake-everyone-is-making)); no primary statement of such a rule was found, and the 12 May 2026 episode summary does not state one ([Shortform summary of The Game Ep 969, 12 May 2026](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2026-05-12-episode-summary-solving-6-scaling-problems-ep-969)).

## 3. Adapting the house style to software

### Why the service-business models need adapting

**[INFERENCE]** Hormozi's models were built for businesses that sell transformation through people: gyms, coaching, done-for-you services and paid communities. Software differs in four ways that matter for product decisions: the subscription is the product and not the last step of a ladder, marginal delivery cost is low except where AI usage is metered, retention norms are far higher, and EU consumer and marketing law constrains several of his promotional tactics. The paragraphs below give the department's adapted defaults. Each adaptation is the researchers' synthesis unless a source is cited.

### What transfers to software unchanged

**[INFERENCE; adapts HOUSE STYLE]** Five house-style ideas transfer to software without change: the four-lever value test ([Dean Yeong, book notes on $100M Offers](https://www.deanyeong.com/book-notes/100m-offers)), the four market indicators as a segment screen ([Hormozi, The Key To Finding a Profitable Market, third-party transcript, video dated 21 Jan 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/the-key-to-finding-a-profitable-market)), the obstacle list before, during and after the outcome as a discovery prompt ([SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/)), the fast first win as an activation goal ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)), and the tagged-cancellation routine as a feedback loop ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)). His focus rule also agrees with SVPG's first strategy principle, focus ([SVPG, The Product Operating Model, Feb 2023, modified Jul 2024](https://svpg.com/the-product-operating-model-an-introduction)); both imply a short list of problems and an explicit not-doing list.

### The obstacle list becomes an opportunity list

**[INFERENCE; adapts HOUSE STYLE]** In software discovery, Hormozi's list of every obstacle the customer meets before, during and after the outcome ([SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/)) is the same artefact as the opportunity space in Teresa Torres's opportunity solution tree ([Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees, Dec 2023, modified 29 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)). The difference is the evidence standard: Torres requires opportunities to come from story-based customer interviews, so the department drafts the obstacle list as a hypothesis and confirms each item against real customer stories before it enters the tree.

### The delivery cube applied to SaaS

**[INFERENCE; adapts HOUSE STYLE]** The table maps the six parts of the delivery cube ([Hormozi, I Broke Down Every High Margin Service Into 6 Buckets (Delivery Cube), third-party transcript, video dated 17 May 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/i-broke-down-every-high-margin-service-into-6-buckets-delivery-cube)) onto software packaging choices. ### Trim and stack for software

**[INFERENCE; adapts HOUSE STYLE]** For software, trimming means removing or declining features whose cost-to-serve and added complexity exceed their value, and stacking means packaging the high-value, low-marginal-cost remainder. Hormozi's subtraction case (churn cut by deleting content) supports removing low-use features, but it is one unaudited community example ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)). The only usage dataset in this research, Pendo's 2019 report, found about 80% of tagged features rarely or never used, with a definition that inflates the unused share (section 9; [Pendo, The 2019 Feature Adoption Report](https://www.pendo.io/resources/the-2019-feature-adoption-report/)). Features required by law, accessibility or security are never candidates for trimming regardless of usage.

### The offer ladder mapped to SaaS

**[INFERENCE; adapts HOUSE STYLE]** The table maps the four stages of Hormozi's offer ladder ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/)) onto SaaS mechanics. The key change is order: in SaaS the subscription is the core product, so continuity comes first and not last.

| Ladder stage | His version | SaaS equivalent | What changes | Source of the original |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Attraction | Win-your-money-back, giveaway, decoy, buy-X-get-Y, pay less now or more later | Free trial, freemium, low-priced entry tool | Money-back, giveaway and penalty mechanics need legal review for EU consumers | [SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/) |
| Upsell | Classic, menu, anchor, rollover | Higher tier, add-ons, seats, usage or credits | Trigger on usage signals, not on a sales conversation alone | [SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/) |

| Continuity | Bonus, commitment discount, waived fee; built last | The subscription itself; annual commitment discount | Continuity is the starting point in SaaS | [SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/) |

### The 30-day payback rule is an aspiration in SaaS

**[CONTESTED; adapts HOUSE STYLE]** Hormozi's aim of recovering acquisition cost within about 30 days, and his rule that 30-day gross profit should exceed twice the sum of acquisition cost and cost of goods ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/); [jxnl.co report on the Money Models launch, 20 Aug 2025](https://jxnl.co/writing/2025/08/20/the-100m-money-models-the-complete-report)), are cash rules for service businesses. **[INFERENCE]** The researchers' reading is that the rule is stricter than typical SaaS payback norms and should be treated as an aspiration and not a benchmark. **[GAP]** No SaaS payback benchmark was fetched in this research, so the comparison is unquantified here.

### His churn figures are not SaaS benchmarks

**[CONTESTED]** Hormozi's community figures (80% average monthly retention, churn over 20% in the first three months) describe paid community memberships ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)). The last large public panel on software retention, from June 2020 and therefore possibly outdated, put good six-month user retention at about 60% for SMB and mid-market SaaS and about 70 to 75% for enterprise SaaS ([Lenny's Newsletter, What is good retention, Jun 2020, modified Jan 2024](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-good-retention-issue-29)). Agents must not use his churn figures as targets or as acceptable levels for a B2B SaaS product; they use his diagnostic routine (front-loaded churn, activation, subtraction, tagged cancellation reasons) and the company's own cohorts.

### Premium pricing needs a predictability check in software

**[CONTESTED; adapts HOUSE STYLE]** Hormozi's premium-price advice ([Dean Yeong, book notes on $100M Offers](https://www.deanyeong.com/book-notes/100m-offers)) assumes high-touch delivery where a higher price funds better service. Software pricing in 2026 must also track usage cost and buyer predictability: internal cost and margin was the top factor in pricing AI features in a 2025 survey ([Growth Unhinged, 2025 State of B2B Monetization, survey fielded Apr-May 2025](https://kylepoyar.substack.com/p/2025-state-of-b2b-monetization)), and 2026 guidance treats the customer's ability to predict the bill as a pre-change check ([Growth Unhinged, The AI monetization debate shifts again, Oct 2026](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/usage-based-pricing-for-ai)). The department keeps his principle of pricing on value and his rule against discounting the identical product, and applies the pricing evidence in section 10 for model and structure.

### Lock-in is a weak retention strategy in the EU

**[INFERENCE; adapts HOUSE STYLE]** The second-hand summary of the 12 May 2026 episode has him favouring recurring revenue with switching costs ([Shortform summary of The Game Ep 969, 12 May 2026](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2026-05-12-episode-summary-solving-6-scaling-problems-ep-969)). For a company selling in the EU, the Data Act has applied since 12 September 2025 and gives customers a framework for switching between data-processing providers while restricting unfair contract terms (checked on the Commission's own policy page, not on the regulation text; [European Commission, Data Act policy page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/data-act)). Retention therefore has to come from delivered value and not from making exit hard. Whether a given SaaS product falls inside the Data Act's switching rules is a question for Legal.

### Promotional tactics that need legal review before software use

**[INFERENCE] [GAP]** His course lists scarcity and urgency as a module ([Acquisition.com, Offers training, checked Nov 2026](https://www.acquisition.com/training/offers)), and his attraction and downsell offers include win-your-money-back, giveaways, trials with penalties and cards on file ([SuperSummary, $100M Money Models summary, book published 2025](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-money-models/summary/)). The EU legal fit of these tactics was not researched. Fake countdown timers, pre-selected paid add-ons and cancellation that is harder than sign-up are among the expected themes of the planned Digital Fairness Act according to a law-firm summary (secondary source only; [Heuking, 8 May 2026](https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/digital-fairness-act-implementation-obligations-companies-need-to-prepare-for.html)). Until Legal has cleared a specific mechanic for a specific market, agents treat these tactics as unavailable for consumer-facing flows.

## 4. Precedence: compliance first, evidence second, house style third

### The order of precedence

**[INFERENCE]** When sources conflict, the department applies them in this order. Compliance comes first: a legal or regulatory requirement overrides any evidence finding and any house-style model, and where the legal position is unverified the agent escalates to Legal instead of deciding. Evidence comes second: where independent data or an established standard contradicts a house-style rule for software, the evidence wins and the house-style rule is kept only as framing. House style comes third: it is the default wherever the first two layers are silent, and it supplies the vocabulary agents use to explain decisions. The order is the owner's instruction recorded as a convention; no external source states it.

### His numbers are never customer-facing proof

**[INFERENCE]** Hormozi's figures (retention percentages, the 30% to 5% churn case, the $500,000 a month pricing case, the 20 to 40% premium) are his own unaudited claims and come from other industries. Agents may use them internally as prompts for what to investigate. Agents must not present them to customers, in marketing copy, in forecasts or in business cases as evidence of what this product will achieve.

### Brand voice: structures yes, impersonation no

**[INFERENCE]** The house style governs structure (the levers, the ladder, the cube, the focus rule) and not wording. Because all of his material in this research is paraphrased from summaries and auto-generated transcripts, agents have no verified quotations to use and must not attribute exact phrases to him or imitate his persona in product copy.

### Scarcity and urgency versus EU rules on manipulative design

**[CONTESTED] [GAP]** Hormozi's own course includes a module on scarcity and urgency ([Acquisition.com, Offers training, checked Nov 2026](https://www.acquisition.com/training/offers)). On the compliance side, press reports of 9 November 2026 say the draft Digital Fairness Act would restrict manipulative interface design and require disclosure of the stock remaining at an advertised price ([PYMNTS/CPI relaying Reuters, 9 Nov 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/cpi-posts/eu-draft-bill-targets-unfair-digital-practices-with-fines-of-up-to-6/)), and a law-firm summary of 8 May 2026 lists fake countdown timers among the expected themes (secondary source only; [Heuking, 8 May 2026](https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/digital-fairness-act-implementation-obligations-companies-need-to-prepare-for.html)). The act is not law. The existing Unfair Commercial Practices Directive and the Commission's December 2021 guidance on dark patterns were not read in this research, so the current legal position on false urgency is unverified background here. Ruling: agents may state real limits that the system can verify (a genuine deadline, a genuine capacity limit) and may not create artificial ones; any urgency or scarcity element in a consumer flow needs Legal approval.

### Guarantees versus the statutory withdrawal right

**[INFERENCE]** In the house style a guarantee raises perceived likelihood ([SuperSummary, $100M Offers, section 3 summary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/section-3-summary/)). For consumer distance contracts in the EU a statutory withdrawal right exists independently of any guarantee, and since 19 June 2026 it must be exercisable through a dedicated online function (secondary source only; [CMS, Ny "ångerknapp" krävs på e-handelsplattformar, 15 Jun 2026](https://cms.law/sv/swe/legal-updates/ny-aangerknapp-kraevs-paa-e-handelsplattformar-fraan-den-19-juni-2026); [Greenberg Traurig, May 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/5/eu-consumer-law-new-withdrawal-button-requirements-for-online-contracts)). Ruling: a commercial guarantee never replaces, shortens or obscures the statutory right, and the guarantee claim flow is kept visibly distinct from the withdrawal function and from subscription cancellation (section 18).

### Subtraction versus mandatory functions

**[INFERENCE]** Hormozi's advice to subtract before adding ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)) applies to features and content the company chose to build. It does not apply to functions the law requires, which include accessibility support under the Swedish act where it applies ([Riksdagen, SFS 2023:254, consolidated text amended up to SFS 2026:1026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2023254-om-vissa-produkters-och-tjansters_sfs-2023-254/)), consent controls, the withdrawal function and AI disclosure. Ruling: the trim test runs only on discretionary scope, and a worker agent proposing a removal must state that no legal, accessibility or security function is affected.

### Price complaints versus the evidence on losing deals on price

**[CONTESTED]** Hormozi advises ignoring price complaints from non-ideal customers and adding a lower tier only when ideal customers cite price ([Hormozi, How to Get Your Customers to Stay Forever, third-party transcript, transcribed Aug 2026](https://sozai.app/transcript/get-customers-to-stay-forever/)). A July 2026 practitioner benchmark treats about 20% of lost deals lost on price as healthy, 40% or more as a red flag, and almost no pushback as a sign of under-pricing ([Growth Unhinged, Pricing used to last 18 months, Jul 2026](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/saas-pricing-out-of-date)). The two are compatible if the complaint is segmented first. Ruling: tag every price objection with the customer's segment, apply his filter to decide whose complaints count, and use the 20% and 40% levels as tripwires for a pricing review.

### AI agents as "done for you" delivery versus disclosure and approval rules

**[INFERENCE]** The delivery cube's done-for-you option ([Hormozi, I Broke Down Every High Margin Service Into 6 Buckets (Delivery Cube), third-party transcript, video dated 17 May 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/i-broke-down-every-high-margin-service-into-6-buckets-delivery-cube)) maps naturally to an AI agent doing work for the customer. That design is subject to AI Act Article 50 as summarised by a law firm: an agent must disclose that it is AI and whom it acts for at the point of contact and again at key steps (secondary source only; [Faegre Drinker, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations)). Apple's 2026 guidance adds that destructive or hard-to-undo actions such as deleting data or purchasing should not be automated without confirmation ([Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Generative AI, created 9 Jun 2025, updated 8 Jun 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)). Ruling: done-for-you by agent is allowed only with disclosure at entry and approval gates tiered by reversibility and cost (section 19).

## 5. Product strategy

### Strategy is a leader-owned choice of a few problems

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** In the SVPG model, product strategy is the choice of the few most important problems to solve in service of a customer-centric vision. Its principles are focus, being powered by insights (from data, customers, technology and the industry), transparency, and placing bets, meaning a quarterly portfolio of bets against annual business objectives. Product leaders usually make these calls, not a committee of stakeholders, and teams then receive problems and outcomes instead of feature lists ([SVPG, The Product Operating Model, Feb 2023, modified Jul 2024](https://svpg.com/the-product-operating-model-an-introduction)). **[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** Hormozi's focus rule points the same way: strategy is choosing what to refuse ([Hormozi, The Biggest Mistake Everyone is Making, third-party transcript, video dated 16 Aug 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/the-biggest-mistake-everyone-is-making)).

### A strategy one-pager for the manager agent

**[INFERENCE]** The manager agent keeps product strategy on one page with seven parts: the vision in one paragraph; the target segment and the problem it has; the two to four problems chosen for this period and the insight behind each; what is explicitly not being done; one outcome metric per problem; the bets and the evidence level of each; and the principles for trade-offs. The outline is the researchers' synthesis from SVPG's strategy principles ([SVPG, The Product Operating Model, Feb 2023, modified Jul 2024](https://svpg.com/the-product-operating-model-an-introduction)) and is not a published template.

### Segment choice: screen with the four indicators

**[INFERENCE; adapts HOUSE STYLE]** For segment choice the department screens each candidate segment against Hormozi's four indicators (real pain, stable or growing, easy to reach, money to spend; [Hormozi, The Key To Finding a Profitable Market, third-party transcript, video dated 21 Jan 2021](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/the-key-to-finding-a-profitable-market)) and then refines the choice with the positioning method below, which finds the segment that cares most about the product's differentiated value ([April Dunford, A Quickstart Guide to Positioning, 15 Mar 2021](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning)). **[GAP]** Market sizing methods and formal segmentation methods were not researched; no sourced guidance is recorded.

### Positioning in five components, worked in order

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** April Dunford's positioning method (15 March 2021) has five components: competitive alternatives, differentiated capabilities, customer value, the segment that cares most, and market category. The work is done in that order. Begin by asking what a customer would do if the product did not exist; the answer is often a spreadsheet, a manual process or nothing. Then list what the product has that those alternatives lack, ask "so what" of each attribute to reach the value, find who cares most about that value, and only then pick the market category that makes the value obvious ([April Dunford, A Quickstart Guide to Positioning, 15 Mar 2021](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning)).

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** Dunford's practical rules for positioning are: do not rely on a fill-in-the-blank positioning statement; position only against alternatives that actually show up in deals and not against "phantom" rivals; do not assume a new category is needed, because creating one is slow and costly; and test the positioning in customer conversations ([April Dunford, A Quickstart Guide to Positioning, 15 Mar 2021](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning)).

### Competitive analysis starts from the customer's real alternatives

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** The only sourced competitive-analysis guidance in this research is Dunford's: the competitive set is defined by the customer's real alternatives, including the status quo, and other vendors are watched as future threats until they appear in deals ([April Dunford, A Quickstart Guide to Positioning, 15 Mar 2021](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning)). Jobs-to-be-done theory widens the set beyond the product category: in the Christensen Institute's milkshake case, the morning rivals were bananas and bagels ([Christensen Institute, Jobs to Be Done, page modified 30 May 2025](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/)). **[GAP]** Competitive-analysis frameworks beyond this (feature matrices, win-loss analysis, battlecards) were not researched.

### Build, buy or partner

**[VENDOR; practitioner opinion]** Pragmatic Institute, a training company, gives these criteria (page modified 12 September 2025): buy when existing parts can be added without reinventing them; build when nothing similar exists and the capability would become part of the company's core; partner when both companies and the customer gain. The decision should rest on market need. Its three steps are to define the problem through market and competitive analysis, define the ideal solution with the architect, lead engineer or chief technology officer, and then choose the route ([Pragmatic Institute, Buy, Build or Partner, page modified 12 Sep 2025](https://pragmaticinstitute.com/product/framework/buy-build-or-partner)). **[INFERENCE]** A sharper SaaS reading by the researchers: build what differentiates the product in the positioning exercise, buy or integrate commodity capability, and partner where the customer's job spans two products. **[GAP]** No source with quantified criteria (total cost of ownership, time-to-market thresholds) was read; a Forrester framework exists but is paywalled ([Forrester, The Build, Buy, Or Partner Decision Framework, title only, paywalled](https://www.forrester.com/report/the-build-buy-or-partner-decision-framework/RES173053)). Platform and ecosystem strategy was not researched.

### Product-market fit: the Sean Ellis survey protocol

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** The Sean Ellis test asks users how they would feel if they could no longer use the product, with three answers: very disappointed, somewhat disappointed, not disappointed. The 40% threshold comes from Ellis benchmarking close to 100 startups; those at or above 40% "very disappointed" mostly had traction and those below mostly struggled. It is one practitioner's benchmark and not a peer-reviewed result ([First Round Review, How Superhuman Built an Engine to Find Product Market Fit, 12 Nov 2018](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)). Mixpanel, an analytics vendor, repeats more than 40% as the conventional threshold ([Mixpanel, How to find PMF with data, Jul 2024, edited Jan 2025](https://mixpanel.com/blog/how-to-find-product-market-fit-with-data/)). Ellis's own original post was not fetched; the protocol is recorded through Rahul Vohra's 2018 account.

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** The protocol in Vohra's account of the Sean Ellis test: ask users who used the product at least twice in the past two weeks; ask each user once; about 40 responses give a directional read; and add three open questions on who would benefit most, the main benefit, and how to improve the product ([First Round Review, How Superhuman Built an Engine to Find Product Market Fit, 12 Nov 2018](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)). Superhuman, a single-company case, scored 22% in summer 2017, 33% after narrowing to the segment that loved it, and 58% about three quarters later ([First Round Review, How Superhuman Built an Engine to Find Product Market Fit, 12 Nov 2018](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)).

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** Vohra's method for acting on Sean Ellis survey results: segment by answer and build the target profile from the "very disappointed" group; ignore the "not disappointed" group; among the "somewhat disappointed", listen to those whose main benefit matches the fans'; split the roadmap half on deepening what fans love and half on removing what holds the middle group back; re-survey continuously and make the score a team key result ([First Round Review, How Superhuman Built an Engine to Find Product Market Fit, 12 Nov 2018](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)).

### Product-market fit: the retention curve

**[VENDOR]** Mixpanel's guidance (July 2024, edited January 2025) is that a retention curve that flattens signals product-market fit; its illustration is 50% of a cohort active at day 5 and still 50% at day 20. Once the curve is flat, the job is to raise the plateau. It lists habit-moment examples attributed to Reforge: Slack, messages sent on 4 of 7 days; Zoom, 4 meetings hosted in the first 28 days; Pinterest, a first pin within 7 days and then weekly pinning ([Mixpanel, How to find PMF with data, Jul 2024, edited Jan 2025](https://mixpanel.com/blog/how-to-find-product-market-fit-with-data/)). **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** Mixpanel reports that getting new users to share insights with colleagues flattened its own retention curve, with a claimed threefold effect on six-month retention ([Mixpanel, How to find PMF with data, Jul 2024, edited Jan 2025](https://mixpanel.com/blog/how-to-find-product-market-fit-with-data/)).

### A product-market fit routine for a small SaaS

**[INFERENCE]** A workable product-market fit routine for a small B2B SaaS company: survey recently active users once each; wait for roughly 40 or more responses; report the "very disappointed" share by segment; treat 40% as a rule of thumb and not a law; treat any result from fewer than 40 responses as qualitative; and require a flattening logo and revenue retention curve before scaling acquisition. The routine combines the Ellis protocol ([First Round Review, How Superhuman Built an Engine to Find Product Market Fit, 12 Nov 2018](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)) and the retention-curve signal ([Mixpanel, How to find PMF with data, Jul 2024, edited Jan 2025](https://mixpanel.com/blog/how-to-find-product-market-fit-with-data/)) and is the researchers' synthesis.

## 6. Discovery

### Continuous discovery: the opportunity solution tree

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion, page revised 29 Sep 2026]** Teresa Torres's opportunity solution tree has four levels: an outcome at the root; opportunities, meaning unmet customer needs, pains and desires drawn from interview stories; solutions beneath opportunities; and assumption tests beneath solutions. If a statement can be addressed in only one way, it is a solution in disguise and not an opportunity ([Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees, Dec 2023, modified 29 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)). Torres sells courses on this method, so it is a practitioner position; it remains the reference practice in 2026.

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** The build steps for an opportunity solution tree are: start with a customer theory, a defined outcome and three to four story-based interviews; map the customer's experience; group the opportunities; choose one target opportunity; brainstorm solutions for that opportunity only; pick three; break each into assumptions; test the riskiest assumptions; then discard, refine or pick a new opportunity ([Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees, Dec 2023, modified 29 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)).

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** The operating rules for an opportunity solution tree are: one target opportunity at a time; compare at least three solutions when the opportunity is risky or differentiating; interview week over week and revisit the opportunity space every three to four interviews; work as a trio of product manager, designer and engineer; and keep one tree per outcome ([Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees, Dec 2023, modified 29 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)).

### Choose a product outcome as the root

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** Torres distinguishes three outcome types: business outcomes, which are financial; product outcomes, which are a customer behaviour or sentiment and are the recommended root of the tree; and traction metrics, which measure adoption of a single feature and are usually too narrow ([Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees, Dec 2023, modified 29 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)). **[INFERENCE]** The manager agent assigns each worker a product outcome and keeps the business outcome at department level.

### Jobs-to-be-done

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion, page modified 30 May 2025]** In the Christensen Institute's statement of jobs-to-be-done theory, a job is the progress a person seeks in a given circumstance, and people "hire" products when the job arises. Every job has functional, social and emotional dimensions. To find a job, look past demographics and features to circumstances; ask for the story behind a decision instead of only asking why; and look for circumstances shared across people. Jobs shift when circumstances shift ([Christensen Institute, Jobs to Be Done, page modified 30 May 2025](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/)).

**[DURABLE; illustrative cases]** Two jobs-to-be-done cases from the Christensen Institute: morning milkshake buyers wanted something to fill them and occupy a commute, while afternoon buyers wanted a treat for children; and a tax-software firm found the main job was spending less time on taxes, after which simplifying the product raised sales ([Christensen Institute, Jobs to Be Done, page modified 30 May 2025](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/)). **[GAP]** Moesta's switch interview and "forces of progress" method were not fetched.

### Interview technique: ask for specific past events

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** NN/g's interviewing guidance (25 July 2010) says to avoid leading questions, hypothetical "would you use" questions, questions about specific design choices, "how do you usually" questions (they yield idealised accounts) and detailed recall of past use. Prefer critical-incident questions about a specific hard or smooth case, open exploration of attitudes, and questions asked right after an observed task. People will voice an opinion on almost anything when asked, so weight unprompted comments more ([NN/g, Interviewing Users, 25 Jul 2010](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/interviewing-users/)). Torres's method likewise builds opportunities from stories about specific past events ([Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees, Dec 2023, modified 29 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)).

### An interview snapshot format

**[INFERENCE]** After each discovery interview a worker agent writes a snapshot with five parts: participant and context; the specific story told; opportunities heard, in the customer's words; short quotations; and follow-ups. The interviewer asks about the last time something happened and not for opinions about features. The outline is the researchers' synthesis from Torres's method ([Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees, Dec 2023, modified 29 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)).

### Assumption testing: five categories and a ranking method

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion, page revised 30 Sep 2026]** Torres names five categories of assumption: desirability, viability, feasibility, usability and ethical. Generate assumptions by story-mapping the solution step by step; rank them with David Bland's assumption map, which plots importance against evidence; and test the ones that are important and weakly evidenced ([Product Talk, Assumption Testing, Oct 2023, modified 30 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/assumption-testing/)).

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** Torres's rules for designing an assumption test: phrase assumptions as things customers will do; choose among four test types (prototype tests, one-question surveys, data mining and research spikes); begin with something that can be completed in about an hour; set success criteria before running the test; and compare solutions against each other instead of asking whether one is good enough ([Product Talk, Assumption Testing, Oct 2023, modified 30 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/assumption-testing/)). **[GAP]** Her assumption-testing page gives no sample sizes, and her interview guide sits inside a course that was not accessible.

### Prototypes: build to learn before build to earn

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul and Nov 2026; practitioner opinion]** SVPG's 2026 position is that AI has made prototypes cheap, so teams should prototype to learn before building to earn. Prototypes are tested with users for value and usability, with stakeholders for viability, and with engineers for feasibility. AI helps with research but does not supply product judgment ([SVPG, AI and the Future of the Product Role, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.svpg.com/ai-and-the-future-of-the-product-role/); [SVPG, The AI Productivity Paradox, 23 Jul 2026](https://www.svpg.com/the-ai-productivity-paradox/)). **[INFERENCE]** Dual-track work in 2026 terms is "build to learn" (throwaway prototypes) running alongside "build to earn" (production quality); the two tracks should not share quality standards.

### Why test before building: most ideas do not work

**[DURABLE; academic, 2022]** A 2022 review of online controlled experiments reports that more than half of tested ideas fail to produce a meaningful improvement, which is the empirical case for testing before building ([Larsen, Stallrich, Sengupta, Deng, Kohavi and Stevens, Statistical Challenges in Online Controlled Experiments, 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11366v1)). **[GAP]** No rigorous study quantifying the outcome uplift from continuous discovery itself was found; the support is practitioner opinion plus this experiment-failure data.

### How much discovery teams actually do

**[FAST-MOVING, Q4 2025 fieldwork; survey]** In the PLA/ProductPlan survey, 34% of respondents regularly collect insights and use them in prioritisation, 18.9% collect but struggle to act, 12.3% have no consistent process and 10.2% rarely research. On AI in discovery, 47.1% use it to summarise feedback, 39.8% to find themes, and 27.9% do not use it; 39.3% name research and synthesis as their top 2026 investment ([Product-Led Alliance statistics page citing the PLA/ProductPlan State of Product Management 2026, published May 2026](https://www.productledalliance.com/product-management-statistics/)). In Atlassian's State of Product 2026, 80% of teams do not involve engineers in ideation, problem definition or roadmap creation ([Mind the Product summary of Atlassian's State of Product 2026, Sep 2025](https://www.mindtheproduct.com/inside-atlassians-state-of-product-2026-key-takeaways/)).

### Sweden and EU: recording customer interviews

**[GAP]** The GDPR practicalities of recording customer interviews in Sweden and the EU (consent, retention periods, participant data handling) were not researched. **[INFERENCE]** Until Legal provides a rule, agents record or transcribe interviews only with documented consent and store participant data under controlled permissions, in line with NN/g's repository advice on participant data ([NN/g, Research Repositories, 5 Jul 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/research-repositories/)).

## 7. Prioritisation and roadmaps

### Scoring models are decision aids, not decision makers

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026; survey]** ProductPlan's State of Product Management 2026 (250 product leaders) reports that over 60% of prioritisation frameworks are overridden by leadership escalations and that becoming outcome-focused is among the top challenges ([ProductPlan, State of Product Management Report 2026, landing page](https://www.productplan.com/ebooks/the-state-of-product-management-report-2026)). Intercom's own RICE article says the score is not a hard rule ([Intercom, RICE: simple prioritization for product managers, Jan 2018](https://www.intercom.com/blog/rice-simple-prioritization-for-product-managers/)). In Atlassian's State of Product 2026, 49% say internal politics held them back ([Mind the Product summary of Atlassian's State of Product 2026, Sep 2025](https://www.mindtheproduct.com/inside-atlassians-state-of-product-2026-key-takeaways/)). **[INFERENCE]** Because overrides are the norm, the manager agent records every override and its reason next to the score, so that the trade-off stays visible.

### Opportunity assessment

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** Torres's alternative to scoring ideas is to assess opportunities on four factors (opportunity sizing, market factors, company factors and customer factors) and then to compare solutions for the one chosen opportunity ([Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees, Dec 2023, modified 29 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)).

### RICE

**[VENDOR; canonical statement, Jan 2018]** Intercom's RICE score is Reach multiplied by Impact multiplied by Confidence, divided by Effort. Reach is people or events per period taken from real metrics. Impact uses a fixed scale: 3 massive, 2 high, 1 medium, 0.5 low, 0.25 minimal. Confidence is 100% high, 80% medium or 50% low. Effort is person-months across product, design and engineering. Intercom's caveats are that dependencies and table-stakes features can justify working out of score order if the trade-off is visible ([Intercom, RICE: simple prioritization for product managers, Jan 2018](https://www.intercom.com/blog/rice-simple-prioritization-for-product-managers/)).

### ICE

**[VENDOR; glossary]** ICE scores Impact, Confidence and Ease from 1 to 10 each and multiplies them; for example 7 × 6 × 5 = 210. Sean Ellis created it for fast ranking of growth experiments. Its weaknesses: it is highly subjective; a low Ease score can sink high-impact work and bias teams toward small wins; scorers need a shared definition of each scale point; and it suits comparing a few options, not ranking a whole roadmap. RICE differs by adding Reach and dividing by Effort instead of multiplying by Ease ([ProductPlan glossary, ICE Scoring Model](https://www.productplan.com/glossary/ice-scoring-model/)).

### Kano

**[DURABLE; reference summary, Wikipedia]** The Kano model sorts features into must-be (absence hurts, presence is neutral), one-dimensional (more is better), attractive (presence delights, absence is not missed), indifferent, and reverse. The method asks two questions per feature: a functional one (how would you feel if the product had it) and a dysfunctional one (how would you feel if it did not), each with five answers: I like it, I expect it, I am neutral, I can tolerate it, I dislike it ([Wikipedia, Kano model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kano_model)).

**[DURABLE; reference summary, Wikipedia]** Kano answer pairs map to categories as follows: expect plus dislike is must-be; like plus dislike is one-dimensional; like plus neutral is attractive; neutral plus neutral is indifferent; dislike plus expect is reverse. Contradictory pairs such as like plus like are flagged questionable and dropped. Categories decay: as competitors improve, a delighter becomes a performance feature and then a basic expectation ([Wikipedia, Kano model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kano_model)). **[INFERENCE]** Kano needs survey volume; for a small B2B customer base, the question pair can be used in interviews as a qualitative probe and not as statistics. **[GAP]** Kano sample-size guidance and the satisfaction and dissatisfaction coefficient formulae were not retrieved.

### Opportunity scoring

**[DURABLE; reference summary, Wikipedia]** Ulwick's opportunity score in Outcome-Driven Innovation is importance plus the larger of zero and importance minus satisfaction. Customers rate each desired outcome from 1 to 10 for importance and for current satisfaction, and the inputs are the share of high ratings, so importance counts double ([Wikipedia, Outcome-Driven Innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outcome-Driven_Innovation)). **[VENDOR]** Strategyn, the firm that sells the method, says (page modified 7 July 2026) that a market typically yields 50 to 150 outcome statements, that surveys run from 180 to 3,000 respondents, and that outcomes should be solution-free, measurable and stable over time ([Strategyn, Outcome-Driven Innovation process, page modified 7 Jul 2026](https://strategyn.com/outcome-driven-innovation-process/)).

**[VENDOR; weak basis]** Strategyn's headline 86% success rate for Outcome-Driven Innovation rests on 21 launches at its own clients, of which 18 were rated successful by the sponsoring companies, and its 17% industry comparison is a blend of 12 outside sources with no underlying data on the page ([Strategyn, Outcome-Driven Innovation process, page modified 7 Jul 2026](https://strategyn.com/outcome-driven-innovation-process/)). Agents must not quote the 86% figure as evidence. **[GAP]** Numeric thresholds for an "underserved" outcome and Ulwick's desired-outcome statement template were not on any fetched page; do not quote one from this document.

### Cost of delay and WSJF

**[DURABLE; framework owner, page modified Apr 2026]** Weighted shortest job first (WSJF) is relative cost of delay divided by relative job duration. Cost of delay is the sum of user or business value, time criticality, and risk reduction or opportunity enablement. Sunk cost is ignored by construction ([Scaled Agile, WSJF, page modified Apr 2026](https://framework.scaledagile.com/wsjf)). **[INFERENCE]** WSJF fits work whose time criticality differs sharply, such as regulatory deadlines. **[GAP]** Scaled Agile's estimation-scale guidance for WSJF is behind a login and was not read.

### The now-next-later roadmap

**[VENDOR; canonical statement, Oct 2022]** In ProdPad's now-next-later roadmap, Now holds defined, specified work in progress; Next is less detailed and may depend on the results of Now; Later holds broad problems without solutions. Items are framed as problems with candidate experiments attached, each linked to a business objective; any real deadline goes on the item itself; and OKRs carry the commitment so that the roadmap can stay flexible ([ProdPad, Now-Next-Later roadmap, Oct 2022](https://www.prodpad.com/blog/invented-now-next-later-roadmap/)).

### Roadmap alignment in practice

**[FAST-MOVING, Q4 2025 fieldwork; survey]** In the PLA/ProductPlan survey, 61% rate roadmap-to-strategy alignment at 4 out of 5 or higher (mean 3.6), and organisations in mid-transition to product-led score lower (3.1 to 3.2 out of 5) than fully product-led ones (4 out of 5) ([Product-Led Alliance statistics page citing the PLA/ProductPlan State of Product Management 2026, published May 2026](https://www.productledalliance.com/product-management-statistics/)).

### Saying no

**[HOUSE STYLE; primary transcript]** Hormozi treats saying no as discipline: skill brings more opportunities than can be pursued, splitting attention across two things stops either becoming large, and new ideas are tested by whether they serve the core ([Hormozi, You Have To Learn To Say "No", third-party transcript, 11 Mar 2026](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/you-have-to-learn-to-say-no)). **[INFERENCE]** The department's script for declining a request has four moves: restate the request as a problem; show which current outcome it would displace; give the evidence threshold that would change the answer; and log it under Later if it is a real problem. The script is the researchers' synthesis.

## 8. Specification and delivery

### Delivery principles

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion]** SVPG's delivery principles are small, frequent, uncoupled releases; instrumentation; monitoring; and deployment infrastructure that includes A/B testing capability. Releases should happen at least fortnightly and ideally continuously ([SVPG, The Product Operating Model, Feb 2023, modified Jul 2024](https://svpg.com/the-product-operating-model-an-introduction)). In an empowered team the product manager is not a backlog administrator or project manager, and unclear boundaries between product manager, designer and delivery manager are a symptom of a feature team ([SVPG, Product vs Feature Teams, Aug 2019, modified Oct 2023](https://svpg.com/product-vs-feature-teams)).

### Do not speed up unproven work

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul 2026; practitioner opinion]** SVPG warns that using AI to produce business cases, roadmaps, product requirements documents and code faster only speeds up the wrong work if the idea is unproven ([SVPG, The AI Productivity Paradox, 23 Jul 2026](https://www.svpg.com/the-ai-productivity-paradox/)). **[INFERENCE]** A worker agent writes a full specification only after the riskiest assumptions have test results attached.

### A one-page specification outline

**[INFERENCE]** The department's one-page specification has nine parts: the problem and its evidence; the target user and segment; the outcome metric and target; non-goals; a solution sketch or prototype link; the riskiest assumptions and their test results across value, usability, feasibility, viability and ethics; the release plan (flag, cohort, beta criteria); the measurement plan; and open questions. For AI features it adds the location of the eval set, pass thresholds, a cost-to-serve budget and human-review points. The outline is the researchers' synthesis, consistent with SVPG's four risks ([SVPG, The Product Operating Model, Feb 2023, modified Jul 2024](https://svpg.com/the-product-operating-model-an-introduction)), and is not a published template. **[GAP]** Published templates for product requirements documents and one-pagers (Lenny's collection, Atlassian, Intercom) were not fetched.

### User stories

**[DURABLE; official guidance, last updated 23 May 2016, still the live page]** The GOV.UK Service Manual format for a user story is: as a (who), I need or want (what), so that (why). Any format is acceptable if it names the actor, the narrative and the goal. The goal matters most; if it is hard to write, question whether the feature is needed. Stories needing more than a few weeks are epics and should be split to fit an iteration ([GOV.UK Service Manual, Writing user stories, last updated 23 May 2016](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/writing-user-stories)).

### INVEST

**[DURABLE; practitioner body]** INVEST is a checklist for a user story: Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small enough to fit one iteration, and Testable. It originated with Bill Wake in 2003 and was popularised by Mike Cohn in 2004. A story that fails a criterion should be reworded or split ([Agile Alliance, INVEST](https://agilealliance.org/glossary/invest/)).

### Acceptance criteria

**[DURABLE; official guidance and canonical practitioner statement]** In the GOV.UK Service Manual, acceptance criteria are a checklist of outcomes showing that the need is met, often phrased "it's done when" ([GOV.UK Service Manual, Writing user stories, last updated 23 May 2016](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/writing-user-stories)). The Given-When-Then form, from Dan North and Chris Matts as part of behaviour-driven development, states the precondition (Given), the behaviour (When) and the expected change (Then), with "And" to chain conditions. Martin Fowler's example (21 August 2013): given holdings of 100 and 150 shares, when 20 are sold, then 80 remain and a sell order exists ([Martin Fowler, GivenWhenThen, 21 Aug 2013](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/GivenWhenThen.html)). **[INFERENCE]** The department's rule: use "done when" checklists for simple stories and Given-When-Then for behaviour with preconditions or edge cases, and pass every story through INVEST before it enters a sprint.

### Acceptance criteria for AI features are eval tasks

**[VENDOR; Jan 2026]** Anthropic's guidance on evaluations says that writing eval tasks forces a team to define success and exposes vague requirements: two engineers reading the same specification may read edge cases differently, and eval tasks settle the difference. A good task is one where two domain experts independently reach the same pass or fail verdict ([Anthropic, Demystifying evals for AI agents, 9 Jan 2026](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)). A September 2025 practitioner piece describes evals as the new product requirements documents, with eval prompts acting as living requirements for AI behaviour ([Lenny's Newsletter, Why AI evals are the hottest new skill, 25 Sep 2025](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-ai-evals-are-the-hottest-new-skill)). **[INFERENCE]** Acceptance criteria for AI features should be written as eval tasks with pass rates over multiple trials, because a single pass or fail is not meaningful for non-deterministic output (section 12).

### Involve engineering early

**[FAST-MOVING, reported Sep 2025; survey]** In Atlassian's State of Product 2026, 80% of teams do not involve engineers in ideation, problem definition or roadmap creation ([Mind the Product summary of Atlassian's State of Product 2026, Sep 2025](https://www.mindtheproduct.com/inside-atlassians-state-of-product-2026-key-takeaways/)). Torres's method assumes the opposite: a trio of product manager, designer and engineer does discovery together ([Product Talk, Opportunity Solution Trees, Dec 2023, modified 29 Sep 2026](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)). **[INFERENCE]** The Product department brings the Application department's engineering agents into problem definition and not only into delivery (section 22).

### Feature flags: four types with different lifetimes

**[DURABLE; canonical practitioner statement, revised 9 Oct 2017]** Pete Hodgson's taxonomy of feature toggles has four types. Release toggles hide unfinished work and should live a week or two. Experiment toggles run A/B tests and live hours to weeks. Ops toggles are kill switches changed during incidents. Permission toggles gate features by user group, such as premium or beta, and can last years ([Pete Hodgson on martinfowler.com, Feature Toggles (aka Feature Flags), revised 9 Oct 2017](https://martinfowler.com/articles/feature-toggles.html)).

**[DURABLE; canonical practitioner statement]** Hodgson's management rules for feature flags: treat flags as inventory with a carrying cost; add a removal task when creating a release flag, and set expiry dates or cap the total number; test the expected production configuration and the fallback, not every combination; and by convention Off means the existing behaviour ([Pete Hodgson on martinfowler.com, Feature Toggles (aka Feature Flags), revised 9 Oct 2017](https://martinfowler.com/articles/feature-toggles.html)). **[GAP]** Feature-flag guidance newer than 2017, such as vendor data on flag debt, was not retrieved.

### Beta programmes and release planning

**[INFERENCE]** A beta programme can be run on permission toggles for a named customer group, the one flag type Hodgson expects to be long-lived ([Pete Hodgson on martinfowler.com, Feature Toggles (aka Feature Flags), revised 9 Oct 2017](https://martinfowler.com/articles/feature-toggles.html)); release toggles should not be reused for this purpose. **[GAP]** Beta programme design, sizing, duration and exit criteria, release planning, and progressive-rollout percentages were not researched, and no data on them was found.

### Trustworthy rollout tooling

**[DURABLE; academic, 2022]** The 2022 review of online controlled experiments says trustworthy rollout and experiment tooling needs A/A tests and sample-ratio-mismatch checks, and that experiment horizons are commonly about two weeks ([Larsen, Stallrich, Sengupta, Deng, Kohavi and Stevens, Statistical Challenges in Online Controlled Experiments, 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11366v1)).

## 9. Metrics and experimentation

### The north-star metric

**[VENDOR; Aug 2024]** Amplitude's criteria for a north-star metric are that it reflects the moment customers get value, represents the product strategy, and leads future results. Revenue and average revenue per user are lagging measures and unsuitable. A product picks one of three "games": attention (time spent), transaction (number of transactions) or productivity (work done efficiently); most B2B SaaS plays the productivity game ([Amplitude, Product North Star Metric, 20 Aug 2024](https://amplitude.com/blog/product-north-star-metric)). Amplitude states the framework's purpose as connecting customer language, product language and business language so that everyone can tie work to a sustainable growth strategy ([Amplitude, North Star Playbook](https://amplitude.com/books/north-star)).

**[VENDOR; Aug 2024]** Amplitude recommends one north-star metric per product with three to five input metrics that the team can directly move, and offers a heuristic for inputs: breadth, depth, frequency and efficiency. Its example for self-serve SaaS is trial accounts with more than three users active in week one ([Amplitude, Product North Star Metric, 20 Aug 2024](https://amplitude.com/blog/product-north-star-metric)). **[INFERENCE]** For a B2B SaaS product a productivity-game north star of the form "accounts completing the core job at least N times a week" fits these criteria, with inputs following breadth (accounts), depth (jobs per account), frequency and efficiency (time to complete). **[GAP]** The checklist chapter of Amplitude's playbook returned only a table of contents; the criteria above come from its 2024 blog version.

### A metric tree for the department

**[INFERENCE]** The department's metric tree has six layers: one north-star metric that expresses value delivered to customers and leads revenue; three to five inputs that teams can move; an activation definition with a time bound; cohort retention, both logo and revenue, by signup month; feature adoption per feature measured as reach, depth and repeat use; and guardrails for latency, support contacts and cost-to-serve. The tree is the researchers' synthesis from the Amplitude criteria ([Amplitude, Product North Star Metric, 20 Aug 2024](https://amplitude.com/blog/product-north-star-metric)) and the activation guidance below ([Lenny's Newsletter, What is a good activation rate, 25 Oct 2022](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-activation-rate)).

### HEART: five categories of user-centred metrics

**[DURABLE; academic, primary paper, CHI 2010]** The HEART framework from Rodden, Hutchinson and Fu at Google defines five categories. Happiness covers attitudes such as satisfaction and ease of use, tracked by survey. Engagement is the frequency, intensity or depth of use, best expressed as per-user averages. Adoption is new users starting to use a product or feature in a period. Retention is users from one period still active in a later one. Task success covers completion rate, time on task and error rate ([Rodden, Hutchinson and Fu, Google, CHI 2010 paper (PDF)](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/36299.pdf); [Google Research publication page for the HEART paper, 2010](https://research.google/pubs/measuring-the-user-experience-on-a-large-scale-user-centered-metrics-for-web-applications/)).

**[DURABLE; academic, primary paper, CHI 2010]** The HEART paper's Goals-Signals-Metrics process is: agree goals first without worrying about measurability; identify the behaviour or attitude signals of success and failure, noting that failure signals such as abandonment are often easier to find; then turn signals into normalised metrics such as ratios or per-user averages. The paper contrasts HEART with PULSE (page views, uptime, latency, seven-day actives, earnings), which it treats as business and technical health measures that can rise for bad reasons ([Rodden, Hutchinson and Fu, Google, CHI 2010 paper (PDF)](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/36299.pdf)).

### Activation: choose the earliest milestone that predicts retention

**[DURABLE; practitioner opinion with survey data, Oct 2022]** Guidance from Lenny Rachitsky and Yuriy Timen on choosing an activation milestone: it should be predictive, meaning users who hit it retain at least twice as well; actionable at the level of the individual user; neither too early, such as signup, nor too late, such as a repeat purchase; and simple. Start from a hypothesis and then confirm that moving the milestone moves later metrics. Activation rate is users reaching the milestone divided by users completing signup ([Lenny's Newsletter, What is a good activation rate, 25 Oct 2022](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-activation-rate)). **[INFERENCE; adapts HOUSE STYLE]** Hormozi's "activation point" is the same construct, and his method of comparing retained and churned customers ([Shortform summary of The Game Ep 964, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-game-w-alex-hormozi-2026-04-23-episode-summary-before-you-run-ads-fix-this-one-thing-first-ep-964)) matches the "predictive" test.

### Activation benchmarks, dated 2022

**[FAST-MOVING, 25 Oct 2022, possibly outdated; self-reported survey]** The last large public activation survey in this research covered more than 500 products in October 2022. Overall the mean activation rate was 34% and the median 25%; for SaaS only, the mean was 36% and the median 30%. The authors define good as the 60th percentile and great as the 80th. About 6% of respondents used a time-bound definition, with a median window of 10 days and 7 days the most common; about 10% used a two-part milestone ([Lenny's Newsletter, What is a good activation rate, 25 Oct 2022](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-activation-rate)). ### Feature adoption

**[VENDOR; 2019, pre-2024]** Pendo's 2019 Feature Adoption Report measured 615 Pendo customer subscriptions over three months and found that about 80% of features were rarely or never used and that about 12% of features produced 80% of daily usage. A "feature" in this data is a Pendo tag, so the result depends on what customers chose to tag, which inflates the unused share ([Pendo, The 2019 Feature Adoption Report](https://www.pendo.io/resources/the-2019-feature-adoption-report/)). ### Metrics for usage-priced products

**[FAST-MOVING, Oct 2026; practitioner opinion]** For usage-priced products, Growth Unhinged recommends adding two metrics: time-to-ramp, the time for the average customer to reach 80% of its usage allowance, and share of wallet ([Growth Unhinged, The AI monetization debate shifts again, Oct 2026](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/usage-based-pricing-for-ai)).

### A/B testing needs large samples

**[DURABLE; academic, 2022]** The sample-size rule of thumb for an A/B test is n ≈ 16σ²/δ² per variant at 80% power and a 0.05 significance level. The paper's worked example, with 5% conversion and a $25 average order, shows that detecting a 5% change in revenue needs about 147,456 users per variant. If fewer than 20% of users are exposed to a change, a whole-population test can be badly under-powered ([Larsen, Stallrich, Sengupta, Deng, Kohavi and Stevens, Statistical Challenges in Online Controlled Experiments, 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11366v1)).

**[DURABLE; academic, 2022]** The same review lists common A/B testing errors: peeking at results and stopping early inflates false positives, with always-valid p-values (mSPRT) as one remedy; p-values are commonly misread; and long tests can lose over 75% of users to cookie churn ([Larsen, Stallrich, Sengupta, Deng, Kohavi and Stevens, Statistical Challenges in Online Controlled Experiments, 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11366v1)). **[GAP]** Per-company experiment success rates and Twyman's law were not in the retrieved portion of the paper.

### When not to A/B test

**[INFERENCE]** The department does not run an A/B test in four cases: when weekly eligible users are far below the n ≈ 16σ²/δ² requirement for the smallest effect worth having; when the change is a strategic or legal necessity; when the exposed fraction is small and cannot be triggered cleanly; and when the question is why something happens. Most early-stage EU B2B SaaS products fall into the first case for everything except top-of-funnel pages. The rule is the researchers' synthesis from the sample-size mathematics ([Larsen, Stallrich, Sengupta, Deng, Kohavi and Stevens, Statistical Challenges in Online Controlled Experiments, 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11366v1)). Low-traffic B2B products lean on qualitative tests, evals and cohort analysis instead.

### Qualitative for why, quantitative for how much

**[FAST-MOVING, Oct 2026; practitioner opinion]** SVPG's guidance on experimentation is to use qualitative methods for why and quantitative methods for causality and size. For AI products, quantitative tests cost more and their conclusions may not persist, so evals are used for quality early and A/B tests are reserved for establishing value. SVPG recommends that product teams own their experiments with support from an enablement group it calls experimentation ops ([SVPG, Experimentation, AI and the Product Model, Oct 2026](https://www.svpg.com/experimentation-ai-and-the-product-model/)). **[VENDOR; Jan 2026]** Anthropic makes a compatible point: A/B testing measures real outcomes but is slow, needs traffic and says little about why, so teams should combine automated evals, production monitoring, A/B tests, user feedback, transcript review and human studies as overlapping layers ([Anthropic, Demystifying evals for AI agents, 9 Jan 2026](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)).

### Sweden and EU: consent shrinks the measurable sample

**[FAST-MOVING, law-firm guide undated, refers to 2023 events; secondary source only]** In Sweden the cookie rule sits in the Electronic Communications Act (2022:482), chapter 9, section 28: consent is required for any cookie or similar technology that is not necessary for a service the user expressly requested, and analytics cookies get no exemption ([Bird & Bird, Global Cookie Review, Sweden, undated, refers to 2023 events](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/trending-topics/global-cookie-review/sweden)). **[INFERENCE]** Product analytics that stores or reads data on the user's device therefore needs prior opt-in consent, and the measured sample for EU traffic will be materially smaller than the real one; experiments must be sized on consented traffic. The consent rules are detailed in section 18. This is orientation and not legal advice.

## 10. Go-to-market, pricing and packaging

### Launch tiers

**[VENDOR; practitioner opinion, page modified 2 Nov 2026, original date not shown]** Pragmatic Institute defines three launch tiers. Tier 1 is a rare launch with major strategic impact, such as a new category or an acquisition, and gets the most resources. Tier 2 is a meaningful incremental improvement and gets some support. Tier 3 has minimal market impact, such as a maintenance release, and gets general communications only. The tier is assigned by expected market impact and business impact, not by engineering effort: the same work could be tier 3 as internal tooling and tier 1 if it fixes a severe churn problem ([Pragmatic Institute, Use product launch tiers, page modified 2 Nov 2026](https://www.pragmaticinstitute.com/blog/post/prioritize-product-launch-resources-with-launch-tiers)).

**[INFERENCE]** The department's tiering rule: score each release on market impact and business impact; only releases high on both get tier 1; and decide the tier at kickoff so that Marketing's lead time is known. **[GAP]** Product-marketing handoff checklists and per-tier deliverable lists were not found; Intercom's announcement-tier worksheet appeared only as a third-party copy and was not read.

### Pricing models are moving from seats and flat fees to hybrid

### Make variable pricing predictable

**[FAST-MOVING, Oct 2026; practitioner opinion]** Growth Unhinged lists four ways to make variable pricing predictable for buyers: hard budget caps; usage tiers with annual drawdown; a platform fee plus included usage; and an adaptive flat rate with a true-up the next year. ### Review pricing about every six months

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul 2026; practitioner opinion]** Growth Unhinged argues that pricing used to hold for about 18 months and can now be stale within 6. ### A decision outline for pricing an AI feature

**[INFERENCE]** The department's default sequence for pricing an AI feature: bundle AI into the core product when cost-to-serve is low and it defends retention, which was the majority pattern in 2025 ([Growth Unhinged, 2025 State of B2B Monetization, survey fielded Apr-May 2025](https://kylepoyar.substack.com/p/2025-state-of-b2b-monetization)); add credits or usage pricing when marginal cost is material or value scales with volume; price per countable output when a unit of work is obvious ([Growth Unhinged, The AI monetization debate shifts again, Oct 2026](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/usage-based-pricing-for-ai)); always give caps or drawdown so that bills are predictable; and schedule a pricing review every six months ([Growth Unhinged, Pricing used to last 18 months, Jul 2026](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/saas-pricing-out-of-date)). ## 12. Building AI products

### Start with the simplest architecture that works

**[VENDOR; Dec 2024, modified Oct 2026; the page notes tooling has changed]** Anthropic's guidance on building agents is to prefer the simplest solution: a single well-prompted call with retrieval is often enough, and complexity should be added only when it measurably improves outcomes. Workflows, which follow predefined paths, suit well-defined tasks; agents suit open-ended ones. Agents cost more, are slower and compound errors, so they need sandbox testing, guardrails, stopping conditions and human checkpoints, and they should show their plan ([Anthropic, Building effective agents, Dec 2024, modified Oct 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)).

### Choosing AI use cases

**[INFERENCE]** The department favours AI use cases with three properties: the outcome can be checked in the environment, errors are cheap or reviewable, and a countable output exists to price on. The rule is the researchers' synthesis from Anthropic's outcome-grading point ([Anthropic, Demystifying evals for AI agents, 9 Jan 2026](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)) and the pricing guidance on countable outputs ([Growth Unhinged, The AI monetization debate shifts again, Oct 2026](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/usage-based-pricing-for-ai)). **[FAST-MOVING, Jan 2026; practitioner opinion]** NN/g expects 2026 to be a year of "AI fatigue" with low-value AI features and recommends using AI where it solves a user problem and keeping it in the background ([NN/g, State of UX 2026, 16 Jan 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/state-of-ux-2026/)).

### Evaluation vocabulary

**[VENDOR; 9 Jan 2026]** Anthropic's evaluation vocabulary: a task is one test case; a trial is one attempt at it; a grader scores the result; a transcript is the record of the attempt; the outcome is the real end state, for example whether the booking exists; and a suite is a set of tasks. Graders come in three kinds: code-based (cheap, objective, brittle), model-based (flexible, needs calibration against humans) and human (the gold standard, slow) ([Anthropic, Demystifying evals for AI agents, 9 Jan 2026](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)).

### Building an eval suite

**[VENDOR; 9 Jan 2026]** Anthropic's guidance on building an eval suite: start with 20 to 50 tasks drawn from real failures, manual pre-release checks, bug trackers and support queues. Capability evals should start with low pass rates; regression evals should sit near 100%. Balance cases where a behaviour should occur with cases where it should not. Grade outcomes and not step sequences. Read the transcripts. Replace evals that have saturated ([Anthropic, Demystifying evals for AI agents, 9 Jan 2026](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)).

### Consistency: pass@k versus pass^k

**[VENDOR; 9 Jan 2026]** Anthropic distinguishes two metrics for non-deterministic systems: pass@k, at least one success in k tries, and pass^k, all k tries succeed. A 75% per-trial success rate gives about 42% for three successes in a row, and consistency across repeated runs is what customer-facing use requires ([Anthropic, Demystifying evals for AI agents, 9 Jan 2026](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)). **[INFERENCE]** Customer-facing AI flows should carry a pass^k target in the specification, not a single-run pass rate.

### Error analysis before writing evals

**[FAST-MOVING, Sep 2025; practitioner opinion]** Husain and Shankar's error-analysis method for AI products: appoint one domain expert as quality authority; sample about 100 real interactions; write free-form critiques with a pass or fail judgment (open coding); cluster the critiques into fewer than about 10 failure modes (axial coding); count them; use code checks for objective failures and a language-model judge for subjective ones; use binary labels and not 1 to 5 scales; split labelled data roughly 10 to 20% for prompt examples, 40 to 45% for development and 40 to 45% for test; and judge the judge by its true-positive and true-negative rates ([Lenny's Newsletter, Building eval systems that improve your AI product, 9 Sep 2025](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/building-eval-systems-that-improve)). The same authors say always to begin with error analysis before writing evals, and that dogfooding alone is not enough for most AI products ([Lenny's Newsletter, Why AI evals are the hottest new skill, 25 Sep 2025](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-ai-evals-are-the-hottest-new-skill)).

### Evals for quality, A/B tests for value

**[FAST-MOVING, Oct 2026; practitioner opinion]** SVPG's position on AI product discovery is that it includes an eval process, that many quality calls are subjective, and that evals are used for quality while A/B tests establish value ([SVPG, Experimentation, AI and the Product Model, Oct 2026](https://www.svpg.com/experimentation-ai-and-the-product-model/)).

### An AI feature specification outline

**[INFERENCE]** The department's specification for an AI feature has nine parts: the user job and the cost of failure; the simplest viable architecture and why; the eval suite, capability and regression, with a pass^k target for customer-facing flows; grader types per failure mode; human-in-the-loop points for irreversible or high-cost actions; disclosure and marking under Article 50; cost-to-serve per unit of output and how price covers it; monitoring and feedback capture; and rollback. The outline is the researchers' synthesis from the Anthropic guidance ([Anthropic, Building effective agents, Dec 2024, modified Oct 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents); [Anthropic, Demystifying evals for AI agents, 9 Jan 2026](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)).

### Cost-to-serve and trust

**[FAST-MOVING, 2025 to 2026; survey and practitioner reports]** Cost and margin was the leading factor in AI pricing decisions in the 2025 survey ([Growth Unhinged, 2025 State of B2B Monetization, survey fielded Apr-May 2025](https://kylepoyar.substack.com/p/2025-state-of-b2b-monetization)), and pass-through-plus-fee models appeared in 2026 ([Growth Unhinged, The AI monetization debate shifts again, Oct 2026](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/usage-based-pricing-for-ai)). ## 13. UX fundamentals

### Nielsen's ten usability heuristics

**[DURABLE; published 24 Apr 1994, last reviewed 30 Jan 2024]** Nielsen's ten usability heuristics are: visibility of system status; match between the system and the real world; user control and freedom; consistency and standards; error prevention; recognition rather than recall; flexibility and efficiency of use; aesthetic and minimalist design; helping users recognise, diagnose and recover from errors; and help and documentation. The explanations were expanded in 2020 and the heuristics themselves are unchanged ([NN/g, 10 Usability Heuristics, 24 Apr 1994, last reviewed 30 Jan 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)).

**[DURABLE]** Practical readings of the heuristics from the same NN/g source: give timely feedback on what the system is doing; use the user's words and not internal jargon; offer clearly marked exits and undo; prefer preventing errors, through constraints and confirmations before commit, over reporting them; write error messages in plain language that state the problem and suggest a fix; and make help task-focused, searchable and in context ([NN/g, 10 Usability Heuristics, 24 Apr 1994, last reviewed 30 Jan 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)).

### Forms: one thing per page and a question protocol

**[DURABLE; official guidance backed by service research]** The GOV.UK Design System's guidance on question pages is to start with one question per page and group questions only when user research supports it. It prescribes a question protocol: know why each question is asked, ask only what is needed, ask each item once per journey and pre-populate it if it is needed again, and tell users why you ask. Allow "I do not know" where that is a valid answer ([GOV.UK Design System, Question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/)).

**[DURABLE; official guidance]** GOV.UK Design System rules for form details: mark optional fields with "(optional)" and do not mark mandatory fields with asterisks; put a back link at the top of each question page and never break the browser back button; for one-time actions such as payment, show a sensible message instead of repeating the action; use the label or legend as the page heading so that screen-reader users hear it once; label the button "Continue" and not "Next"; keep hints to one short sentence without links; and avoid range sliders or, if one is used, offer a non-drag way to answer ([GOV.UK Design System, Question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/)).

**[DURABLE; single case]** The GOV.UK Design System advises testing a form without a progress indicator first: the Carer's Allowance team removed a 12-step indicator with no effect on completion rates or times ([GOV.UK Design System, Question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/)).

### A forms checklist for agents

**[INFERENCE]** The department's forms checklist has seven items: run the question protocol on every field; one question per page by default; "(optional)" labels and no asterisks; never re-ask data, which is also WCAG 2.2 criterion 3.3.7 (section 17); show the total cost before the last step; offer guest checkout where a purchase does not need an account; and aim for 12 to 14 form elements in a checkout. The checklist is the researchers' synthesis from GOV.UK ([GOV.UK Design System, Question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/)) and Baymard ([Baymard, Cart abandonment rate statistics, updated 28 Oct 2026](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)).

### Performance as part of UX

**[DURABLE; official guidance, page updated 31 Oct 2024]** Google's Core Web Vitals "good" thresholds are: Largest Contentful Paint at most 2.5 seconds, Interaction to Next Paint at most 200 milliseconds, and Cumulative Layout Shift at most 0.1. ### Design for trust

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** Trust shows up in both the checkout data and the AI guidance. ### Empty states

**[DURABLE; practitioner guidance, 19 Sep 2021]** NN/g's guidance on empty states in complex applications: never leave an empty state blank, because users cannot tell loading from failure from no data. ## 14. Design process and design systems

### The Double Diamond

**[DURABLE; codified 2003]** The UK Design Council's Double Diamond has four phases: Discover (understand the problem with the people affected), Define (reframe the challenge), Develop (generate a range of answers, co-design) and Deliver (test at small scale, drop what fails, improve what works). It was later extended as the Framework for Innovation, which allows returning to earlier stages. The Design Council itself hosts a piece by its former design director questioning whether the Double Diamond is still fit for purpose ([Design Council, The Double Diamond](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/our-resources/the-double-diamond/)).

### The design sprint

**[DURABLE; process dates from 2010 to 2012]** The GV design sprint runs five days: Monday, map the challenge and pick a target; Tuesday, sketch solutions individually and start recruiting customers; Wednesday, critique, decide and storyboard; Thursday, build a realistic "fake it" prototype and finalise the interview guide; Friday, interview customers and summarise. Jake Knapp began running sprints at Google in 2010 and took the process to GV in 2012 ([GV, The Design Sprint](https://www.gv.com/sprint/)). **[INFERENCE]** A sprint's Friday test and the five-user guidance in section 15 are consistent: both are problem-discovery exercises and not measurement.

### Design critique

**[DURABLE; practitioner guidance, 23 Oct 2016]** NN/g defines a design critique as a session that improves a design against its objectives; it is not an approval review. Roles are a presenter, critiquers from several disciplines, a rotating facilitator who sets scope and time-boxes, and optionally a recorder. The rules: agree objectives and scope first; tie comments to goals and not taste; ask questions instead of issuing directives; and do not solve in the room. Presenters share work beforehand, state what feedback they want and avoid over-explaining. Formats include a round robin or a quota of positive and negative comments to start discussion, and the suggested starting cadence is 30 minutes weekly within the team ([NN/g, Design Critiques, 23 Oct 2016](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-critiques/)).

### Design tokens

**[FAST-MOVING, 2025-2026; community specification, not a W3C Standard]** The Design Tokens Format Module's latest published version is 2025.10. Token files are JSON with the media type `application/design-tokens+json` and the extensions `.tokens` or `.tokens.json`. The specification defines types (colour, dimension in px or rem, font family, font weight, duration, cubic Bézier, number) and composite types (stroke style, border, transition, shadow, gradient, typography), and every token must declare or inherit a type. Aliasing uses `{group.token}` to reference a whole token and JSON Pointer `$ref` to reach a single property; circular references are errors. The fetched page is a preview draft dated 8 September 2026 that says not to implement that draft, so implement 2025.10 ([Design Tokens Community Group, Format Module, preview draft dated 8 Sep 2026](https://www.designtokens.org/tr/drafts/format/)).

**[INFERENCE]** For an agent-run product team, tokens in the 2025.10 JSON format are the natural single source of truth for a design system, because both design tools and code tooling can read them; keep aliases and not resolved values in the source files so that themes stay switchable.

### Figma's design-system features

**[PLATFORM CLAIM; Schema 2025]** At Schema 2025 Figma announced native variables import and export against the Design Tokens Community Group 1.0 specification; "extended collections" for multi-brand systems that inherit updates from a parent; "slots" so that instances can take custom layers without detaching; Code Connect UI (beta) to map components to code; a Figma MCP server (beta) so that language models can generate design-informed code; and "Make kits" that turn libraries into React components and CSS for Figma Make. Variable modes rose to 10 on Professional plans and 20 on Organization plans ([Figma blog, Schema 2025 recap](https://www.figma.com/blog/schema-2025-design-systems-recap/)). **[GAP]** Figma's Schema 2026 announcements and the current state of Figma Make, Dev Mode and the MCP server (general availability, limits) were not checked.

### AI design tools in 2026 and their limits

**[FAST-MOVING, 16 Jan 2026; practitioner opinion]** NN/g's 2026 position is that design systems plus AI prototyping make decent-looking interfaces cheap, so surface-level interface work is becoming replaceable, while curated taste, research-based contextual understanding, critical thinking and judgment are harder to automate. It says AI in 2025 improved and found real uses but still shows inconsistency, hallucinations, edge-case failures and a need for human oversight, and it recommends making the business impact of design work explicit ([NN/g, State of UX 2026, 16 Jan 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/state-of-ux-2026/)). Apple's guidance adds that generative features are easy to prototype but hard to make robust in all real-world situations, because the same input can give different outputs ([Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Generative AI, created 9 Jun 2025, updated 8 Jun 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)).

### Accessibility of AI-generated interface code

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul 2026; secondary mention]** An index called AIMAC, from the GAAD Foundation with ServiceNow, audits the accessibility of code generated by AI models using axe-core ([Agiledrop, 7 Jul 2026](https://www.agiledrop.com/blog/web-accessibility-just-got-worse-a-year-into-eaa-enforcement)). **[INFERENCE]** AI-generated interface code should pass the same accessibility gate as human-written code (section 17); the existence of a dedicated benchmark suggests this is a known weak point. **[GAP]** The AIMAC results themselves were not read, and no independent 2026 evaluation of AI design tools was found.

## 15. User research

### Choosing a research method

**[DURABLE; published 17 Jul 2022, reviewed 15 Jul 2026]** NN/g's framework for choosing a research method uses three dimensions: attitudinal (what people say) versus behavioural (what people do); qualitative (why, and how to fix) versus quantitative (how many, how much); and context of use (natural, scripted, limited, or no product use). Usability studies should lean behavioural rather than attitudinal ([NN/g, When to Use Which User-Experience Research Methods, 17 Jul 2022, reviewed 15 Jul 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/which-ux-research-methods/)).

**[DURABLE]** NN/g's methods by product phase. The table lists them; every row is from the same article.

| Phase | Typical methods | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Strategise | Field studies, diary studies, interviews, surveys, participatory design, concept testing | [NN/g, When to Use Which User-Experience Research Methods, 17 Jul 2022, reviewed 15 Jul 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/which-ux-research-methods/) |
| Design | Card sorting, tree testing, usability testing, remote moderated and unmoderated testing | [NN/g, When to Use Which User-Experience Research Methods, 17 Jul 2022, reviewed 15 Jul 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/which-ux-research-methods/) |

### Sample sizes by goal

**[DURABLE; pre-2024 but still current guidance]** NN/g's sample-size guidance is 5 users for qualitative usability testing, at least 20 for quantitative studies, at least 15 per user group for card sorting, and 39 for eye-tracking heatmaps ([NN/g, How Many Test Users in a Usability Study?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-many-test-users/)).

### The five-user rule and its limits

**[DURABLE; binomial mathematics]** The five-user figure comes from binomial probability: five users give about an 85% chance of seeing a problem that affects 31% of users, and 31% is an average problem frequency from several studies and not a constant. Five users find only about half of the problems that affect 15% of users; detecting a problem that affects 10% of users needs about 18 users, and a 1% problem needs about 189 ([MeasuringU, Why you only need to test with five users (explained)](https://measuringu.com/five-users/)).

**[DURABLE]** MeasuringU's limits on the five-user rule: problem frequency is often below 10% for released products, so five users is weakest on mature products. The rule applies only to discovering problems, not to comparing designs or estimating task times or completion rates. The advised pattern is to test about 5 users, fix, and repeat with new users, for 15 to 20 in total, and a new user group or task set needs a new sample ([MeasuringU, Why you only need to test with five users (explained)](https://measuringu.com/five-users/)). Small-sample scores are unreliable for attitude metrics: a Net Promoter Score from a five-user test should not be reported ([NN/g, Net Promoter Score, 21 Jun 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/nps-ux/)).

### A decision rule for sample size

**[INFERENCE]** The department's rule: if the question is what is wrong and why, run 5 users per distinct user group and iterate; if it is how much, or whether A is better than B, plan 20 or more and report confidence intervals; if the product is mature and remaining problems are rare, five-user rounds will mostly return nothing new, so add analytics, support-ticket mining or larger unmoderated tests. The rule is the researchers' synthesis from NN/g ([NN/g, How Many Test Users in a Usability Study?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-many-test-users/)) and MeasuringU ([MeasuringU, Why you only need to test with five users (explained)](https://measuringu.com/five-users/)).

### Recruiting and the state of research practice

**[VENDOR; surveyed 25 Jul to 9 Aug 2025]** User Interviews, a vendor, surveyed 485 researchers. Finding enough qualified participants, recruiting time and participant quality remain the biggest recruiting problems; 54% cite time to recruit, and more than 70% use a tool for qualitative recruiting. On AI, 80% of researchers use it, up 24 points in a year; 91% worry about accuracy and hallucinations; 63% fear AI devalues human insight; and 41% see AI's effect on UX research as negative against 32% positive ([User Interviews, State of User Research 2025, surveyed 25 Jul to 9 Aug 2025](https://www.userinterviews.com/state-of-user-research-report)). The sample shrank 36% year on year, which limits trend claims, and no 2026 edition was found.

### Synthetic users

**[FAST-MOVING, 21 Jun 2024; small comparative study]** NN/g compared synthetic users with three earlier real-user studies and found that synthetic users gave shallower and more favourable answers than real participants and did not reflect real behaviour well. The failure modes were sycophancy (a synthetic learner claimed to finish all seven courses where a real learner finished three), idealised one-dimensional behaviour, long unprioritised lists, no real behavioural data, overly positive concept tests, patchy coverage of niche groups, and a skew toward what people post online ([NN/g, Synthetic Users, 21 Jun 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/synthetic-users/)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 21 Jun 2024; practitioner guidance]** NN/g's acceptable uses of synthetic users are desk research on an unfamiliar user group; hypotheses, proto-personas and proto-journey maps to test later with people; and piloting an interview guide. Not acceptable are final decisions, replacing real users, or presenting synthetic output as user findings. NN/g warns the risk is highest in organisations with low UX maturity, where stakeholders may treat synthetic output as a substitute for research ([NN/g, Synthetic Users, 21 Jun 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/synthetic-users/)). **[INFERENCE]** The study predates current models; its direction is likely still valid because sycophancy and missing behavioural data are structural, but the size of the gap today is unverified. **[GAP]** No peer-reviewed 2025 to 2026 study comparing synthetic and human participants was read.

### AI-moderated interviews

**[FAST-MOVING, 30 Jan 2026; small qualitative study]** NN/g tested two AI interviewer products with 10 research professionals using the same guide. Strengths: concise summaries that made people feel heard, follow-up on short answers when told to probe, consistent questions, and a full interview in another language on request. Weaknesses: the AI stuck to the script and missed unexpected leads; it could not judge when a topic was covered, with sessions running 13 to 56 minutes; there were interruptions or long pauses; questions were repetitive and praise felt insincere; and behaviour varied between sessions. Only 3 of 10 found the conversation natural and 5 of 10 felt comfortable, and some withheld information ([NN/g, AI-Moderated Interviews, 30 Jan 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)). The study is small and shows kinds of problems, not rates.

**[FAST-MOVING, 30 Jan 2026; practitioner guidance]** NN/g's recommendation is that AI-moderated interviews suit structured work, such as post-launch feedback, screening, multilingual interviews and teams without researchers, and are unsuitable for exploratory discovery, deep-domain topics and task observation; they supplement human moderation and do not replace it ([NN/g, AI-Moderated Interviews, 30 Jan 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-interviewers/)). **[INFERENCE]** The department's policy: AI moderators for structured follow-ups at volume and humans for discovery interviews, with a clear consent and introduction step, since its absence made participants hold back.

### Synthesis: thematic analysis and affinity diagramming

**[DURABLE; method guidance, 17 Aug 2022, reviewed 2 Sep 2026]** NN/g's thematic analysis has six steps: gather the data, transcribing instead of relying on memory; read everything; code segments; group codes into candidate themes, looking for contradictions as well as similarities; take a day's break; and test each theme for support across participants and agreement between analysts. Affinity diagramming is the quick collaborative version: fast, cheap and visual, but less thorough and hard with large or varied data. The pitfalls are skimming, restating instead of interpreting, ignoring data that conflicts with beliefs, and having no defined goal ([NN/g, How to Analyze Qualitative Data: Thematic Analysis, 17 Aug 2022, reviewed 2 Sep 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/thematic-analysis/)).

### Research repositories

**[DURABLE; practitioner guidance, 5 Jul 2024]** NN/g defines a research repository as a central store of research outputs and artefacts: reports, individual insights, plans and screeners, recordings and transcripts, and raw notes. There are two models, which can be combined: document libraries, which are easy to contribute to and hard to search, and insight databases, which are easy to search and costly to maintain, with insights that lose context and need retiring ([NN/g, Research Repositories, 5 Jul 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/research-repositories/)). **[FAST-MOVING, Jul 2024; survey]** In NN/g's survey of 411 respondents, 43% used general collaboration tools as their repository, 32% dedicated research tools and 14% database tools ([NN/g, Research Repositories, 5 Jul 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/research-repositories/)).

**[DURABLE; practitioner guidance, 5 Jul 2024]** NN/g's success factors for a research repository are easy access, controlled permissions for participant data, a few broad tags to start, strong search, a small first import, and onboarding with reminders. Common failures are over-granular tagging, unfamiliar tools and no change-management plan. Ownership usually sits with research operations ([NN/g, Research Repositories, 5 Jul 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/research-repositories/)). **[INFERENCE]** Synthetic-user and AI-moderated outputs should be stored and labelled separately from human findings in the repository, following NN/g's rule against presenting synthetic output as user findings ([NN/g, Synthetic Users, 21 Jun 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/synthetic-users/)).

## 16. Measuring UX

### Pair a behavioural measure with a standard attitude scale

**[INFERENCE]** The department pairs behavioural measures (task success, time on task, errors) with a standard attitude scale. A minimal set for a SaaS product: task success and time on 5 to 8 key tasks; the Single Ease Question after each task; UMUX-Lite in-product every quarter, because it is short enough for in-app surveys; the System Usability Scale in periodic benchmark studies; and Net Promoter Score only as a company-level loyalty number. The set is the researchers' synthesis from the NN/g and MeasuringU sources below.

### System Usability Scale: scoring

**[DURABLE]** The System Usability Scale (SUS) has 10 items on a 5-point scale. For odd-numbered items subtract 1 from the response; for even-numbered items subtract the response from 5; sum the results and multiply by 2.5 for a score from 0 to 100 ([MeasuringU, Measuring Usability with the SUS](https://measuringu.com/sus/)). A SUS score is not a percentage ([NN/g, How Do You Quantitatively Assess Usability?, reviewed 19 Aug 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/measuring-perceived-usability/)).

### System Usability Scale: benchmarks

**[DURABLE; benchmark dataset first described in 2011, still cited in 2026]** The average SUS score across about 500 studies is 68. Above 80.3 is an A, the top 10%; 74 is about the 70th percentile; about 73 is the top 30%; below 51 is an F, the bottom 15%. Users who score a product above about 80 are more likely to recommend it. SUS can be run with very few users, but the estimate is imprecise, so report a confidence interval ([MeasuringU, Measuring Usability with the SUS](https://measuringu.com/sus/); [NN/g, How Do You Quantitatively Assess Usability?, reviewed 19 Aug 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/measuring-perceived-usability/)). **[INFERENCE]** A reasonable internal bar is 74 or more with about 80 as the stretch goal; the grade bands are MeasuringU's curve and not a formal standard. **[GAP]** No refreshed 2025 to 2026 SUS norm was found.

### UMUX-Lite

**[DURABLE; pre-2024]** UMUX-Lite has two items: the system's capabilities meet my requirements, and the system is easy to use, on a 7-point scale in the original and a 5-point variant. It correlates with SUS at r = .83 and with Net Promoter Score at r = .72, and its reliability alpha is .86 against .91 for SUS. The published conversion on the 7-point version is: SUS-equivalent = 0.65 × ((item 1 + item 2 − 2) × 100/12) + 22.9 ([MeasuringU, UMUX-Lite](https://measuringu.com/umux-lite/)). **[GAP]** No numeric UMUX-Lite benchmark table was on the fetched page.

### Post-task and workload scales

**[DURABLE]** NN/g's guidance is to use the Single Ease Question after every task to find the hardest parts, to use SUS after the whole session for benchmarking, and to reserve NASA-TLX for complex, high-consequence domains, since it rarely suits consumer UX ([NN/g, How Do You Quantitatively Assess Usability?, reviewed 19 Aug 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/measuring-perceived-usability/)).

### Net Promoter Score and its limits for UX

**[DURABLE; 21 Jun 2024]** Net Promoter Score is the percentage of promoters (scores of 9 to 10) minus the percentage of detractors (0 to 6) on a 0 to 10 likelihood-to-recommend question, with a range of −100 to +100 ([NN/g, Net Promoter Score, 21 Jun 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/nps-ux/)).

**[CONTESTED; practitioner opinion with reasoning]** NN/g lists the limits of Net Promoter Score as a UX measure: it measures perception, so struggling users can still score high; its three bins discard detail, so a move from 2s to 5s shows no change; price, mandated use and brand also drive it; it rarely moves for specific interface changes; it is easy to game; and scores vary by country and industry. NN/g's recommendations are to keep the standard calculation, also report the mean rating with a margin of error, track it over time, pair it with task success and task time, and use adequate samples ([NN/g, Net Promoter Score, 21 Jun 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/nps-ux/)).

### HEART and performance metrics as UX measures

**[DURABLE]** The HEART categories and the Goals-Signals-Metrics process (section 9; [Rodden, Hutchinson and Fu, Google, CHI 2010 paper (PDF)](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/36299.pdf)) and the Core Web Vitals thresholds (section 13; [web.dev, Web Vitals, updated 31 Oct 2024](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)) also serve as UX metrics. **[GAP]** Customer satisfaction (CSAT) benchmarks, task-success benchmarks such as an average completion rate, and time-on-task analysis methods were not sourced.

**[DURABLE; checked on the Commission's page]** The European Accessibility Act, Directive (EU) 2019/882, covers among other things computers and operating systems, smartphones, ATMs and ticketing machines, telephony, access to audiovisual media, passenger transport services, banking services, e-books and e-commerce; member states had to transpose it by June 2022 ([European Commission, European Accessibility Act page](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/policies/justice-and-fundamental-rights/disability/union-equality-strategy-rights-persons-disabilities-2021-2030/european-accessibility-act_en)).

### Sweden: the accessibility act (2023:254) and who it covers

**[DURABLE; checked on primary source, Riksdagen's consolidated text]** The Swedish act on the accessibility of certain products and services, lag (2023:254), entered into force on 28 June 2025. Under 2 §, e-commerce services are services provided at a distance, through websites and mobile services, electronically and at a consumer's individual request, in order to conclude a consumer contract; a consumer is a natural person acting mainly outside business activity. A new version of 2 § enters into force on 20 November 2026 (SFS 2026:1026), updating a reference to the consumer credit act ([Riksdagen, SFS 2023:254, consolidated text amended up to SFS 2026:1026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2023254-om-vissa-produkters-och-tjansters_sfs-2023-254/)).

**[DURABLE; checked on primary source]** Under 2 § and 10 § of the Swedish accessibility act, services provided by a microenterprise, meaning a company with fewer than ten employees and an annual turnover or balance sheet total of at most EUR 2 million, need not meet the requirements ([Riksdagen, SFS 2023:254, consolidated text amended up to SFS 2026:1026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2023254-om-vissa-produkters-och-tjansters_sfs-2023-254/)). **[FAST-MOVING, 2025; secondary source only]** According to answers from the regulator relayed by a trade body, headcount and financials may have to be aggregated across group companies ([Svensk Handel Q&A with PTS answers, 2025](https://svenskhandel.se/api/documents/dokument/tillganglighetslagen-fragor-svar.pdf)).

**[INFERENCE]** Scope test for a Swedish SaaS company: the act's e-commerce definition is tied to concluding a consumer contract, so purely B2B SaaS appears to be outside the e-commerce service category, while any self-service sign-up or checkout open to consumers is inside it. This is the researchers' reading of 2 §; regulator guidance on mixed B2B and B2C sales was not found, so confirm with Legal. This is orientation and not legal advice.

### Sweden: exemptions and service-provider duties

**[DURABLE; checked on primary source]** Under 7 § and 8 § of the Swedish accessibility act, a requirement does not apply if it would fundamentally alter the service or impose a disproportionate burden; the operator must document and keep the assessment, notify the authority when it relies on the exemption, and supply the assessment on request. Under 24 § to 26 §, a service provider must keep the service compliant continuously, prepare information showing how the service meets the requirements and which requirements are exempted, make sure that information accompanies the service, and on non-compliance fix it and notify the supervisory authorities in the member states concerned at once ([Riksdagen, SFS 2023:254, consolidated text amended up to SFS 2026:1026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2023254-om-vissa-produkters-och-tjansters_sfs-2023-254/)).

### Sweden: enforcement and sanction fees

**[DURABLE; checked on primary source]** Under 32 § to 40 § of the Swedish accessibility act, the authority first asks for correction within a set time and may then issue orders, which can carry a conditional fine (vite). Sanction fees run from SEK 10,000 to SEK 10,000,000 depending on seriousness, with none in minor cases, and cannot be decided more than five years after the breach ended ([Riksdagen, SFS 2023:254, consolidated text amended up to SFS 2026:1026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2023254-om-vissa-produkters-och-tjansters_sfs-2023-254/)).

### Sweden: transitional rules

**[DURABLE; checked on primary source]** The Swedish accessibility act does not apply to pre-recorded media, document file formats and archived content published before entry into force and not changed since. Service contracts signed before entry into force may run to expiry, at the latest 27 June 2030 ([Riksdagen, SFS 2023:254, consolidated text amended up to SFS 2026:1026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2023254-om-vissa-produkters-och-tjansters_sfs-2023-254/)).

### Sweden: how supervision works

**[FAST-MOVING, 2025-2026; secondary sources]** According to a professional-services firm's summary of 22 May 2025, PTS supervises compliance with the Swedish accessibility act, reviews documentation, runs tests and handles user complaints, and coordinates with Mediemyndigheten, Konsumentverket, Transportstyrelsen and MTM for their areas ([EY Sverige, 22 May 2025](https://www.ey.com/sv_se/insights/law/tillganglighetslagen-trader-i-kraft-den-28-juni-2025)). According to answers relayed by a trade body, PTS does not rely on automated checks in supervision, prefers expert review combining tools and skill, and recommends EN 301 549 as the starting point ([Svensk Handel Q&A with PTS answers, 2025](https://svenskhandel.se/api/documents/dokument/tillganglighetslagen-fragor-svar.pdf)). **[FAST-MOVING, 21 Apr 2026; authority press release]** PTS measured 576 Swedish websites in nine sectors in April and October 2025 (report PTS-ER-2026:7) and found shortcomings in every sector, more often among private operators; eight of nine sectors improved between the two measurements, most in pharmacies and banking. ### Accessibility statements

**[DURABLE; W3C guidance]** W3C says an accessibility statement should at least contain a commitment to accessibility, the standard applied (for example WCAG 2.2), and contact details for reporting problems; advisable extras are known limitations, measures taken, technical prerequisites, environments tested and applicable laws. W3C offers a free statement generator ([W3C WAI, Developing an Accessibility Statement](https://www.w3.org/WAI/planning/statements/)). **[INFERENCE]** The Swedish act requires information on how the service meets the requirements to accompany the service (25 §; [Riksdagen, SFS 2023:254, consolidated text amended up to SFS 2026:1026](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/lag-2023254-om-vissa-produkters-och-tjansters_sfs-2023-254/)); publishing a W3C-style statement that also lists exempted requirements is a reasonable way to meet it, though the act does not use the public-sector term for an accessibility statement.

### An accessibility release gate for agents

**[INFERENCE]** The department's accessibility release gate has seven steps: an automated scan with zero errors in the six WebAIM categories; a keyboard-only pass of each key flow, checking that focus is visible and unobscured; a screen-reader pass of sign-up, checkout and search; a check of 24-pixel targets and drag alternatives; a check that login works with paste and password managers; a zoom and reflow check; and a periodic expert audit using WCAG-EM together with sessions with disabled users. Steps two to seven exist because automated tools cannot establish conformance ([W3C WAI, Evaluating Web Accessibility Overview](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/)). The gate is the researchers' synthesis.

**[INFERENCE]** The six WebAIM failure types are all preventable in a design system through contrast tokens, required alt and label properties, lint rules for empty links and buttons, and a `lang` attribute in the page template, so they belong in component-level checks ([WebAIM Million 2026, scan Feb 2026, report updated 30 Mar 2026](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)). **[GAP]** Mobile app accessibility, PDF and document accessibility, and assistive-technology test matrices were not researched.

### Scope and status of this section

### GDPR and deceptive design: the EDPB's six families

**[DURABLE; checked on primary source, EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 version 2.0, adopted 14 Feb 2023]** The European Data Protection Board names six families of deceptive design patterns. Overloading: continuous prompting, privacy maze, too many options. Skipping: deceptive snugness, look over there. Stirring: emotional steering, hidden in plain sight. Obstructing: dead end, longer than necessary, misleading action. Fickle: lacking hierarchy, decontextualising, inconsistent interface, language discontinuity. Left in the dark: conflicting information, ambiguous wording ([EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 v2.0, adopted 14 Feb 2023 (PDF)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-02/edpb_03-2022_guidelines_on_deceptive_design_patterns_in_social_media_platform_interfaces_v2_en_0.pdf)). The guidelines are written for social media interfaces and are listed as final ([EDPB, Guidelines 03/2022 page](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-032022-deceptive-design-patterns-social-media_en)).

**[DURABLE; checked on primary source]** In the EDPB guidelines, fairness under GDPR Article 5(1)(a) is the umbrella test; the consent rules in Article 7 require withdrawal to be about as easy as giving consent; Article 12 requires clear, plain, easily accessible information; and Article 25, data protection by design and by default, is engaged when the most invasive options are pre-selected ([EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 v2.0, adopted 14 Feb 2023 (PDF)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-02/edpb_03-2022_guidelines_on_deceptive_design_patterns_in_social_media_platform_interfaces_v2_en_0.pdf)). **[GAP]** The text of GDPR Article 25 and EDPB Guidelines 4/2019 on data protection by design were not read.

**[DURABLE; checked on primary source]** EDPB best practices that translate into design: shortcuts to the relevant setting wherever related information appears; a contact point and a link to the supervisory authority; a collapsible table of contents in the privacy policy; dated previous versions with changes highlighted; consistent wording; plain-language definitions; contextual just-in-time notices; privacy onboarding early in use; and concrete examples of processing ([EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 v2.0, adopted 14 Feb 2023 (PDF)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-02/edpb_03-2022_guidelines_on_deceptive_design_patterns_in_social_media_platform_interfaces_v2_en_0.pdf)). The EDPB Cookie Banner Taskforce published its report on 18 January 2023 ([EDPB, Report of the Cookie Banner Taskforce, 18 Jan 2023](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/other/report-work-undertaken-cookie-banner-taskforce_en)); its contents were not read.

### A consent interface checklist

**[INFERENCE]** The department's consent interface checklist, derived from the EDPB families ([EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 v2.0, adopted 14 Feb 2023 (PDF)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-02/edpb_03-2022_guidelines_on_deceptive_design_patterns_in_social_media_platform_interfaces_v2_en_0.pdf)): equal visual weight for accept and refuse; no pre-selected invasive options; no repeated prompting after refusal; one language throughout; the same wording in the banner and the policy; and withdrawal reachable in the same number of steps as consent. The checklist is the researchers' synthesis.

### Sweden: cookie consent and analytics

**[FAST-MOVING, law-firm guide undated, refers to 2023 events; secondary source only]** According to a law-firm country guide, the Swedish cookie rule is in the Electronic Communications Act (2022:482), chapter 9, section 28. Consent is required for any cookie or similar technology not necessary for a service the user expressly requested; necessary cookies need no consent but still need information. Analytics cookies get no exemption and are grouped with advertising and social media cookies as requiring consent. Consent must meet the GDPR standard: no implied consent from browsing, no pre-ticked boxes, no cookie walls, and withdrawal as easy as giving consent ([Bird & Bird, Global Cookie Review, Sweden, undated, refers to 2023 events](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/trending-topics/global-cookie-review/sweden)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 2023 events; secondary source only]** The same guide says supervision in Sweden is split: PTS enforces the cookie placement rule and IMY supervises where cookie use involves personal data processing. In 2023 PTS reviewed four large organisations and found all in breach, mainly on the validity of consent; PTS's position is that rejecting must be as easy as accepting and offered in the same layer, and that colour or contrast must not be used to favour acceptance. No fine amounts are reported ([Bird & Bird, Global Cookie Review, Sweden, undated, refers to 2023 events](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/trending-topics/global-cookie-review/sweden)). PTS's 2023 supervision documents name Tele2 and the Public Health Agency among those reviewed (primary documents confirmed by title only; [PTS notice to Tele2, 2023, title only](https://www.pts.se/globalassets/startpage/dokument/legala-dokument/beslut/2023/internet/underrattelse-beslutad-tele2.pdf); [PTS notice to Folkhälsomyndigheten, 2023, title only](https://www.pts.se/globalassets/startpage/dokument/legala-dokument/beslut/2023/internet/underrattelse-beslutad-folkhalsomyndigheten.pdf)).

**[INFERENCE]** Product analytics in Sweden: any analytics that stores or reads data on the user's device needs prior opt-in consent with an equally prominent reject option on the first layer. Server-side or first-party measurement that reads nothing from the device may fall outside the cookie rule but still falls under the GDPR; this is the researchers' reading and needs counsel. **[GAP]** PTS's and IMY's own cookie guidance pages were not fetched; whether the EU Digital Omnibus changes cookie consent, whether Swedish supervision has been reformed since 2023, and the GDPR lawful basis for product analytics beyond cookies are unknown here.

### Digital Services Act Article 25

**[DURABLE; secondary source only, text read on an unofficial mirror and not on EUR-Lex]** Article 25 of the Digital Services Act says providers of online platforms may not design, organise or operate interfaces in ways that deceive or manipulate users or materially impair free and informed decisions. The ban does not apply to practices already covered by the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive or the GDPR. Examples on which the Commission may give guidance: making some choices more prominent when asking for a decision; repeatedly asking for a choice already made, such as through pop-ups; and making termination harder than subscription ([DSA Article 25, unofficial mirror](https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_25.html)).

**[INFERENCE]** Article 25 binds online platforms, meaning services that store and publicly disseminate user content, so a typical SaaS tool is probably outside it, and consent or sales manipulation is instead caught by the GDPR and the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive. The department treats the three Article 25 examples as a design standard anyway; they also match the expected themes of the Digital Fairness Act. **[GAP]** Article 25 and the small-enterprise exemption were not verified on EUR-Lex, and the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive guidance of December 2021 was not read.

### The withdrawal function: EU rule

**[FAST-MOVING, applies from 19 Jun 2026; secondary source only, law-firm commentary]** Directive (EU) 2023/2673 adds Article 11a to the Consumer Rights Directive; transposition was due by 19 December 2025 and the rules apply from 19 June 2026. The scope is distance contracts concluded through an online interface where a statutory withdrawal right exists, including digital subscriptions and SaaS sold online to consumers, and it applies to any trader targeting EU consumers; contracts with no withdrawal right are outside it ([Taylor Wessing, Feb 2026](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2026/02/withdrawal-button-as-compliance-risk-for-eu-and-non-eu-businesses)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026; secondary source only]** Design requirements for the withdrawal function, per law-firm commentary: a function labelled "withdraw from contract here" or an unambiguous equivalent, prominently displayed, easily accessible and available throughout the withdrawal period. It works in two steps: the consumer gives or confirms name, contract identification and means of communication, then submits through a function labelled "confirm withdrawal" or equivalent, and where several contracts exist the consumer must be able to select which one. The trader must send an acknowledgement on a durable medium such as email without undue delay, including the content of the withdrawal and the date and time received ([Greenberg Traurig, May 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/5/eu-consumer-law-new-withdrawal-button-requirements-for-online-contracts)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026; secondary source only]** Further points on the withdrawal function from law-firm commentary: no forced registration, authentication or app download to reach the function; it must be visibly distinct from a subscription-cancellation button; and consumers must be told before purchase that the function exists and where it is ([Reed Smith, 20 Jan 2026](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102m2ba/one-click-to-withdraw-why-the-new-withdrawal-button-changes-online-contracts/)). One firm reads placement behind a login as tolerated only where the contract can only be concluded through a customer account, and says vague labels such as "Cancel" are likely insufficient and that hidden placement or friction may be treated as dark patterns ([Taylor Wessing, Feb 2026](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2026/02/withdrawal-button-as-compliance-risk-for-eu-and-non-eu-businesses)).

**[CONTESTED; secondary source only]** Law firms disagree on the German sanction cap for the withdrawal function. One reports that the withdrawal period can extend from 14 days to 12 months and 14 days if consumers are not properly informed, with fines up to EUR 50,000 or up to 4% of annual turnover ([Reed Smith, 20 Jan 2026](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102m2ba/one-click-to-withdraw-why-the-new-withdrawal-button-changes-online-contracts/)); another states a maximum of EUR 2 million or 4% ([Greenberg Traurig, May 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/5/eu-consumer-law-new-withdrawal-button-requirements-for-online-contracts)). These are reports about Germany and not about Sweden.

### The withdrawal function: Swedish transposition

**[FAST-MOVING, 15 Jun 2026; secondary source only, one law firm]** According to a law-firm update, Sweden added the withdrawal function to the Distance and Off-Premises Contracts Act (2005:59), implementing Directive (EU) 2023/2673, with application from 19 June 2026. The design requirements it reports: a clearly labelled, easy-to-read function, with "ångra avtalet här" given as example wording; easy to reach on the same site or app where the contract was made; available throughout the withdrawal period; and it may sit behind a login ([CMS, Ny "ångerknapp" krävs på e-handelsplattformar, 15 Jun 2026](https://cms.law/sv/swe/legal-updates/ny-aangerknapp-kraevs-paa-e-handelsplattformar-fraan-den-19-juni-2026)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 15 Jun 2026; secondary source only]** The Swedish flow reported by the same firm: the consumer gives a name, identifies the contract and chooses how to receive confirmation; a second explicit confirmation step prevents accidental withdrawal; and the business sends an acknowledgement on a durable medium without undue delay, stating when the withdrawal was received. Pre-contract information must say that the function exists and where. The scope is consumer distance contracts, including subscriptions, apps and digital platforms, and no new withdrawal right is created. Non-compliance can be treated as unfair marketing, with injunctions under penalty of a fine and a market disruption fee possible ([CMS, Ny "ångerknapp" krävs på e-handelsplattformar, 15 Jun 2026](https://cms.law/sv/swe/legal-updates/ny-aangerknapp-kraevs-paa-e-handelsplattformar-fraan-den-19-juni-2026)). The government bill is Proposition 2025/26:84 and the committee report is CU11 (primary documents confirmed by title only; [Riksdagen, Prop. 2025/26:84, title only](https://data.riksdagen.se/dokument/HD0384); [Riksdagen, Civilutskottets betänkande CU11, title only](https://data.riksdagen.se/dokument/HD01CU11)).

**[CONTESTED; secondary source only]** The law-firm sources differ on login: the Swedish update says the function may sit behind a login ([CMS, Ny "ångerknapp" krävs på e-handelsplattformar, 15 Jun 2026](https://cms.law/sv/swe/legal-updates/ny-aangerknapp-kraevs-paa-e-handelsplattformar-fraan-den-19-juni-2026)), while commentary on the directive says no forced registration or authentication ([Reed Smith, 20 Jan 2026](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102m2ba/one-click-to-withdraw-why-the-new-withdrawal-button-changes-online-contracts/)) or tolerates login only where the contract required an account ([Taylor Wessing, Feb 2026](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2026/02/withdrawal-button-as-compliance-risk-for-eu-and-non-eu-businesses)). **[GAP]** The Swedish statute text, the exact mandated label wording and Konsumentverket's guidance were not read; all Swedish detail is from one law firm. Legal must confirm the label and the login question before build.

### A build checklist for the withdrawal function

**[INFERENCE]** The department's build checklist for the withdrawal function: a persistent, clearly labelled entry point in the footer and on the order or account page, reachable without login where sign-up did not need an account; a first step pre-filled where possible, with a contract selector; a second step with a "confirm withdrawal" button; an automatic timestamped email receipt; updated pre-contract information and terms; and a separate, differently labelled subscription-cancellation flow. For B2B-only SaaS the function is not triggered, because it follows the consumer withdrawal right, but any self-serve plan that consumers or sole traders acting privately can buy brings it into scope. The checklist is the researchers' synthesis from the law-firm sources ([Greenberg Traurig, May 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/5/eu-consumer-law-new-withdrawal-button-requirements-for-online-contracts); [CMS, Ny "ångerknapp" krävs på e-handelsplattformar, 15 Jun 2026](https://cms.law/sv/swe/legal-updates/ny-aangerknapp-kraevs-paa-e-handelsplattformar-fraan-den-19-juni-2026)).

### AI Act Article 50: disclosure and marking

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul-Sep 2026; secondary source only, law-firm summaries of the Act, the Commission's guidelines and the Code of Practice]** Article 50 obligations have applied since 2 August 2026 whether or not a system is high-risk ([Houthoff, 1 Sep 2026](https://www.houthoff.com/insights/news/ai-act-transparency-obligations-and-the-ai-omnibus-regulation/)). Under Article 50(1), systems that interact directly with people must make their artificial nature apparent; hiding this in terms of service, using a generic label such as "assistant", or relying on metadata alone is not enough. AI agents must disclose both that they are AI and whom they act for, at the point of contact and again at key steps: authorisation, reporting, validation and each new interaction ([Faegre Drinker, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations)). An unofficial reproduction of Article 50 adds that disclosure must come by the first interaction unless the AI nature is obvious ([AI Act Article 50, unofficial reproduction at artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)).

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul-Sep 2026; secondary source only]** Under Article 50(2), providers mark outputs in a machine-readable, detectable way; the Code of Practice describes a layered approach, generally signed metadata plus imperceptible watermarking, with lighter rules for free-form text. Systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 have until 2 December 2026 to meet this marking duty ([Faegre Drinker, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations); [Houthoff, 1 Sep 2026](https://www.houthoff.com/insights/news/ai-act-transparency-obligations-and-the-ai-omnibus-regulation/)).

**[FAST-MOVING, Jul 2026; secondary source only]** Under Article 50(4), deepfakes and AI-generated text on matters of public interest need a clear visible label at first exposure; the Commission's standard icon satisfies this, and audio-only formats need a spoken or written disclaimer. An editorial exception applies only with genuine editorial responsibility. Under Article 50(3), deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorisation must inform the people exposed. The Code of Practice is voluntary and not a safe harbour; fines reach EUR 15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover; and the Act applies where outputs are intended for use in the EU ([Faegre Drinker, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations)). **[GAP]** Article 50, the Commission guidelines and the Code of Practice were not read in the original; the icon design and exact label wording are not recorded, and Swedish supervisory arrangements for the AI Act were not researched.

## 19. Designing AI and agent experiences

### Microsoft's 18 guidelines for human-AI interaction

**[DURABLE; published set with pre-2024 origin]** Microsoft's HAX guidelines are organised by four moments: initially, during interaction, when the system is wrong, and over time ([Microsoft HAX Toolkit, Guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/)). The 18 guidelines are: make clear what the system can do; make clear how well it can do it; time services based on context; show contextually relevant information; match relevant social norms; mitigate social biases; support efficient invocation; support efficient dismissal; support efficient correction; scope services when in doubt; make clear why the system did what it did; remember recent interactions; learn from user behaviour; update and adapt cautiously; encourage granular feedback; convey the consequences of user actions; provide global controls; and notify users about changes ([Microsoft HAX Toolkit, Design Library](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/library/)).

### Apple's generative AI guidance: disclosure, expectations and control

**[FAST-MOVING, updated 8 Jun 2026; platform guidance]** Apple's Human Interface Guidelines for generative AI say: tell people when and where AI is used, never let someone believe they are dealing with a human or human-authored content when it is AI, and align with regional regulation. State what the feature can and cannot do, offer curated example prompts for open-ended inputs, and explain known limitations up front. Let people dismiss generated content and revert or retry; place Edit, Undo, Retry or Adjust next to the output; and confirm visibly when a correction took effect, a point added in June 2026 ([Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Generative AI, created 9 Jun 2025, updated 8 Jun 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)).

### Apple's generative AI guidance: approval, uncertainty and recovery

**[FAST-MOVING, updated 8 Jun 2026; platform guidance]** Apple advises against automating destructive or hard-to-undo actions such as deleting data or purchasing, and generally asks for confirmation before a significant action on someone's behalf. On uncertainty: state clearly that generated content may contain errors, avoid asking the model for facts unless it has verified, current information, and do not use generated content where a hallucination could harm. On recovery: when output is blocked or poor, coach the person toward a better request and offer examples, and on failure explain in plain language with a next step ([Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Generative AI, created 9 Jun 2025, updated 8 Jun 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)).

### Apple's generative AI guidance: latency, fallback, privacy, feedback and testing

**[FAST-MOVING, updated 8 Jun 2026; platform guidance]** Apple's further points: design a loading state or generate in the background, with specific progress messages; consider offering several meaningfully different results; keep the product useful when AI is unavailable or declined; ask permission before using personal or usage data, use the minimum, offer opt-out, and say whether data is sent to a server or used for training; make feedback voluntary and non-interrupting, with a quick thumbs up or down plus an optional detailed route; ask rather than infer personal characteristics; and test with diverse people and with vague, out-of-scope, sensitive and adversarial requests ([Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Generative AI, created 9 Jun 2025, updated 8 Jun 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)).

### Google PAIR: calibrated trust and explanation

**[VENDOR; undated chapter, no version shown]** The explainability and trust chapter of Google's People + AI Guidebook aims for calibrated trust, where users neither trust fully nor dismiss. It advises explaining what data is used, whether it is personal or aggregated, and whether it can be reset, and scaling the explanation to the stakes. In high-stakes cases give the reason with the output; skip explanation when behaviour matches expectations; and say when missing data means the user should rely on their own judgment ([Google PAIR, People + AI Guidebook, Explainability + Trust chapter, undated](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)). The guidebook describes itself as a toolkit for teams building human-centred AI products ([Google PAIR Guidebook home page](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook/)).

### Google PAIR: showing confidence

**[VENDOR; undated chapter]** Google PAIR's options for displaying model confidence are categories such as high, medium and low with an action per bucket; several best alternatives; a numeric percentage, which is risky for novices; or visual ranges for experts. The first decision is whether showing confidence helps decisions at all, and the display should be tested with varied users ([Google PAIR, People + AI Guidebook, Explainability + Trust chapter, undated](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)). **[GAP]** Only this one chapter of the guidebook was readable; its chapters on mental models, feedback and control, and errors are not recorded.

### NN/g on agents in 2026

**[FAST-MOVING, 16 Jan 2026; practitioner opinion]** NN/g's 2026 overview says more interactions are mediated by assistants, agents and AI summaries, so people delegate tasks instead of navigating interfaces; that agents are often released before they are ready, which erodes confidence; that users are tiring of low-value AI features; and that trust requires transparency, control, consistency and support on failure ([NN/g, State of UX 2026, 16 Jan 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/state-of-ux-2026/)). NN/g describes an agent as a system that pursues a goal through steps, checks progress and chooses next steps itself ([NN/g, Designing AI Agents, 8 May 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/designing-ai-agents/)).

### NN/g's agent usability study

**[FAST-MOVING, 8 May 2026; small qualitative study]** NN/g observed six participants in China using one consumer agent to place orders. Five of six defaulted to familiar apps over the agent. The problems observed: a prefilled prompt sent the wrong request; a one-at-a-time carousel hid how many options existed; granting access exposed a full home address at once; a listed price of ¥1.6 became ¥10.2 at checkout; and missing details such as baggage allowance sent a user elsewhere ([NN/g, Designing AI Agents, 8 May 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/designing-ai-agents/)). The study is small and covers a consumer ordering agent, so it shows kinds of problems and not rates, and transfer to B2B SaaS agents is by analogy.

**[FAST-MOVING, 8 May 2026; practitioner guidance]** NN/g's recommendations for agent design: offer both free-text and menu entry; ask a clarifying question instead of auto-sending a guess; reuse familiar interface patterns and show result counts; state what data is accessed, require acknowledgement and reveal only what the step needs; show full costs and decision-critical details before commitment; and let users adjust, review and authorise payment ([NN/g, Designing AI Agents, 8 May 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/designing-ai-agents/)).

### A pattern set for agent experiences

**[INFERENCE]** The department's pattern set for agent experiences, meant to satisfy the Microsoft, Apple and Article 50 points together, has ten parts: a statement of capability and limits at entry; a plan preview before multi-step work; an approval gate before any irreversible, external or costly action, showing exactly what will happen; a visible activity log with specific status text; undo or rollback for every reversible action; sources or evidence next to factual claims, with an explicit "may be wrong" where no source exists; a correction path that confirms the change took effect; stop and global off controls; a non-AI fallback route; and change notices when behaviour updates. The set is the researchers' synthesis ([Microsoft HAX Toolkit, Design Library](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/library/); [Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Generative AI, created 9 Jun 2025, updated 8 Jun 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai); [Faegre Drinker, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations)). Showing sources is the researchers' extension: it is not stated as a guideline on the fetched Apple or Microsoft pages, where the closest points are explaining why the system did what it did and avoiding unverified factual output.

### A checklist for an agent that acts on a user's behalf

**[INFERENCE]** Combining the NN/g and Google PAIR points gives a checklist for an agent that acts for a user: clarify before acting; show what data is used; show full consequences before commitment; give confidence only where it changes the decision; and keep an approve-or-edit step for payments and irreversible actions ([NN/g, Designing AI Agents, 8 May 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/designing-ai-agents/); [Google PAIR, People + AI Guidebook, Explainability + Trust chapter, undated](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)). Approval frequency is a trade-off: Apple says to confirm significant actions "generally" ([Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Generative AI, created 9 Jun 2025, updated 8 Jun 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)), and the Article 50 summary lists disclosure at authorisation steps ([Faegre Drinker, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations)), so tiering actions by reversibility and cost is the workable design.

## 20. Localisation and content design

### Internationalisation basics

**[DURABLE; W3C guidance]** W3C's internationalisation quick tips are: use UTF-8 for content, databases and forms, and declare it; declare the language of each page and mark language changes inside it, which supports accessibility, styling and search; avoid text baked into images and check images for cultural bias; label numeric date fields, because day, month and year order varies; do not validate phone numbers on assumed lengths; plan for text to change length in translation; do not build sentences by concatenating strings, and use format strings with numbered placeholders because word order differs between languages; and do not assume that names and addresses follow local rules or over-split address fields ([W3C Internationalization Quick Tips](https://www.w3.org/International/quicktips/)).

**[INFERENCE]** Setting `lang="sv"` or `lang="en"` per page and on inline language switches fixes a WCAG Level A requirement and an internationalisation requirement in one step; missing document language was found on 13.5% of home pages in the February 2026 WebAIM scan ([WebAIM Million 2026, scan Feb 2026, report updated 30 Mar 2026](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)).

### Sweden: date, time and week conventions

**[DURABLE; reference summary citing the Swedish language authority's writing rules]** In Sweden numeric dates are written year-month-day, for example 2001-08-31, and long dates use a lowercase month, as in "31 augusti 2001". Weeks start on Monday and week numbers are in everyday use ("vecka 32"); week 1 contains the year's first Thursday. Time uses the 24-hour clock; the traditional separator is a full stop (23.59), and colons also appear because of digital displays ([Wikipedia, Date and time notation in Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_and_time_notation_in_Sweden)). **[INFERENCE]** Swedish interfaces default to year-month-day dates, 24-hour time and a Monday week start, and support week numbers in date pickers. The colon as the usual software separator is the researchers' inference; CLDR locale data was not checked.

### Sweden: numbers in running text

**[DURABLE; Swedish healthcare language guidelines, updated 10 May 2022]** Swedish healthcare writing guidelines say to use digits for decimals or when the figure matters, otherwise to write numbers as words in running text, and to keep one style within a sentence ([1177, Skriv tal med bokstäver, updated 10 May 2022](https://www.1177.se/Sormland/riktlinjer-och-material/sprakliga-riktlinjer/stavning-skrivregler-stil/skriv-tal-med-bokstaver/)).

### Sweden: formats that remain unverified

**[GAP]** The Swedish decimal comma, the space as thousands separator, currency placement after the amount, the sorting of å, ä and ö at the end of the alphabet, and phone and personal identity number formats were not confirmed on any fetched page; they are unverified background. The official source is the language authority's writing rules, confirmed by title only ([Regeringen, Myndigheternas skrivregler, title only, not read](https://regeringen.se/contentassets/5b473488ef174cff9d6dc405f9da6f1b/myndigheternas-skrivregler)). Agents use locale libraries with CLDR data rather than hand-coding these formats, and confirm against CLDR before treating them as fact. Swedish plain-language requirements (the Language Act and "klarspråk") and Swedish-language UX writing norms were not researched.

### Language continuity across legal and product content

**[DURABLE; checked on primary source for the EDPB point, secondary source only for the label point]** The EDPB lists "language discontinuity", meaning privacy information that is not in the language of the rest of the service, as a deceptive pattern ([EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 v2.0, adopted 14 Feb 2023 (PDF)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-02/edpb_03-2022_guidelines_on_deceptive_design_patterns_in_social_media_platform_interfaces_v2_en_0.pdf)). Law-firm commentary says the withdrawal-function label must be an equally clear phrase in the local language ([Taylor Wessing, Feb 2026](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2026/02/withdrawal-button-as-compliance-risk-for-eu-and-non-eu-businesses)). **[INFERENCE]** If a Swedish interface is offered, the privacy notice, consent interface, withdrawal function and accessibility information should all exist in Swedish too.

### Cross-market measurement

**[DURABLE]** Net Promoter Score varies widely by country, so cross-market score comparisons mislead ([NN/g, Net Promoter Score, 21 Jun 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/nps-ux/)). For AI features, Apple advises asking people for personal or cultural details rather than inferring them, and testing across diverse people to catch stereotypes ([Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Generative AI, created 9 Jun 2025, updated 8 Jun 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)).

### Content design rules for interface text

**[DURABLE]** The sourced rules for interface text converge on plain, specific language in the user's vocabulary. Use words and concepts familiar to users rather than internal jargon; write error messages in plain language that state the problem precisely and suggest a solution; and make help content task-focused, searchable and in context, with concrete steps ([NN/g, 10 Usability Heuristics, 24 Apr 1994, last reviewed 30 Jan 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)). In forms, the label or legend doubles as the page heading; hints are one sentence without full stops or links; use "(optional)" rather than asterisks and "Continue" rather than "Next"; do not reuse the same heading on several pages; and explain why a question is asked ([GOV.UK Design System, Question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/)).

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026; platform guidance]** Apple's microcopy guidance for AI features: use specific status messages such as "Summarizing key themes from your notes" instead of "Processing…", and when a request is refused or blocked, say what could not be done and offer example requests that would work ([Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Generative AI, created 9 Jun 2025, updated 8 Jun 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)).

### Legally mandated wording

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026; secondary source only]** Unambiguous action labels are now mandated in places: law-firm commentary gives "withdraw from contract here" and "confirm withdrawal" for the withdrawal function ([Greenberg Traurig, May 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/5/eu-consumer-law-new-withdrawal-button-requirements-for-online-contracts)), and says a generic label such as "assistant" does not make the artificial nature of an AI system apparent ([Faegre Drinker, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations)). **[DURABLE; checked on primary source]** For privacy content the EDPB calls for consistent terms across product and policy, plain-language definitions of jargon, examples of processing and short contextual notices, and names ambiguous wording and conflicting information as deceptive patterns ([EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 v2.0, adopted 14 Feb 2023 (PDF)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-02/edpb_03-2022_guidelines_on_deceptive_design_patterns_in_social_media_platform_interfaces_v2_en_0.pdf)).

### Content failures are accessibility failures

**[FAST-MOVING, Feb 2026; automated scan]** Several of the most common accessibility failures are content-authoring failures: missing alternative text on 53.1% of home pages, empty links on 46.3% and empty buttons on 30.6% in the WebAIM Million 2026 ([WebAIM Million 2026, scan Feb 2026, report updated 30 Mar 2026](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)).

### A content checklist for agents

**[INFERENCE]** The department's content checklist: every button and link has a text name that describes the action; every image has alt text or is marked decorative; every error says what happened and what to do; every waiting state says what is being done; one term per concept across the interface, help and legal pages; and legally required labels use the mandated plain wording in each language. Avoid emotional or guilt-based wording on decline options; where personal data choices are involved this falls under the EDPB's "emotional steering" family ([EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 v2.0, adopted 14 Feb 2023 (PDF)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-02/edpb_03-2022_guidelines_on_deceptive_design_patterns_in_social_media_platform_interfaces_v2_en_0.pdf)), and whether it also breaches unfair-practice rules elsewhere was not verified. The checklist is the researchers' synthesis.

### What was not sourced on content design

**[GAP]** GOV.UK's writing guidance has moved to a new guidance site with subpages on clear language, titles and summaries; the landing page carries no numeric rules ([GOV.UK publishing guidance, Writing guidelines](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/)), so reading-age, sentence-length and title-length numbers must not be quoted from this document. ### What an agent may do alone

**[INFERENCE]** A Product agent may act alone on work that is internal, reversible and reviewable: maintaining discovery artefacts from real customer material, scoring and ranking, drafting specifications and eval tasks, running design checks, tagging feedback, building throwaway prototypes, and using synthetic users for the preparation tasks NN/g accepts ([NN/g, Synthetic Users, 21 Jun 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/synthetic-users/)). Every such output carries the labels of this document: what is sourced, what is inference and what is a gap.

### What needs human approval

**[INFERENCE]** Human approval is required wherever an action is external, costly, hard to reverse or legally exposed: pricing and packaging, strategy and commitments, customer releases, feature removal, legal and compliance wording, contact with customers and participants, first use of personal data in a workflow, reliance on an accessibility exemption, and any promotional mechanic drawn from the house style that has not been cleared for the market. Apple's guidance to confirm before significant actions on someone's behalf ([Apple Human Interface Guidelines, Generative AI, created 9 Jun 2025, updated 8 Jun 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)) and Anthropic's call for human checkpoints and stopping conditions for agents ([Anthropic, Building effective agents, Dec 2024, modified Oct 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)) are the nearest sourced support.

### What an agent must never do

**[INFERENCE]** A Product agent must never misrepresent evidence (synthetic output as real findings, his unaudited numbers as proof, memory as a sourced fact), never ship undisclosed AI, never build deceptive patterns, never set non-necessary cookies before consent, never remove a legally required function, and never treat an automated scan as proof of accessibility. Each prohibition traces to a source cited in the table above, except the rule against filling gaps from memory, which is this document's own convention.

### Evidence thresholds agents apply before recommending

**[INFERENCE]** Before a worker agent recommends a decision to the manager agent it states the evidence level in the terms of this document: the number of real interviews behind an opportunity; whether a survey figure rests on roughly 40 or more responses ([First Round Review, How Superhuman Built an Engine to Find Product Market Fit, 12 Nov 2018](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)); whether a usability finding comes from problem discovery with about 5 users or measurement with 20 or more ([NN/g, How Many Test Users in a Usability Study?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-many-test-users/)); whether an A/B result met the planned sample size without peeking ([Larsen, Stallrich, Sengupta, Deng, Kohavi and Stevens, Statistical Challenges in Online Controlled Experiments, 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11366v1)); and, for AI features, the pass^k result on the eval suite ([Anthropic, Demystifying evals for AI agents, 9 Jan 2026](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)). A recommendation that lacks its evidence line is returned by the manager agent.

### Agent security

**[GAP]** Security risks specific to language-model agents (prompt injection, tool misuse, data exfiltration) and platform terms that constrain agents were not researched for this department. The IT infrastructure and Data and AI departments own those controls.

## Conclusion

**[INFERENCE]** The research changes the picture of what a Product knowledge base is for. The methods are stable and well documented: the operating model, the opportunity tree, the scoring formulas, the heuristics, the usability scales and the accessibility criteria have barely moved in years. What has moved is the cost structure around them. Delivery and prototyping are now cheap, so the scarce inputs are real customer stories, honest evidence levels and evaluation design, which are exactly the things an agent department is most tempted to simulate. The house style fits this moment better than its service-business origins suggest, because its strongest ideas are subtractive: fewer markets, fewer offers, fewer features, a faster first win, and a refusal to add before retention is fixed. Its weakest parts for software are its numbers and its promotional mechanics, and the precedence rule exists to keep those out of customer-facing decisions.

**[INFERENCE]** Two things should be done before this document is relied on in production. First, Legal should convert the secondary-source paragraphs on the withdrawal function, cookie consent, Article 50 and the Digital Fairness Act into rulings, starting with the question of whether consumers can buy the product at all, since that one answer switches several regimes on or off. Second, the department should replace the dated public benchmarks with its own cohort baselines within the first quarter of operation, because the best external retention, activation and adoption figures available are between three and seven years old and no agent should steer by them.
