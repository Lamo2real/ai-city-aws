# Automation runs campaigns; inputs decide outcomes

As of November 2026, every major ad platform defaults to automated campaign types, so the knowledge a marketing agent team needs is less about buttons and more about inputs, proof and permissions. Meta (Andromeda, GEM, Advantage+), Google (AI Max, Performance Max, Demand Gen, which is now absorbing standalone Display), TikTok (Smart+, GMV Max), Pinterest (Performance+), Snapchat and Reddit (Max Campaigns) all reward the same four things: conversion-signal quality through server-side APIs and consent, distinct creative concepts in volume, enough budget per learning unit (roughly 50 optimisation results per ad set per week on Meta, confirmed in Meta's own help centre), and incrementality testing in place of platform-reported ROAS, which is now within reach of small accounts because Google's Conversion Lift is self-service at US$5,000 and 1,000 conversions and Meta's at US$5,000 and 500 conversions. Strategy evidence points the same way: growth comes from penetration among all category buyers, including the roughly 95% not in market today, and the classic frameworks an agent needs (positioning, ideal customer profile, jobs-to-be-done, category entry points, briefs, unit-economics formulas) are now sourced in this document. Organic search is being reshaped by AI Overviews, which roughly halve clicks on affected queries, yet Google says optimising for AI answers "is still SEO", and the best-evidenced GEO levers remain ranking well in retrieval and being authentically mentioned off-site. Lifecycle practice is governed by hard mailbox-provider rules (SPF, DKIM, DMARC, one-click unsubscribe, complaint rate under 0.3% and ideally under 0.1%), and automated flows carry 30 to 41% of email revenue from 2 to 5% of sends. Compliance moved on several fronts: EU AI Act Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026 with fines up to EUR 15 million or 3% of turnover, the EU green-claims bans have applied since 27 September 2026, political and issue ads are closed on Meta and Google in the EU, Sweden requires prior consent for email and SMS marketing to individuals and treats "samarbete" labels as insufficient, and Meta's proposed terms making advertisers "solely responsible" for its AI edits were paused on 5 November 2026 and never took effect. For agents specifically, Google's official Ads MCP server is read-only while Meta's and TikTok's allow full campaign writes with no documented approval step, so spend limits and human approval must be enforced outside the model. Most benchmark numbers below come from vendors or platforms and are labelled as such; where the research found no fetched source, this document states a gap instead of filling it from memory.

## How to read and split this document

Every claim carries its source date and URL so that an entry split out of this document still stands alone. Labels used throughout: **[DURABLE]** marks a principle that has held across several years of platform change; **[FAST-MOVING, date]** marks a platform detail, feature status or number that will age, with the source date; **[VENDOR]** marks a figure from a tool vendor, agency or publisher with a commercial interest; **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** marks a self-reported platform figure that has not been independently verified; **[CONTESTED]** marks a point where sources disagree; **[INFERENCE]** marks a synthesis by the researchers that no single source states; **[GAP]** marks something the research did not verify. This is the second research pass. It merges the first report with gap-fill research completed on 11 November 2026, and where the second pass corrected the first, only the corrected claim appears.

Five caveats apply to the whole document. First, source reliability varies by platform: Meta's Business Help Center pages, TikTok's help pages, Google Ads and Analytics Help pages, X's business pages and Swedish statutes were read on the platform's or legislature's own pages in the second pass, but LinkedIn's help pages could only be read as search-index excerpts, so LinkedIn figures are official wording with incomplete context. Second, many pages in both passes were read through an automated summariser or page-question tool, so exact wording of quotes and legal text is a close extraction, not always verbatim. Third, legal items are orientation, not legal advice. Fourth, benchmarks are overwhelmingly US-dollar and US-weighted. For Sweden and the Nordics the research found **one credible country-level benchmark (email) and one European ecommerce Google Ads dataset; no credible Swedish or Nordic benchmark exists for paid search or paid social cost, click or conversion rates**, so agents working on Swedish accounts must build baselines from the account's own data. Fifth, vendor datasets disagree with each other because their customer bases differ (email open rates of 31% at Klaviyo against 43% at MailerLite), so compare an account against one consistent source over time.

## 1. Strategy and planning: diagnose, position, fund reach and memory, then activate

### Put diagnosis before strategy and strategy before tactics

**[DURABLE]** Mark Ritson's planning rule is that diagnosis comes first (update the segmentation, run proper tracking, interview loyal customers), strategy second (targeting, positioning, objectives) and tactics third; the four Ps are tactical levers to leave alone until the strategy is complete ([Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/mark-ritson-brand-strategy-marketing/)). His marketing-plan structure is a three-part "triptych" of diagnosis with data, then strategy, then tactics with costs, with tactics listed per target segment beneath that segment's objective and summarised on one Gantt chart ([Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/mark-ritson-marketing-planning-14-steps/)). He also notes that communications and tactics are "only a third of what we do" and that marketers over-spend on short-term activation partly because companies plan in 12-month cycles ([Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/ritson-ten-traits-successful-marketer/)). **[INFERENCE]** For a manager agent this gives a gating order: no worker agent builds a campaign until a diagnosis artefact (market, segments, current performance) and a strategy artefact (target, positioning, objectives) exist.

### Build positioning bottom-up, then compress it into one sentence

**[DURABLE; secondary summary of April Dunford's *Obviously Awesome*, 2019]** Dunford frames positioning as context setting: it lets customers quickly grasp what the product is, why it is special and why it matters to them. Her components are competitive alternatives (what customers would do if the product did not exist), unique attributes, value and proof, target market characteristics (who cares most about that value), market category, and optionally relevant trends. Her ten steps in order are: understand the customers who love the product; form a positioning team; align vocabulary and drop positioning baggage; list true competitive alternatives; isolate unique attributes; map attributes to value themes; determine who cares a lot; find a market frame of reference that puts strengths at the centre; layer on a trend carefully; and capture the positioning so it can be shared. She describes three market-category styles: head to head (win an existing category), big fish in a small pond (dominate a sub-segment the leader under-serves) and create a new game (build a new category) ([Nat Eliason notes](https://nateliason.com/notes/obviously-awesome-april-dunford)). **[DURABLE; secondary reproductions]** Geoffrey Moore's template from *Crossing the Chasm* (1991) compresses the result: "For (target customer) who (statement of the need or opportunity), the (product name) is a (product category) that (statement of key benefit). Unlike (primary competitive alternative), our product (statement of primary differentiation)" ([MistyWest worksheet](https://www.mistywest.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/MistyWest-Building-a-Positioning-Statement.pdf); [Tailor Brands](https://www.tailorbrands.com/blog/positioning-statement)). **[INFERENCE]** A workable agent procedure is to run Dunford's steps four to eight to generate the raw positioning with evidence for each slot, then render it in Moore's sentence for briefs; Moore's template forces a single alternative and a single benefit. **[GAP]** Neither book was read directly, and no sourced step-by-step for segmentation methods (cluster analysis, needs-based segmentation) or segment attractiveness scoring was collected.

### Segment for relevance, but define the reachable target broadly

**[DURABLE, CONTESTED in emphasis]** Christensen, Cook and Hall argued in Harvard Business Review (2005) that demographic and product segmentation yields "one-size-fits-none" products and that the job, not the customer, should be the unit of analysis; demographic targeting works mainly where the job coincides with the demographic group ([HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/what-customers-want-from-your-products)). The Ehrenberg-Bass counterpoint is that growth requires reaching all category buyers and that a brand with "excess loyalty" usually has a penetration deficit caused by restricted targeting or distribution ([Ehrenberg-Bass](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/the-double-jeopardy-law-in-b2b-shows-the-way-to-grow)). **[INFERENCE]** A common reconciliation is to segment for message and offer relevance while defining the reachable media target as broadly as the category allows; this is the researchers' synthesis, not a sourced rule.

### Reach all category buyers, because about 95% are not in market

**[DURABLE]** The 95-5 rule from the LinkedIn B2B Institute and Professor John Dawes of Ehrenberg-Bass holds that only about 5% of B2B buyers are in market at any time; if 80% of companies switch bank about once every five years, about 20% are in market in a year, 2% in a month and 1% in two weeks. Marketers cannot push buyers into market, so advertising works mainly by building memory for a future purchase. The practical guidance is to target the whole category (current and future buyers) instead of hyper-targeting in-market signals, to aim creative at memorability over hard persuasion, to spread media continuously, and to set realistic short-term sales targets ([Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/peter-weinberg-jon-lombardo-95-5-rule)). **[INFERENCE]** An agent can apply this arithmetically: estimate purchase-cycle length, derive the share of the category in market per month, fund always-on broad reach for future buyers, and aim activation at the in-market slice.

### Growth comes from penetration, mental availability and physical availability

**[DURABLE]** The Double Jeopardy law says smaller-share brands have fewer buyers who are also slightly less loyal; loyalty is largely a predictable function of market share, so sustained growth through loyalty alone is not possible. The Ehrenberg-Bass Institute shows the law in UK business banking, US business insurance, concrete and aircraft markets and summarises it as "You have more loyalty because you are big, rather than you are big because you have more loyalty." Loyalty metrics have natural ceilings, so loyalty KPIs should be set against the Double Jeopardy norm for a brand of that size. To test the law in a category, survey a cross-section of category buyers (not only customers), ask which brands they use from a prompted list plus a loyalty measure (number of products held, share solely loyal, share naming the brand as main supplier), and order brands by penetration. Mental availability is being thought of by more customers in more buying situations, a function of media, branding and message quality; physical availability is being easy to find and buy, through presence and prominence in channels, sales coverage and portfolio; each amplifies the other ([Ehrenberg-Bass](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/the-double-jeopardy-law-in-b2b-shows-the-way-to-grow)). **[DURABLE]** Byron Sharp adds that most of a brand's buyers are very light buyers; growth nudges everyone's propensity up and shows mostly as non-buyers becoming once-buyers. Hitting even modest share targets typically needs quarterly penetration to double or triple while average purchase rate rises by a fraction of one occasion, and changes in sales and profit are largely due to acquisition performance, not retention ([Byron Sharp, 2016](https://byronsharp.wordpress.com/2016/09/18/answering-critics/)). **[GAP]** *How Brands Grow* was not read directly and no formal measurement method for physical availability was sourced.

### Use category entry points as the message plan

**[DURABLE; Jenni Romaniuk, July 2022]** Category entry points (CEPs) are the cues buyers use to access memory in a buying situation, both internal (motives, emotions) and external (location, time). Identify them with the seven W's: when, where, while, with what, why, how feeling, and with or for whom; in B2B, do this from both the business's and the professional's perspective. Prioritise with the three C's: credibility (the brand is not at a mental disadvantage on the CEP and the portfolio can deliver), competitiveness (fewer rivals hold a mental advantage) and commonality or currency (how often the situation occurs or its money value). The output is a shortlist of five to eight CEPs, and each execution should carry one CEP ([Ehrenberg-Bass](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/category-entry-points-in-a-business-to-business-b2b-world); [LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/marketing-solutions/b2b-institute/cep-in-b2b)). **[DURABLE]** To measure mental availability, show each CEP with a brand list and a "none" option and let respondents pick any brands. The expected score for a brand on a CEP is (column total × row total) ÷ grand total; a deviation above +5 percentage points is a mental advantage and below −5 a disadvantage. Three metrics follow: Mental Market Share is the brand's CEP links divided by all brands' links (the institute's Barclays example is 3,732 ÷ 37,948 = 9.8%); Mental Penetration is buyers with at least one link divided by the sample (472 ÷ 609 = 78%); Network Size is the brand's links divided by buyers with any link (about 7.9) ([Ehrenberg-Bass](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/category-entry-points-in-a-business-to-business-b2b-world)). The institute's worked example contains a small internal inconsistency (3,732 versus 3,742 links) that does not affect the formulas. A fourth metric, Share of Mind, appears in vendor implementations and not in the institute's page ([quantilope](https://www.quantilope.com/methods/mental-availability)). **[INFERENCE]** A CEP shortlist is a ready-made message plan: five to eight situations, one per execution, rotated over time, each carrying the same distinctive assets.

### Protect distinctive brand assets

**[DURABLE; Romaniuk, c. 2013]** Distinctive brand assets are non-name elements (colour, logo, character, jingle, font) that trigger the brand. They are judged on uniqueness (the asset evokes only this brand) and fame (everyone links it to the brand), and uniqueness matters more. The Distinctive Asset Grid has four quadrants: use or lose (famous and unique), invest (unique but not famous), avoid (famous but not unique) and test or ignore (neither). Consistency builds assets; Tropicana's 2009 pack change is cited at an estimated $26.3 million in lost sales ([Ehrenberg-Bass](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/brands-of-distinction)). In practice, show the asset unbranded to category buyers and ask which brand it belongs to; fame is the share linking it to your brand and uniqueness is its exclusivity against competitors ([Umbrex, secondary](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/distinctive-brand-assets-framework-ehrenberg-bass/)). **[GAP]** A 2026 International Journal of Advertising paper benchmarking asset types surfaced but could not be read ([tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02650487.2026.2637295)).

### Describe accounts with an ICP and people with personas mapped to buying jobs

**[DURABLE; Gartner, 10 July 2019]** An ideal customer profile (ICP) is the set of firmographic, environmental and behavioural attributes of accounts expected to become a company's most valuable customers, developed through qualitative and quantitative analysis and optionally predictive analytics. It is narrower than the total addressable market and narrower than "target customer": it covers only the most valuable customers and prospects that are also most likely to buy. Gartner reports that in some firms the ICP is poorly defined or used only in briefs and onboarding instead of in strategy and execution ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/the-framework-for-ideal-customer-profile-development)). **[VENDOR]** Salesforce distinguishes the ICP (the type of company that is an ideal fit) from the buyer persona (a semi-fictional representation of an ideal individual customer based on research and data) and lists environmental attributes such as industry trends, geography, economic conditions and regulation ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/sales/ideal-customer-profile/)); N.Rich names "ICP based on gut feeling" as the most common mistake and derives the profile from analysis of a defined set of best accounts on firmographic, environmental and technographic parameters ([N.Rich](https://nrich.io/blog/how-to-create-an-ideal-customer-profile-that-drives-pipeline)). **[INFERENCE]** Minimum ICP fields implied by these sources are firmographics (industry, size, geography), environmental attributes (regulation, economic conditions, technology stack), behavioural attributes (buying triggers, usage, engagement) and a value criterion (retention, LTV, win rate) used to pick the seed accounts.

**[DURABLE in direction; figures dated]** Complex B2B purchases involve many people. Gartner's long-standing figure is 6 to 10 decision makers, each holding 4 or 5 independently gathered pieces of information; buyers spend 17% of purchase time meeting potential suppliers (5 to 6% with any one supplier when comparing several), and 77% called their latest purchase very complex or difficult ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/b2b-buying-journey)). The 17% figure traces to Gartner's 2017 Digital B2B Buyer Survey (n = 750): 27% of time researching independently online, 18% researching offline, 22% meeting with the buying group, 17% meeting suppliers and 16% other ([Gartner e-book via SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/gartner-buyer-enablement-winmoreb2bsalesdealspdf/258207352)). Gartner's six "buying jobs" are problem identification, solution exploration, requirements building, supplier selection, validation and consensus creation; buyers who found supplier information helpful were 2.8 times more likely to report high purchase ease and 3 times more likely to buy a bigger deal with less regret ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/b2b-buying-journey)). **[FAST-MOVING, Jan 2026]** Forrester's State of Business Buying 2026 (nearly 18,000 global business buyers surveyed in 2025) puts the average at **13 internal stakeholders and 9 external participants** per decision; 94% of buyers use AI during the buying process, procurement is a decision-maker 53% of the time, and over 60% have bought some form of trial. The same post is summarised as saying purchases with generative-AI features involve buying groups twice as large (14 versus 7 members), which sits oddly beside the 13-person average and should be checked against the report before quoting ([Forrester, 21 Jan 2026](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/state-of-business-buying-2026)). **[INFERENCE]** B2B persona work should map to buying-group roles and the six buying jobs, not to a single "decision maker". **[GAP]** No authoritative source was read for a standard list of persona fields or researched persona mistakes; Forrester and Gartner persona templates are paywalled.

### Use jobs-to-be-done to find the message, and the four forces to structure it

**[DURABLE]** Customers "hire" products to do a job, and nearly every job has functional, social and emotional dimensions. In the milkshake case, attribute research on a demographic segment did not lift sales; observation showed morning commuters hiring the shake to make a dull drive interesting and stave off hunger one-handed, competing with bagels, bananas and doughnuts, while parents hired it for a different job. Job-defined markets are usually larger than product-category markets ([HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/what-customers-want-from-your-products)). **[DURABLE; Ulwick/Strategyn]** Outcome-Driven Innovation writes each need as a desired outcome statement (direction of improvement + unit of measure + object of control + contextual clarifier) and scores it with two survey questions, importance and satisfaction. The opportunity algorithm, first published in Harvard Business Review in January 2002, is Opportunity = Importance + max(Importance − Satisfaction, 0), with Strategyn's bands at over 10 for a solid opportunity, over 12 high and over 15 extreme ([Strategyn white paper](https://innovationroundtable.com/summit/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/Strategyn_what_is_Outcome_Driven_Innovation.pdf); [Strategyn template](https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done-template/)). **[DURABLE; Moesta]** The four forces of progress are the push of the situation and the pull of the new solution, which drive a switch, and the anxiety of the new and the habit of the present, which restrain it; a switch happens only when push plus pull exceed anxiety plus habit. Pull is the customer's imagined better life, not a feature list; anxiety is reduced with guarantees, social proof, onboarding and risk removal, not features; and teams over-invest in pull while neglecting anxiety and habit ([jobstobedone.org](https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/)). The switch-interview method is to interview recent buyers, reconstruct the purchase timeline, ask what changed, and push vague words down to specific examples ([Business of Software](https://businessofsoftware.org/talks/understanding-your-customer-jtbd/)). **[Practitioner claim, not a study]** Moesta reports that about 10 deep interviews of 30 to 60 minutes with recent buyers typically reveal 3 to 5 buying patterns, with little new after 6 or 7, and cites a mattress ad built on the struggling moment that lifted sales 37%; both are podcast claims ([SaaS Club podcast](https://saasclub.io/podcast/jobs-to-be-done-bob-moesta-423/)). **[INFERENCE]** A messaging map from the four forces: the hook states the push (the struggling moment); the promise states the pull (the progress); proof, guarantee and trial address anxiety; migration help and "works with what you already use" address habit. Ulwick's opportunity scores give a defensible order for which benefits to lead with. **[GAP]** No controlled evidence was found that jobs-based messaging outperforms persona-based messaging.

### Value proposition and messaging hierarchy are organising devices, not proven formulas

**[DURABLE; vendor-owned frameworks]** Strategyzer's Value Proposition Canvas pairs a Customer Profile (jobs, pains, gains) with a Value Map (products and services, pain relievers, gain creators). Pains are negative emotions, undesired costs, situations and risks before, during or after the job; gains are benefits expected, desired or surprising; each is ranked by relevance and frequency ([Strategyzer](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/achieve-product-market-fit-with-our-brand-new-value-proposition-designer-canvas); [Strategyzer](https://www.strategyzer.com/value-proposition)). Strategyzer's listed mistakes are mixing several segments in one profile (for example payers and users), listing only the jobs, pains and gains the product already solves, confusing pains with pain relievers, and trying to address every pain and gain instead of the highest-priority ones; social and emotional jobs often carry the most deeply felt pains ([Strategyzer](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/5-common-mistakes-to-avoid-when-using-the-value-proposition-canvas)). Its book summary says strong fit typically means addressing 50 to 70% of the customer's most significant pains and gains, a heuristic with no data shown ([Strategyzer](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/value-proposition-design-book-summary)). **[VENDOR]** StoryBrand's SB7 (Donald Miller, 2017) orders a message as: a character (the customer) has a problem, meets a guide (the brand) who gives them a plan and calls them to action, which ends in success and helps them avoid failure. The brand is the guide and never the hero, showing empathy plus authority, and a simple plan of about three steps reduces perceived purchase risk ([StoryBrand workbook](https://storybrand.com/downloads/StoryBrand-Online-Marketing-Course-Workbook.pdf)). The problem is commonly taught in three layers (external, internal, philosophical) and calls to action in two kinds (direct and transitional) ([Gravity Global, secondary](https://www.gravityglobal.com/blog/complete-guide-storybrand-framework)). A message house is a claim-and-proof hierarchy with a roof (umbrella message), pillars (key messages) and evidence (proof points) ([Umbrex, secondary](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/storybrand-sb7-framework/)). **[INFERENCE]** A usable hierarchy for an agent is positioning, then value proposition (canvas fit), then a message house with one umbrella claim, three pillars and proof under each, then channel copy in a narrative order such as SB7. **[GAP]** No primary or experimental source was found for the effectiveness of AIDA, PAS, BAB or similar copy formulas, no originator was found for the message house, and StoryBrand has no independent effectiveness study; treat all of these as organising devices and test the copy.

### Treat 60/40 as a starting prior, and about half-and-half in B2B

**[DURABLE, CONTESTED]** Les Binet's suggested split is about 60% brand building and 40% activation, on the argument that sales effects account for only 40% of advertising payback while 60% comes from long-term brand effects; the common mistake is trying to do both jobs in one execution, and a strong brand makes people more responsive to performance marketing ([Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/les-binet-brand-performance/)). The critique, from Neil Krikul of the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute on 13 August 2026, is that the split came from IPA Databank cases submitted for awards and so may not be representative, and that Binet and Field themselves said it varies by category. Krikul recommends combining four budgeting methods instead of a "magic split": objective-and-task (zero-based) budgeting; performance spend taken to the point of diminishing returns with CPA capped at break-even; brand spend planned on reach, frequency and continuity adjusted for purchase cycle; and share-of-voice benchmarking against competitors. Smaller brands can lean more to short-term activation to grow fast ([B&T, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.bandt.com.au/the-magic-split-between-brand-performance-spending-needs-to-end/)).

**[DURABLE in direction; small sample]** Binet and Field's B2B study for the LinkedIn B2B Institute (2019, *The 5 Principles of Growth in B2B Marketing*) concluded that B2B best practice is "mainly the same as in B2C" ([The Drum, 2019](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/2019/05/23/exclusive-look-binet-and-field-s-new-b2b-marketing-research)). Its five principles are: invest in share of voice; balance brand and activation; expand the customer base; maximise mental availability; and harness the power of emotion ([IPA](https://ipa.co.uk/effworks/marketing-marketing-v2/five-principles-of-growth-in-b2b-marketing/)). The B2B optimum in the data is **46% brand and 54% activation**, against roughly 60/40 for B2C, described as a guiding principle and not a precise rule. Measured as the average number of very large business effects per campaign: loyalty strategies scored 0.0, acquisition 1.0 and reach strategies aimed at both 1.6; by primary objective, activation goals scored 0.7, other brand goals 0.9, awareness 1.6 and fame 2.2; emotional campaigns scored 1.4 against 0.2 for rational campaigns, and fame campaigns 2.4. A foreword survey found only 4% of B2B marketers measure impact beyond six months. The evidence base is IPA Databank B2B cases from 1998 to 2018, **fewer than 50 cases** out of almost 1,500 campaigns, biased to award entrants and skewed to the UK ([LinkedIn B2B Institute PDF](https://news.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/news/en-us/linkedin-pressroom-page/LIN_B2B-Marketing-Report-Digital.pdf)). **[INFERENCE]** With so few cases, quote the B2B split as "about half and half, slightly activation-heavy", not as a precise target; the acquisition, fame and emotion findings agree with the Double Jeopardy evidence from an independent dataset. **[GAP]** No post-2019 update of the B2B split was found.

### Budget size beats ROI as a profit lever

**[DURABLE]** At Google Marketing Live Sydney (reported 19 June 2026), Binet cited the "Go Big or Go Home" analysis: 500 surveyed CMOs rated ROI about twice as important as budget, but outcome analysis found budget size drives profit "by a factor of eight or nine to one" versus ROI. His guidance is to track net profit, not ROI; to recognise that over-tight targeting and over-investment in performance raise efficiency slightly while cutting effectiveness heavily, which can start a "death spiral" of shrinking budgets; to cut production before media when cuts are needed, making fewer ads and running effective campaigns longer; and to set budgets with econometrics. He calls the budget decision "the most important decision in marketing" and notes that few firms run budget-level experiments and most over-focus on intermediate metrics such as likes, clicks and awareness ([B&T, 19 Jun 2026](https://www.bandt.com.au/not-the-main-driver-les-binet-warns-advertisers-against-obsessing-over-roi/)).

### Use excess share of voice as a sanity check on growth targets

**[DURABLE, older data]** Nielsen's 2009 analysis of 123 brands in 30 categories found that 10 points of excess share of voice (share of voice minus share of market) yields about 0.5 points of extra market share growth per year on average. Leaders gain about 1.4 points per 10 points of excess share of voice and challengers about 0.4, so a typical FMCG challenger must be at least 3.5 times as effective as the leader to match it; launches grew 15 to 25% more per point than the norm. Variance across categories was large, and share of voice alone is not enough because creative quality matters ([Nielsen, 2009](https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2009/budgeting-for-the-upturn-does-share-of-voice-matter)). In Binet and Field's B2B data, 10 points of extra share of voice go with +0.7 points of market share a year, against +0.6 in their B2C data ([LinkedIn B2B Institute PDF](https://news.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/news/en-us/linkedin-pressroom-page/LIN_B2B-Marketing-Report-Digital.pdf)). **[INFERENCE]** Use it to test whether a growth target is plausible given spend relative to competitors, not as a forecast.

### Budget norms give outer bounds only

**[FAST-MOVING, May 2026]** Gartner's 2026 CMO Spend Survey (401 marketing leaders in North America, the UK and Europe, mostly at companies above $1B revenue, fielded January to March 2026) puts marketing budgets at **7.8% of company revenue** (7.7% in 2025), with 15.3% of the marketing budget allocated to AI and 56% of CMOs saying budget cannot fund their 2026 strategy ([Gartner, 11 May 2026](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-11-gartner-2026-cmo-spend-survey-finds-cmos-allocate-15-point-3-percent-of-marketing-budgets-to-ai-but-only-30-percent-are-ready-to-scale-ai-capabilities)). **[VENDOR]** A compilation of SEC filings from 40+ public DTC and CPG brands shows 17.2% of revenue spent on marketing in 2025; the methodology has inconsistent sample counts, so treat it as indicative ([Eightx, 2026](https://eightx.co/blog/ecommerce-kpi-benchmark-report-2026)). **[INFERENCE]** Derive the working budget from target volume times allowable customer acquisition cost, then check it against these bounds and against response curves once they exist. **[GAP]** Gartner's channel-level allocation was not retrieved.

### Allocate with objective-and-task, a 70/20/10 portfolio and marginal ROI

**[DURABLE; secondary]** Objective-and-task budgeting defines objectives, identifies the tasks needed and costs them; it is sometimes equated with zero-based budgeting and is usually contrasted with percentage-of-sales, competitive-parity and affordable methods ([Digital Marketing Institute](https://digitalmarketinginstitute.com/resources/lessons/budget-and-resourcing_budget-process_94mt)). **[DURABLE heuristic, not evidence-based]** The 70/20/10 rule for marketing puts 70% in "now" (proven channels), 20% in "next" (emerging channels with early promise) and 10% in "new" (untested), and is attributed to Coca-Cola's Content 2020 and to Google's resource rule; the origin story is secondary and partly contested ([Growth Method](https://growthmethod.com/70-20-10/)). **[DURABLE]** The analytical standard for moving money between channels is the response curve: a curve of incremental outcome against spend per channel, where saturation shows as diminishing returns. Marginal ROI is the incremental outcome from a small increase in spend divided by that extra cost, and Google's Meridian definitions assume cost per media unit stays at its historical average ([Google Meridian docs](https://developers.google.com/meridian/docs/post-modeling/roi-mroi-response-curves)). Average ROI describes how past spend performed while marginal ROI says where the next unit of spend should go, and a channel can have higher average ROI and lower marginal ROI than another ([Measured, vendor explainer](https://www.measured.com/faq/media-mix-modeling-diminishing-return-curves-mmm-budget-decision/)). **[INFERENCE]** The implied decision rule is to shift budget from the channel with the lowest marginal ROI to the one with the highest until marginal ROIs equalise, subject to the brand and activation balance; the experimental 10% creates the data that lets a new channel acquire a response curve.

### Write briefs with one objective, a real insight and agreed direction

**[DURABLE; survey, Oct 2021]** The BetterBriefs Project with Flood + Partners, supported by the IPA, surveyed over 1,700 marketers and agency staff in more than 70 countries. **80% of marketers think they write good briefs, and 10% of creative agencies agree.** On clear strategic direction the split is 78% of marketers against 5% of agencies; on clear, concise language 83% against 7%. Respondents estimate that 33% of marketing budget is wasted through poor briefs and misdirected work; 69% of marketers and 73% of agencies say rebriefs happen too often; 90% and 92% call the brief valuable yet neglected; and 89% and 86% say good creative is hard without a good brief ([IPA](https://ipa.co.uk/news/betterbriefs)). **[CONTESTED]** Marketing Week's report of the same study says "only 1 in 5 agencies" agree briefs are good, which conflicts with the IPA's 10% for creative agencies; prefer the IPA figure. Practitioner guidance in that article: pick one focused goal and do not use the brief as a shopping list; show where the brand and the business want to go; structure the brief as intent, insight, inspiration; agree the brief before anyone starts creating; and brief in person with time for questions instead of by email ([Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/clarity-direction-marketing-briefs/)). **[INFERENCE]** Minimum brief fields supported by these sources: business and marketing objective (one primary, measurable), the target and the insight about them, the positioning or single-minded proposition, strategic direction, mandatory brand assets, budget, timing, success measures, stakeholders and sign-off. For agent teams the "agree before creating" rule translates into the manager agent approving the brief before a worker agent generates assets. **[GAP]** The full BetterBriefs report and the IPA/ISBA briefing guide were not read, so no official brief template is quoted.

### Objectives and KPIs: SMART for one objective, OKRs for alignment, a north star with inputs

**[DURABLE]** SMART originates in George T. Doran's November 1981 Management Review article; in Doran's version the A was "assignable", and later variants substitute attainable, relevant and so on. Per John Doerr's What Matters site, SMART shapes a single objective and is a guideline, whereas OKRs tie measurable key results to the objective so that hitting the key results means the objective is met ([What Matters](https://www.whatmatters.com/resources/okrs-smart-goals-difference-between)). OKRs were introduced by Andy Grove at Intel and taken to Google by John Doerr, and they are meant to be aspirational ([15Five, secondary](https://www.15five.com/blog/okrs-vs-smart-goals-which-model-is-right-for-your-employees)). **[VENDOR framework]** Amplitude's North Star Framework uses one North Star Metric (a single rate, count or ratio representing product strategy and customer value) plus three to five complementary input metrics that together produce it; its templates are "Our North Star Metric is called X, which we define as Y" and "I believe [the metric] is a function of [X, Y and Z]". John Cutler's test is "If you can move your North Star directly, it's probably not a good North Star"; the grocery-delivery example is "total monthly items received on time" with inputs such as orders placed, orders fulfilled and on-time delivery ([Amplitude playbook](https://info.amplitude.com/rs/138-CDN-550/images/Amplitude-The-North-Star-Playbook.pdf); [Amplitude](https://amplitude.com/books/north-star/defining-your-North-Star); [Amplitude blog](https://amplitude.com/blog/product-north-star-metric)). **[DURABLE]** AARRR (Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue) comes from Dave McClure's "Startup Metrics for Pirates" (video dated 25 August 2007), which assigns each stage a conversion step ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=irjgfW0BIrw); [SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/startup-metrics-for-pirates-long-version/89026)); RARRA reorders the stages to put retention first ([Koji, secondary](https://www.koji.so/docs/aarrr-pirate-metrics-framework)). Growth loops (Reforge, 31 July 2018) are closed systems where inputs, through a process, generate output that can be reinvested as input; funnels run one way and silo functions, while loops change how teams are organised and goaled ([Reforge](https://www.reforge.com/blog/growth-loops)). **[DURABLE; Ritson]** For an annual marketing plan, Ritson says "you need four or five objectives" and that beyond that objectives become "dreams that never happen"; he does not cite the research behind the number ([Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/ritson-ten-traits-successful-marketer/)). **[INFERENCE]** A sensible stack for a marketing team is OKR objective, then north star or primary KPI, then three to five input KPIs, then per-channel diagnostics such as AARRR stage rates; a KPI tree is the same construct as the north-star-plus-inputs model, with the unit-economics formulas in section 2 supplying the financial branch. A single campaign brief should carry fewer objectives than the annual plan. **[GAP]** No authoritative source specifically on KPI trees or driver trees was found.

## 2. Unit economics: define the metrics, use margin-based LTV and incrementality-adjusted CAC

### Definitions and formulas an agent should apply consistently

**[DURABLE; a16z, 21 Aug 2015]** Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is the full cost of acquiring users on a per-user basis. Blended CAC is total acquisition cost divided by total new customers across all channels; paid CAC is total acquisition cost divided by new customers acquired through paid marketing. Omitting referral fees, credits or discounts understates CAC, and blended CAC does not show whether paid campaigns are profitable. Lifetime value (LTV) is the present value of future net profit from the customer over the relationship. The building blocks are revenue per customer per month (average order value × number of orders), contribution margin per customer per month (revenue from the customer minus variable costs associated with the customer, including selling, administrative and operational costs), and average lifespan in months (1 ÷ monthly churn, where monthly unit churn is lost customers ÷ prior-month total); LTV is contribution margin per customer × average lifespan. a16z warns that treating LTV as revenue or gross margin instead of net profit is a common mistake and prefers 12- and 24-month LTV when history is short ([a16z, 16 Startup Metrics](https://a16z.com/16-startup-metrics/)). **[DURABLE; a16z, 22 Aug 2023]** For LTV:CAC, LTV is taken as gross profit from a customer over time and CAC as total sales and marketing spend to acquire that customer; investors often use 3x as a rough benchmark for consumer companies, with LTV reaching 3x CAC or more within five years ([a16z](https://a16z.com/2023/08/22/why-do-investors-care-so-much-about-ltvcac/)).

**[DURABLE]** CAC payback period is the months needed to recoup acquisition cost: sales and marketing expense ÷ (new monthly recurring revenue × gross margin), or per customer, CAC ÷ (average revenue per account × gross margin %); for example $560 ÷ ($50 × 80%) = 14 months ([Wall Street Prep](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/cac-payback-period/); [Geckoboard](https://www.geckoboard.com/resources/kpi-examples/cac-payback-period/)). ROAS per Google Ads Help is conversion value ÷ cost × 100%, and target ROAS is the average conversion value wanted per unit of ad spend ($5 of sales for $1 of spend is 500%) ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268637?hl=en)). Break-even ROAS per Shopify is 1 ÷ gross profit margin before ad spend: a $100 product costing $60 has a 40% margin and a break-even ROAS of 2.5 ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/break-even-roas-calculator)). **[VENDOR and practitioner definitions]** Marketing efficiency ratio (MER), also called blended ROAS, is total revenue ÷ total marketing or ad spend across all channels for the same period; it is a blended health check, not causal and not a profit measure, and it has no standards-body definition ([Sellforte](https://sellforte.com/dictionary/marketing-efficiency-ratio); [Bloom Analytics](https://www.bloomanalytics.io/blog/the-smart-way-to-calculate-mer-in-ecommerce)).

**[INFERENCE]** Three definitional traps follow. Sources disagree on the LTV base (a16z in 2015 says contribution margin or net profit; a16z in 2023 uses gross profit as a proxy), so an agent must state which base it used. Sources disagree on the MER denominator (ad spend versus all marketing spend), so fix one definition per account. Break-even ROAS on gross margin ignores variable costs outside cost of goods (shipping, payment fees), so using contribution margin before ad spend gives a stricter and safer threshold. **[GAP]** No neutral standards source (for example MASB) was read for these metrics, and Bessemer and David Skok were not read directly on payback.

### B2B SaaS benchmarks cluster near 3:1 and 16 to 18 months

**[VENDOR, 2026, single secondary compilation]** For B2B SaaS, median CAC payback is **16 months** (top quartile 6 months or less, bottom quartile 24+; Aleph and Benchmarkit, 198 companies, 2026), with investor heuristics of 12 months as healthy, 18 as a yellow flag and above 24 as a serious concern. Median LTV:CAC is 3.2:1 with a 3:1 floor, and above 5:1 suggests under-investment in growth. Median CAC is about $702 for self-serve versus about $11,400 for enterprise sales-led; payback by contract value runs 8 to 12 months at $5K to $25K, 14 to 18 months at $25K to $50K, and 18 to 24 months above $50K (the source's FAQ says 18 to 36, which is inconsistent). Two measurement cautions from the same source: pixel-only tracking understates CAC by 25 to 45%, and revenue-based LTV overstates LTV by about 30% compared with margin-based LTV ([SaaS Hero, 2026](https://www.saashero.net/strategy/b2b-saas-cac-benchmarks-2026/)). **[VENDOR, secondary]** A second source reports that a 2025 Benchmarkit report put the 2024 median payback at 18 months, up from 14, and repeats 12 months or less as the usual SaaS target ([Drivetrain](https://www.drivetrain.ai/finance-planning-metric/cac-payback-period-formula-benchmarks-and-how-to-reduce-it)).

### Ecommerce targets depend on vertical margin

**[VENDOR, 2026]** For DTC ecommerce, the target is LTV:CAC of at least 3:1 on a 24-month lifetime, with CAC payback under 12 months for venture-backed and under 6 months for bootstrapped brands. Public DTC median payback is 4 to 8 months in beauty and outdoor, 6 to 14 in apparel, 9 to 18 in food and beverage, and 14 to 24+ in low-margin commodity categories. Contribution margin after marketing runs 8 to 22% in aggregate (beauty leaders 22%+, apparel about 12 to 18%, lower-margin CPG about 4 to 10%). The same source's channel-level CAC ranges (for example Meta prospecting $35 to $120) have no stated origin and are low confidence ([Eightx, 2026](https://eightx.co/blog/ecommerce-kpi-benchmark-report-2026)).

### Correct platform numbers before using them

**[VENDOR, 2026]** Platform-reported CPA and ROAS should be adjusted by an incrementality factor from a calibrated model; Recast's example turns a TikTok-reported $27 CPA into about $22 after MMM calibration, anchored on a lift-tested Google Ads incremental cost per signup of $20 ([Recast](https://getrecast.com/top-consumer-brands-dont-pick-one-measurement-method-they-triangulate)). **[INFERENCE]** Use margin-based LTV, decide scale-up on marginal instead of average CAC because returns diminish, and for lead generation compute allowable cost per lead as allowable CAC times the client's own lead-to-customer rate.

**[GAP]** No primary benchmark report was fetched directly, and no reliable MER benchmark by vertical was found. Lead-generation cost benchmarks now exist for Meta (section 4) and Google Search (section 6) but are US-weighted vendor data.

## 3. Measurement and experimentation: triangulate, then pre-register

### Use three methods, each for the job it does well

**[DURABLE]** Last-touch attribution gives all credit to the final touchpoint and overvalues paid search and retargeting; multi-touch attribution is not causal; MMM is correlational, and different models can fit the same data while disagreeing on channel contribution; experiments give causal lift but cover one campaign and one period and cost withheld spend. The recommended division is attribution for fast feedback, MMM for cross-channel budget allocation, and experiments as ground truth used to reject MMM variants or to set priors ([Haus](https://www.haus.io/open-haus/attribution-vs-traditional-mmm-vs-incrementality-the-ultimate-measurement-showdown)). **[VENDOR]** Recast reports that of 42 top consumer brands in its database, more than 40% use at least two methods and about 20% use all three; its workflow has lift tests calibrate the MMM, which then produces correction factors for platform numbers, with the largest channel tested quarterly and smaller channels annually ([Recast](https://getrecast.com/top-consumer-brands-dont-pick-one-measurement-method-they-triangulate)). **[VENDOR]** Below roughly $100K a year in ad spend, rely on platform tools and consistency, adding MMM and experiments as spend grows ([Haus](https://www.haus.io/open-haus/attribution-vs-traditional-mmm-vs-incrementality-the-ultimate-measurement-showdown)). **[FAST-MOVING, 2026; secondary]** The IAB's 2026 State of Data is reported as finding that among US buy-side decision-makers 76% use incrementality tests, 73% attribution and 67% media mix modelling ([Genius Monkey, Oct 2026](https://geniusmonkey.com/blog/incrementality-testing/)).

### Platform ROAS errs in both directions

**[VENDOR, Aug 2026]** Haus geo-tests show platform-reported ROAS overstating incremental ROAS by 1.5 to 3 times, widest for retargeting ([Jetfuel, 7 Aug 2026](https://jetfuel.agency/meta-ads-attribution-settings-in-2026-windows-best-practices-and-what-dtc-brands-get-wrong/)). **[PLATFORM CLAIM, via agency]** The opposite bias also exists: 31% of incremental conversions driven by Meta are attributed to other channels, and H&M reported a threefold improvement in incremental ROAS over two years after calibrating MMM with Conversion Lift experiments ([Brainlabs, 19 May 2026](https://www.brainlabsdigital.com/meta-ads-strategy-2026-whats-driving-performance-now/)). **[INFERENCE]** Agents should report platform numbers as directional, keep a blended metric as the top-line check, and label every number with the method that produced it.

### Lift tests are now within reach of small accounts

**[FAST-MOVING, Oct to Nov 2026]** Platform experiments have become self-service at low thresholds. Meta's Conversion Lift is available in Experiments or through a representative at no extra fee for a campaign started in the past year with about US$5,000 of spend and 500 conversions, plus a signal-quality condition (details in section 4) ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/221353413010930)). Google's Conversion Lift needs a US$5,000 study budget and 1,000 observed conversions and no longer needs a representative for Search, Performance Max, Display, Video, Demand Gen and App campaigns (details in section 6) ([Search Engine Roundtable, 8 Oct 2026](https://seroundtable.com/google-ads-conversion-lift-measurement-self-service-42250.html)). For geo experiments, Meta's open-source GeoLift is described as needing at least 25 pre-test periods across 20 or more regions with other media held constant ([Genius Monkey, Oct 2026](https://geniusmonkey.com/blog/incrementality-testing/)). **[INFERENCE]** The binding constraint for small advertisers is now conversion volume, not budget; an account below 500 to 1,000 conversions per study should use geo holdouts or accept directional evidence.

### Open-source tooling matured in 2026, with a conflict of interest

**[FAST-MOVING, Sept 2026]** Google's Meridian is an open-source Bayesian MMM; version 2.0.0 (2 September 2026) added an automated module that converts experiment results into model inputs, a code-free Scenario Planner arrived in February 2026, and an "agentic skills" repository supports model building. Meridian does not forecast by default. Google's GeoX geo-experiment tool reached general availability on 9 September 2026 with holdback, go-dark and heavy-up designs and multi-cell tests sharing one control; its results feed Meridian as priors. Google's claims for GeoX (more than 31% budget savings, design generation more than 94% faster) are **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** with no sample disclosed. Platform-built MMMs mean the media seller builds the model that grades its own media ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/googles-meridian-geox-exits-beta-claiming-31-cheaper-geo-experiments/)). **[FAST-MOVING, May 2026]** Meridian is also integrated into Google Analytics 360 and analyses Google, TikTok, Pinterest and Snap together ([PPC Land, GML 2026](https://ppc.land/google-marketing-live-2026-every-announcement-that-actually-matters)).

### Third-party cookies survive in Chrome, but user-level data stays incomplete

**[FAST-MOVING, status as of mid-2026]** Google retired Privacy Sandbox on 17 October 2025; Chrome continues to support third-party cookies subject to consent, while Safari and Firefox keep strict blocking ([Freevacy, 7 Apr 2026](https://www.freevacy.com/news/consent-brief/whats-changed-in-the-6-months-since-google-killed-its-privacy-sandbox/7280)). The retired technologies include the Topics API, Protected Audience and Attribution Reporting; CHIPS, FedCM and Private State Tokens remain, and consent under GDPR and ePrivacy is still required for cookies ([CookieYes, 25 Jun 2026](https://www.cookieyes.com/knowledge-base/cookies-101/is-google-ending-privacy-sandbox.md)). **[INFERENCE]** Because of browser blocking and EU consent rates, consent-based first-party data and aggregate methods remain the durable foundation regardless of Chrome's reversal.

### Fix the test design before launch and analyse once

**[DURABLE]** CXL's guidance for A/B tests is 95% confidence and 80% power, with baseline conversion rate, number of variants, minimum detectable effect and weekly traffic as inputs that determine runtime. Reach the planned sample before analysing and analyse once, because repeated looks inflate false positives ([CXL](https://cxl.com/ab-test-calculator/)). **[INFERENCE]** With low traffic, compute the detectable effect first; if it exceeds any plausible uplift, test bolder variants or use qualitative research instead of an under-powered test. **[PLATFORM CLAIM, Oct 2026]** Meta's suggested durations are 3 to 4 weeks for brand lift and 3 to 8 weeks for conversion lift, covering at least one conversion cycle ([PPC Land, 11 Oct 2026](https://ppc.land/meta-says-partnership-ads-cut-incremental-purchase-costs-45-in-emea-tests/)). Base rates for how often tests win are in section 14.

### UTM governance: three mandatory parameters, lowercase, one owner

**[DURABLE; Google Analytics Help]** In GA4, `utm_source`, `utm_medium` and `utm_campaign` should always be used; `utm_id` and `utm_source_platform` are strongly recommended; `utm_term` and `utm_content` are optional; `utm_creative_format` and `utm_marketing_tactic` are accepted but not currently reported. Missing parameters produce "(not set)" in reports. Values are case sensitive (google and Google are different sources), and Google recommends lowercase, one unique source per platform, one medium per channel (for example `paid_social`), campaign names matching the platform's exact name, and dynamic parameters where the platform supports them ([Google Analytics Help](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10917952?hl=en)). **[VENDOR, practitioner norms]** GA4's default channel grouping depends mainly on medium, and on source for paid social and paid search; unrecognised mediums such as qr, event or partner land in Unassigned unless a custom channel group catches them. The practitioner standard is lowercase, no spaces, one separator style, a fixed list of approved sources and mediums, a campaign pattern such as type-topic-yyyy-mm, tagging only external links, one named approver, a shared sheet of every tagged link, a test in GA4 Realtime before launch, and a quarterly review. When conventions change, do not rename live campaigns; use a GA4 custom channel group to merge old and new values, which applies retroactively (standard properties allow two) ([Leadfeeder](https://www.leadfeeder.com/blog/marketing-analytics/utm-best-practices/)). With Google Ads auto-tagging, leave the gclid in place instead of overriding with manual UTMs, and date or version campaign names so they are not reused across years ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/utm-parameters-guide-complete-tracking-reference)). **[INFERENCE]** Because Google's own examples use `paid_social`, underscores in `utm_medium` are the safer default for channel mapping; `utm_content` is the natural home for creative-variant IDs and `utm_id` for joining cost data. **[GAP]** Google's default channel group rules page was not opened directly, and no industry standards body for UTM taxonomy exists that the research could find.

**[GAP]** Still unverified for measurement: the current status of Meta's Robyn, power and holdout sizing rules for geo experiments beyond the GeoLift minimums above, and geo-based Conversion Lift requirements at Google.

## 4. Meta ads: consolidate, diversify concepts, and audit every AI toggle

### The delivery system starts from creative and signal, not audiences

**[FAST-MOVING in detail; DURABLE in outline]** Meta's delivery stack is a chain of AI models. Andromeda is the retrieval stage that decides which ads are eligible, evaluating creative, copy, format and historical engagement before predicting which users will engage; GEM is the foundation ranking model learning across organic interactions and ad sequences ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/meta-ai-driven-advertising-system-andromeda-gem-468020)). Lattice is the unified prediction architecture that replaced many smaller models predicting ad performance across datasets and optimisation goals. Meta's AI blog says it "built and deployed Meta Lattice", secondary sources date that post to **May 2023**, and a research paper followed in December 2025; Lattice is therefore a 2023 architecture that later work builds on, not a 2025 or 2026 launch ([Meta AI blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/ai-ads-performance-efficiency-meta-lattice/); [Triple Whale, 13 Apr 2026](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/meta-ads-ai-system); [arXiv 2512.09200](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09200v2)). **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** Meta attributes 5% more Instagram conversions and 3% more Facebook conversions to GEM ([Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/meta-ads-ai-system)). **[GAP]** The exact date on Meta's Lattice blog post was not read directly.

### Default to fewer campaigns and broad targeting, then verify per account

**[DURABLE in outline, CONTESTED in degree]** The agreed response is to consolidate campaigns and ad sets, since fewer campaigns, broader targeting and pooled budgets help the system learn faster while interest stacks and lookalikes have lost strength ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/meta-ai-driven-advertising-system-andromeda-gem-468020)). A practical three-layer structure is bounded learning (ad set budgets, one hypothesis, capped from maximum allowable CAC), consolidated scale (Advantage+ campaign budget across proven ad sets with the same objective, bid strategy and economics), and true exceptions kept separate only for real differences in margin, market, catalog, or acquisition versus retention ([growthmarketer.com, updated 3 Nov 2026](https://growthmarketer.com/blog/meta-campaign-structure-2026/)). The counter-view is that broad targeting has hurt some accounts and manual targeting still wins for others; Triple Whale suggests tracking first-time impression ratio and aiming for 10 to 20% of monthly impressions to reach new people ([Triple Whale, 13 Apr 2026](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/meta-ads-ai-system)). Value rules can adjust bids by age, location or conversion location in place of hard restrictions ([Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-ads-lead-generation/)).

**[FAST-MOVING, Meta Help Center read 10 Nov 2026]** Meta's own wording is that a sales campaign gets "the most advanced AI optimizations" when it uses Advantage+ audience, placement and budget together. **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** Meta says advertisers using Advantage+ sales campaigns saw a 9% improvement in cost per conversion on average. Meta now steers advertisers to "audience segments" for reporting breakdowns of new, engaged and existing audiences, and surfaces Advantage+ recommendations through Opportunity Score, which it says does not guarantee performance ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1362234537597370)). For Advantage+ leads campaigns, Advantage+ is "on" when budget, audience and placements are all automated; manually choosing placements or using ad set budgets turns it off; it works with instant forms, website forms, calls and click-to-message leads. **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** Meta's early tests showed 14% lower cost per lead and 10% lower cost per qualified lead against leads campaigns with Advantage+ off, and the page notes the feature may not be available to every account yet ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/992035952809423)).

### The existing-customer budget cap is gone; replicate it with two ad sets

**[FAST-MOVING, Meta Help Center read 10 Nov 2026]** Meta states that the existing customer budget cap "is no longer available. You can still do the same thing manually." The replacement recipe is to use a campaign budget and then either run one ad set that excludes existing customers, or run two ad sets where the existing-customer ad set carries a spending limit ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1142614290190459); [Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/830005979993164)). Meta's own Advantage+ shopping marketing page still lists the cap as an optional setup step and is stale relative to the help centre ([Meta marketing page](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/meta-advantage/advantage-plus-shopping-ads)). **[INFERENCE]** Any playbook instruction to "cap existing-customer spend inside Advantage+ sales" must be rewritten as the two-ad-set structure with a spending limit.

### Choose campaign budget unless ad sets need different bids or schedules

**[FAST-MOVING, Meta Help Center read 10 Nov 2026]** For the Sales, App promotion and Leads objectives, budget strategy is chosen at campaign level. Campaign budget automatically distributes spend across ad sets to get the best overall results and allows ad set spending limits when control is needed; a campaign with one ad set behaves the same as an ad set budget. Ad set budgets are for cases where ad sets need different bid strategies or budget schedules ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1602924913861363)). **[GAP]** No source quantifies when campaign budget beats ad set budget, and Meta's daily-budget overspend rule (commonly cited as up to 75% over on a single day) was not on the page read.

### Size budgets to about 50 results per ad set per week

**[DURABLE; Meta Help Center read 10 Nov 2026]** The learning phase is the period when the delivery system still needs to learn how an ad set will deliver; it starts when a new ad or ad set is created or a significant edit is made. Ad sets exit as soon as they can deliver stably, which "usually occurs after about 50 results in the week after the ad set's last significant edit". The unit is results (the ad set's optimisation outcome), counted per ad set, in a rolling week from the last significant edit. Shops ads are an exception, needing a minimum of 17 website purchases and 5 purchases through Meta. Ads Manager has a "Last significant edit" column to compare with the Results column. "Learning limited" is the delivery status when an ad set is not getting enough results to exit. Meta's four stated practices are to wait to edit until out of learning, avoid unnecessary edits, avoid high ad volumes by combining similar ad sets, and use realistic budgets without frequent budget changes; Meta also says not to try to avoid the learning phase entirely, because testing is necessary ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700)).

**[DURABLE; Meta Help Center read 10 Nov 2026]** Edits that are always significant: any change to targeting, any change to ad creative, any change to optimisation event, adding a new ad to the ad set, pausing the ad set for 7 days or longer (learning restarts on unpause), and changing bid strategy. Edits that are significant only depending on magnitude: ad set spending limit, bid control, cost-per-result goal or ROAS goal amount, and budget amount. Meta's only worked example is that $100 to $101 is unlikely to reset learning while $100 to $1,000 may. **Meta publishes no percentage threshold; the widely quoted "20% rule" is not in the official page.** With Advantage+ campaign budget, changing the campaign bid strategy or campaign budget can send multiple ad sets back into learning, but budget redistribution between ad sets does not reset learning, an ad-set-level edit does not reset sibling ad sets, and adding a new ad set does not reset the others ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/316478108955072)).

**[VENDOR rule of thumb]** Weekly budget of about 50 times expected cost per event, so $60 purchases need about $3,000 a week; "learning limited" usually reflects a small audience, low budget, tight cost controls, audience overlap, an infrequent event or too many ads ([Segwise, 3 Nov 2026](https://segwise.ai/blog/meta-advantage-plus-app-campaigns-guide)). Set a no-touch window before launch, such as one week or 50 to 75 conversions, and judge on 3 to 7 day rolling windows ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/meta-ai-driven-advertising-system-andromeda-gem-468020)). **[CONTESTED]** One practitioner rejects both a universal seven-day rule and a universal "scale 20 to 30% at a time" rule, recommending a window committed to in advance and steps sized to the account ([growthmarketer.com](https://growthmarketer.com/blog/meta-campaign-structure-2026/)); Meta's own page supports neither percentage. **[INFERENCE]** If the true goal event cannot reach about 50 a week, consolidate ad sets, raise budget, or optimise to a higher-funnel proxy. For an automated agent, a conservative default is small budget steps, one change at a time, with the step size recorded as an account-level heuristic and not a Meta rule.

### Creative diversity means distinct concepts, not variants

**[DURABLE principle; mechanics FAST-MOVING]** Agency executives told Marketing Brew on 28 September 2026 that Meta will not rank an ad highly if it shares the same look or opening hook as the advertiser's other creative, because the models treat it as essentially the same ad; two videos of the same person differing only in on-screen text count as one. Meta did not confirm this and said only that diversification is a "longstanding best practice" ([Relevant Audience, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.relevantaudience.com/meta/meta-ad-creative-diversification-same-hook-same-ad/)). Meta's recommended axes are format, text, persona, pain point, solution and visuals ([Jon Loomer, 20 Jan 2026](https://pubcast.jonloomer.com/stack-creative-diversity-in-phases/)). **[INFERENCE]** Count a concept as a unique combination of hook, visual look, persona and angle. **[CONTESTED on volume]** For modest budgets Loomer advises about five ads in a 7-day test using Meta's creative testing tool, running one to two weeks, and adding a second set "completely different from the first" only if performance disappoints, because Meta concentrates spend on a few ads ([Jon Loomer](https://pubcast.jonloomer.com/stack-creative-diversity-in-phases/)). **[VENDOR]** TheOptimizer's playbook is 8 to 12 distinct creatives per test at $20 to $50 a day per test ad set, evaluated after 7 days, with an expected hit rate of 5 to 10%, hook and hold rate targets of 20%+, fatigue windows of 2 to 3 weeks, and a directional heuristic to keep creative similarity below 40%; Meta has published no threshold ([TheOptimizer, 23 May 2026](https://theoptimizer.io/blog/how-to-test-ad-creatives-on-meta-after-the-andromeda-update-2026-playbook)). Loomer's approach suits small accounts and the vendor tables suit accounts spending $15K+ a month; no source reconciles them. Meta provides a creative test set-up in Ads Manager, and practitioner guidance is one variable per test with conversion lift studies as the incrementality check ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1423851372208214); [Hunch](https://www.hunchads.com/blog/creative-testing-on-meta)).

### Partnership ads carry the strongest multi-source evidence

**[PLATFORM CLAIM, Oct 2026]** A Meta meta-analysis of 20 EMEA conversion lift studies (January to June 2025) found campaigns with at least 30% of assets as partnership ads had 45% lower median cost per incremental purchase and 35% higher median incremental ROAS, at 92% confidence, below the usual 95%. Independent support exists: Charlie Oscar found 30% more incremental conversions across 94 marketing mix models, and the IPA (October 2025) scored creators at a long-term ROI index of 151. Setup: obtain account-level permissions from creators, run partnership ads in the same ad set as regular ads (or the same campaign with Advantage+ campaign budget), and use Advantage+ placements and Reels; creators without a Facebook Page will not deliver to Facebook placements ([PPC Land, 11 Oct 2026](https://ppc.land/meta-says-partnership-ads-cut-incremental-purchase-costs-45-in-emea-tests/)). **[PLATFORM CLAIM; two differing sets]** Meta is also relayed as saying partnership ads achieve 19% lower CPA and 53% higher CTR when added to business-as-usual campaigns, while a Meta for Business post cites 19% higher CTR, 10% higher conversion rate and 5% lower CPA; quote the specific claim with its source ([Aspire, relaying Meta](https://www.aspire.io/blog/insider-tips-meta-partnership-ads); [Meta for Business post](https://www.facebook.com/metaforbusiness/posts/your-audience-already-trusts-creators-partnership-ads-put-that-trust-to-work-wit/1610033204497712/)).

**[FAST-MOVING; official pages read as search excerpts]** "Branded content ads are now called partnership ads." Creators must tag business partners whenever there is an exchange of value, and the post then shows a "Paid partnership with" label; affiliate-link content needs the label but not a tagged partner; brands pay creators off-platform only. Account-level permission lets an advertiser run ads from partner content that tags it through the paid partnership label, an @mention, a people tag, a product tag or Collab, and both sides must meet eligibility requirements ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/788160621327601); [Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/711659943695726); [Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/906775900606811)). Platform labels do not replace legal disclosure wording (sections 17 and 19).

**[DURABLE risk; secondary]** The practical exposure is unchanged by the pause. ### Tracking: two 2026 definition changes break year-on-year comparisons

**[FAST-MOVING, Jan and Mar 2026]** From 12 January 2026 the 7-day view and 28-day view windows were removed from the Ads Insights API, leaving 1-day view, 1-day click, 7-day click and 28-day click for reporting only ([Dataslayer, 19 Jan 2026](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/meta-ads-attribution-window-removed-january-2026)). On 3 March 2026 Meta announced that click-through attribution counts link clicks only (previously any click such as a like, share or save); conversions after those other actions moved into engaged-view, which was renamed **engage-through**; and the engaged video view threshold was cut from 10 seconds to **5 seconds**. Rollout began later in March 2026 for website and in-store conversion campaigns. Meta says this brings its numbers closer to tools such as Google Analytics and names Northbeam and Triple Whale as partners adding view signals ([Meta for Business, 3 Mar 2026](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/click-attribution)).

**[FAST-MOVING, Meta Help Center read 10 Nov 2026]** The Standard attribution model's windows for website and in-store conversions are now: click-through at 1 day or 7 days after a link click; view-through at 1 day after an impression; and engage-through at 1 day after a non-link-click action, which for video means the video was played for 5 seconds, or for 97% of its length if shorter than 5 seconds. Fifteen seconds is not a Meta attribution threshold; vendor explainers repeat the 10-to-5-second change and none found supports 15 seconds. Some accounts may still be on prior versions during rollout. Meta offers three attribution models at ad set level: Standard, Incremental, and Custom (which shares attribution data from an external analytics tool so delivery optimises to the advertiser's own logic), and results cannot be compared in the campaign table across ad sets using different models. iOS 14+ app campaigns have restricted settings (1-day for re-engagement under Aggregated Event Measurement, 1-day or 7-day click for installs, SKAdNetwork reported separately) ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/460276478298895); [Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1055388958765938); [TheOptimizer](https://theoptimizer.io/blog/how-meta-ads-attribution-actually-works-in-2026)). **[INFERENCE]** Any comparison of Meta-attributed conversions across the January and March 2026 boundaries needs a caveat or a metric unaffected by the change, such as blended revenue or first-party orders. **[GAP]** The official default attribution setting for new ad sets was not stated on the pages read; a vendor claims the new default includes 1-day engage-through, unverified.

**[FAST-MOVING, Meta Help Center read 10 Nov 2026]** Incremental attribution is a model that optimises delivery for incremental conversions using machine-learning models that predict whether a conversion was caused by an ad, and it enables both optimisation and reporting on incremental conversions; the help page gives no eligibility rules, minimums or numbers ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2366718460372682)). It is a platform estimate, not a replacement for geo tests or holdouts ([Enrich Labs](https://www.enrichlabs.ai/blog/meta-ads-attribution-settings-best-practices)).

### Conversions API and Event Match Quality: what Meta says versus what vendors say

**[DURABLE]** Run Pixel plus Conversions API with the same event_id on both for deduplication, and turn on extended matching. **[FAST-MOVING, Meta Help Center read 10 Nov 2026]** Event Match Quality (EMQ) is scored 0 to 10 from the quality of customer information sent with a server event and the share of event instances matched to Meta accounts, using the last 48 hours of data, and exists only for website events sent through the Conversions API. Parameter priority is high for email and click ID; medium for Facebook Login ID, birthdate, country, phone number, external ID and browser ID; and low for lead ID, first name, last name, city and postal code. The help page states no numeric "good" score ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/765081237991954)). The only Meta numbers found are an EMQ "greater than 5" for self-serve Conversion Lift eligibility and a Meta business post saying "the best practice for the EMQ score is over 6" ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/221353413010930); [Meta for Business, Performance 5](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/unlock-businesss-potential-with-the-new-performance-5)). Developer documentation updated 28 June 2026 adds that for offline, app and conversion-lead events advertisers must contact a Meta representative, and that events carrying only very broad parameter combinations (for example gender, city, state and country) are rejected as invalid ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/conversions-api/best-practices)). **[VENDOR, Aug 2026]** Agency guidance is to check Purchase EMQ monthly, flag below 7 and target 7.5 to 9.3; a 20 to 30% gap between Meta-reported and Shopify purchases is typical and a larger gap suggests a Conversions API problem; use 7-day click for most DTC products, hold the window constant across comparisons, and cross-check against total revenue over total ad spend, since a window change alone can move reported ROAS 30 to 50% ([Jetfuel](https://jetfuel.agency/meta-ads-attribution-settings-in-2026-windows-best-practices-and-what-dtc-brands-get-wrong/)). EMQ targets of "8+" in vendor content are not Meta figures.

### Conversion Lift is self-serve at $5,000 and 500 conversions

**[FAST-MOVING, Meta Help Center read 9 Nov 2026]** Conversion Lift is available self-serve in Experiments or through a Meta representative at no extra fee. Guide thresholds are a campaign started in the past year with spend of **$5,000 or more and 500 or more conversions** on 1-day click, 7-day click or 1-day view, prorated beyond 90 days (180 days needs $10,000 and 1,000 conversions). One signal-quality condition also applies: either the Conversions API is adopted with any event at EMQ above 5, or a supported data source sends a lower-funnel or mobile app install event; this second route is unavailable to advertisers mainly targeting ePrivacy-Directive countries, so **EU advertisers need the Conversions API route** ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/221353413010930)). The page's window wording has not been updated for engage-through, so expect wording drift across Meta pages during rollout.

### Playbooks by objective

**Ecommerce.** **[VENDOR, calendar 2025, nearly 35,000 brands]** Triple Whale medians are CPM $13.48, CTR 2.19%, CPA $38.17, ROAS 1.93 and conversion rate 1.57%, with CPMs up 8 to 38% year on year by industry ([Triple Whale, 7 Apr 2026](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)). **[INFERENCE]** Use the full Advantage+ sales configuration, keep catalog quality high, brief roughly 30% of assets as partnership ads, separate existing customers with the two-ad-set structure, and measure on 7-day click cross-checked against blended efficiency.

**Lead generation.** **[DURABLE]** Start simple with few questions and high volume, fix internal follow-up speed first, sync leads to the CRM immediately, and judge on lead value, since a sub-$1 lead can be worthless and a $50 lead worthwhile. Instant forms suit advertisers with a weak landing page; test both against website forms ([Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-ads-lead-generation/)). **[FAST-MOVING; official pages, form-type page read as excerpt]** Meta has three instant form types: More volume (the default, for quick mobile submission and larger lead counts), Higher intent (for very intentional leads; Meta's example is booking a test drive) and Rich creative (a more flexible, personalised form whose intro section is mandatory) ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/252352181957512); [Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/761812391313386)). Lead performance goals are Maximize number of leads, Maximize number of qualified leads (formerly "conversion leads"), Maximize number of leads through messaging, and Maximize value of conversions. **Since April 2026 the qualified leads goal is unavailable for new campaigns without a Conversions API (CRM) integration, and existing campaigns were affected from August 2026.** **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** Meta says the qualified-leads goal with a CRM integration gave 21% lower cost per quality lead on instant forms and 9.5% lower on website forms against Maximize leads. For value optimisation on leads, send value and currency through Pixel and Conversions API, with at least 5 unique values per event and 100 or more conversions in the past 14 days; run at least 3 weeks aiming for 50 or more conversions per week, with peak performance typically after 2 weeks ([Meta Business Help Center, read 10 Nov 2026](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/782657799338685)). **[INFERENCE]** An agent should not recommend the qualified-leads goal unless a CRM-to-Conversions-API connection exists. **[GAP]** Meta's minimum volume for qualified-lead optimisation (commonly quoted as 250 leads a month) was not found on the page read.

### Meta policy points for pre-flight checks

**[DURABLE; Meta Help Center read 11 Nov 2026]** Every campaign must declare a special ad category or none. The current category names are Financial products and services, Employment, Housing, and Social issues, elections or politics; "Financial products and services" became required on 21 January 2025 for advertisers based in or targeting the US, replacing the narrower credit category. "Core setup", which Meta or the advertiser can apply, blocks custom parameters and everything in a URL after the domain. Affected custom audiences cannot be prefilled, may populate slowly or not at all, and cannot be created or updated if they rely on device rules or aggregation with custom parameters or URLs; custom events must be confirmed before use in campaigns, custom conversions and audiences ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/124742407297678)). Secondary sources report that the system was announced in November 2024, rolled out from early 2025 and has three tiers (core setup, restriction of mid- and lower-funnel standard events, and full restriction) ([Tealium](https://tealium.com/blog/customer-experience/metas-new-data-sharing-restrictions-and-what-it-means-to-healthcare-marketing/); [Ours Privacy](https://oursprivacy.com/blog/meta-platform-restrictions-explained-core-setup); [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/meta-health-and-wellness-brands)). **[INFERENCE]** Health and finance advertisers should plan for URL-parameter and custom-parameter loss by default and treat lower-funnel event loss as account-specific. **[VENDOR, 2026]** In sensitive verticals describe the product, not the viewer: no references to a person's condition, no before-and-after imagery for health or weight, no guaranteed results; landing pages are reviewed too ([wetracked.io](https://www.wetracked.io/post/meta-ads-new-sensitive-categories-restrictions)).

**[FAST-MOVING; Meta Help Center read 11 Nov 2026]** Meta automatically labels ads created or edited with some of its generative tools (Background Generation, Image Generation and Add Animation are named) and with third-party tools detected through C2PA metadata; the "AI info" label usually sits in the three-dot menu under "About this ad". Advertisers can self-disclose in regions with AI transparency rules, in which case the label appears next to "Sponsored"; the regions listed are the European Region, California, New York, India and Taiwan. Labelled organic posts keep their label when boosted. The only mandatory advertiser disclosure on Meta is for social issue, election or political ads containing photorealistic image or video or realistic audio made or altered with AI ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1010479435004531); [Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1486382031937045); [ContentGrip, 10 Jul 2026](https://www.contentgrip.com/meta-ai-ad-labels/)). **[INFERENCE]** For non-political advertisers AI labelling on Meta is mostly automatic; the practical duties are not stripping C2PA metadata and using the self-disclosure toggle in the listed regions, alongside the legal duties in section 19. EU-specific points on less-personalised ads are in section 19.

**[GAP]** Not covered for Meta: which standard events are blocked per data-source category (official pages do not spell this out), instant-form conditional logic and question limits, incremental-attribution eligibility and minimum volumes, and any performance comparison of Meta's generative creative against human-made creative on Meta itself (cross-platform evidence is in section 13).

## 5. Instagram and Facebook organic: design for sends and originality

### Instagram ranks on watch time, likes and sends

**[DURABLE signals; weights unknown]** Adam Mosseri names watch time, likes and sends as the most important signals, with likes counting slightly more for reaching followers and sends slightly more for reaching non-followers; Instagram has published no weights. Track average watch time, likes per account reached and shares per account reached. Reels and Explore reach mostly non-followers, Feed and Stories mostly followers, and Stories are not the best way to reach new people. Four stated principles are originality, recency (the share of impressions going to posts under 24 hours old has risen), auditioning of small accounts' posts with non-followers, and larger ranking models ([Buffer, 23 Oct 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/)). **[INFERENCE]** "Would someone send this to a friend?" is the best single design test on both apps.

### Use Reels for reach and carousels for engagement

**[VENDOR study, Oct 2026]** Buffer's analysis of 4M+ posts found carousels earn about 109% more engagement per person reached than Reels, while Reels earn 36% more reach. Carousels hold up to 20 items and unseen slides can be re-served. Trial Reels show to non-followers first, which allows hook testing without reaching followers. Consistent posters get nearly 5 times more engagement per post than occasional posters, and replying to comments is associated with 21% more engagement (correlation) ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/)). **[FAST-MOVING, Apr 2026]** Instagram limited how many Trial Reels can be posted in 30 days; the limit was not stated ([SocialBee](https://socialbee.com/blog/instagram-updates/)).

### What to avoid on Instagram, and the five-hashtag cap

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** Since April 2026, accounts that mostly post others' content are not recommended to non-followers. Engagement bait, like-gated contests, regulated goods and Reels carrying other apps' watermarks are ineligible for or pushed down in recommendations. Keywords in captions and profile help search and topic classification; Mosseri says hashtags do not help reach ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/)). **[FAST-MOVING, announced Dec 2025; secondary]** Instagram set a universal limit of **five hashtags per post**, down from 30; reports of a three-tag limit come from a test some accounts saw and from "three to five" advice ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/)). Use at most five highly relevant tags and put keyword effort into captions, on-screen text and profile fields. **[GAP]** The five-hashtag limit was not verified on an Instagram-owned page.

### Facebook is a recommendation feed plus Groups

**[FAST-MOVING, Sept 2026]** Roughly 50% of a Facebook user's feed is recommended content from accounts they do not follow (Meta, October 2025; the same article's FAQ says 30 to 50%). New posts go to a small test group first, and saves and shares trigger wider distribution; private shares through Messenger or WhatsApp are described as the strongest quality signal. Limited Originality rules mean adding logos, music or subtitles to others' content does not make it original; engagement bait and slow or spammy landing pages reduce distribution. More than 1.8 billion people use Groups monthly, which suits discussion depth. Several circulating rules in the same source are unsourced and should not be stored as fact: "5+ word comments weighted 3x", a 12-hour velocity boost, and daily 7 to 10 second Reels ([SocialPilot, 23 Sep 2026](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/facebook-algorithms-change)).

**[GAP]** No primary Mosseri or Instagram statements were read directly, no reliable 2026 Facebook Page organic reach rate was sourced, and maximum Reels length, Trial Reels limits, Groups-versus-Page reach and link-post penalties are unverified. Swedish usage of both platforms is in section 20.

## 6. Google Ads: goals and guardrails on top, keywords and data underneath

### Account structure for late 2026

**[FAST-MOVING, May 2026]** Google's direction at Google Marketing Live on 20 May 2026 was consolidation, with control moving to goals and guardrails while Gemini handles matching, creative and optimisation. **Dynamic Search Ads automatically upgrade to AI Max in September 2026**, so any plan built on Dynamic Search Ads is out of date ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-marketing-live-2026-every-announcement-that-actually-matters)). **Standalone Display campaigns are being retired into Demand Gen** (announced 26 May 2026, full migration expected in 2027; details below). **[DURABLE]** Google's structure guidance is to group keywords by theme, since Smart Bidding learns at query level and learnings transfer across the account, and to choose the bidding goal first because it determines targeting ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167110?hl=en)). **[INFERENCE]** A sensible default is brand Search on exact and phrase with tight controls; non-brand Search by theme with broad match or AI Max under conversion-based bidding; Performance Max (feed-based for retail, CRM-qualified conversions for lead generation); Demand Gen for mid-funnel video, image and creator assets and, from 2026, for Display Network inventory; and Standard Shopping only with a defined role. Legacy Dynamic Search Ads campaigns should be audited for URL exclusions, brand controls and text-customisation settings.

### Match types: exact for efficiency, phrase as workhorse, broad only with rich signals

**[VENDOR study, Feb 2026 data, 30,000 accounts]** Optmyzr found that for non-brand search, exact match leads on efficiency, broad generates the most volume at lower efficiency, and phrase takes the largest share of spend; in lead generation broad loses more ground than in ecommerce. Broad match is "a bet on the algorithm" that performs best when Smart Bidding has conversion value or strong conversion volume ([Optmyzr, 11 May 2026](https://optmyzr.com/blog/google-ads-match-type-performance)). **[DURABLE]** Use negatives to block irrelevant queries, not queries that merely failed to convert on a small sample ([Linear Design, 18 Sep 2026](https://lineardesign.com/blog/google-performance-max-a-practical-guide/)).

### Responsive search ads: fill the asset slots, pin sparingly, run two per ad group

**[DURABLE; Google Ads Help, fetched 11 Nov 2026]** A responsive search ad (RSA) takes up to **15 headlines and 4 descriptions**, with a minimum of 3 headlines and 2 descriptions; headlines are 30 characters, descriptions 90 and path fields 15 each, and double-width languages count each character as 2. Headline 1 and Description 1 usually show; Headline 2, Headline 3 and Description 2 are not guaranteed and depend on predicted performance or space. Up to 3 headlines can serve in the headline position, and unused headlines can serve as link-based assets, sensitive verticals excluded ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791)). Up to two unused RSA headlines can serve this way, and unused headlines and descriptions from other ads in the same ad group can also be used ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7332837)).

**[DURABLE]** Pins to Headline 1, Headline 2 or Description 1 always show in position; pins to Headline 3 and Description 2 are not guaranteed to show; and if every position is pinned, unpinned assets never show. Google does not recommend pinning for most advertisers because it cuts available combinations and can lower Ad Strength. The recommended compromise is to pin only one or two essential keyword headlines and, when compliance forces pinning, to pin two or three different assets to the same position instead of near-duplicates ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791); [Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9921843)). Google's checklist is at least 2 RSAs rated Good or Excellent per ad group, each with a unique final URL; at least 6 sitelinks plus dynamic sitelinks; 4 or more unique image assets plus dynamic image assets; and business name and logo added ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9921843)). The Ads table totals all combinations of an RSA; asset-level and combination-level reports exist separately.

### Ad Strength is a completeness diagnostic, not an auction input

**[DURABLE; Google Ads Help]** Ad Strength ratings are Incomplete, Poor, Average, Good and Excellent, recalculated as assets, keywords or structure change. Inputs are keyword relevance (a keyword must fit entirely inside one headline), asset quantity, asset diversity, sitelinks (at least 6 across account, campaign and ad group, with dynamic sitelinks counting) and text customisation (Google-generated text counts the same as advertiser text when enabled). **Ad Strength does not determine serving eligibility and is not used to calculate Ad Rank, Quality Score or auction outcomes**, and it does not measure landing page quality ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9921843)). **[PLATFORM CLAIM; internal data dated 15 to 20 August 2025]** Google says moving RSAs and sitelinks from Poor to Excellent is associated with about 15% more conversions on average, going from 1 to 2 RSAs in an ad group with about 6.6% more conversions at similar cost per conversion, and going from 2 to 3 with about 3.7% more ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9921843); [Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791)). **[INFERENCE]** These figures are correlational; advertisers that improve Ad Strength also tend to add sitelinks and assets, so the label itself is unlikely to be the cause. An agent should use Ad Strength as a checklist and never as a KPI.

### Ad assets: coverage matters because expected asset impact is in Ad Rank

**[DURABLE; Google Ads Help]** Asset types are location, call, sitelink, callout, structured snippet, text disclaimer (guaranteed to appear in the RSA description line), price, promotion, app, lead form and image. Google suggests at least four asset types and using every asset relevant to the business. Showing assets requires a minimum Ad Rank; higher positions get first claim on asset space; a lower ad cannot receive an asset combination with a higher expected CTR than the ad above it; and approved assets only show when predicted to help. Assets are free to add; clicks are charged as usual (seller ratings are not charged) and no more than two clicks per impression are billed ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7332837)). **[INFERENCE]** Because Ad Strength is excluded from the auction but the expected impact of assets is one of the six Ad Rank factors, the practical priority is asset coverage (sitelinks, images, callouts, business name and logo), not chasing an "Excellent" label. **[GAP]** No Google page fetched gives a measured CTR or conversion uplift per asset type, business name and logo verification requirements were not read, and no independent study of pinning or Ad Strength against performance was fetched.

### Quality Score is a keyword-level diagnostic; ad quality in the auction is what counts

**[DURABLE; Google Ads Help]** Quality Score is scored 1 to 10 at keyword level from three components (expected CTR, ad relevance and landing page experience), each rated Above average, Average or Below average against advertisers whose ads showed for the exact same search over the last 90 days. Google states that it "is not a key performance indicator", should not be optimised or aggregated, and does not factor into the ad auction. The score is based on impressions for exact searches of the keyword, so changing match type does not change it; a dash means too few exact-match searches to compute a score; and the score may not reflect device, location, time of day or assets. To diagnose, add the Quality Score, Landing Page Exp., Exp. CTR and Ad Relevance columns (and their historical versions) in the Keywords table and segment by day ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118)). **[DURABLE]** Ad Rank is calculated twice per auction (for eligibility, then for ranking) from six factors: bid; ad and landing page quality; Ad Rank thresholds; auction competitiveness; search context (terms, location, device, time, other results, user signals); and the expected impact of assets and formats. Ad quality helps set the thresholds an ad must clear and is a component of position, and "Higher quality ads can often lead to lower CPCs"; the bid is a ceiling and the actual CPC is often lower ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722122)). **[INFERENCE]** Use Quality Score for triage: a Below-average component points to the lever (ad copy and keyword fit for ad relevance, creative and assets for expected CTR, page relevance and usability for landing page experience). With broad match, AI Max and Performance Max taking more traffic, more auctions happen on queries with no visible keyword-level score. **[GAP]** No current independent study quantifies CPC change per Quality Score point; discount tables from the 2010s should not be used.

### AI Max: run it as a controlled experiment

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** AI Max for Search is a feature suite on Search campaigns combining search term matching, text customisation and final URL expansion ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15910366?hl=en)). **[PLATFORM CLAIM, 2025 data]** Google says activating it yields about 14% more conversions or value at similar CPA or ROAS for non-retail advertisers, and 27% for campaigns relying mainly on exact and phrase. **[CONTESTED; independent data from late 2025]** Smarter Ecommerce's analysis of 250+ retail campaigns found AI Max conversions at roughly 35% lower ROAS than other match types in the same campaigns, and a four-month single-account test showed $100.37 cost per conversion for AI Max against $43.97 for phrase. Google's reported test requirements are a $50 minimum daily budget and an eight-week test with a one to two week learning phase ([PPC Land, 6 Nov 2025](https://ppc.land/independent-tests-show-ai-max-underperforms-traditional-match-types/)). Controls include brand inclusions and exclusions, locations of interest and URL exclusions. **[DURABLE]** Landing-page hygiene matters more under AI Max because page content drives generated headlines and matching ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167110?hl=en)). **[INFERENCE]** Roll out via campaign experiment, non-brand first, with brand and URL exclusions set, judged over about eight weeks. No independent post-May-2026 study was found.

### Ads in AI Overviews and AI Mode need broad or keywordless matching

**[FAST-MOVING, June 2026]** Per Google's Ads Liaison, within AI Overviews only broad match or keywordless targeting is eligible and exact match cannot serve there, though both can serve above or below. Broad match stays eligible without AI Max. Ads in AI Overviews and AI Mode are reported with other top-of-page ads, with no segmented reporting yet ([TechWyse, 11 Jun 2026](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/ginny-marvin-ai-max-ai-search-eligibility-gml-2026)). **[CONTESTED]** PPC Land reported that the new AI Search formats require AI Max or Performance Max with text customisation ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-marketing-live-2026-every-announcement-that-actually-matters)), while the Liaison named only Business Agent for Leads as requiring them; treat format-level requirements as unsettled. **[PLATFORM CLAIM, May 2026]** AI Overviews has more than 2.5 billion monthly users and AI Mode over 1 billion.

### Performance Max: inputs matter more than controls

**[FAST-MOVING, Sept 2026]** Performance Max now offers campaign-level negative keywords (limit raised from 100 to 10,000), brand exclusions, up to 50 search themes, URL exclusions, and search-term, asset and channel reporting ([Linear Design](https://lineardesign.com/blog/google-performance-max-a-practical-guide/); [Optmyzr](https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/performance-max-2025-updates-study-analysis/)). **[DURABLE]** Build each asset group around one theme so every combination stays accurate, upload deliberate video instead of relying on auto-generated video, and treat Ad Strength as a completeness flag that does not predict profit. Audience signals guide learning without restricting delivery, so prioritise first-party lists. Split campaigns by budget, target, country, promotion, inventory or new-customer objective, not by catalog category alone. Exact-match Search keywords identical to a query generally take priority over Performance Max. The channel report is diagnostic only. Reconcile results against analytics, CRM, revenue, margin, returns and cancellations ([Linear Design](https://lineardesign.com/blog/google-performance-max-a-practical-guide/)). **[INFERENCE]** Brand handling is the highest-impact decision for honest measurement: without brand exclusions or a brand split, reported ROAS includes demand that would have converted anyway. **[VENDOR, 2024 data, older]** Optmyzr's study of 24,702 campaigns cites 60+ conversions a month for Performance Max to work effectively ([Optmyzr](https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/performance-max-2025-updates-study-analysis/)).

### Feed quality is the ecommerce foundation

**[DURABLE]** Audit titles, product type, Google product category, brand, GTIN, MPN, price, availability, images and promotions, and use custom labels for margin, season, bestseller status and inventory priority ([Linear Design](https://lineardesign.com/blog/google-performance-max-a-practical-guide/)). **[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** Google's retail guide adds: `short_title` under 65 characters, largest available image (minimum 300 x 300), availability synced with stock, and at least 50 approved products when applying manual product filters ([Google retail PDF, 2026](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/youtube_demand_gen_for_retail_best_practices_2026.pdf)). **[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** Agentic commerce is arriving: the Universal Commerce Protocol launched 11 January 2026, with checkout buttons in main search from 5 May 2026 and Universal Cart rolling out in the US from 19 May 2026; expansion was announced for Canada, Australia and the UK, with no EU date in the notes ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-marketing-live-2026-every-announcement-that-actually-matters)). Merchant Center free-listing requirements and Product structured data are in section 8.

### Bidding: bid to value, change slowly, judge by conversion count

**[DURABLE]** Use Maximize Conversions only when conversions are similar in value, Maximize Conversion Value when values are trustworthy and different, and set targets from business economics and recent actuals; a 500% ROAS means very different things at 70% versus 15% gross margin ([Linear Design](https://lineardesign.com/blog/google-performance-max-a-practical-guide/)). **[FAST-MOVING, Sept 2026]** Google's updated documentation says calibration takes up to around **50 conversion events or three conversion cycles**, an approximate ceiling and not a requirement ([TechWyse, 23 Sep 2026](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/google-ads-smart-bidding-learning-period-50-conversions)). **[CONTESTED thresholds]** Store Growers gives minimums of 15 conversions in 30 days for target CPA and 50+ recommended for target ROAS, target changes in 20 to 30% steps with a week between, and daily budget of at least twice target CPA when limited by budget ([Store Growers, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.storegrowers.com/google-ads-bid-strategy/)); Linear Design argues no universal minimum or fixed learning period exists. For Demand Gen, Google's own rule is stricter: adjust bids and budgets 5 to 10% per week and never more than 15%, do not evaluate before 50 conversions, and budget at least $100 a day for Maximize strategies or 10 times target CPA for target strategies ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14693848)). **[INFERENCE]** A conservative default for an automated agent is 10 to 15% per change, one change at a time, waiting at least one conversion cycle. **[FAST-MOVING, May 2026]** New features include journey-aware bidding (beta, target CPA Search, suited to complex lead-generation cycles) and demand-led budget pacing, which raises day-to-day spend variance, so pacing alerts should watch monthly run-rate ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ads-smart-bidding-exploration-budget-pacing-41288.html)).

### Seasonality adjustments are for 1 to 7 day events; data exclusions are for broken tracking

**[DURABLE; Google Ads Help, fetched 11 Nov 2026]** A seasonality adjustment tells Smart Bidding about an expected conversion-rate change from an upcoming event such as a promotion. Google says to use it only for major changes because "Smart Bidding already manages seasonal events", that it is "ideal for short events of 1-7 days", and that it may be less effective over extended periods such as more than 14 days. It is supported for Search, Shopping, Demand Gen and Display campaigns on Target ROAS or Target CPA and for Performance Max and App (beta) campaigns on any bid strategy, and not for Travel campaigns. Google's example is a 3-day sale expected to lift conversion rate 50%, which gets an adjustment of up to 50% for those 3 days; no negative adjustment is needed afterwards because bidding returns to its prior state. Manager accounts can apply one adjustment across client accounts ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10369906)). **[INFERENCE]** For a 1 to 7 day sale with a predictable conversion-rate jump, set an adjustment sized to the realistic lift and bounded to the sale dates. For a month-long sale, or a multi-week period such as Black Week into Christmas, do not use one; adjust targets and budgets and let Smart Bidding learn. Seasonality adjustments address conversion rate, not order value, so a sale that mainly changes average order value is better handled through target ROAS.

**[DURABLE; Google Ads Help]** A data exclusion tells Smart Bidding to ignore a period with faulty conversion data (tag failures, site outages, import failures). It excludes the clicks that could have produced the affected conversions, while excluded conversions still appear in reports. It is supported for Search, Display, Shopping and Performance Max with conversion-based or value-based Smart Bidding, not for Hotel and Travel campaigns, and can be scoped by campaign, account and device. Cover the conversion delay by excluding at least 90% of potentially affected clicks: in Google's example, an issue on 15 to 18 October with a 5-day conversion delay means excluding 10 to 18 October. Performance usually stabilises within a few days; if a week or more of clicks is affected, fluctuation can last one to two conversion cycles, and bids and spend may temporarily drop. Do not use exclusions often or for long periods, do not remove one after it is applied, and avoid backfilling conversions; exclusions for planned outages can be scheduled ahead ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10370710)). **[GAP]** No Google page fetched gives explicit guidance on target or budget changes during multi-week sales, and whether data exclusions cover Demand Gen and App campaigns is not stated.

### Display is moving into Demand Gen

**[FAST-MOVING, announced 26 May 2026; secondary reporting of Google guidance]** Google confirmed that standalone Display campaigns are being retired. Display inventory is not going away, and Display-only buying remains possible inside Demand Gen through channel controls. A migration tool rolls out in phases from June 2026; creation of new standalone Display campaigns ends at a later unspecified date; remaining eligible campaigns are auto-migrated at an unspecified date; and full migration is expected in 2027. Existing campaigns stay editable until migrated ([Search Engine Journal, 26 May 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-is-retiring-standalone-display-campaigns-in-favor-of-demand-gen/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/googles-display-ads-are-dead-here-is-what-replaces-them-in-2026/)). Mechanics reported from Google's guidance: use "Upgrade to Demand Gen" from the Edit menu, with up to 100 campaigns per batch recommended; the new campaign is named "[Original name] #2" and the original is set to Removed; up to 42 days of history carries over, cutting learning to about 1 to 2 days; migrated ads go through approval again; Pay for Conversions switches to pay-per-click with target CPA; and active Conversion Lift studies should not be migrated. Demand Gen does not offer Manual CPC, viewable-impressions bidding or Pay for Conversions; it offers Maximize conversions, Maximize conversion value, target CPA, target ROAS, Maximize clicks and a new Target CPC, plus campaign total budgets for flighted campaigns. HTML5 for the Display Network in Demand Gen is "coming soon", GIF is not listed, and Lookalike segments replace Similar Audiences. Demand Gen surfaces are YouTube, Gmail, Discover, the Google Display Network and Maps (beta). Migration advice attributed to Google is to keep Display-Network-only selected during migration and add other channels later, start with similar bids, limit bid changes to plus or minus 15% with at least a week between changes, and consolidate ad groups with fewer than about 30 conversions in 30 days ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/googles-display-ads-are-dead-here-is-what-replaces-them-in-2026/)). A practitioner adds: audit placement exclusions, app exclusions, managed placements and brand-safety controls and rebuild them in Demand Gen before migration is forced ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-is-retiring-standalone-display-campaigns-in-favor-of-demand-gen/)). **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** Google says adding the Display Network to Demand Gen yields an average 9.5% ROI increase ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/googles-display-ads-are-dead-here-is-what-replaces-them-in-2026/)). **[VENDOR, 21,425 accounts]** Optmyzr reports Display volume down 17.3% year on year from Q1 2025 to Q1 2026 while Demand Gen volume rose 53.2% and Demand Gen CPC rose 33.33% ([Optmyzr](https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/google-ads-benchmark-report-q1-2026/)). **[INFERENCE]** Plan on Display as a channel inside Demand Gen; remarketing setups that rely on Manual CPC need a new bidding plan, and migrating first with Display-only and widening channels later is the lower-variance path. **[GAP]** The cutoff date for creating new Display campaigns, the auto-migration date, and whether Responsive Display Ads and exclusions carry over unchanged were not published in the sources fetched.

### App campaigns: budget-to-bid ratios and stability through 100 conversions

**[DURABLE; Google Ads Help, fetched 11 Nov 2026]** App campaigns have three subtypes: installs, engagement (re-engaging existing users) and pre-registration. Ads run on Search and search partners, Google Play, YouTube, the Display Network and Discover, using text, image, video and HTML5 assets that can draw on the store listing ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6247380)). Set daily budget at **at least 50 times the bid for install campaigns on target cost per install and for pre-registration, 10 times for install campaigns on target CPA, and 15 times for engagement campaigns on target CPA**. Avoid disruptive changes until the first 100 conversions register, avoid cycling bids up and down, and avoid drastic changes to conversion settings, optimisation goal, targeted events or geography. For target CPA, target one in-app action, because multiple actions behave as a blended target. For target ROAS, confirm the event carries a value and keep conversion windows under 30 days while capturing about 90% of conversions; leave the install event without a value unless installs have inherent economic value. Each ad group should reach at least "Good" Ad Strength with at least one headline, description, image and video, and deep links (deferred deep links for install campaigns) are recommended. Pre-registration campaigns only serve in countries where the app is open for pre-registration in Play Console ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14104492)).

**[FAST-MOVING; Google Ads Help]** For iOS, Google advises consolidating to 8 or fewer install campaigns and says this limit will be removed for apps with on-device measurement event data. On-device conversion measurement has an event-data version (no user-identifying data leaves the device) and a first-party version for apps with significant sign-in; events must be imported from a primary GA4 or Firebase property, and Firebase iOS SDK v12.12.1 or later is recommended. SKAdNetwork reporting sits under Reports > Other > SKAN conversions, and the conversion value schema should include every biddable event. The page states Google does not currently support AdAttributionKit for attribution. Integrated Conversion Measurement works with attribution partners (Adjust, Airbridge, AppsFlyer, Branch, Kochava, Singular, Tenjin) ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10384955)). App campaigns targeting iOS are excluded from Conversion Lift ([Search Engine Roundtable, 8 Oct 2026](https://seroundtable.com/google-ads-conversion-lift-measurement-self-service-42250.html)). **[GAP]** App asset maximums and recommended orientations, and any conversions-per-day threshold for App bidding, were not on the pages fetched.

### Tracking changed materially on 15 June 2026

### Consent Mode and the Digital Markets Act make consent a performance input in the EEA

**[DURABLE since March 2024; Google's own pages]** Under the Digital Markets Act, Google requires user consent to link Search, YouTube, Ad services, Google Play, Google Shopping and Google Maps for data sharing with each other and with other Google services; unlinked services keep working but personalise only on that service's own data ([Google Business Safety](https://business.safety.google/privacy/google-services/)). For EEA traffic, advertisers must collect consent and send it through the consent mode v2 parameters `ad_user_data` (sending user data to Google for advertising) and `ad_personalization` (personalised advertising) to keep measurement, ad personalisation and remarketing; conflicting values disable personalisation, and the requirement also covers Google Analytics data used in Google Ads, Search Ads 360 and Display & Video 360. If tags are blocked until banner interaction, Google cannot verify consent choices, which "may lead to loss in data"; status can be checked in the conversion diagnostics tab, and uploaded data such as enhanced conversions for leads or store sales must meet customer data policies ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13695607)).

**[FAST-MOVING, June 2026; VENDOR source]** Consent Mode became the single control for Google Ads data on 15 June 2026. When `ad_storage` is denied, only URL parameters reach Google Ads; `ad_user_data` governs whether first-party data can be sent for matching, so enhanced conversions can fail without it. Conversion modelling partly compensates but needs enough consented sessions, and smaller accounts in high-refusal markets can fall below thresholds. The audit checklist: default `ad_storage` and `analytics_storage` to denied in GDPR regions, send all four parameters in default and update calls, check every page type, and test the update call on first and return visits ([UniConsent, 2026](https://www.uniconsent.com/blog/google-ads-consent-mode-change-2026)). The June 2026 detail rests on a consent-platform vendor blog; the underlying EEA consent requirement is confirmed on Google's own pages above. **[INFERENCE]** In the EEA consent rate is a direct performance lever: without `ad_user_data` and `ad_personalization` signals, remarketing lists and personalisation for those users are unavailable.

**[FAST-MOVING, 23 July 2026; secondary reading of the decision]** The European Commission adopted a non-compliance decision against Alphabet under Article 6(5) of the Digital Markets Act (Case DMA.100193) with a **EUR 460 million fine** for self-preferencing in Search across nine services, including product ads and free product units, flights, trains, lodging, places, Google Maps, translation, sports information and games; a separate EUR 430 million Google Play steering fine brings the total to EUR 890 million. Alphabet must end the non-compliance within 60 days of notification (about 21 September 2026 if counted from adoption), either by giving similar third-party services equal prominence or by removing the extra prominence of its own units, with periodic penalties of up to 5% of average daily worldwide turnover possible. The decision records that in the EU product ads unit only comparison shopping services (CSS) can bid, so merchants must supply inventory through a CSS, of which Google Shopping is one. Google was reported to be weighing an appeal, which does not suspend the obligation; AI Overviews and AI Mode are outside this decision ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-faces-cease-and-desist-orders-on-nine-services-in-eu-search-decision/)). **[INFERENCE]** EU Shopping advertisers should expect layout and auction changes in the product unit during late 2026 and 2027 and keep CSS arrangements under review. **[GAP]** What Google changed after the September 2026 deadline was not verified, the Commission's own press release was not fetched, and Customer Match and offline-import consent mechanics for EEA users were not covered.

### GA4 configuration: the settings that change what Google Ads bids on

**[DURABLE; Analytics Help, fetched 11 Nov 2026]** Attribution settings sit under Admin > Data display > Events > Attribution settings. The reporting attribution model applies to historical and future data and affects event-scoped traffic dimensions, not session- or user-scoped ones. First click, linear, time decay and position-based models were deprecated in November 2023; data-driven attribution assigns fractional credit across touchpoints and affects key events and revenue metrics, and the help page does not explain its algorithm. Lookback windows default to 30 days for acquisition key events (`first_open`, `first_visit`, with a 7-day option) and 90 days for all other key events (with 30- or 60-day options), and changes apply going forward. **"Channels eligible for credit"** can be set to "Google paid channels" or "Paid and organic channels"; the choice applies to all linked Google Ads accounts going forward, **can affect Google Ads bidding and reporting**, and can take a few days to appear. Paid and organic applies to web conversions only; Google Ads reports default to Google paid channels and Analytics reports default to Paid and organic. With Paid and organic selected, conversion window and counting method are managed in Analytics under Advertising > Conversion management. Time zones can differ between the property and the Google Ads account; to compare, use the "All conv." or "All conv. (by conv. time)" columns in Google Ads ([Analytics Help](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10597962)).

**[DURABLE; Analytics Help]** The daily BigQuery export on standard properties is capped at **1 million events per day** (20 billion on 360), and filtering can keep a property under the cap. Streaming export has no volume limit, is best-effort, costs an added $0.05 per GB (about 600,000 events) and omits traffic source fields for new users. The daily export uses "Last click observed, no modeling" attribution, includes cookieless pings when consent mode is implemented, and excludes wBRAID and gBRAID identifiers; exported data cannot be re-exported, and changing property time zone can cause skipped exports ([Analytics Help](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9358801)). **[INFERENCE]** BigQuery numbers will not match the GA4 interface or Google Ads because the export is last-click and unmodelled.

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026 release log]** Google's GA4 release notes for 2026 record: 16 January, cross-channel budgeting, web conversion management and conversion attribution analysis (beta), including per-conversion attribution settings for Google Ads; 29 April, a Task Assistant for configuration recommendations; 7 May, the Data Manager API for server-side events as an alternative to Measurement Protocol; **13 May, an "AI Assistant" channel grouping visits from tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude**; 8 June, a Google Business Profile integration with seven local metrics; 11 June, a Source Group dimension and hostname exclude filters; 13 July, AdMob links that let ad impressions count as conversions for target ROAS campaigns; 28 July, currency required for imported campaign cost data; 30 July, an aggregate parameter diagnostic flagging URLs missing GBRAID or gad_ identifiers; 10 August, a campaign data import validation report; **11 August, custom conversion windows (engaged-view 1 to 30 days, click-through 1 to 90 days)**; 9 September, dashboards; 29 September, app conversions for Google Ads customers with separate attribution settings; and 6 November, Data Manager in Google Analytics (beta) ([Analytics Help, What's new](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9164320)). **[INFERENCE]** An advertiser audit should check, in order: the Google Ads link and auto-tagging, key events marked and shared as conversions, the channels-eligible-for-credit setting, lookback windows, consent mode status, and whether daily event volume is approaching the BigQuery export cap. **[GAP]** A claimed April 2026 "GA4 attribution model restructure" appears in third-party headlines but not in Google's release log and is unverified; data-driven attribution internals, audience configuration and predictive audience thresholds were not fetched.

### Lift testing in Google Ads: Conversion Lift, Brand Lift and Search Lift

**[FAST-MOVING, Oct 2026; via trade reports of the help page]** Conversion Lift requires **at least 1,000 observed conversions and a US$5,000 minimum study campaign budget**, plus at least one compatible conversion action. As reported on 7 to 9 October 2026, the help page says a representative is no longer needed and lists Display, Search, Video, Demand Gen, App and Performance Max for user-based studies; iOS App campaigns and Travel Ads are excluded and Shopping is not named. A campaign can sit in only one study (Brand, Search or Conversion Lift) at a time. The design is a user-based holdback of 1 to 50% (30% when combined with Brand Lift and Search Lift); outputs are incremental conversions, incremental CPA and incremental ROAS; minimum duration is 7 days with more than 14 recommended; Google reports up to a 17% drop in absolute lift for long-lag businesses on studies shorter than 14 days; and study power is shown from 50% to 95% with 90% recommended. "Delayed Incremental Conversions", a cool-down projection, began on 14 April 2026 for eligible Demand Gen-only studies ([Search Engine Roundtable, 8 Oct 2026](https://seroundtable.com/google-ads-conversion-lift-measurement-self-service-42250.html); [Relevant Audience, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.relevantaudience.com/google-ads-en/google-ads-conversion-lift-search-pmax-thresholds/)). Google announced the US$5,000 minimum on 11 November 2025, down from a level described as "approaching $100,000"; the method is Bayesian, feasibility is rated in the interface ("High" equals a 60 to 90% probability of a conclusive result), and **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** improved models give up to 50% more conclusive results. Google's own observations are that upper- and mid-funnel actions show higher lift than purchases, conversion-based bidding shows higher lift than manual CPC, and most advertisers run one to two studies a year ([PPC Land, 13 Nov 2025](https://ppc.land/google-lowers-incrementality-testing-threshold-to-5-000-for-advertisers/)). **[INFERENCE]** Standard Shopping incrementality still needs a representative or a third-party geo test. **[GAP]** The window over which the 1,000 conversions are counted, and whether US$5,000 is total or per campaign, are not specified in the reports; the underlying help page was not fetched directly.

**[DURABLE; Google Ads Help]** Brand Lift requires a Google account representative and applies to Video, audio and Demand Gen campaigns, measuring ad recall, awareness, consideration and brand association through surveys on YouTube. Minimum budgets over a 10-day window depend on country tier and number of questions: tier A (including Brazil, India, Mexico and Indonesia) US$5,000, US$10,000 and US$20,000 for one, two and three questions; tier B (including the US, UK, France and Spain) US$10,000, US$20,000 and US$60,000; tier C (including Australia, Canada, Germany, Japan, South Korea and Switzerland) US$15,000, US$30,000 and US$60,000. Multi-country studies use the highest tier. Enhanced Lift needs three times the standard minimum, and budgets should not be adjusted until the study reaches 100% ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9049373); [Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9049825)). **[GAP]** Sweden's and the other Nordic countries' tier was not among the examples retrieved. **[DURABLE; Google Ads Help]** Search Lift estimates how ads change the likelihood of searching for the brand or product on Google Search and YouTube; it requires a representative, and its minimum budget equals the one-question Brand Lift minimum and is not additive. Use up to 5 search term groups (one or two usually suffice) with one to three specific brand or product terms per group ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14716878)). Demand Gen also has "Attributed Branded Searches", introduced in the January 2026 product drop and activated through a Google representative ([Google Ads & Commerce blog, 22 Jan 2026](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/demand-gen-drop-january-2026/)); a later headline saying the metric was narrowed to a 7-day window and dropped Performance Max support was seen but not fetched.

## 7. YouTube ads: buy by objective with a full asset matrix

### Choose the campaign type from the objective

**[FAST-MOVING naming, 2026; DURABLE logic]** For awareness use Video Reach Campaigns (non-skippable, target frequency or efficient reach); for consideration use Video View Campaigns; for action use Demand Gen. **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** Mixing in-stream, in-feed and Shorts drove about 35% more unique reach than in-stream alone in reach campaigns and about 94% higher ROAS in view campaigns. **[DURABLE]** Plan for about 3 exposures per week, keep targeting broad, and avoid content or placement targeting that limits delivery; start bids from historic skippable in-stream levels because bids set too low cause under-pacing ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15542751)).

### Supply three orientations and several lengths, built on ABCD

**[DURABLE]** Upload horizontal, square and vertical video. For reach campaigns mix short (under 10 seconds or a 6-second bumper), medium (10 to 20 seconds) and long (over 30 seconds) cuts with the brand shown early and often; for view campaigns build a narrative over 30 seconds with authentic content; aim for at least 80% of the ABCD awareness principles ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15542751)). ABCD stands for Attention (hook early, tight framing and pacing, audio and text reinforcement), Branding (show up in the first 5 seconds and throughout, early, often and richly, seen and said), Connection (make people think or feel, humanise, focus on one message) and Direction (a specific call to action, reinforced with audio) ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551?hl=en)). **[PLATFORM CLAIM]** Google says ABCD-aligned ads deliver on average a 30% lift in short-term sales likelihood and a 17% lift in long-term brand contribution; the underlying study (Google and Kantar, April 2021, 11,000 ads for the 30% figure) is footnoted and not shown ([Think with Google](https://business.google.com/us/think/future-of-marketing/youtube-video-ad-creative/); [Google Business](https://business.google.com/us/resources/articles/abcds-of-effective-video-ads/)).

### Demand Gen creative, creators and lag

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** Google's creative benchmark per Demand Gen campaign is at least one ad with 3 vertical, 3 square and 3 horizontal images plus one video in each orientation. Add new assets before removing old ones, pause only low performers that have had at least 14 days, and merge any ad group with fewer than 30 conversions in 30 days ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14693848)). **[PLATFORM CLAIM, 2026]** Creator assets deliver an average 20% lift in conversions, 45% of Shorts viewers are not on TikTok, and only 40% of Demand Gen conversions happen in the first 30 days ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-marketing-live-2026-every-announcement-that-actually-matters)). Google has a commercial interest in the long-lag framing, so validate with holdouts. When comparing Demand Gen to paid social use the "Conversions (Platform Comparable)" metric ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14693848)).

### Sequencing suits fixed-order narratives; target frequency suits reach

**[DURABLE; Google Ads Help, fetched 11 Nov 2026]** A video ad sequence campaign is made of steps, each with one ad group and one video, with bidding and targeting at campaign level; progression to the next step is triggered by an impression, a view or a skip. Four templates exist: Introduce and reinforce (long then short), Prompt and inspire (short then long), Attract and direct (short, long, short) and Engage and differentiate (four short ads). Target CPM is recommended; formats are skippable in-stream, non-skippable in-stream, bumper or a mix; inventory is YouTube only; targeting is by audience and demographics only, with keywords, placements and topics available as exclusions. By default a person sees one sequence every 7 days, optionally every 30 days, and no traffic estimates are provided ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9161595)). **[DURABLE; Google Ads Help]** Target frequency is a Video reach campaign subtype chosen at creation. Goals are **2 to 7 per week or 4 to 12 per month for multi-format**, and 2 to 4 per week or 4 to 8 per month for non-skippable or skippable-plus-bumper. It is a target, not a cap, so some users can exceed it. It does not support Google video partners, contextual targeting, shared budgets or political campaigns, and allows one ad group per campaign. Google's practice notes are to supply at least as many unique creatives as the frequency number, target broadly, run at least 7 days with 2 to 4 weeks working best, and avoid reusing creatives in other campaigns because reported frequency will undercount; reach and frequency reports can lag up to 72 hours ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12400225)).

### Connected TV: raise TV bids, keep all surfaces on

**[DURABLE; Google Ads Help]** Google does not recommend building frequency on connected TV alone, because target frequency is not optimised for single-surface delivery; the suggested approach is to raise bids for the TV surface while keeping all surfaces on ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12400225)). **[FAST-MOVING, Jan 2026; PLATFORM CLAIM]** Shoppable connected TV became available in Demand Gen in the January 2026 drop, and Google says Demand Gen campaigns that include TV screens average 7% more conversions at the same ROI in an internal 2025 global experiment ([Google Ads & Commerce blog, 22 Jan 2026](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/demand-gen-drop-january-2026/)).

### Shorts ads: vertical, under 60 seconds, sound on

**[FAST-MOVING; Google Ads Help, fetched 11 Nov 2026]** Shorts inventory is reachable through Performance Max, App, Demand Gen, Video view, Video reach and Reservation campaigns; only Demand Gen lets an advertiser anchor a specific video to a format, while multi-format view and reach campaigns allow format opt-out at campaign level. Use 9:16 vertical video (horizontal gets blurred bars). Only the first 60 seconds play in the feed and an overlay appears at 50 seconds, so keep ads under 60 seconds; suggested lengths are 10 to 30 seconds for action goals, 6 to 60 for reach and 10 to 60 otherwise. The call-to-action button appears at 3 seconds in Performance Max, App and Demand Gen and at 10 seconds in view and reach campaigns; a 40-character headline and 90-character description are recommended, and on TV screens Shorts ad descriptions truncate after 2 lines. Google's guidance is social-first creative that resembles organic Shorts, creator content, text overlays and multiple creatives. **[PLATFORM CLAIM, undated]** Using sound increases conversions by over 20%. The page notes AI-content disclosure or labelling requirements in the EU, India, New York, California, South Korea and Vietnam and an AI label setting in Google Ads ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16041697)).

### Measurement is partly access-gated

**[DURABLE]** Brand Lift and Search Lift both require a Google account representative and the market-specific budget minimums in section 6; Format Segment Reports give like-for-like comparison inside multi-format campaigns ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15542751)). **[INFERENCE]** Small advertisers should rely on self-service Conversion Lift where they have 1,000 conversions, platform-comparable conversions and geo holdouts.

## 8. SEO: the classic foundation still decides eligibility

### Technical and content basics, restated by Google in May 2026

**[DURABLE]** To be eligible for Google's AI features a page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet; no extra technical requirement exists. Keep content crawlable, reduce duplicate content, follow JavaScript SEO practice on script-heavy sites, make pages display well on all devices with low latency, and verify the site in Search Console. Structured data is not required for AI features, and no special AI schema exists, but it remains worthwhile for rich results. On content, write from your own knowledge and experience and avoid "commodity" content a generative model could easily produce; organise with clear headings; no ideal page length exists; and no page per long-tail variant is needed ([Google Search Central, AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)). **[INFERENCE]** A working checklist: crawl and index health, snippet eligibility, page experience, one page per distinct intent with query-matching headings, valid structured data, internal links between related pages, and earned links and mentions.

### Core Web Vitals thresholds are unchanged in 2026

**[DURABLE; Google pages last updated 2024 to 2025, fetched 11 Nov 2026]** The "good" thresholds are **Largest Contentful Paint at or under 2.5 seconds, Interaction to Next Paint at or under 200 milliseconds and Cumulative Layout Shift at or under 0.1**, each assessed at the 75th percentile of page loads and segmented by mobile and desktop; a page passes only if all three are met ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). For Interaction to Next Paint, above 200 ms up to 500 ms needs improvement and above 500 ms is poor ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/inp)). Search Central says "We highly recommend site owners achieve good Core Web Vitals for success with Search" and frames them as part of the page experience that core ranking systems seek to reward, without giving a weight ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals)). Interaction to Next Paint replaced First Input Delay, and stable metrics can change definition no more than about once a year. None of the three Google pages was updated in 2026 and all agree, so the thresholds stood unchanged as of November 2026. **[GAP]** The "needs improvement" and "poor" bands for the other two metrics and the Chrome metrics changelog were not re-verified. The conversion effect of speed is in section 14.

### Update cadence and how to respond: a core update is rolling out now

**[FAST-MOVING, status 11 Nov 2026]** Google's Search Status Dashboard lists eight ranking incidents in 2026: the February Discover update (5 February, about 21 days); the March spam update (24 March, 19.5 hours); the March core update (27 March, about 12 days); the May core update (21 May, about 12 days); the June spam update (24 June, about 2 days); the August spam update (18 August, about 2.7 days); the September spam update (24 September, 13 days 16 hours, the longest spam rollout of the year, finishing about 8 October with a second phase reported on 30 September); and the **November 2026 core update, which started on 10 November 2026 and was still in progress on 11 November** ([Google Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history); [Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/november-2026-google-webmaster-report-42431.html)). That makes three core updates and four spam updates in 2026 to date, after three core updates in 2025 ([PPC Land, 27 Mar 2026](https://ppc.land/googles-march-2026-core-update-is-here-and-it-follows-spam-by-just-3-days/); [TechWyse, 25 Jun 2026](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/google-june-2026-spam-update)). No core update occurred in October 2026; unconfirmed volatility was reported on 16, 20 to 21, 24 and 27 October ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/category/google-updates)). **[INFERENCE]** Treat ranking data from 10 November 2026 onward as unstable until the dashboard shows the update complete; the two earlier 2026 core updates each took about 12 days. **[DURABLE]** Core updates do not target specific sites; substantial recovery typically needs another core update; avoid changing content that still performs after small drops; deletion is a last resort; wait at least a full week after rollout completes before comparing Search Console data ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/googles-march-2026-core-update-is-here-and-it-follows-spam-by-just-3-days/)). Spam-update recovery can take many months ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-september-2026-spam-update-42163.html)). Volatility is large: SE Ranking found nearly 15% of top-10 pages before the December 2025 core update had left the top 100 by 5 January 2026 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/googles-march-2026-core-update-is-here-and-it-follows-spam-by-just-3-days/)). **[GAP]** Google's expected duration and commentary for the November 2026 core update, and what the September spam update targeted, were not retrieved.

### Spam policies now name AI-answer manipulation

**[DURABLE list; FAST-MOVING addition, 15 May 2026]** Google's spam policies cover scaled content abuse (many pages generated primarily to manipulate rankings, whatever the method), site reputation abuse ("parasite SEO"), expired domain abuse, link spam, thin affiliation, doorway pages, scraping and keyword stuffing, and since 15 May 2026 also "attempting to manipulate generative AI responses in Google Search" ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies); [TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/google-june-2026-spam-update)). Creating pages for every query variation or fan-out query violates the scaled content abuse policy ([Google AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)). AI-generated content is permitted when it meets Search Essentials, but Google says it is "critical to manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content", including titles, meta descriptions, structured data and alt text. In Merchant Center, AI-generated images must carry IPTC TrainedAlgorithmicMedia metadata and AI-generated titles and descriptions must be labelled ([Google, Using generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)).

### AI Overviews roughly halve clicks, and being cited softens the loss

**[FAST-MOVING studies]** Pew's March 2025 panel (900 US adults, 68,879 searches) found users clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits with an AI summary versus 15% without, and clicked a link inside the summary on 1% ([Pew Research Center, 22 Jul 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)). **[VENDOR]** Ahrefs' update (300,000 keywords, December 2023 versus December 2025, desktop) found position-1 CTR 58% lower with AI Overviews ([Ahrefs via Business Wire, 18 May 2026](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20260518322756/en/New-Research-Googles-AI-Overviews-Now-Cost-Websites-58-of-Their-Clicks)). Seer Interactive (53 brands, 5.47M queries, January 2025 to February 2026) found organic CTR of about 3.3% with no AI Overview, **about 2.1% when the brand is cited in it, and about 0.9% when it is not**; CTR on AI Overview queries recovered from 1.3% in December 2025 to 2.4% in February 2026. AI Overviews appear on about 86% of question queries and 95% of comparison queries but only about 5% of transactional queries ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-ctr-recovery-study-475566)). The cited-versus-uncited gap is observational and brand strength likely confounds it. Practitioner surveys show the downstream effect: 31% of B2B marketers saw website traffic fall in the past year and 79% of those blame AI-powered search ([Content Marketing Institute, 2026](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/articles/b2b-content-marketing-trends-research)).

### Local SEO essentials

**[Expert survey, published 6 Nov 2025; opinion-based]** Whitespark's 2026 local ranking factors put primary Google Business Profile category, proximity, keywords in the business title, physical address in the city of search, and **being open at time of search** at the top for the local pack; for local organic the top factor is a dedicated page for each service, followed by geographic relevance and inbound link quality. Accurate hours rose from #85 to #21, and citations are gaining value for AI visibility ([Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/whats-working-in-local-seo)). **[INFERENCE]** Stuffing keywords into the business name is a suspension risk, not a tactic.

### Ecommerce SEO: merchant listings, free listings, facets, pagination, out-of-stock

**[DURABLE; Google Search Central, fetched 11 Nov 2026]** Google separates product snippets, which accept `Offer` or `AggregateOffer`, from merchant listings, which require `Offer` because the merchant must be the seller; only pages where a shopper can buy qualify. Required Product properties are `name`, `image` and `offers`; recommended properties include `aggregateRating`, `brand.name`, `category`, `color`, `description`, GTIN properties, `audience` and `hasCertification`. Three price types are supported: active, strikethrough (`priceType` `StrikethroughPrice`) and member price (`validForMemberTier`); sale timing uses `validFrom` with `validThrough` or `priceValidUntil`; and unit pricing uses `referenceQuantity`, which Google notes is important in the EU. Shipping uses `OfferShippingDetails`, and standard return policies should be nested under Organization as `MerchantReturnPolicy`, with product-level policies only as overrides. Use a distinct URL per currency, and note that JavaScript-generated Product markup can make Shopping crawls less frequent and less reliable ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/merchant-listing)). **[DURABLE; Merchant Center Help]** Free listings can appear on the Shopping tab, Search, Images and Lens, YouTube, Maps, Gemini and the Business Profile products module. Required attributes for all products are `id`, `title`, `link`, `image_link`, `price`, `description` and `availability`; conditional attributes include `condition`, `brand`, and `gtin` or `mpn`; apparel also needs `color`, `size`, `age_group`, `gender` and `item_group_id`. Return policy information is required, and shipping settings are mandatory in listed countries including the US, UK, Germany and France; new accounts are opted in by default ([Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/9199328)). Merchant Center feeds and Business Profiles also help products and services appear in AI responses ([Google AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)).

**[DURABLE; Google Search Central, updated Dec 2025]** For faceted navigation, if facet URLs do not need indexing, block crawling with robots.txt or use URL fragments, which Google generally does not crawl; `rel="canonical"` and `rel="nofollow"` are weaker long-term alternatives. If facets should be indexed, use the standard "&" separator, keep filter order consistent, and return HTTP 404 for empty, duplicate or nonsensical filter combinations instead of redirecting ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/crawling-managing-faceted-navigation)). For pagination, link pages with crawlable anchor links, give each page a unique URL and its own canonical, and avoid fragments for page numbers; Google no longer uses `rel="next"` and `rel="prev"` and does not click "load more" buttons ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/pagination-and-incremental-page-loading)). **[Possibly outdated, 2020 to 2021]** On out-of-stock products, the only direct Google statements found are John Mueller's that such pages "generally tend to end up as soft 404 pages" with "no fixed timeframe" ([Search Engine Roundtable, 19 Jan 2021](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-timeframe-out-of-stock-product-soft-404-30777.html)). **[INFERENCE]** Keeping availability accurate in both markup and feed is the safest route for temporarily unavailable products. **[GAP]** The merchant listing page was read to about half its length, so the full Offer table, availability value list and review markup rules were not read; no current Google statement on out-of-stock or discontinued products exists in the sources; product variant markup and any 2026 additions for AI shopping surfaces were not checked.

### YouTube SEO: relevance, engagement and quality, with watch time deciding tests

**[DURABLE; YouTube Help, fetched 11 Nov 2026]** YouTube says search "prioritizes three key elements": relevance, engagement and quality. Relevance considers how well the title, tags, description and video content match the query; engagement signals such as watch time of a video for a particular query are used to judge relevance; and quality signals identify channels showing expertise, authoritativeness and trustworthiness on a topic. Results can be personalised by search and watch history, and YouTube takes no payment for organic placement ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16090438)). Recommendations use viewing behaviour, likes, dislikes, subscriptions and feedback including satisfaction surveys, and YouTube elevates high-quality sources for news, personal finance and medical or scientific topics ([How YouTube Works](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/product-features/recommendations/)). The native A/B test lets creators test up to 3 titles, thumbnails or combinations, and **the winner is the variant with the highest watch time share, not CTR**; tests can take up to two weeks, are unavailable for Shorts, and stop if the title or thumbnail is edited mid-test; thumbnails under 1280 x 720 cause all variants to be downscaled ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714?hl=en)). Manual chapters need a first timestamp of 00:00, at least three timestamps in ascending order and chapters of at least 10 seconds ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579)). **[INFERENCE]** Because the native test optimises for watch time share, a high-CTR thumbnail that attracts the wrong viewers can lose; packaging should promise what the video delivers. YouTube mentions are also the strongest measured correlate of AI brand visibility (section 9). **[GAP]** YouTube's organic Shorts ranking statements, and any statement on hashtags, captions, auto-dubbing or the weight of tags, were not fetched.

**[GAP]** Still unverified for SEO: Google's E-E-A-T and people-first content documentation, and evidence on refresh cadence, internal links or title tags.

## 9. GEO: rank in retrieval, earn real mentions, add verifiable substance

### Google's official position is that GEO "is still SEO"

**[DURABLE policy; published 15 May 2026]** Google's guide states that AI Overviews and AI Mode use retrieval-augmented generation and query fan-out over the normal Search index and the same ranking and spam systems. For Google Search specifically: llms.txt and special markup are not used; content chunking and rewriting for AI are unnecessary; inauthentic mentions do not help; and no third-party tool has access to Google's internal AI metrics ([Google AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide); [Semrush, 19 May 2026](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-publishes-generative-ai-search-guide/)). **[INFERENCE]** This guidance is scoped to Google and does not settle what works in ChatGPT, Perplexity or Claude.

### How engines pick sources

**[VENDOR studies; FAST-MOVING]** ChatGPT issued 4 or more searches on 63% of prompts in a 420-prompt study (October 2025), with only 8.3% URL overlap with Google's top 10; the first sub-query supplied the primary citation in 67.2% of multi-query prompts ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/chatgpt-query-fanout-explained)). In AirOps's 2026 study of 50,553 ChatGPT responses, pages in the top retrieval position were cited 58.4% of the time versus 14.2% at position 10; heading-to-query match (41.0% versus about 30%), JSON-LD (38.5% versus 32.0%), 4 to 10 subheadings, 500 to 2,000 words, age of 30 to 89 days and narrow focus also correlated with citation ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/chatgpt-citations-ranking-precision-length-study-474538)). Google AI Mode averaged 12.6 cited URLs per answer with 14% overlap with the organic top 10, and only 9.2% of URLs appeared in all of three same-day runs (June 2025 data) ([SE Ranking, 29 Aug 2025](https://seranking.com/blog/ai-mode-research/)). **[INFERENCE]** Cover the sub-questions that fan-out generates (comparisons, pricing, "best for", how-to) inside strong pages, without building a page per sub-query.

### Which sources get cited differs by engine and is unstable

**[VENDOR, 31 Mar 2026, US, 30M sources]** Peec AI's top cited domains overall are Reddit, YouTube, LinkedIn, Wikipedia, Forbes, G2, Yelp, Facebook, Medium and TechRadar. YouTube leads in Google AI Mode and AI Overviews; Reddit leads in Gemini and Perplexity; Wikipedia leads in ChatGPT ([Peec AI](https://peec.ai/blog/top-domains-cited-by-ai-search-analysis-based-on-30m-sources)). "Best X" lists are 43.8% of cited page types ([Ahrefs, 15 May 2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-chatbot-traffic/)). **[CONTESTED, volatile]** Tinuiti found Reddit at 24% of Perplexity citations in January 2026 and TikTok near zero at 0.02% of all citations ([Tinuiti](https://tinuiti.com/research-insights/research/ai-citation-trends-report-q1-2026/)), but Reddit's share of ChatGPT citations fell from about 3.8% to about 0.5% within weeks in August 2026 after a fan-out change (provisional data), so "Reddit SEO" is not a reliable route to ChatGPT visibility ([Semrush, Aug 2026](https://www.semrush.com/blog/reddits-citations-in-chatgpt-fall/)).

### Citations and mentions are different outcomes

**[VENDOR, 2026]** About 40% of citations do not name the cited brand, ranging from 52% on Perplexity to 19% on Copilot; track four states per prompt and engine: cited and mentioned, cited only, mentioned only, neither. Putting the brand name in the same sentence as a key statistic is a hypothesis for reducing unnamed citations, not a proven tactic ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/ghost-citation-problem-ai-483794)).

### The best-evidenced levers, in order of evidence quality

**[Correlational; updated Aug 2026]** Ahrefs' study of 75,000 brands found YouTube mentions the strongest correlate of AI brand visibility (about 0.737), ahead of branded web mentions (0.66 to 0.71), branded anchors, branded search volume and Domain Rating (0.27 to 0.33), with backlink counts very weak; Ahrefs cautions these may be markers of existing brand strength ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations)). Mention frequency mattered more than view counts ([The Next Web, 27 May 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/ahrefs-youtube-mentions-ai-visibility-brand-search)). **[Lab study, 2023 to 2024, possibly outdated]** The GEO paper found adding quotations (+40.9%), statistics (+30.6%) and cited sources (+27.5%) raised visibility in generative answers, while keyword stuffing reduced it (−8.3%); lower-ranked sources gained most ([arXiv 2311.09735](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3)). **[Practitioner analysis, Sept 2026]** Orbit Media's survey author observes that when AI assistants recommend a brand, buyers arrive later through citations, brand search or direct visits, which is an argument for earned mentions and original research as inputs to AI visibility ([Orbit Media](https://www.orbitmedia.com/blog/blogging-statistics/)). **[INFERENCE]** Strongest evidence: retrieval rank and third-party brand presence. Moderate: statistics, quotations and citations in content. Weak: schema effects, ideal word counts, freshness windows. No controlled 2026 field experiment was found, so effect sizes should not be promised.

### Crawler access and llms.txt

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** Search and training crawlers are controlled independently. For OpenAI, OAI-SearchBot governs inclusion in ChatGPT search and GPTBot governs training ([OpenAI crawler docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)). For Anthropic, Claude-SearchBot governs search indexing and ClaudeBot training; PerplexityBot handles Perplexity indexing; Google-Extended opts out of Gemini training while staying in Search ([Search Engine Journal, 25 Feb 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/anthropic-claude-bots-robots-txt-ai-decisions/568253/)). **[INFERENCE]** Allow the search and retrieval bots and treat training bots as a separate business choice. **[Study, May 2026]** llms.txt has no demonstrated search value: 97% of such files across 137,210 domains received zero requests in a month, and Ahrefs flags a prompt-injection risk ([Ahrefs, 15 Jun 2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/llmstxt-study/)).

### Measure with repeated sampling and state the method

**[FAST-MOVING]** Search Console's generative AI report shows impressions only, without queries or clicks, and began rolling out with UK site owners (reported 3 June 2026) ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-adds-ai-performance-reports/)). Since 13 May 2026 GA4 has an "AI Assistant" default channel that groups visits from tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude ([Analytics Help](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9164320)). AI chatbots sent 0.28% of total web traffic across 74,752 sites in March 2026; conversion anecdotes (Ahrefs: 0.5% of visitors but 12.1% of signups) are single-company data. Where the GA4 channel is not enough, build a referral segment for chatgpt.com, perplexity, gemini.google.com, copilot.microsoft.com and claude.ai, noting some app visits arrive as direct ([Ahrefs, 15 May 2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-chatbot-traffic/)). **[INFERENCE]** Because answers are unstable, sample each prompt repeatedly and report any share-of-voice figure with prompt count, run count and date. In Sweden, where six in ten people use AI services (section 20), this is a real channel to measure.

### What to avoid in GEO

**[INFERENCE, grounded in the policies above]** Astroturfing Reddit, seeding fake "best of" lists and manufacturing mentions are the GEO equivalent of link schemes and fall under Google's inauthentic-mention and AI-manipulation language. Self-serving "best X" lists that rank the publisher first are effective per citation data but carry elevated policy risk. **[GAP]** No engine-specific data for Claude or Copilot source selection, no confirmation of which index each assistant uses, no quantified effect for entity tactics (Organization schema, Wikidata), and no enforcement cases for AI-answer manipulation were found.

## 10. TikTok: automation-first paid, completion-first organic

### Smart+ and budget sizing

**[FAST-MOVING, official page updated Apr 2025]** TikTok's Smart+ guidance: set up Pixel and Events API, add at least six creative assets, run at least 7 days and validate with split testing. With Target CPA or Minimum ROAS, base the target on similar campaigns that ran Maximum Delivery, set **daily budget at ideally 30 times and no less than 10 times historical CPA**, avoid significant edits in the first 7 days, then adjust bids by up to 15% every 2 days; raise budget by up to 30% when a campaign regularly uses 90%+ of it ([TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/best-practices-for-smart-plus-web-campaigns)). Where vendor and official step sizes differ, prefer the official, more conservative numbers.

### The learning phase: stability from about 25 results or 7 days, 50 conversions as the passing indicator

**[FAST-MOVING; TikTok help pages read 11 Nov 2026]** TikTok's main learning-phase article says volatility "typically starts to decline after about 25 campaign results or 7 days" and advises against pausing campaigns or ad groups, making edits that retrigger learning, and setting unreasonable budgets or creative volumes ([TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/learning-phase?lang=en)). Its FAQ says "Achieving 50 conversions is the most significant indicator of passing the learning phase" and that an ad that does not pass should get broader targeting, a higher bid or better creative ([TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/learning-phase-faq?redirected=2)). For scaling, TikTok says to start once an ad group generates at least 50 conversions within a week and meets the cost goal, while a TikTok small-business blog describes learning as lasting until 20 or more conversions within 5 days ([TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/scaling-auction-ad-spend-solutions?lang=en); [TikTok blog](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/generate-small-business-leads-on-tiktok)). **[INFERENCE]** The official pages are not fully consistent; store the rule as "about 50 conversions per ad group per week is the passing indicator and scaling trigger, and TikTok's own article says stability begins around 25 results or 7 days".

### GMV Max is the only TikTok Shop campaign type

**[FAST-MOVING, Mar 2026]** Since July 2025 GMV Max is the only supported campaign type for TikTok Shop ads ([TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/gmv-max-migration-tiktok-shop-ads?lang=en)). **[VENDOR, 2026]** Its ROI is total GMV over ad spend, a revenue ratio that counts all same-day orders of promoted products including organic and affiliate orders, so it is blended shop ROI and not incremental ROAS. Compute break-even ROI from margins per product group, run 5 to 7 days of Max Delivery for new shops before switching to Target ROI, and record organic sales before launch for comparison. Product GMV Max availability is listed for the US and Southeast Asian markets; EU availability is unverified ([Pattern, 2026](https://www.pattern.com/blog/tiktok-gmv-max)).

### Spark Ads and Search Ads

**[FAST-MOVING, Jun 2026]** Spark Ads promote organic posts from the brand or creators via authorisation code, and paid engagement accrues to the original post ([TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/spark-ads)). **[FAST-MOVING, Nov 2026]** Automatic Search Placement is on by default for new campaigns; keyword Search Ads Campaigns (broad, phrase, exact) launched in the US in September 2024, with Traffic availability listed in 11 countries as of April 2026. Automated Keywords are on by default for small-business accounts without notification, and negatives do not cover plurals or misspellings ([Search Engine Journal, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/tiktok-search-ads-are-becoming-real-search-advertising/596016)). Official search optimisation: learning takes 3 to 7 days and 15 to 20 conversions, daily budget about 20 times target CPA, launch on broad and move proven terms to phrase or exact ([TikTok Ads Help Center, Jul 2026](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/search-ads-optimization)).

### Lead generation on TikTok

**[FAST-MOVING; TikTok help pages, lead-quality page updated June 2026]** For Instant Forms, choose the Lead generation objective, set Instant Form as the optimisation location at ad group level, and attach or create the form at ad level; TikTok suggests instant forms when the aim is to lower friction "and qualify them later" ([TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/set-up-lead-generation-with-instant-form?lang=en)). Documented CRM integrations include HubSpot, Salesforce, Klaviyo, Mailchimp, Constant Contact, Google Sheets, Zapier, LeadsBridge and Make, plus a Leads Center for download and management; the suite also includes Messaging Ads, Call Ads and website-form lead campaigns ([TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/available-crm-integrations-tiktok-lead-generation?lang=en); [TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/lead-generation-objective?lang=en)). Lead quality optimisation matches ads to people more likely to convert further after form submission; the practice is to post back upper- and deeper-funnel events through website, offline or CRM connection and map them to standard events, and it suits longer funnels ([TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/about-lead-quality-optimization?lang=en)). **[GAP]** Instant form build details (form types, question limits, logic, lead retention) and minimum event volumes for lead quality optimisation were not on the pages retrieved.

### Creative burns fast on TikTok

**[DURABLE principles, official page Jun 2025]** Shoot vertical 9:16 at 720p or better with sound, keep content inside safe zones, introduce the proposition in the first 3 seconds, and end with a clear call to action; use 3 to 5 creatives per ad group; refresh when delivery declines consistently ([TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)). **[VENDOR, 31 Jul 2026]** Fatigue arrives in days to two weeks and in 48 to 72 hours at high spend; suggested refresh is every 2 to 3 weeks at $50 to $200 a day, weekly at $200 to $1,000, and every 3 to 5 days above $1,000; a modular approach of 1 body, 5 hooks and 3 calls to action gives 15 variants ([Influee](https://influee.co/blog/tiktok-ad-fatigue)).

### Symphony and AI disclosure: an approval gate, not an automatic label

**[FAST-MOVING; TikTok pages updated Apr to Nov 2026]** Symphony Creative Studio is a free AI creative platform for organic and paid content covering video generation, image generation, avatar videos, translation and dubbing, a video editor and a Symphony Agent; Symphony also comprises Symphony Automation inside Ads Manager and a Symphony API ([TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/about-symphony-creative-studio); [TikTok blog](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/tiktok-symphony-ai-creative-suite)). TikTok's ad policy requires disclosure of significantly AI-modified content: fully AI-generated image, video or audio; real footage substantially altered so the main subject is shown doing something they did not do; and voice cloning of the main subject. Disclose with TikTok's label or a clear disclaimer, caption, watermark or sticker. For undisclosed content, "your ad will be rejected or restricted". Minor edits such as lighting, colour, background removal and denoising are exempt, and TikTok's advice is to label if unsure ([TikTok Advertising Policies](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-misleading-and-false-content)). Symphony output that meets disclosure requirements is auto-labelled "AI-generated"; Ads Manager has an AI-generated content disclaimer toggle for third-party-tool content; the label cannot be changed after submission; and TikTok may auto-label content carrying C2PA Content Credentials ([TikTok blog](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/symphony-creative-studio); [TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/about-ad-disclaimers-in-tiktok-ads-manager); [TikTok AI Safety Essentials PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/AI_Safety_Essentials_for_Advertisers_on_TikTok.pdf)). **[INFERENCE]** Unlike Meta's mostly automatic labelling, AI disclosure on TikTok is an approval gate: the agent should set the disclaimer toggle for any third-party AI video with synthetic people or voices. **[GAP]** No Symphony performance data was found.

### Tracking and benchmarks

**[DURABLE]** TikTok's recommended setup is Pixel plus Events API with event deduplication and all match keys ([TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/events-api)). **[VENDOR, Aug 2025 to Jul 2026, 5,900+ ecommerce brands]** Triple Whale medians are CPM $4.08, CTR 0.61%, CPA $17.07 and ROAS 1.51; low ROAS in beauty and health likely reflects sales landing in TikTok Shop outside pixel attribution ([Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/tiktok-benchmarks)).

### Organic: watch time, completion, and saying the keyword

**[FAST-MOVING, updated 14 Oct 2026]** The strongest signals are watch time, completion rate, replays and shares; TikTok has never confirmed a points system. The system reads on-screen text and spoken words, so keyword-rich captions, voiceover and overlays matter as much as or more than hashtags for search. ## 13. Creative: the largest measured driver, with shared rules across platforms

### Creative quality outweighs reach and targeting in measured sales lift

**[DURABLE in direction; CPG data]** NCSolutions' "Five Keys to Advertising Effectiveness" (2023 update of the 2017 Nielsen Catalina meta-study, nearly 450 CPG campaigns across digital and TV) attributes incremental sales **49% to creative** (49% in 2017), 21% to brand (15%), 14% to reach (22%), 11% to targeting (9%) and 5% to recency (5%) ([NCSolutions](https://info.ncsolutions.com/hubfs/2023%20Five%20Keys%20to%20Advertising%20Effectiveness/NCS_Five_Keys_to_Advertising_Effectiveness_E-Book_08-23.pdf)). **[VENDOR research on award entrants]** Kantar and WARC matched about 450 ads from Kantar's Link database with ROMI data from WARC and found the most creative and effective ads generated more than four times the profit ([Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/north-america/inspiration/advertising-media/the-art-of-proof-how-creative-quality-drives-profit)). **[INFERENCE]** The 49% figure covers short-term CPG sales lift and should not be generalised to B2B or long-term brand effects; the often-quoted Nielsen "47%" was not verified. The practical consequence matches Binet's advice in section 1: when cutting, protect the quality and longevity of fewer ads.

### Emotion and "right-brain" features predict long-term effect

**[VENDOR]** System1's Star Rating (1 to 5) is built from emotional response and is presented as predicting long-term growth; its Spike Rating predicts short-term sales over 8 to 10 weeks from branding strength and emotional intensity, with 1.33 and above "exceptional" ([System1](https://system1group.com/uncategorized/our-metrics-explained)). **[DURABLE; Orlando Wood, *Lemon*, IPA 2019]** UK ads averaged 3.9 "left-brain" features against 2.3 "right-brain" features, and in 100 UK and 100 US TV ads over 60% of ads with the fewest right-brain features scored 1 star. Right-brain features include characters, dialogue, a sense of place, humour and melodic music; left-brain features include voiceover, product focus, on-screen text and rhythmic soundtracks ([LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/are-ads-becoming-too-far-left-brain)). Across 126 radio ads, right-brain feature count correlated with Star Rating at R = +0.47 and left-brain count at R = −0.44 ([System1 and Radiocentre](https://2235762.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/2235762/Listen%20Up!%20System1%20and%20Radiocentre%20eBook.pdf)). This agrees with Binet and Field's B2B finding that emotional campaigns produced 1.4 very large business effects against 0.2 for rational ones (section 1). **[INFERENCE]** There is a tension to manage: direct-response platform guidance favours on-screen text and product focus for sound-off feeds, while brand-building evidence favours character, story and music. Use the first for activation assets and the second for brand assets, and do not ask one execution to do both jobs.

### Five rules recur across every platform source

**[DURABLE, INFERENCE drawn from the platform sections]** First, the hook window is the first 2 to 5 seconds: Meta cites 46% of Reels purchase conversions within 2 seconds ([Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/click-attribution)), TikTok asks for the proposition within 3 seconds ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)), and Google's ABCD puts the brand in the first 5 seconds ([Google Business](https://business.google.com/us/resources/articles/abcds-of-effective-video-ads/)). Second, design for sound-off while rewarding sound-on: about half of Instagram videos are watched muted ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/)) and TikTok reports 30%+ of viewers on mute ([Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/tiktok-algorithm/)), so captions and on-screen text are required, while Google says sound raises Shorts ad conversions by over 20% ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16041697)). Third, variety means distinct concepts (hook, look, persona, angle), not recolours ([Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/meta/meta-ad-creative-diversification-same-hook-same-ad/)). Fourth, people outperform logos: partnership ads on Meta, Spark Ads on TikTok, Thought Leader Ads on LinkedIn and creator assets in Demand Gen are the bridge between organic and paid. Fifth, supply native aspect ratios per placement (9:16, 4:5, 1:1, 16:9, and 2:3 for Pinterest) and keep text inside safe zones.

### Test creative in three layers

**[INFERENCE from the sources above and in section 4]** A test hierarchy consistent with the evidence is: pre-test for emotion and branding (System1 or Kantar Link style) where budget allows; then platform split tests on one variable using the platform's own tool ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1423851372208214); [Hunch](https://www.hunchads.com/blog/creative-testing-on-meta)); then holdout lift tests for incrementality (sections 3, 4 and 6). Carry the creative variant ID in `utm_content` so results join across systems. Hook-rate and hold-rate benchmarks remain vendor-only.

### AI-generated creative: adequate for product-led direct response, weaker for emotion

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** Generative AI is mainstream in production: 82% of 142 surveyed brand and agency professionals use it for creative production (Q4 2025) ([Digiday, 21 Apr 2026](https://digiday.com/marketing/digiday-research-marketing-workflows-benefit-from-ai-but-trust-is-still-a-barrier-to-adoption/)), and the WFA reports that 96% of major brands now use AI in marketing ([WFA](https://wfanet.org/ai-community)). Platform tools generate and edit assets inside ad managers (Meta Advantage+ creative, Google Asset Studio, TikTok Symphony, Pinterest Canvas), which raises the review burden described in section 4.

**[FAST-MOVING, 2025 to 2026; mixed evidence]** Four studies now compare AI and human creative, and they measure different things. Ipsos with Syracuse University's Newhouse School (published 18 May 2026; 20 ads across 10 brands, 3,000 US respondents, each pre-2021 human ad paired with a fully AI-made ad from the same brief) found viewers could barely tell them apart (13% of AI-ad viewers were at least somewhat confident it was AI-made, and 13% of human-ad viewers suspected AI), but on Ipsos's sales-validated measure **human ads over-indexed by 11 points and AI ads under-indexed by 5**; AI did best on product-led briefs and worst on storytelling and emotion ([Syracuse University](https://news.syr.edu/2026/05/18/newhouse-research-finds-ai-ads-fall-short-on-sales-impact/)). A Taboola-commissioned field study by academics (reported 29 January 2026; over 500 million impressions on Taboola's platform) found raw CTR of 0.76% for AI ads against 0.65% for human ads, but comparable results under the strictest controls; large, clear human faces were a key trust cue ([AdNews](https://www.adnews.com.au/opinion/study-ai-ads-match-human-creative)). Kantar with Affectiva-iMotions (4 November 2025; hundreds of generative-AI-assisted ads) found stronger emotional reactions but lower net positivity, with branding lower mainly where AI use was obvious; over 40% of ads with non-obvious AI use reached the top tier for branded cut-through. Kantar's advice is to train the AI on brand assets and tone, keep the AI seamless, and pre-test ([Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/inspiration/advertising-media/rethinking-ai-generated-advertising)). NIQ (December 2024) found AI-generated ads produced a negative brand halo, weaker memory activation and cognitively taxing visuals, though they reinforced existing brand associations ([NIQ](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/news-center/2024/niq-research-uncovers-hidden-consumer-attitudes-toward-ai-generated-ads/)). **[INFERENCE]** Read together: AI creative is adequate for product-led, direct-response formats and currently weaker for emotional brand building, especially when the AI use is visible or off-brand. Caveats: the Taboola study was read through trade press only, the Ipsos work is not peer reviewed and its human ads predate 2021.

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** On attitudes, the IAB's "The AI Ad Gap Widens" reports that 82% of ad executives think Gen Z and Millennial consumers feel positive about AI ads while 45% of those consumers do; 71% of them believe they have seen an AI-made ad, up from 54% in 2024; and the IAB describes disclosure as having more upside than downside ([IAB](https://www.iab.com/insights/the-ai-gap-widens/)). Against that, the IAB's disclosure framework cites an NYU study in which telling consumers an ad was AI-made cut click-through by 31.5% ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-ad-labels-cut-click-through-31-5-iab-framework-cites-nyu-study/)). **[INFERENCE]** The label penalty creates a commercial incentive to under-disclose; the legal and framework tests in sections 19 and 21 should decide labelling, not CTR.

**[GAP]** No Meta-published creative effectiveness study was read, no System1 study specifically on AI-generated ads was retrieved, and no evidence-backed benchmark exists for hook rate or hold rate.

## 14. Landing pages and conversion optimisation: speed, burden and honest test expectations

### Speed has the strongest quantified evidence

**[DURABLE; Google-commissioned study by 55 and Deloitte, data late 2019]** "Milliseconds Make Millions" tracked mobile load times hourly for 30 days across 37 European and US brand sites and over 30 million sessions. A **0.1-second improvement across four speed metrics lifted conversions 8.4% in retail and 10.1% in travel**, and retail consumer spend rose 9.2%. In the funnel, retail product-detail-to-basket rose 9.1%, luxury product-detail-to-basket rose 40.1% on a small base, and lead-generation form step one to submission rose 21.6% ([web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/milliseconds-make-millions)). **[INFERENCE]** The study is observational across sites with no redesigns during the window; it shows association, not a randomised effect. The Core Web Vitals thresholds to hit are in section 8.

### Checkout and form burden: remove fields that are not needed

**[DURABLE; Baymard, vendor research]** Average documented cart abandonment is **70.22%** across 50 studies (range 55 to 84%). Reasons given by US shoppers include extra costs too high (40%), delivery too slow (20%), not trusting the site with card details (19%), having to create an account (18%) and a checkout that is too long or complicated (17%) ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/cart-abandonment-rate)). The average checkout in 2024 had 5.1 steps and **11.3 form fields** (12.7 in 2019), while most sites need about 8; the number of fields matters more than the number of steps, and the average US checkout shows 23.48 form elements by default against an ideal of 12 to 14 ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/checkout-flow-average-form-fields)). Baymard estimates the average large ecommerce site could gain 35.26% in conversion from checkout design fixes alone, a vendor estimate ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/ecommerce-checkout-usability-report-and-benchmark)). **[INFERENCE]** The advice is "remove fields that are not needed", not "fewer is always better": the evidence concerns checkout burden, no rigorous lead-generation form study was found, and platform data shows that shorter lead forms raise volume while lowering lead quality (section 4). Swedish checkout specifics (Swish, BankID, delivery options) are in section 20, where the abandonment reasons are locally measured.

### Message match, single-purpose pages and social proof are recommended but unquantified

**[VENDOR]** Unbounce defines message match as how closely the landing page headline, copy and design match the ad or link that brought the visitor, and a landing page as a campaign-specific page with one call to action and no site navigation; it gives no effect size ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/landing-page-articles/landing-page-best-practices/); [Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/landing-page-articles/what-is-a-landing-page/)). Social proof, guarantees and risk removal are asserted as anxiety reducers in the four-forces model ([jobstobedone.org](https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/)). **[INFERENCE]** Message match also matters mechanically on Google, where landing page experience is a Quality Score component and page content drives AI Max headlines (section 6), and on Meta, where landing pages are reviewed for policy (section 4). **[GAP]** No quantified, independent evidence for message match or social proof effect sizes was found, and no CXL or Nielsen Norman Group research on forms or testimonials was retrieved.

### Most A/B tests do not win; plan for it

**[DURABLE]** Optimizely reports that only **12% of experiments win** (blog dated 23 December 2024; reported as drawn from more than 127,000 experiments), that over 90% of experiments target five metrics (call-to-action clicks, revenue, checkout, registration, add-to-cart), and that the highest-uplift programmes run more complex tests with more variations ([Optimizely](https://www.optimizely.com/insights/blog/metrics-for-your-experimentation-program/); [Metricuno](https://www.metricuno.com/experimentation-benchmarks)). At Microsoft roughly one third of ideas tested were positive and significant, one third flat and one third negative, with Bing's success rate lower ([Dmitriev, 2016](https://www.exp-platform.com/Documents/2016-12PavelUPittNR.pdf)). Other reported rates are unverified secondary figures (VWO roughly 1 in 7; 20% of 28,304 tests at CXL and Convert) ([Conversion Team](https://www.conversionteam.com/ab-test-win-rate/)). **[INFERENCE]** With base win rates of 12 to 33%, a programme reporting much higher win rates probably has weak stopping rules or metric selection. Agents should plan on most tests being flat, value them for learning, and apply the design discipline in section 3 (fixed sample, one analysis).

### Use qualitative research when traffic is thin

**[DURABLE; Jakob Nielsen, 2000]** Nielsen Norman Group recommends usability testing with no more than 5 users per round and running many small rounds, and maps 20 research methods by attitudinal versus behavioural and qualitative versus quantitative dimensions ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/which-ux-research-methods/)). Switch interviews with recent buyers (section 1) are the equivalent for messaging. **[GAP]** Typical minimum sample sizes and duration guidance beyond the calculator inputs in section 3 were not sourced, and CXL's ResearchXL process was not retrieved.

### Accessibility is a legal requirement for EU consumer purchase flows

**[DURABLE since 28 Jun 2025]** The European Accessibility Act covers B2C ecommerce purchase journeys including payment and requires conformance with EN 301 549, which incorporates WCAG 2.1 AA; the Swedish implementation and sanctions are in section 19.

## 15. Email, SMS and lifecycle: flows first, deliverability as a hard gate

### Mailbox-provider rules are now enforced by rejection

**[DURABLE since Feb 2024; Google's own pages, read Nov 2026]** All senders to personal Gmail accounts need SPF or DKIM, valid forward and reverse DNS, TLS, RFC 5322 formatting and a spam rate in Postmaster Tools below 0.3%. Senders of more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail additionally need **SPF and DKIM, DMARC (the policy may be p=none), a From domain aligned with either the SPF or the DKIM domain, and one-click unsubscribe on marketing and subscribed messages plus a clearly visible unsubscribe link in the body**. Google's wording on complaints is to keep the spam rate "below 0.10% and avoid ever reaching a spam rate of 0.30% or higher". One-click unsubscribe needs both headers, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` and `List-Unsubscribe: <https://...>` (RFC 8058); preference centres are allowed but must not replace one-click. DKIM keys must be 1024 bits or longer, with 2048 recommended ([Google Email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126?hl=en)). A bulk sender is one sending close to 5,000 or more messages to personal Gmail accounts in 24 hours, counted across the same primary domain, and a sender that meets the threshold once is permanently treated as a bulk sender. Bulk senders above 0.3% user-reported spam are ineligible for mitigation until they stay under 0.3% for 7 consecutive days. **"Starting November 2025, Gmail is ramping up its enforcement on non-compliant traffic"**, with temporary and permanent rejections. Google recommends honouring unsubscribes within 48 hours; transactional messages are excluded from the one-click requirement; and the guidelines apply only to personal Gmail accounts, not Google Workspace recipients ([Google sender guidelines FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414?hl=en)).

**[DURABLE; Yahoo's own page, read Nov 2026]** Yahoo requires SPF or DKIM at minimum for all senders and, for bulk senders, SPF and DKIM, DMARC of at least p=none that passes, a working List-Unsubscribe header with one-click, a visible unsubscribe link, unsubscribes honoured within 2 days, and a spam rate below 0.3% calculated on mail delivered to the inbox. Yahoo adds: send marketing mail from different IPs and DKIM domains than transactional mail, use confirmed opt-in, enrol every DKIM domain in the Complaint Feedback Loop, and remove invalid addresses promptly ([Yahoo Sender Hub](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/)). **[DURABLE; Microsoft, April 2025]** For domains sending more than 5,000 emails a day to outlook.com, hotmail.com and live.com, SPF and DKIM must pass and DMARC must be at least p=none aligned with SPF or DKIM; enforcement began 5 May 2025 and non-compliant mail is rejected with error 550 5.7.515 ([Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/outlook/strengthening-email-security-outlook-new-requirements-for-high-volume-senders/4399730)).

**[DURABLE; Google]** Other Google rules that shape marketing practice: use one From address per message category; do not mix promotions into transactional messages; no "Re:" or "Fwd:" subject lines unless real; display names must identify the sender only (no "Last Chance", no recipient name, no emojis imitating verification); do not hide content with HTML or CSS; do not buy lists; avoid pre-checked opt-in boxes; confirm addresses before subscribing; and consider unsubscribing people who do not open or read. Google also states that it does not track open rates and that low open rates are not necessarily a sign of deliverability problems ([Google Email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126?hl=en)). **[INFERENCE]** A safe operating rule for an agent: block any marketing send from a domain that lacks SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment or one-click headers; alert at a 0.1% Gmail spam rate and pause non-essential sends at 0.25%, because recovery from 0.3% requires 7 clean days; and build to the 2-day unsubscribe standard, which is far tighter than the 10 business days in US law. B2B senders to Workspace and corporate Outlook are not formally bound by these consumer-mailbox rules, but the same authentication is the practical baseline. **[GAP]** Apple iCloud Mail's postmaster requirements were not retrieved, and Microsoft has published no numeric complaint threshold in the source read.

### Warm-up, hygiene and sunsetting

**[DURABLE; Google]** Google's ramp guidance has no numeric schedule: start with low volume to engaged users and increase slowly; send at a consistent rate, not in bursts; "immediately doubling previously sent volumes suddenly could result in rate limiting or reputation drops"; ramp separately after changing infrastructure, headers or format; and if bounces or deferrals start, cut volume until errors fall. Gmail tracks volume per domain and per IP, and a shared IP's quota is shared by everyone on it ([Google Email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126?hl=en)). **[VENDOR]** One platform's warm-up for a new dedicated sending domain takes about 4 weeks, capped at 100 an hour and 1,000 a day in stage one and 300 an hour and 2,500 a day in stage two, sending only to opted-in, likely-to-engage contacts ([HighLevel](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000001021-email-sending-guide-email-best-practices-email-warm-up)); another advises starting at 20 to 100 emails a day and treats a bounce rate above 5% as a hygiene warning ([Mailforge, 13 Sep 2026](https://www.mailforge.ai/blog/domain-warming)). **[VENDOR; Klaviyo help centre]** Engagement-tiered sending: a daily sender should send daily only to profiles engaged or added within 30 days, three times a week to those engaged within 60 to 90 days, and weekly to those engaged within 180 days; profiles added more than 180 days ago who have never opened or clicked go to a re-engagement or sunset flow ([Klaviyo](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360037527052); [Klaviyo](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360017518492)). **[VENDOR, 17 Jul 2026]** Validity cautions that inactive segments can reach 50% of a list yet account for about 25% of programme revenue, so suppress on evidence instead of a blanket date rule; it puts global inbox placement at 87% (UK about 91%), a gap that a 99% "delivered" rate hides ([Validity](https://www.validity.com/blog/the-2026-dma-email-benchmark-report-what-the-numbers-really-mean-for-revenue/)). **[VENDOR, modified 16 Mar 2026]** BIMI (the brand logo in the inbox) needs DMARC at enforcement (p=quarantine or p=reject), an SVG Tiny PS logo and in practice a certificate: a Verified Mark Certificate requires a registered trademark and gives the Gmail blue checkmark, while a Common Mark Certificate needs no trademark but the logo must have been publicly displayed for at least 12 months ([Red Sift](https://redsift.com/blog/bimi-in-2026-verified-logos-cmcs-and-the-fastest-path-to-inbox-display)).

### Opens are no longer a trustworthy metric

**[VENDOR, Sept 2026]** Litmus attributes 76.56% of tracked opens to Apple, 16.10% to Gmail, 3.48% to Outlook desktop and 2.35% to Yahoo, and says opens affected by Apple Mail Privacy Protection "are not considered reliable opens" ([Litmus](https://www.litmus.com/email-client-market-share)). MailerLite notes that Mail Privacy Protection auto-records opens, inflating open rate and understating click-to-open rate, and that click rate is the most reliable engagement indicator ([MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/blog/compare-your-email-performance-metrics-industry-benchmarks)). Zeta Global is reported as measuring 40% unique opens against an 11% "true open" rate, and Gmail open rates dropped in Q1 2026 after image-fetching changes while clicks held steady ([Validity](https://www.validity.com/blog/the-2026-dma-email-benchmark-report-what-the-numbers-really-mean-for-revenue/)). **[INFERENCE]** Define "engaged" by clicks, purchases, site activity or replies; do not set open-rate targets except as a deliverability smoke alarm.

### Gmail's AI inbox changes what gets seen

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026; secondary]** In January 2026 Gmail added AI Overviews (thread summaries and natural-language inbox questions), an experimental AI Inbox (suggested to-dos, "topics to catch up on", VIP sender identification, deprioritising low-value mail) and deadline detection. At that date inbox questions were limited to US English web users on paid AI plans and AI Inbox to US trusted testers, and **Smart Features are off by default in the EEA, UK, Switzerland and Japan** ([emailexpert, 3 Feb 2026](https://emailexpert.com/gmail-launches-ai-inbox/)). The Promotions tab sorts by relevance instead of date, and Google has not published ranking criteria ([MarTech, 4 Mar 2026](https://martech.org/how-gemini-in-gmail-changes-inbox-visibility-for-marketers/)). Practitioner advice is to write so the message survives summarisation: lead with what it is, what changed and what is required; format dates, amounts and deadlines consistently; use precise subject lines stating offer and deadline; and track clicks and replies. **[GAP]** No primary Google documentation explains how AI Inbox or Promotions ranking chooses messages; all tactical advice is practitioner inference.

### Automated flows carry disproportionate revenue

**[VENDOR, 2026, 183,000+ customers]** Klaviyo reports that flows are 5.3% of sends but generate nearly **41% of email revenue**, with flow revenue per recipient nearly 18 times that of campaigns and placed-order rate about 13 times; about 48% of flow revenue comes from new buyers against 16% for campaigns. Averages: campaign open rate 31% (top decile 45.1%) and click rate 1.69% (top decile 3.38%); flow open rate 32.2% and click rate 5.58% (top decile 10.48%). Placed-order rate by industry for campaigns and flows respectively: clothing and accessories 0.97% and 1.62%; electronics 1.23% and 2.01%; food and beverage 1.16% and 1.79%; health and beauty 1.02% and 1.65%; home and garden 1.34% and 2.17% ([Klaviyo](https://klaviyo.com/products/email-marketing/benchmarks)). **[VENDOR, calendar 2025, 150,000 brands]** Omnisend independently reports automations at 2% of email sends and **30% of email-driven revenue**, at $2.87 per automated send against $0.18 per campaign send (16 times); abandoned cart and welcome produced 76% of automation-generated orders; back-in-stock had the highest conversion of any automation at 6.46%; and birthday messages had an average order value of $744.37. Its "$79 return per $1" figure is its own internal analysis ([Omnisend](https://www.omnisend.com/2026-ecommerce-marketing-report)). **[VENDOR, 2026, 110,000+ customers]** Klaviyo's flow revenue per recipient by type, average and top decile: abandoned cart $6.77 and $13.70; welcome series $5.75 and $13.27; browse abandonment $2.08 and $4.32; post-purchase $1.80 and $2.77; win-back $0.82 and $1.60 ([Klaviyo](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/abandoned-cart-benchmarks)). **[CONTESTED]** A secondary playbook citing Klaviyo gives lower figures (abandoned cart $3.56, browse abandonment $0.92) that appear to be per-entry-email or a different edition; treat the Klaviyo-hosted figures as primary ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/klaviyo-lifecycle-email-flows-ecommerce-2026-playbook)).

**[INFERENCE]** Build order for an ecommerce programme, from the revenue-per-recipient data: welcome; abandoned checkout and cart; browse abandonment; post-purchase (cross-sell, review request, replenishment); back-in-stock and price-drop; win-back; sunset. Post-purchase and win-back have low revenue per recipient but protect retention and list health. Build and optimise flows before raising campaign volume.

### Campaign benchmarks vary by vendor; pick one and track clicks

**[VENDOR, Dec 2024 to Nov 2025, 3.6M campaigns, medians]** MailerLite's all-industry medians are open rate 43.46%, click rate 2.09%, click-to-open 6.81% and unsubscribe 0.22% (up from 0.08% in 2024, which it links to Gmail making unsubscribing easier). By industry: ecommerce 32.67% open and 1.07% click; software and web apps 39.31% and 1.15%; non-profit 52.38% and 2.90%; media 42.97% and 4.10%; consulting 45.96% and 2.41% ([MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/blog/compare-your-email-performance-metrics-industry-benchmarks)). Omnisend reports open rates rising from 26.6% in 2024 to 30.7% in 2025 and click-to-conversion rising from 5.9% to 9% ([Omnisend](https://www.omnisend.com/2026-ecommerce-marketing-report)). The DMA UK 2026 benchmark, via Validity, gives click rates of 1.0% for retail and 1.2% for travel and a B2B delivered rate up from 90.7% to 95.6% ([Validity](https://www.validity.com/blog/the-2026-dma-email-benchmark-report-what-the-numbers-really-mean-for-revenue/)). **[FAST-MOVING, 2026; secondary report of the GDMA benchmark]** The one credible Swedish benchmark: **Sweden's unique open rate is 42.27% against a global 34.15%, its click rate 3.0% against 2.99%, and its click-to-open 8.03% against 7.73%** ([Spotler](https://spotler.com/blog/what-sweden-can-teach-the-rest-of-europe-about-email-marketing)). **[INFERENCE]** Working "healthy" ranges for ecommerce campaigns in 2026 are click rate of about 1.1 to 1.7% (3.4% is top decile), unsubscribe of about 0.2% and flow click rate of about 5.6%. Year-on-year unsubscribe comparisons need care because one-click headers and Gmail's subscription manager raised rates mechanically. **[GAP]** No sourced subject-line length or send-time studies, no evidence on replenishment timing or RFM model performance, and no Mailchimp or Litmus 2026 benchmarks were retrieved.

### B2B email, nurture and cold outreach

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026 survey]** B2B evidence points to owned media gaining budget as AI search erodes web traffic: 40% of B2B marketers are spending more on owned media and it is the top 2027 investment priority at 42% ([Content Marketing Institute](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/articles/b2b-content-marketing-trends-research)). MailerLite's consulting, software and media medians above serve as B2B newsletter proxies. **[INFERENCE]** For nurture, the thought-leadership evidence in section 16 supports writing for non-obvious stakeholders (finance, legal, IT, procurement) and measuring by account-level engagement instead of individual lead score. **[VENDOR practitioner guidance, 2026]** Cold-outreach tooling advice is to send from a separate domain aged 2 to 4 weeks, with 2 to 5 mailboxes per domain, 20 to 40 cold emails per mailbox per day, and each mailbox warmed for 2 to 4 weeks, keeping Postmaster spam rate below 0.1% ([Inboxlogy](https://www.inboxlogy.com/blog/cold-email-deliverability-rules-2026)). **[DURABLE; Google]** Google's primary rules cut against this tooling: do not buy email addresses, do not send to people who did not sign up, and do not impersonate threaded replies ([Google Email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126?hl=en)). **[INFERENCE]** Lookalike domains and mailbox rotation are deliverability workarounds, not compliance. An agent should treat cold outreach as a human-approved activity with jurisdiction checks: the US is opt-out under CAN-SPAM, Denmark requires consent even for B2B, and Sweden, Norway and Finland allow opt-out only to role-relevant or generic business addresses (section 19). **[GAP]** No rigorous 2026 source on lead-nurture sequence performance was found.

### US email law

**[DURABLE, US law]** CAN-SPAM requires truthful headers and subject lines, identification as an ad, a postal address, and opt-outs honoured within 10 business days; it applies to B2B email and liability cannot be contracted away ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)). EU, Nordic and Swedish email and SMS consent rules are in section 19.

### SMS: written consent, registration, quiet hours

**[FAST-MOVING, US; law-firm and vendor summaries]** The FCC's one-to-one consent rule was vacated by the Eleventh Circuit in January 2025 and removed from the regulations on 14 July 2025. In McLaughlin Chiropractic v. McKesson (US Supreme Court, 20 June 2025) the court held that district courts are not bound by FCC interpretations of the TCPA, which opens long-standing positions on consent and revocation to challenge and is expected to increase litigation ([Pierce Atwood, 21 Jul 2025](https://www.pierceatwood.com/alerts/fcc-deletes-tcpa-regulations-following-supreme-court-and-eleventh-circuit-rulings)). Prior express written consent remains the standard for marketing texts; opt-outs must be honoured through any reasonable means within about 10 business days; one confirmation text after opt-out is allowed; the "revoke-all" requirement was delayed to 31 January 2027; and statutory damages are $500 to $1,500 per violation, uncapped ([ComplianceHub, 2026](https://compliancehub.wiki/tcpa-2026-consent-revocation-one-to-one-rule-vacated-compliance/)). **[VENDOR summary, verify before relying]** Opt-in must be affirmative, identify the sender, state that consent is not a condition of purchase and include "message and data rates may apply"; quiet hours are 8am to 9pm recipient local time; application-to-person traffic on 10-digit long codes needs brand and campaign registration with The Campaign Registry, and carriers have blocked unregistered traffic since February 2025; restricted categories (sex, hate, alcohol, firearms, tobacco) need age-gating or are barred; and several states are stricter, for example Florida's limit of 3 messages per 24 hours and Texas's extension of its telemarketing law to SMS with a private right of action. The same vendor guide wrongly states that a one-to-one consent rule took effect in January 2026, which the law-firm sources contradict ([Infobip](https://cdn-web.infobip.com/uploads/markdown-exports/www.infobip.com/blog/tcpa-compliance-sms.md)). **[INFERENCE]** Agent guardrails for SMS: never send a marketing text without a stored, dated written-consent record for that brand; enforce recipient-local quiet hours; treat any natural-language opt-out as revocation; and cap frequency, with Florida's 3 per 24 hours as a sensible national floor.

**[VENDOR benchmarks]** Klaviyo's 2026 SMS averages are click rate 5.6% for campaigns and 9.65% for flows, and placed-order rate 0.27% for campaigns and 1.9% for flows; SMS flow revenue per recipient is $4.48 for abandoned cart, $3.33 for welcome and $1.04 for browse abandonment ([Klaviyo SMS benchmarks](https://www.klaviyo.com/products/sms-marketing/benchmarks); [Klaviyo](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/abandoned-cart-benchmarks)). The widely quoted "98% SMS open rate" traces to a 2010 figure its publisher no longer stands behind ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/sms-marketing-statistics-2026-open-ctr-data)). Postscript's phone-capture popup conversion is 3.6% at the median and 8.6% at the 90th percentile ([Postscript](https://postscript.io/sms-benchmarks)). Omnisend reports SMS volume up 40% year on year, automated SMS at $0.74 per send against $0.15 for campaigns, and automated push notifications with nearly 10 times the conversion rate of campaign push ([Omnisend](https://www.omnisend.com/2026-ecommerce-marketing-report)).

### WhatsApp and RCS

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** For US numbers WhatsApp is a service and utility channel, not a marketing channel, while Meta's pause on marketing templates to +1 numbers continues; elsewhere it remains a marketing channel subject to opt-in, per-user caps and the 2026 pricing in section 4 ([Wati, 31 Aug 2026](https://www.wati.io/en/blog/whatsapp-marketing-messages-us-2026/)). In Sweden 51% of people use WhatsApp and 66% use Messenger (section 20). **[VENDOR, Aug 2026]** RCS for Business reached iPhones with iOS 18.1 in October 2024 on select carriers and is carrier-dependent; senders must be verified to get a branded name, logo and checkmark; business RCS is encrypted in transit but not end-to-end; and SMS fallback is handled by the messaging platform. Performance and reach claims for RCS are vendor-only ([Sinch](https://sinch.com/blog/apple-support-rcs/); [Infobip](https://cdn-web.infobip.com/uploads/markdown-exports/www.infobip.com/blog/how-rcs-works.md)).

### Loyalty and referral: demand a holdout

**[VENDOR-SPONSORED, Feb 2026]** Antavo's Global Customer Loyalty Report 2026 says nearly 9 in 10 programme owners who measure performance report positive ROI, 7 in 10 consumers join mainly to save money, and 91% of programme owners struggle with data analysis ([Bloomreach summary](https://www.bloomreach.com/en/blog/antavo-loyalty-report-2026)). **[INFERENCE]** "Positive ROI among those who measure" is a survivorship statistic, and member-versus-non-member comparisons are confounded because better customers join. The honest test is a holdout that withholds enrolment offers or rewards from a random group, tracking incremental margin and not member revenue. This fits the Double Jeopardy evidence in section 1 that loyalty has a ceiling set by brand size. **[GAP]** No independent 2025 to 2026 evidence on loyalty programme incrementality and no reliable referral programme statistics were found.

## 16. Content marketing: effort, originality and owned distribution

### AI made content faster, not better

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026 surveys]** The Content Marketing Institute and MarketingProfs survey (fielded 23 June to 11 August 2026; 861 B2B marketers analysed, mostly North America) found 60% rate their marketing effective. The top drivers of effectiveness are content relevance and quality (47%), team skills (43%), sales alignment (26%) and channel selection (26%); AI-powered applications were named by only 14%. 65% faced more pressure to produce content faster, with reported effects of burnout (35%), less original thinking (32%) and more generic content (29%). 88% of organisations use AI content tools, and marketers credit AI with speed (78%), efficiency (59%) and volume (44%) but **quality only 17%**; the main AI challenges are accuracy (54%), quality control (54%) and brand voice (52%). 75% worry AI is making audiences more sceptical of online content, 52% have no policy on disclosing AI use and 37% have no guidelines on when AI should not be used ([Content Marketing Institute](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/articles/b2b-content-marketing-trends-research)). **[FAST-MOVING, 8 Sep 2026]** Orbit Media's 13th annual survey of 1,042 content marketers found only **13.9% report "strong results", an all-time low** against a historical range of 20 to 30%; the average post is 1,312 words and takes 3 hours 20 minutes, both down from their peaks; and 92 to 93% use AI, with users and non-users both reporting strong results at 14% ([Orbit Media](https://www.orbitmedia.com/blog/blogging-statistics/)).

### What still correlates with results

**[Survey correlations, Sept 2026]** Against a 14% benchmark for strong results, Orbit Media finds collaborating with influencers and experts is the strongest predictor at 2.6 times the benchmark, although adoption fell from 25% in 2017 to 7%; original research raises the likelihood by about 50%; teams with a formal human editor are nearly 2 times the benchmark, and AI-only editing has the lowest rate; marketers using 0 to 1 strategies report 4% strong results against 39% for 6 or more; 4 or more strategies plus 6 or more hours per post gives 31%; and 2 to 3 visuals per post is the best-performing range. Of the 39% who watch volume metrics most, only 11% report strong results; the author's analysis is that traffic to informational-intent articles has dropped sharply and will not recover, while conversion rates rise ([Orbit Media](https://www.orbitmedia.com/blog/blogging-statistics/)). **[DURABLE in direction; survey published 26 Jun 2025]** The Edelman and LinkedIn B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report (nearly 2,000 professionals) found that among "hidden buyers", the internal influencers outside the visible buying committee, 63% spend more than an hour a week on thought leadership, 64% trust it more than product sheets, 71% say it beats traditional marketing material at showing a vendor's value, 79% are more likely to advocate for a vendor that consistently produces high-quality thought leadership, and 53% say strong content can outweigh brand recognition ([Edelman](https://www.edelman.com/insights/hidden-buyer-b2b); [Edelman report page](https://www.edelman.com/expertise/business-marketing/2025-b2b-thought-leadership-report)). The "more than 40% of B2B deals stall" figure often attached to that report comes from a 2022 book, not the survey. Trust builders named by B2B marketers are useful practical information (64%), demonstrated industry expertise (59%) and customer stories (50%) ([Content Marketing Institute](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/articles/b2b-content-marketing-trends-research)).

### Process and measurement an agent can run

**[INFERENCE; the researchers' synthesis, not a single source]** A process consistent with the evidence: pick a small number of pillars tied to buyer problems and commercial intent instead of informational keywords; anchor each pillar with one original-research or expert-collaboration asset per quarter; derive cluster pieces, newsletter issues, social cuts, a webinar and sales enablement from that asset; require a named human editor for everything; distribute through owned channels (email list, community, events) because search referral is shrinking; and refresh existing pieces instead of adding thin posts. The measurement stack implied by the data is pipeline or revenue influenced, conversion rate of content-sourced visits, email subscriber growth and engagement, branded search and direct traffic, share of AI answers and citations, and retention and referral rate, with sessions and rankings treated as diagnostics only. For an AI agent producing content, the three top-reported failure modes (accuracy, quality control and brand voice) are exactly what review gates should check, and Google's requirement to fact-check all AI-generated content (section 8) applies. **[GAP]** Pillar-and-cluster and editorial-calendar methods are standard practice but no rigorous 2026 evidence testing them was found, and no B2C content research was retrieved.

### Events, webinars, community and digital PR

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** 82% of B2B marketers allocated budget to content-led experiences in 2026 (78% in 2025), and 31% gave them a large or majority share (17% in 2025); in-person events (82%) and virtual events (70%) are the most used formats, and events are the second 2027 investment priority at 35%, behind owned media at 42% and ahead of paid media and AI tools at 30% each ([Content Marketing Institute](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/articles/b2b-content-marketing-trends-research)). **[VENDOR, 2025 platform data]** ON24 reports a **60% average registrant-to-attendee conversion**, 239 average attendees per webinar, average viewing time of about 50 minutes, and on-demand viewing at 43 to 50% of attendance; the page gives inconsistent figures for the last two ([ON24](https://www.on24.com/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-webinar-benchmarks-report/)). **[INFERENCE]** Webinars and events suit considered B2B purchases where several stakeholders need education and sales can follow up on engagement signals; community suits products with frequent use and peer-to-peer value; digital PR suits brands with real data or expertise to earn coverage and, now, AI-answer citations. Measure webinars by registrant-to-attendee rate, engaged minutes, call-to-action and meeting conversions and influenced pipeline; community by active-member rate, member-to-member reply share and retention of members against matched non-members; and digital PR by quality of referring publications, branded search lift, share of voice in AI answers and assisted conversions, not raw link counts. **[GAP]** No 2026 data on community-led growth, digital PR outcomes or event ROI from a credible independent source was retrieved.

## 17. Creator marketing: buy content and credibility, verify with lift tests

### Budgets are rising and moving to smaller creators

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026; mostly vendor and secondary]** eMarketer expects US influencer marketing spending to grow 15.7% in 2026 and reach $13.7 billion by 2027 ([EMARKETER](https://www.emarketer.com/content/faq-on-influencer-marketing--what-how-brands-use)); a secondary source puts 2026 US spend at $12.17 billion and says marketers put a median 26% of social budgets into creators, up from 18% in 2024 ([Gigapay, 6 Jul 2026](https://www.gigapay.com/blog/the-state-of-us-influencer-marketing-spend-in-2026)). The IAB's broader measure of US creator ad spend including paid amplification is about $37 billion in 2025 and about $44 billion forecast for 2026 ([FirstPromoter roundup, Oct 2026](https://firstpromoter.com/blog/influencer-marketing-statistics)). CreatorIQ reports that 71% of organisations increased creator investment year on year and that nearly two-thirds of new spend was reallocated from other paid and digital channels ([CreatorIQ](https://www.creatoriq.com/white-papers/state-of-creator-marketing-trends-2026)). **[VENDOR survey, 4 May 2026, 600+ marketers]** Influencer Marketing Hub finds 87.49% expect budgets to rise and 66.3% run programmes in-house. Marketers are expanding nano (51.43% expanding, 10.00% contracting), micro (52.83%, 7.55%) and UGC creators (50.00%, none contracting) far more than macro creators (20.59% expanding, 20.58% contracting). The top reported problems are rising creator costs (35.4%) and fake or bot followers (56.5% of fraud issues); measurement leans on promo codes (45.9%), affiliate links (26.0%) and native shop features (25.0%), and the top KPI is brand awareness (55.1%). Formats rated most effective are long-form video (83%), short-form video (80%) and live shopping (62%) ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-marketing-benchmark-report/?Sid=1)).

### Rates, usage rights and whitelisting

**[VENDOR, Jul 2026, methodology undisclosed]** Later's rate ranges per post run $100 to $500 for nano creators (1K to 10K followers), $500 to $2,500 for micro, $2,500 to $10,000+ for macro and $10,000 to $50,000+ for mega, with usage rights and whitelisting each adding 30 to 50% ([Later](https://later.com/blog/influencer-pricing-benchmarks-the-complete-2026-guide/)). **[VENDOR compilation, 1,527 paid deliverables, Jan 2025 to Jun 2026, skewed to sports, wellness and food]** Actual median cash fees in OpenSponsorship data are far lower: nano (under 10K) $134, micro (10K to 50K) $150, mid (50K to 250K) $417, macro (250K to 1M) $517 and mega (1M+) $2,727 on only 53 deliverables. Marketplace averages on Collabstr in 2025 were $193 paid on Instagram, $186 on TikTok and $255 on YouTube, with UGC content averaging $180. Usage-rights uplift on the base fee is quoted at 20 to 30% for 3 months, 25 to 40% for 6 months, 30 to 50% for 12 months and 100 to 150% for perpetual use; whitelisting or Spark Ads access typically costs 5 to 20% of the ad spend behind the post; and exclusivity costs 20 to 100% of base depending on length and category ([inBeat](https://inbeat.agency/blog/influencer-marketing-benchmark-report)). **[CONTESTED]** The two rate sources differ by several times at every tier above nano; asking rates in guides exceed paid medians in transaction data, and all rate data is thin, vendor-sourced and US-skewed. **[INFERENCE]** Follower count alone is a weak price and performance signal: the median macro fee is only about 3.9 times the nano fee in the transaction data.

### Selection, contracts and the brand's duties

**[INFERENCE from the data above]** A selection checklist: audience fit and geography first; engagement quality (comment authenticity, follower-growth anomalies), because fake followers are the top reported issue; past sponsored-content performance; brand-safety history; and disclosure track record. Treat UGC creators, who are paid for content and not audience, as a creative-production line for paid social, and influencers as distribution plus credibility. Contract essentials implied by the pricing norms: deliverables and approval rounds; usage rights by channel and term (organic versus paid); whitelisting or partnership-ad access and duration; exclusivity category and window; mandatory disclosure wording per market; claim restrictions and substantiation; a clause on AI likeness and synthetic edits; takedown and morality terms; and reporting access to first-party analytics. **[DURABLE, regulator]** Brands must train and monitor endorsers, and using an agency does not remove responsibility ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)). Disclosure wording by market (Sweden, EU, UK, US) is in section 19; the short version is that "#samarbete" fails in Sweden, "Sponsored" fails in the UK, and platform paid-partnership labels alone are insufficient everywhere.

### Measure creators with lift tests, not promo codes

**[VENDOR, self-reported]** CreatorIQ reports that 86% of paid-media leaders claim at least 2 times ROI from creator marketing ([FirstPromoter roundup](https://firstpromoter.com/blog/influencer-marketing-statistics)). **[INFERENCE]** Self-reported ROI multiples and promo-code attribution overstate impact. The stronger evidence is the lift-test and mix-model evidence for partnership ads in section 4. The sound approach is to run creator content as partnership ads, Spark Ads or Thought Leader Ads inside a platform lift test, or to run geo holdouts for organic creator pushes, and to calibrate code and affiliate numbers against those results. **[GAP]** No rigorous 2026 source on B2B creator programmes, no independent fraud-detection benchmark, and no published study isolating the incrementality of organic influencer posts were found.

## 18. Brand safety and suitability: set your own policy and curate supply

### No shared standard has replaced GARM

**[DURABLE structural point; Aug 2024]** X sued the World Federation of Advertisers and members over the Global Alliance for Responsible Media in August 2024, and the federation disbanded it days later. Forrester's advice at the time was to set brand-specific standards bottom-up instead of relying on industry floors or vendor defaults, keep humans in the decisions, actively buy known-good inventory instead of only blocking, and not coordinate content standards with competitors ([Forrester, 15 Aug 2024](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/the-curtain-falls-on-garm-revealing-industry-wide-brand-safety-issues)). **[FAST-MOVING; policy issued 18 Oct 2025, grace period to 18 Apr 2026]** The Media Rating Council now says verification services that only classify at property or URL level using text and keywords may not call themselves "brand safety" (they may use "suitability" with disclosures); content-level analysis of images, video and audio is needed for the term. Questions to ask a vendor: does it analyse images, video and audio; how often are classifications human-reviewed; and can it see nested iframes and in-app content ([PPC Land, 24 Oct 2025](https://ppc.land/mrc-restricts-property-level-ad-verification-from-brand-safety-claims/)).

### The bigger measurable waste is low-quality supply

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** In the ANA's Programmatic Transparency Benchmark for Q1 2026, only 43.3% of programmatic spend reached qualified impressions; made-for-advertising inventory rose to 1.1% of impressions from 0.4 to 0.6% through 2025; higher performers converted 54.0% of spend into qualified impressions against 32.1% for lower performers; and the ANA concluded that "quality, not cost, is the primary differentiator" ([ANA, 27 May 2026](https://www.ana.net/content/show/id/pr-2026-05-programmatic)). In Q2 2026 the index was 45.1%, non-viewable spend fell from 13.3% to 10.1%, transaction costs were 27.2% of spend, and **higher performers used about 19,000 fewer domains and apps than lower performers** ([Fiducia summary of ANA Q2 2026](https://www.fiducia.eco/post/ana-programmatic-transparency-benchmark-q2-2026-working-media-efficiency-reaches-a-record-45)). "AI slop", mass-produced low-value AI content made to earn ad revenue, takes 1.3 to 2.4% of open-web programmatic spend, comparable to made-for-advertising exposure, and can look good on viewability, invalid-traffic and CPM metrics; recommended controls are content-quality checks, supply-path curation and backing trusted publishers ([TAG, Q1 2026](https://www.tagtoday.net/insights/ai-slop-industry-briefing-q1-2026)).

### What an agent can operationalise

**[INFERENCE]** Current practice reduces to five controls: a written, brand-specific suitability policy with risk tiers by content category; inclusion lists and curated private marketplaces for open-web buys plus exclusion lists for made-for-advertising and AI-slop domains; platform inventory filters at the most conservative tier appropriate, with placement, content, topic and keyword exclusions (for X, the adjacency controls in section 12; for Google, rebuilding exclusions when Display migrates to Demand Gen, section 6); third-party verification with confirmation that classification is content-level for video and social; and a quarterly log-level audit of where spend ran. Decisions about acceptable content should be made unilaterally per brand and documented; an agent must not benchmark or coordinate exclusion lists with competitors. Over-blocking news and using long keyword lists reduces reach on quality publishers; the lever the ANA data rewards is fewer, better domains. **[GAP]** Platform-specific control documentation (Meta inventory filter, YouTube suitability tiers, TikTok Inventory Filter) and verification-vendor product specifics were not retrieved from primary pages, and 2025 to 2026 antitrust actions touching brand-safety coordination were not sourced and remain an open legal risk to check.

## 19. Compliance: EU, Nordic, Swedish, UK and US rules agents must gate on

### EU cookie consent is unchanged in force through at least 2027

**[FAST-MOVING, status 11 Nov 2026]** Nothing from the Digital Omnibus cookie reform is law. The Commission proposed it in November 2025 with a cookie element under which a user sets a preference once and a site may not re-ask for the same consent for six months after a refusal; the EDPB and EDPS opposed narrowing the personal-data definition in February 2026; and Parliament received up to 1,840 amendments, with a committee vote targeted "before February 2027" ([EU Perspectives, 10 Aug 2026](https://euperspectives.eu/2026/08/after-1840-amendemnts-the-eu-fight-over-data-will-begin/)). The Council agreed its negotiating position on 21 October 2026, keeping the existing GDPR definition of personal data and limiting cookie banners by listing cases where consent is not required; trilogues can start only once Parliament adopts its position ([IAPP, 22 Oct 2026](https://iapp.org/news/a/eu-member-states-finalize-digital-omnibus-negotiating-stance); [Heuking, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/cookie-consent-under-the-digital-omnibus-planned-changes-and-technical-implementation.html)). Headlines that were not fetched indicate Council drafts cut the re-ask wait to four months and dropped a browser-level consent signal. **[INFERENCE]** Plan on current consent rules under ePrivacy Article 5(3) and GDPR through at least 2027.

### Sweden: cookies need prior active consent, and regulators check banners technically

**[DURABLE, Sweden; PTS page read on primary]** The Swedish Post and Telecom Authority (PTS), which supervises the cookie rule in the Electronic Communications Act (2022:482), states that a site must ask for consent before using cookies and that consent must be voluntary, specific, informed and actively given. Only necessary cookies are exempt, and statistics cookies that a site owner merely considers necessary do not qualify. **It must be as easy to say no as yes.** Pre-ticked choices, continued browsing, cookie walls and an "I understand" notice are not consent, and withdrawal must be available at any time ([PTS](https://www.pts.se/internet-och-telefoni/kakor-cookies/)). **[DURABLE, Sweden; secondary]** The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY) polices the GDPR side. In April 2025 it criticised cookie banners at ATG (accept highlighted, reject hidden behind a second step), Aller Media (pre-ticked non-essential categories, multi-step reject, legitimate interest claimed without explanation) and Warner Music (no clear purposes, recipients or storage period, and a false statement that declining impairs the site). No fines were issued. IMY's reported position is that accept and reject must take equal effort and have equal prominence, that the banner must state purposes, recipients and retention, that a consent-platform vendor's default template does not relieve the site owner of responsibility, and that it verifies whether cookies fire after rejection ([Cookie Information, 2 May 2025](https://cookieinformation.com/blog/blog-swedish-dpa-imy-dark-patterns-april-2025/); [Consentmo](https://www.consentmo.com/blog-posts/sweden-warns-major-companies-over-cookie-banner-failures-avoid-these-common-gdpr-mistakes)). **[INFERENCE]** The Swedish banner standard in practice is "reject all" on the first layer with equal prominence, nothing pre-ticked, purposes, recipients and retention stated, and no analytics before consent. **[GAP]** The statute section for the cookie rule was not verified, and IMY's April 2025 decisions were not read on primary.

### Sweden: the Meta Pixel has already produced fines

**[DURABLE, Sweden; IMY news read on primary]** On 3 July 2025 IMY fined Apoteket **SEK 37 million** and Apohem SEK 8 million for using the Meta Pixel in a way that transferred sensitive personal data to Meta; earlier Meta Pixel decisions exist for Avanza, Länsförsäkringar, Apotea and Kry. No new IMY cookie or pixel decision was found for 2026, and IMY's stated 2026 priorities are AI in the public sector, children and young people, and crime prevention. On 10 November 2026 IMY published guidance on location-based direct communication to mobile phones ([IMY](https://www.imy.se/en/news/); [IMY decision, Avanza](https://www.imy.se/globalassets/dokument/beslut/2024/beslut-tillsyn-avanza.pdf); [IMY](https://www.imy.se/nyheter/ny-rapport-imy-vagleder-om-platsbaserad-direktkommunikation-till-mobiltelefoner/)). **[INFERENCE]** The pixel cases turned on what data the pixel sent (including advanced matching and automatic events) combined with a sensitive context such as pharmacy, health or finance. For any Swedish advertiser the lesson is to load the pixel only after consent, audit which parameters and form fields it captures, and disable automatic advanced matching unless deliberately configured; for health and finance this aligns with Meta's own data-source restrictions in section 4.

### Email and SMS in the EU: opt-in, with one existing-customer exception

**[DURABLE; standard reading, plus secondary report of a 2025 ruling]** Email and SMS marketing to individuals is opt-in across the EU under ePrivacy Article 13, with one exception: existing customers may be sent marketing for the sender's own similar products if an opt-out was offered when the address was collected and is offered in every message. The Court of Justice in case C-654/23 (Inteligo Media, reported 9 December 2025) held that email addresses obtained through free account registration can fall within the "in the context of a sale" exception where the free service promotes the paid offer; that a newsletter with informational content is still direct marketing if it has a commercial purpose; and that where the exception applies, the sender need not separately satisfy GDPR lawfulness conditions ([Schjødt](https://schjodt.com/news/marketing-emails-and-consent-key-takeaways-from-recent-court-ruling)). **[INFERENCE]** GDPR still governs the personal data behind the send: named work emails are personal data, legitimate interest is the usual basis for B2B and soft-opt-in sends, and the right to object to direct marketing is absolute, so an objection must end processing for marketing. **[GAP]** The ePrivacy and GDPR articles and the judgment text were not re-read on primary sources in this pass.

### Sweden: Marketing Act sections 19 to 21 for email, SMS, phone and post

**[DURABLE, Sweden; statute read on primary]** Under the Marketing Act (Marknadsföringslagen 2008:486), section 19 allows a trader to use electronic mail, fax or automated calling systems for marketing to a natural person **only if that person has consented in advance**. Consent is not required (the soft opt-in) if the trader obtained the electronic address in connection with the sale of a product to the person and three conditions hold: the person has not objected to marketing use of the address; the marketing concerns the trader's own similar products; and the person is clearly given a free and simple way to object when the address is collected and in every later marketing message. Section 20 requires every marketing email to contain a valid address to which the recipient can send a request to stop, **and this applies to marketing to legal persons too**. Section 21 makes other individual distance communication, such as live telephone calls and addressed mail, opt-out: it is allowed unless the person has clearly objected ([Riksdagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/marknadsforingslag-2008486_sfs-2008-486/); [lagen.nu](https://lagen.nu/2008:486)). **[INFERENCE]** SMS is treated as electronic mail under section 19 in standard practice, although the statute text read does not spell out SMS. Because section 19 protects only natural persons, marketing email to a company's generic address is not caught by the consent rule, but the section 20 opt-out address still applies and GDPR applies to named work addresses.

### B2B email rules differ across the Nordics

**[DURABLE in outline; law-firm table dated January 2024]** Sweden: B2C opt-in with soft opt-in available; B2B opt-out if the email relates to the recipient's professional role, otherwise opt-in. **Denmark: opt-in for both B2C and B2B**, with the soft opt-in as the only exception. Norway: B2C opt-in; B2B individualised addresses opt-in, non-individualised addresses such as post@ opt-out. Finland: B2C opt-in; B2B individualised addresses opt-in unless the person's role is essentially related to the product, in which case opt-out; generic addresses opt-out. All four are described as requiring a completed transaction for the soft opt-in, so an enquiry or quote request alone does not qualify ([Fieldfisher, Jan 2024](https://res.cloudinary.com/fieldfisher/image/upload/v1706627634/PDFs/EU_e-marketing_requirements_-_January_2024_lqpl6s.pdf)). **[INFERENCE]** That "transaction required" reading predates case C-654/23; treat free-sign-up soft opt-in as unsettled at national level. A single Nordic B2B cold-email programme is not possible on one rule set: Denmark needs consent; in Sweden, Norway and Finland use role-relevant content and prefer generic company addresses; always include an unsubscribe address. **[GAP]** The Danish, Norwegian and Finnish statutes were not verified on primary sources, and Nordic telemarketing block lists outside Sweden were not researched.

### Sweden: advertising must be recognisable, "samarbete" is not enough, and several parties are liable

**[DURABLE, Sweden; statute read on primary]** Marketing Act section 9 requires all marketing to be designed and presented so that it is clear that it is marketing ([Riksdagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/marknadsforingslag-2008486_sfs-2008-486/)). State it at the very start, mark affiliate links, and do not rely on an end-of-post disclosure ([Företagarna, 2018](https://www.foretagarna.se/driva-eget-foretag/marknadsforing/vad-galler-vid-annonsmarkning-av-samarbeten-pa-youtube-instagram-och-bloggar)). **[FAST-MOVING, 2026; secondary]** Konsumentverket's report 2026:3 on influencer marketing records about 100 targeted supervision cases in recent years and 52 cases from a spring 2025 sweep on platforms used by young people, finds industry knowledge of marketing law "varying and in many cases deficient", and notes that previously supervised actors re-offend. The case-law principles as reported are that the average consumer must recognise advertising on a fleeting encounter; **"i samarbete med" and "#samarbete" are not enough**; remuneration includes products, trips and other benefits; and an influencer promoting their own company's products can be the primary responsible party. The Consumer Ombudsman can attach conditional fines of up to SEK 1,500,000 per violation ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/swedens-influencer-ad-crackdown-fines-court-rulings-and-eu-law-coming/); [Legal 500 / Rouse, 27 Feb 2026](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/sweden/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/influencer-marketing-in-sweden-challenges-and-regulatory-practice)). **[DURABLE, Sweden; industry self-regulation dated April 2025]** The joint recommendation of Sveriges Kommunikationsbyråer, Sveriges Annonsörer and IAB Sverige, hosted by the advertising ombudsman, recommends the labels **"Reklam från X", "Marknadsföring från X" or "Betalt samarbete med X"**; advises against "I samarbete med"; calls very short labels like "AD" risky; says to put the label first in the text and not hidden behind "more", and in video to show it at the advertising section or throughout; says platform paid-partnership labels may not be sufficient on their own, so an own label should be added; and notes that affiliate links, own-business posts and employees' posts for an employer can all trigger the duty ([Rekommendation för reklammarkering](https://reklamombudsmannen.org/media/1amjljh3/rekommendation-f%C3%B6r-reklammarkering-i-some-komm-sva-iab.pdf)). **[GAP]** Konsumentverket's own social-media guidance page and court case numbers were not retrieved, and rules on marketing to children were not verified on primary text.

### Sweden: sanctions, misleading claims and reviews

**[DURABLE, Sweden; statute read on primary]** Marketing Act section 10 bans incorrect claims and misleading presentations, expressly including a product's effect on health and the environment, and treats omitting or obscuring material information as misleading. Section 12 c requires a trader giving access to consumer reviews to state whether and how it ensures reviews come from consumers who used or bought the product. Under sections 29 to 31 the market disruption fee for intentional or negligent breaches runs from SEK 10,000 up to **4% of the trader's turnover** (up to the equivalent of EUR 2 million where turnover cannot be established), and it also applies to anyone who materially contributed to the breach, which reaches agencies and influencers ([lagen.nu](https://lagen.nu/2008:486)).

### Sweden: sale claims must show the lowest price of the prior 30 days

**[DURABLE, Sweden; statute read on primary]** The Price Information Act (2004:347) requires price information when specific products are offered, including unit price for goods and prices including VAT. Under section 7 a, when a product is offered with a statement that the price has been reduced, the earlier price must also be given, and **the earlier price must be the lowest price the trader applied in the 30 days before the reduction**; for gradual reductions it is the price before the first reduction ([lagen.nu](https://lagen.nu/2004:347)). **[FAST-MOVING, 15 Oct 2026]** A government proposal would limit section 7 a to goods instead of all products from 1 March 2027, keeping the 30-day rule ([Regeringen](https://www.regeringen.se/contentassets/c1ca1ae2860342019326591ba33dc84f/lrr-andring-i-reglerna-om-information-vid-prissankningar-webb.pdf)). **[INFERENCE]** For any "rea" or Black Week claim, show the lowest price of the previous 30 days as the reference price, for goods and services alike until the amendment takes effect. Singles Day discounts on 11 November lower the reference price for Black Week two weeks later.

### Sweden: telemarketing needs written confirmation after the call

**[DURABLE, Sweden; Konsumentverket page read on primary]** When a company calls a consumer or prompted the call, the consumer must confirm the agreement in writing after the call (signed paper, email or SMS approval, or BankID); the company may not collect it during the call and must be able to prove it, and without it the agreement is invalid. Callers must state the sales purpose, a 14-day withdrawal right applies, and consumers can block sales calls through NIX-Telefon ([Konsumentverket](https://konsumentverket.se/en/articles/rules-for-telemarketing)). **[FAST-MOVING, 9 Jul 2026]** A government inquiry is considering a prior-consent requirement, a ban or a strengthened blocking register, with its report due 15 January 2027; no law change yet ([Regeringen](https://www.regeringen.se/contentassets/4d7e166bffd143f781a4edeab6aa38ae/ett-starkt-konsumentskydd-vid-telefonforsaljning-hr202601525/)).

### Green claims: generic environmental claims have been restricted since 27 September 2026

**[FAST-MOVING; law-firm summary, Aug 2026]** Directive (EU) 2024/825 has applied in full since 27 September 2026. It restricts sustainability labels that are not based on a certification scheme or set up by a public authority, generic environmental claims such as "green", "environmentally friendly" or "sustainable" without recognised excellent environmental performance, and claims about a whole product when only one aspect is concerned ([Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/08/european-union-final-call-the-eu-empco-directive)). **[FAST-MOVING, Sweden]** Swedish amendments to the Marketing Act were proposed in SOU 2025:124 and are reported as expected to enter into force on 1 January 2027; Konsumentverket's position is that the directive is fully harmonising and that ongoing marketing falls under the new rules; and business groups report no clear transition rule for products already on shelves ([Konsumentverket remissvar, 24 Mar 2026](https://regeringen.se/contentassets/13ef70e74acc4c0d8f4f064aae104d52/konsumentverket.pdf); [Företagarna, Jul 2026](https://www.foretagarna.se/nyheter/riks/2026/juli/otydliga-regler-kan-leda-till-kasserade-produkter/)). Until then environmental claims fall under section 10 of the Marketing Act. **[INFERENCE]** Plan as if the directive's bans already apply: an agent should block generic claims such as "miljövänlig" and self-made sustainability labels and route any environmental claim to substantiation review. **[GAP]** Formal adoption of the Swedish implementing law and the status of the separate Green Claims Directive were not confirmed.

### Sweden: sector rules for alcohol, gambling, credit and tobacco

**[DURABLE, Sweden; statutes read on primary]** Alcohol marketing requires particular moderation and **may not target or depict children or young people under 25**; commercial alcohol ads are barred from TV, on-demand TV and radio, and video-sharing platform providers may not place them before, during or after user-generated videos; images are limited to the product, its ingredients, packaging or trademark ([lagen.nu, Alkohollag](https://lagen.nu/2010:1622)). Gambling marketing to consumers requires moderation, may not target under-18s, may not be sent as direct marketing to self-excluded players, and must state the minimum age and contact details for a gambling-problem support organisation ([lagen.nu, Spellag](https://lagen.nu/2018:1138)). **[FAST-MOVING, Sweden]** A new Consumer Credit Act (2026:1011) **enters into force on 20 November 2026**. Credit marketing must observe moderation; may not create false expectations about credit availability or cost, state that debts or payment defaults matter little, or imply that credit replaces savings, improves finances or raises living standards; must show the effective interest rate with a representative example at least as prominently as other rates; must include a notice that credit carries costs, the risks of indebtedness and where to get budget and debt advice; and must tell consumers when a personalised credit offer is based wholly on automated processing. Certain cost-free invoice credits repaid within 50 days are exempt from parts of the chapter ([lagen.nu, Konsumentkreditlag 2026:1011](https://lagen.nu/2026:1011)). **[INFERENCE]** A merchant promoting "betala senare" in ads published on or after 20 November 2026 should check whether its provider's product is an exempt invoice credit and otherwise include the risk notice. **[DURABLE; secondary]** Tobacco and e-cigarette advertising is prohibited on digital platforms ([Legal 500 / Rouse](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/sweden/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/influencer-marketing-in-sweden-challenges-and-regulatory-practice)).

### Meta's less-personalised option weakens EU targeting by an unknown amount

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** After a EUR 200M Digital Markets Act fine in April 2025, Meta committed on 8 December 2025 to give EU users a choice between fully personalised ads and "less personalised ads" using less data, presented from January 2026, with the Commission gathering evidence on "impact and uptake" ([European Commission](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/meta-commits-give-eu-users-choice-personalised-ads-under-digital-markets-act-2025-12-08_en); [The Current](https://www.thecurrent.com/marketing/data-privacy-meta-users-option-less-personalized-ads-eu)). Meta's compliance report says the option "uses 90% less data than personalized ads", that revised choice flows rolled out to all users in January 2026, and that **a non-dismissible reminder is scheduled for January 2027** for users who clicked "OK" without choosing; the ad-free subscription costs EUR 5.99 a month on web and EUR 7.99 on mobile ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/metas-2026-dma-report-reveals-whatsapp-ads-a-eu200m-fine-and-a-defiant-stance-on-personalized-advertising/)). Total reach is unchanged but targeting is coarser for those users ([adlibrary.com, 20 Sep 2026](https://adlibrary.com/posts/meta-ads-eu-2026)). A weak secondary report says Meta's ad sales slowed after the model was introduced, without quantifying it ([Renascence, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.renascence.io/news/129219/meta-ad-sales-slow-as-eu-users-opt-for-less-personalised-ads)). **[GAP]** No uptake or performance figure has been published by Meta, the Commission or any measurement firm; plan tests instead of assuming parity with earlier results, and expect the January 2027 reminder to shift the mix again.

### Digital Services Act advertising rules

**[DURABLE, since 17 Feb 2024]** Platforms must show that an ad is an ad, on whose behalf it runs and its main targeting parameters; no ads based on profiling with special-category data, and no profiling-based ads to users the platform is reasonably certain are minors ([Arthur Cox](https://www.arthurcox.com/knowledge/eu-digital-services-act-new-digital-advertising-landscape/)).

### AI Act Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026; law-firm summaries]** Article 50 obligations have applied since 2 August 2026: users must be told when they are dealing with a chatbot unless it is obvious; synthetic audio, image, video and text must be machine-readably marked by providers; and deployers must disclose deepfakes, meaning AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video resembling real persons, places or events. AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest must be labelled unless it has undergone human review and someone holds editorial responsibility ([Goodwin, Aug 2026](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force); [Sidley, 24 Jun 2026](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/24/eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-preparing-for-compliance-by-2-august-2026/)). **The Commission published the final Code of Practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content on 10 June 2026**; it is voluntary and open for signature, and it asks providers for machine-readable marking in at least two layers where necessary plus detection tools, and deployers for clear deepfake labels, a common EU icon or equivalent label, and audio disclaimers where visual labels are impossible ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/european-commission-publishes-final-code-of-practice-on-marking-and-labelling-aigenerated-content)). The Commission adopted its transparency guidelines on 20 July 2026. The AI Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 in force 27 July 2026, gave providers of generative systems already on the market before 2 August 2026 until 2 December 2026 to meet the marking obligation; deployer duties were not delayed ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/)). **Penalties for transparency breaches run up to EUR 15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher** ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force)). The WFA published an Article 50 labelling guide for marketers on 17 September 2026 ([WFA](https://wfanet.org/ai-community)). **[INFERENCE]** For a marketing agent: AI-generated realistic images, video or audio of real people, places or events in ads need a visible AI label now; ordinary AI-assisted copy that a human reviews and takes responsibility for does not need a label under the text rule; and chatbots must identify themselves. **[GAP]** Whether the Commission has formally assessed the Code as adequate was not verified, and the Article 50 text, guidelines and WFA guide were not read in the original.

### Political and issue advertising is closed on Meta and Google in the EU

**[DURABLE since Oct 2025]** The EU political advertising regulation has applied since 10 October 2025 and covers messages designed to influence an election, referendum or "a legislative or regulatory process", which reaches issue-based content; fines run up to 6% of annual turnover. Google stopped serving political ads in the EU and Meta ended political, electoral and social-issue ads there in early October 2025 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/extensive-regulation-kills-digital-political-advertising-in-eu/); [Open Terms Archive](https://opentermsarchive.org/en/publications/political-advertising-after-the-ttpa-exit-adaptation-and-the-future-of-platform-governance/)); Meta's help centre confirms such ads cannot be created or edited in the EU ([Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/298000447747885)), and TikTok does not permit political ads at all (section 10). **[INFERENCE]** Cause-led or sustainability messaging risks being classified as a social issue and rejected with no authorisation route; route such campaigns to legal review.

### Accessibility for B2C purchase flows

**[DURABLE since 28 Jun 2025]** The European Accessibility Act applies to B2C ecommerce services, with micro-enterprises exempt for services; the harmonised standard EN 301 549 incorporates WCAG 2.1 AA, the purchase journey including payment must be accessible, and an accessibility statement is required ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/09/european-accessibility-act-requirements-for-e-commerce-services)). **[DURABLE, Sweden; trade-body guide, March 2025]** Sweden implemented it as Act (2023:254). The Svensk Handel guide describes micro-enterprises as under EUR 2 million turnover or fewer than 10 employees, flags checkout including third-party payment widgets as a risk area, says new content management system content must be accessible and that email and chat must work with screen readers, and notes that PTS supervises with a **sanction fee of SEK 10,000 to SEK 10,000,000** ([Svensk Handel](https://www.svenskhandel.se/api/documents/tillganglighetslagen-vagledning.pdf)). **[INFERENCE]** The Act does not name marketing emails or social posts, but alt text, captions and contrast on site content and screen-reader-compatible email are the safe default.

### The Digital Fairness Act has not yet been proposed

**[FAST-MOVING, status Nov 2026]** The Commission's 2026 work programme lists a Digital Fairness Act legislative initiative for Q4 2026, with expected scope covering dark patterns, influencer marketing, addictive design, unfair personalisation and subscription cancellation; the consultation closed on 24 October 2025 ([European Parliament Legislative Train](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-protecting-our-democracy-upholding-our-values/file-digital-fairness-act)). Ideas under consideration for influencers include clearer labelling duties and advertiser responsibility for influencer compliance ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/swedens-influencer-ad-crackdown-fines-court-rulings-and-eu-law-coming/)). The Commission's Influencer Legal Hub states that existing consumer law already requires disclosure of advertising, including gifted-product content and affiliate marketing, and national sweeps show high non-compliance ([European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/consumers/consumer-rights-and-complaints/influencer-legal-hub_en)). **[GAP]** Whether the proposal will land in Q4 2026 is unknown.

### UK: "#Ad" at the start; "Sponsored" and "Gifted" are not enough

**[FAST-MOVING; law-firm summary dated 23 Oct 2026]** Revised ASA, CAP and CMA influencer guidance is grounded in the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, under which the CMA can impose fines directly. Acceptable labels are #Ad, #Advert, #Advertising, #Advertisement and #AdvertisementFeature; "Sponsored", "Spon" and "Sp" are insufficient and the firm advises avoiding "Gifted"; platform tools are not always enough; gifts, trips and commission all count as payment; brands, agencies and creators are each responsible; and AI-generated content is subject to the same rules ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/10/23/new-influencer-guidance-from-the-asa-cap-and-cma-what-has-changed-and-what-to-d-102o6ar)). **[GAP]** The revised guide and the Act's penalty levels were not read from primary documents.

### US: endorsements, reviews, California and New York

**[DURABLE]** The FTC requires clear disclosure of any material connection (financial, employment, personal or family, including free or discounted products) placed with the endorsement, not in a bio, after "more" or buried in hashtags; in video it must be said and shown in the video itself and repeated during live streams; "#ad" or "Sponsored by" work, while "#partner", "#ambassador", "sp", "spon", "collab" or "Gifted" alone do not; and platform tools alone are insufficient ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking); [FTC, Disclosures 101](https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers/)). Note the cross-market difference: the FTC accepts "sponsored", the UK does not, and Sweden wants "reklam" or "betalt samarbete". The FTC reviews rule, effective 21 October 2024, bans fake reviews and testimonials, incentives conditioned on sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews and buying fake social influence indicators; AI avatars cannot convey fake testimonials ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)). **[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** California regulations effective 1 January 2026 require businesses to show whether an opt-out preference signal was processed ([BakerHostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/california-privacy-in-2026-regulations-enforcement-ai-and-more/)). **[FAST-MOVING, 2026; trade summary, verify against primary texts]** New York's synthetic-performer disclosure law has been in force since 9 June 2026 ($1,000 for a first violation, $5,000 for subsequent ones), and California's AI Transparency Act has been in force since 2 August 2026 and binds large AI providers, not advertisers ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-ad-labels-cut-click-through-31-5-iab-framework-cites-nyu-study/)). US email and SMS rules are in section 15.

## 20. Sweden: market data, benchmarks, seasonal calendar and copy norms

**[FAST-MOVING; Internetstiftelsen, Svenskarna och internet 2026, published 9 Jun 2026, page updated 28 Sep 2026]** Share of Swedes using each platform: **YouTube 77%, Facebook 69%, Instagram 66%, Snapchat 38%, LinkedIn 27%, TikTok 22%, Pinterest 18%, Reddit 16%, X 13%, Threads 7% and Bluesky 2%**. Messaging: Messenger 66%, WhatsApp 51%, Discord 16%, Signal 8% and Telegram 4%. Among internet users aged 8 and over, 94% used some social medium in the past year, 90% weekly and 83% daily. ### The Swedish ad market: SEK 52 billion of media advertising, 78% of it online

**[FAST-MOVING; IRM annual statistics 2025]** Total Swedish advertising and marketing investment was **SEK 92.3 billion in 2025** (+2.8%). Media advertising was SEK 52.2 billion (+3.3%): internet SEK 40.5 billion (+6.4%), TV SEK 3.7 billion (−9.9%), out-of-home SEK 2.2 billion, printed newspapers SEK 1.9 billion (−7.7%), direct mail SEK 1.9 billion (−5.5%), radio SEK 1.05 billion and cinema SEK 0.13 billion. Sponsorship was SEK 10.0 billion (+7.2%) and events SEK 3.3 billion (+10.1%) ([IRM](https://www.irm-media.se/om-oss/irms-aarsstatistik/)). ### Ecommerce, payment and delivery: Swish, BankID and three delivery options

**[FAST-MOVING; PostNord E-barometern Q2 2026 via nShift, 24 Sep 2026]** Swedish ecommerce grew 8% in Q2 2026 against Q2 2025, with all eight categories growing: pharmacy +13%, groceries +10%, consumer electronics +10%, building supplies +9%, sports and leisure +8%, clothing and shoes +5%, furniture and home +4%, and books and media +2%. For the most recent delivery, service points fell from 53% in Q2 2020 to 47%, home delivery from 35% to 30%, and **parcel lockers rose from 3% to 19%**. One in three shoppers abandoned a purchase because the preferred delivery option was missing, and nearly four in ten abandoned one in the past year over payment problems or uncertainty (37% unsure the payment was secure, 36% lacking their preferred payment method, 31% doubting the shop was genuine). **61% say BankID or Freja verification makes checkout feel safe**, and 57% say the same of recognisable payment options. One in three ecommerce parcels came from abroad, and secondhand is at least 12% of parcels ([nShift](https://nshift.com/blog/swedish-ecommerce-trends-postnord-e-barometern-q2-2026)). **[FAST-MOVING; PostNord, E-commerce in the Nordics, Spring 2026]** 88% of Swedes shopped online in the last 30 days (Norway 86%, Denmark 85%, Finland 84%). **Swish is the most used and most preferred payment method in Sweden**, ahead of card and invoice; in Norway it is Vipps; in Denmark card is most used and MobilePay most preferred; in Finland online bank payment leads. Marketplaces Swedes shop from are Amazon (33%), Temu (27%) and Zalando (25%). Only 14% of Swedes will pay extra for greener delivery but 52% will wait longer, 38% bought second-hand in the last month, and 68% say a clear return policy matters. Sweden is the first or second foreign origin for Danish, Finnish and Norwegian shoppers ([PostNord](https://www.postnord.com/siteassets/PDF/PN_E-commerce_Spring_2026.pdf)). **[INFERENCE]** Checkout basics for Sweden are Swish plus card plus an invoice or part-payment option, BankID verification, visible recognisable payment logos, a clear returns page, and all three delivery options (service point, locker, home), since stated preference (home) and actual use (service point) differ. **[GAP]** No Klarna-specific share and no central-bank payment shares were retrieved.

### The Swedish seasonal calendar

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026 dates; vendor calendar consistent with standard date rules]** Alla hjärtans dag is 14 February; the winter school holiday (sportlov) falls in weeks 7 to 10 depending on region; Easter 2026 ran from Skärtorsdag on 2 April through Annandag påsk on 6 April; **Mors dag is the last Sunday in May** (31 May 2026); Nationaldagen is 6 June; **Midsommarafton was 19 June 2026**; school starts in August, varying by municipality; **Fars dag is the second Sunday in November** (8 November 2026); Singles Day is 11 November; **Black Week 2026 runs 23 to 29 November with Black Friday on 27 November** and Cyber Monday on 30 November; Julafton is 24 December; and post-Christmas sales start from 25 December ([Abicart](https://www.abicart.com/blog/key-dates-to-keep-track-of-in-2026-a-complete-guide-for-e-commer)). **[FAST-MOVING, 6 Nov 2026]** HUI Research forecasts about **SEK 104 billion of retail sales in December 2026**, 6% above December 2025 in fixed prices and a record if met; Black Week is when Christmas shopping starts for many, and toys make over a fifth of annual sales in December ([Realtid/TT](https://www.realtid.se/tt-nyhet/sankta-skatten-satter-fart-pa-julhandeln-spas-na-104-miljarder/)). For reference, Svensk Handel forecast Black Week 2024 sales at SEK 9.7 billion with about six in ten Swedes planning to shop ([Aktiespararna, 21 Nov 2024](https://www.aktiespararna.se/nyheter/black-week-spas-ga-starkare-i-ar-enligt-svensk-handel)). **[INFERENCE]** Sweden celebrates on the eve: Julafton and Midsommarafton are the main days, so delivery cut-offs and campaign ends should be set a day earlier than in UK or US calendars, and Mors dag and Fars dag differ from UK and US dates. ### Copy norms for Swedish and Nordic advertising

**[Practitioner guidance from a Nordic translation agency, undated; not evidence]** All Nordic languages address the reader with the informal second person singular; modesty is valued and self-praise can read as bragging, so "You will love this" becomes roughly "We think you are going to like this"; English politeness fillers (please, thank you, sorry, unfortunately) sound unnatural and should be removed; titles are rarely used; and for Sweden the advice is to write from the company's perspective with "vi". The same source characterises Norwegians as getting straight to the point and Finns as frank ([Sandberg Translation Partners](https://stptrans.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/How-to-adjust-tone-of-voice-for-the-Nordic-market.pdf)). **[INFERENCE]** A default Swedish copy style is "du" address, plain factual benefit statements, no superlatives that would need proof under Marketing Act section 10 anyway ("bäst", "billigast", "miljövänlig"), few exclamation marks, and concrete delivery, returns and payment information near the call to action. Do not reuse Swedish copy unchanged in Norway, Denmark or Finland; each needs its own language version and local payment and delivery signals. **[GAP]** No academic or industry-body source on Swedish advertising tone, humour, use of English in ads or gender-portrayal norms was retrieved, and no credible source compares channel effectiveness for Swedish consumers.

## 21. Guardrails for the agent team: staged autonomy, limits outside the model, human sign-off

### Adoption is wide but shallow, and governance lags

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026]** 59% of B2B marketers use or experiment with AI agents (28% a year earlier), but only 13% call agents core to strategy and only 26% use them for campaign execution; the more common uses are content management (69%), customer intelligence (57%) and performance optimisation (55%) ([Content Marketing Institute](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/articles/b2b-content-marketing-trends-research)). In an earlier Digiday survey 54% of professionals said their companies do not use agentic AI in workflows, citing trust (errors propagate downstream), governance of data and permissions, and integration complexity; the documented pattern is to build trust through smaller tasks first ([Digiday, 21 Apr 2026](https://digiday.com/marketing/digiday-research-marketing-workflows-benefit-from-ai-but-trust-is-still-a-barrier-to-adoption/)). Only 30% of CMOs report mature AI readiness ([Gartner](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-11-gartner-2026-cmo-spend-survey-finds-cmos-allocate-15-point-3-percent-of-marketing-budgets-to-ai-but-only-30-percent-are-ready-to-scale-ai-capabilities)). Ownership of agentic workflows is unsettled, and the IAB Tech Lab formed a governance council in April 2026 for AI media buying ([Digiday, 20 May 2026](https://digiday.com/marketing/who-owns-agentic-workflows-agencies-struggle-to-govern-new-tools-as-marketing-budgets-surge/)). **[Older prediction, relayed in sponsored content]** Gartner predicted in June 2025 that more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027 owing to unclear strategy, misapplied use cases and weak governance ([MarTech, sponsored](https://martech.org/gartner-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-fail-making-humans-indispensable/)). **[Secondary]** McKinsey is reported as saying agentic AI could automate up to two-thirds of current marketing tasks for firms that redesign end-to-end workflows, with guardrails of brand-integrity safeguards, legal compliance checks, audit trails and clear accountability ([Diginomica summary, 3 Jun 2026](https://www.pulse.bot/ai/news/re-inventing-marketing-with-agentic-ai-the-mckinsey-method-b55301c6-6320-4dc3-b654-ef4a2910d8e1/)).

### Published guidance converges on the same controls

**[FAST-MOVING, Mar 2026; consultancy]** Alvarez & Marsal's "The Agent-First Marketing Function" has agents execute while humans intervene at predefined approval gates instead of at every decision, with a governance lead setting sign-off limits for high-stakes campaigns. Its five controls are **audit trails, tiered human-in-the-loop oversight, budget safeguards, red-teaming and reasoning transparency**; humans own brand voice and ethical boundaries; and "Autonomy without governance scales mistakes just as efficiently as it scales success" ([Alvarez & Marsal](https://www.alvarezandmarsal.com/sites/default/files/2026-03/The%20Agent-First%20Marketing%20Function_0.pdf)). **[Regulator summary via vendor, Jun 2026]** FINRA's 2026 oversight report names AI agents as a risk because they can act autonomously, exceed intended authority, mishandle sensitive data and create auditability gaps, and recommends prompt and output logging and human approval for high-risk, customer-facing or regulated claims ([Luthor AI, citing FINRA](https://www.luthor.ai/resources/marketing-compliance-ai)). **[Legal commentary, Nov/Dec 2026]** AI marketing claims and AI-generated customer statements remain subject to ordinary advertising law and need substantiation; human oversight must be "meaningful, not ceremonial", meaning the reviewer has context, authority to reject and an escalation path; and documentation is the evidence of reasonable care ([Florida Bar Journal](https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/ai-governance-is-becoming-a-business-law-imperative-in-floridas-emerging-technology-economy-why-florida-businesses-should-move-from-ai-experimentation-to-accountable-ai-oversight/)). **[Older, Sept 2024]** A WFA survey found 80% of multinational brands worried about how agencies use generative AI on their behalf, and only 44% had marketing-specific generative AI policies ([WFA](https://wfanet.org/knowledge/item/2024/09/17/eighty-percent-of-brands-have-concerns-about-agency-use-of-genai)).

### Prompt injection has no complete fix, so controls must sit outside the model

**[DURABLE]** OWASP's prompt-injection guidance says that because of how generative models work "it is unclear if there are fool-proof methods of prevention"; mitigations include input and output filtering and requiring human approval for high-risk actions. Indirect injection arrives through content the model reads, such as web pages, emails and documents ([OWASP](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/)). **[FAST-MOVING, Aug 2026; secondary]** OWASP's 2026 Top 10 for LLM applications keeps prompt injection first, widens it to cross-modal payloads and poisoned memory or retrieval stores, and for excessive agency says irreversible actions should sit behind human approval that shows the raw operation, not the model's summary ([Cycode](https://cycode.com/blog/owasp-top-10-llm-applications/)). **[Practitioner, 2026]** Meta's "Rule of Two" is that an agent should hold at most two of three capabilities at once (access to private data, exposure to untrusted content, and ability to communicate externally) and that a task needing all three requires human approval per action; permission checks belong outside the model because agents "might not follow policies even if you add it to the system prompt" ([Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/llm-security)). **[INFERENCE]** For a marketing agent, untrusted content includes web pages, emails, reviews, comments, creator briefs, scraped competitor copy and llms.txt files (section 9).

### Ad-platform MCP servers differ sharply in what an agent may do

**[FAST-MOVING; Google developer docs updated 30 Oct 2026]** Google's official Ads MCP server is **"strictly read-only"**: it "cannot modify bids, pause campaigns, or create new assets". It exposes three tools (`list_accessible_customers`, `search` for query-language reports, and `get_resource_metadata`), authenticates by OAuth 2.0 or a service account, and Google's docs warn that deploying it to Cloud Run with unauthenticated access and no OAuth client exposes the service account's credentials ([Google Ads API docs](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/developer-toolkit/mcp-server)). A community note adds that the Google Ads API has a single OAuth scope covering read and write, so the read-only limit is enforced by the server, not by the credential ([Reddit r/mcp](https://www.reddit.com/r/mcp/comments/1wlix2u/google_ads_has_no_readonly_permission_so_my_mcp/)). **[FAST-MOVING, 16 Jul 2026; secondary]** Meta opened its ads MCP server to any developer with a Meta app. **Write tools create, edit and delete campaigns, ad sets and ads**, custom audiences, catalogues, A/B tests and conversion lift studies; read tools cover spend, impressions, CTR and ROAS with breakdowns plus signal-health diagnostics. Agencies acting for other businesses need app review and advanced access. The report mentions no confirmation step or safeguard for write actions ([PPC Land, 18 Jul 2026](https://ppc.land/meta-opens-ads-mcp-to-any-app-cutting-integration-code-to-zero/)). **[CONTESTED]** A trade article of 27 August 2026 describes Meta's server as a test with undefined write scope; prefer the July report but confirm on Meta's developer site ([ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/ad-platform-mcp-rollout/)). **[FAST-MOVING; TikTok help page, announced May 2026]** The TikTok for Business MCP Server exposes campaign management, performance reporting, audience configuration and creative operations, and TikTok says agents can run workflows from campaign creation to real-time bidding adjustments "without human intervention at every step" ([TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/about-tiktok-for-business-mcp-server?lang=en); [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/tiktok-launches-mcp-server-to-let-ai-agents-run-campaigns/)). Amazon Ads put its own server into closed beta in November 2025 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-opens-ads-mcp-to-any-app-cutting-integration-code-to-zero/)). **[GAP]** Meta's and TikTok's documentation on rate limits, dry-run modes, spend caps and audit logs for write actions was not retrieved, so absence of a safeguard in the trade report is not proof none exists; LinkedIn, Microsoft, Pinterest, Snap and Reddit agent access was not researched. Pinterest's developer terms require a human to choose each Pin that publishes (section 12).

### When to label AI-generated advertising

**[FAST-MOVING, 2026; voluntary framework, secondary summary of version 2]** The IAB's AI Transparency and Disclosure Framework (first released 15 January 2026, version 2 on 18 August 2026) is risk-based, not blanket: disclose when AI "materially affects authenticity, identity or representation in ways that could mislead". It uses a materiality test with three criteria (deception potential, material impact, expectation alignment) and two layers: a consumer-facing label near the creative (the sparkle character or the text "AI-generated"; "AI-powered" for conversational agents) and machine-readable metadata such as C2PA. Triggers include realistic AI-generated images or video from prompts and synthetic voices or digital twins of real people in fabricated events; routine post-production, stylised imagery, generic voiceovers and text such as headlines and product descriptions do not trigger it. Organisations should name an AI Disclosure Lead ([MarTech, 15 Jan 2026](https://martech.org/iab-launches-ai-transparency-and-disclosure-framework/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-ad-labels-cut-click-through-31-5-iab-framework-cites-nyu-study/)). **[INFERENCE]** Apply the strictest applicable rule: EU Article 50 (section 19) overrides the voluntary framework where stricter; TikTok rejects undisclosed significantly AI-modified ads (section 10); Meta labels mostly automatically (section 4); and Google Ads has an AI label setting for regulated regions (section 7). AI-generated text copy does not trigger disclosure under the framework, but accuracy and substantiation duties are unchanged.

### A guardrail design for a manager agent directing worker agents

**[INFERENCE; the researchers' synthesis from the sources above, not documented practice]** Seven controls follow from the evidence.

Action tiers. Worker agents read, analyse and draft freely. Anything that spends money, publishes externally, messages customers, changes audiences or data uses, signs a creator, touches a regulated category or deletes requires approval from a named human, who is shown the raw operation and the diff, not a summary. Autonomy is promoted per task type as trust is earned, starting with smaller tasks.

Hard limits outside the model. Budget caps per day and per change, allow-listed accounts and least-privilege credentials are enforced in the tool layer, never by a system-prompt instruction alone. This matters most on Meta and TikTok, where the platform supplies no documented approval step, and on Google Ads if a third-party write-capable server is used, since the OAuth scope is read-write. Default change sizes should follow the platform evidence: about 10 to 15% per change with one change at a time on Google (section 6), no significant edits during Meta's learning phase (section 4), and up to 15% bid changes every 2 days on TikTok (section 10).

Untrusted-input handling. Treat everything fetched from outside as data, apply the Rule of Two, and allow no auto-sending or auto-publishing in a session that has read untrusted content.

Content gates before publish. Check brand voice against a maintained guide; check every statistic and every comparative, health, financial or environmental claim against a claims register with a source on file; trigger legal review for regulated categories, pricing claims (the Swedish 30-day rule), endorsements, competitor mentions and real-person likeness; check disclosure wording for the market (Sweden "Reklam från X", UK "#Ad", US "#ad" or "sponsored"); check AI labelling; and review every Meta ad's generative toggles.

Channel compliance gates. For email, confirm authentication and one-click unsubscribe headers and a complaint rate under threshold; for SMS, a consent record, quiet hours and opt-out handling; for EU sends, a consent or soft-opt-in basis per country; for tracking, consent mode signals and a consent-gated pixel.

Evaluation. Use sample-based human grading of outputs for accuracy, voice and compliance (the three failure modes marketers report most); track override and rejection rates; red-team with injected instructions; and log prompts, tool calls, approvals and published versions.

Accountability. Assign one named owner per agent workflow and maintain an incident and rollback procedure. No synthetic testimonials, reviews or inflated engagement under any circumstances, and no coordination of brand-safety exclusion lists with competitors.

The AI Act gives human editorial sign-off legal value, since reviewed public-interest text needs no AI label ([Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/)). A single pre-flight gate per asset should check consent basis, disclosure wording for the market, claims substantiation, special ad category, platform AI toggles, minors and special-category targeting, and political or issue classification.

**[GAP]** No primary McKinsey, Forrester or ANA document on agentic marketing governance was read directly, no named-company case study with measured outcomes of a guardrail programme was found, and no source addresses evaluation methods specific to marketing output such as brand-voice scoring.

## Conclusion

The second pass changes the knowledge base in three ways that matter more than the added volume. First, it shows how quickly a confident entry becomes wrong: within weeks, Meta's "solely responsible" AI terms went from a dated fact to a paused proposal, the existing-customer budget cap disappeared from Advantage+, Display became a channel inside Demand Gen, and a core update began the day before this document was finished. Entries labelled fast-moving need review dates and a re-verification routine against primary pages, and the index should prefer the platform's own help page over any vendor paraphrase wherever one exists. Second, primary sources repeatedly turned out softer than the folklore built on them. Meta publishes no "20% rule" and no "good" Event Match Quality score; Google says Ad Strength and Quality Score are not auction inputs; TikTok's own learning-phase pages give two different numbers. The thresholds agents will be most tempted to apply mechanically belong in the graph as heuristics linked to their source and their dissent, with the official wording stored beside them.

Third, the constraint on good measurement has moved. Lift tests on Meta and Google now cost about $5,000, so the gate is conversion volume and discipline, not access; an agent team that schedules one lift study per major channel per year and uses the results to correct platform numbers will make better budget decisions than one with more sophisticated attribution. For Swedish work the base is now usable for planning (audience, payment, delivery, calendar, consent and disclosure law) but still has no local cost benchmarks, so the first deliverable for any Swedish account should be its own baseline. The honest gaps that remain are LinkedIn's full documentation, X's current ad product, uptake of Meta's less-personalised ads, independent evidence on loyalty and community programmes, and case evidence that agent guardrails work in practice; an agent that says "unverified" at those points will be more reliable than one that sounds complete.
